**40. Detailed Policy and Plan Framework for Achieving SDG-3: Good Health and Well-Being through Institutional and Governmental Collaboration**

Field: Policy and Plans

Objective:

> • To develop and implement comprehensive, evidence-based health policies and institutional plans that strengthen healthcare delivery, promote preventive measures, and ensure holistic well-being across populations.
>
> • To align Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) and government initiatives under the United Nations' SDG-3 framework, focusing on maternal and child health, communicable and non-communicable disease control, and substance abuse prevention.

Key Activities

**1. Improving Maternal and Newborn Health and Preventing Stillbirths**

Purpose:

To reduce maternal mortality and ensure safe childbirth through awareness, capacity building, and access to healthcare.

Key Actions:

> • Policy Formulation:
>
> o Develop institutional policies aligned with National Health Mission (NHM), Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY), and Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA) to enhance maternal and newborn care.
>
> o Establish Maternal Health Cells within HEIs (especially medical and nursing colleges) to monitor community-level maternal health indicators.
>
> • Education and Training:
>
> o Integrate maternal and child health modules in medical, nursing, and social work curricula.
>
> o Provide hands-on training for students through internships at Primary Health Centres (PHCs), Community Health Centres (CHCs), and Anganwadi centres.
>
> • Community Outreach:
>
> o Conduct awareness programs for rural women on antenatal and postnatal care, safe delivery, and nutrition.
>
> o Collaborate with local panchayats and women's self-help groups (SHGs) for awareness on pregnancy care and immunization.
>
> • Research and Innovation:
>
> o Support research on causes of stillbirths, maternal anaemia, and newborn morbidity.
>
> o Promote innovation in low-cost medical devices and mobile health (mHealth) applications for remote maternal monitoring.
>
> • Monitoring:
>
> o Collect and analyze data on Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR) and Neonatal Mortality Rate (NMR) through institutional health audits.

**2. Preventing the Spread of Communicable Diseases (AIDS, Tuberculosis, Malaria, Hepatitis, and Water-Borne Diseases)**

Purpose:

To develop preventive strategies, strengthen awareness, and enhance community health resilience.

Key Actions:

> • Disease Control Policy:
>
> o Formulate institutional policies that emphasize infection control, immunization coverage, and disease reporting protocols.
>
> o Promote adherence to the National AIDS Control Programme (NACP) and Revised National Tuberculosis Control Programme (RNTCP) guidelines.
>
> • Awareness and Education:
>
> o Organize public health campaigns, lectures, and exhibitions on HIV/AIDS prevention, hygiene, and vaccination.
>
> o Develop and distribute IEC (Information, Education, Communication) materials in local languages.
>
> • Infrastructure and Sanitation:
>
> o Ensure safe drinking water, sanitation, and waste disposal systems within HEIs and neighboring communities.
>
> o Implement vector control initiatives such as fogging, cleanliness drives, and stagnant water elimination.
>
> • Healthcare Collaboration:
>
> o Partner with district health authorities and NGOs for screening camps, blood donation drives, and testing programs.
>
> o Support joint research on emerging diseases, antimicrobial resistance, and immunization effectiveness.
>
> • Technology Integration:
>
> o Use digital health tools for monitoring infection trends and disseminating alerts during disease outbreaks.

**3. Preventing Mortality from Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) and Promoting Mental Health and Well-Being**

Purpose:

To address the rising burden of lifestyle diseases and mental health challenges through education, preventive care, and behavioral change.

Key Actions:

> • Policy Development:
>
> o Establish institutional health promotion policies aligned with National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases and Stroke (NPCDCS).
>
> o Create a "Healthy Campus Initiative" promoting physical activity, nutrition, and emotional wellness.
>
> • Health Infrastructure:
>
> o Set up on-campus screening facilities for blood pressure, blood sugar, BMI, and cholesterol.
>
> o Introduce counselling and psychological support centres to address mental health, anxiety, and depression.
>
> • Education and Awareness:
>
> o Include Health and Lifestyle Education modules in undergraduate curricula.
>
> o Organize workshops on stress management, yoga, and mindfulness.
>
> o Conduct campaigns on anti-tobacco, anti-alcohol, and healthy eating practices.
>
> • Research:
>
> o Encourage faculty and students to research the impact of urbanization, diet, and stress on non-communicable diseases.
>
> o Develop data-driven intervention models for mental health management.
>
> • Monitoring and Evaluation:
>
> o Regularly assess the effectiveness of institutional health programs and publish annual "Health and Wellness Reports."

**4. Strengthening the Prevention and Treatment of Alcoholism and Narcotic Drug Abuse**

Purpose:

To create a drug-free and responsible academic environment through awareness, enforcement, and rehabilitation support.

Key Actions:

> • Policy Enforcement:
>
> o Develop and enforce "Zero-Tolerance Policies" against substance abuse on campuses.
>
> o Establish Anti-Drug Monitoring Committees and integrate them with local law enforcement units.
>
> • Awareness and Prevention:
>
> o Organize awareness workshops with local police, narcotics control bureaus, and NGOs highlighting the dangers of drug and alcohol abuse.
>
> o Conduct campaigns on International Day Against Drug Abuse (June 26) and World No Tobacco Day (May 31).
>
> • Counselling and Rehabilitation:
>
> o Establish Drug De-Addiction Counselling Centres in collaboration with rehabilitation and psychotherapy institutes.
>
> o Provide confidential psychological counselling, mentorship, and support for recovery.
>
> • Socio-Psychological Research:
>
> o Conduct studies on youth behavior, substance use patterns, and risk factors within HEIs.
>
> o Use findings to design targeted interventions and educational curricula.
>
> • Monitoring and Security:
>
> o Ensure police patrolling and surprise checks in hostels.
>
> o Encourage peer mentorship and anonymous reporting mechanisms to identify cases early.

Institutional Implementation Mechanism

> • Establishment of SDG-3 Steering Committees:
>
> o HEIs should form interdisciplinary committees including representatives from public health, psychology, social sciences, and administration to monitor SDG-3 activities.
>
> • Partnership and Networking:
>
> o Collaborate with Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, NITI Aayog, WHO, and UNDP for technical guidance.
>
> o Engage state health departments, district collectors, and municipal bodies for local implementation.
>
> • Monitoring and Reporting:
>
> o Develop annual SDG-3 progress reports summarizing health initiatives, research findings, and impact data.
>
> o Use Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) such as reduction in illness rates, participation in awareness programs, and improved well-being scores.
>
> • Capacity Building:
>
> o Organize faculty development programs (FDPs) on health education, policy implementation, and public health research methodologies.
>
> o Train student ambassadors as health leaders to carry forward SDG-3 goals within the community.

Expected Outcomes:

> • Strengthened institutional contribution toward achieving universal health and well-being goals.
>
> • Increased health literacy, preventive action, and responsible behavior among students and faculty.
>
> • Reduced incidence of maternal mortality, communicable diseases, NCDs, and substance abuse.
>
> • Enhanced interdisciplinary collaboration and evidence-based policymaking between HEIs and government agencies.
>
> • Development of a national network of health-conscious campuses contributing to sustainable societal well-being.

Conclusion:

> • Through comprehensive policy planning and execution, HEIs can become active partners in public health transformation.
>
> • The alignment of academic programs, research priorities, and institutional practices with SDG-3 fosters a culture of health, inclusivity, and sustainable living.
>
> • Such a holistic approach will enable India to progress toward the 2030 Sustainable Development Agenda, ensuring that education systems actively contribute to Good Health and Well-Being for All.

**41. Response and Resilience: Strengthening Preparedness and Emergency Health Response Systems in HEIs under SDG-3**

Field:

Response and Resilience

Objective:

> • To develop preparedness, response, and resilience mechanisms within Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) that ensure health safety, rapid response during emergencies, and continuity of essential services.
>
> • To align institutional emergency response systems with SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-Being) and the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction, promoting health resilience and disaster readiness at both campus and community levels.

Key Activities

**1. Design a Comprehensive Preparedness and Response Plan**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To ensure timely, coordinated, and efficient management of public health emergencies, natural disasters, pandemics, or accidents.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Formulate an Institutional Emergency Preparedness and Response Policy that outlines preventive, mitigation, and recovery strategies.
>
> o Develop a Disaster Management and Health Response Manual specifying roles, communication hierarchies, and resource allocation procedures.
>
> o Identify and classify potential hazards (e.g., pandemics, chemical leaks, fire hazards, floods, heatwaves, earthquakes) that can impact campus operations.
>
> o Establish Emergency Response Committees (ERCs) comprising faculty experts, medical staff, student volunteers, and administrative officials.
>
> o Create a Campus Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) to coordinate emergency activities, monitor response, and disseminate verified information.
>
> o Regularly conduct risk assessments and vulnerability mapping of campus facilities.
>
> o Integrate crisis communication protocols including SMS alerts, mobile apps, and website notifications for immediate updates during emergencies.

**2. Develop a Coordinated Mechanism for Implementation**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To ensure a well-organized and multi-sectoral response system that minimizes disruption and protects the health and safety of all stakeholders.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Establish coordination among campus departments (Health Centre, Security, Facilities, Administration) for unified action during crises.
>
> o Partner with local health authorities, police, disaster management agencies, and hospitals for technical and logistic support.
>
> o Assign specific responsibilities to sub-committees for medical assistance, logistics, evacuation, sanitation, and psychological support.
>
> o Conduct simulation exercises and emergency drills at least twice a year (fire safety, earthquake evacuation, medical emergencies).
>
> o Develop standard operating procedures (SOPs) for different emergencies including outbreaks, chemical spills, or mass casualty incidents.
>
> o Ensure that students and staff receive basic first aid and emergency management training.

**3. Ensure Procurement of Emergency Medical Supplies and Equipment**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To maintain immediate access to essential medical resources required during crises.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Maintain emergency stockpiles of first aid kits, disinfectants, PPE kits, thermal scanners, emergency medicines, and oxygen cylinders.
>
> o Establish contracts with local suppliers and pharmacies for rapid procurement during emergencies.
>
> o Equip campus health centres with ambulances, defibrillators, oxygen concentrators, and emergency beds.
>
> o Maintain backup power supply and water resources to sustain medical and sanitation services during emergencies.
>
> o Implement inventory management systems to track expiry, replenishment, and utilization of supplies.
>
> o Develop budgetary provisions for emergency procurement under the institutional annual health and safety budget.

**4. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Health Outcome Assessment**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To track survival rates, response efficiency, and health outcomes during and after emergency events.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Develop a data-driven monitoring system to assess morbidity, mortality, and recovery rates post-emergency.
>
> o Document lessons learned and conduct after-action reviews (AARs) to identify gaps and improve preparedness.
>
> o Implement a Health Resilience Dashboard for real-time monitoring of campus health indicators.
>
> o Collaborate with local health departments for epidemiological surveillance and post-crisis health assessments.
>
> o Integrate findings into future emergency response training and curriculum updates.
>
> o Publish institutional Annual Health Resilience Reports summarizing achievements, challenges, and impact metrics.

Institutional Best Practices and Integration:

> • Establish a Campus Safety and Resilience Cell (CSRC) to oversee implementation and compliance.
>
> • Integrate Emergency Preparedness and Health Safety modules into undergraduate and postgraduate curricula (especially in public health, nursing, and management).
>
> • Engage student volunteers as community health responders through programs like National Service Scheme (NSS) and Red Cross Youth Units.
>
> • Collaborate with district disaster management authorities (DDMA) to host training and certification programs.
>
> • Ensure gender-sensitive, inclusive emergency plans, addressing the needs of women, persons with disabilities, and vulnerable groups.

Expected Outcomes:

> • Improved institutional capacity for emergency health response, disaster management, and rapid coordination.
>
> • Enhanced resilience and recovery systems, reducing mortality and morbidity during crises.
>
> • Availability of critical emergency infrastructure and medical supplies at all times.
>
> • Data-informed decision-making leading to continuous improvement of response mechanisms.
>
> • Increased public confidence in HEIs as safe, health-secure, and socially responsible institutions.

Broader Impact on SDG-3 and Related Goals:

> • Directly contributes to SDG 3.d (Strengthen the capacity of all countries for early warning, risk reduction, and management of national and global health risks).
>
> • Supports SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) by fostering resilient institutional environments.
>
> • Aligns with SDG 13 (Climate Action) through proactive adaptation and disaster risk mitigation.
>
> • Promotes SDG 17 (Partnerships for the Goals) through coordination among government bodies, HEIs, and community organizations.

Conclusion:

> • A well-structured Preparedness and Response Plan transforms HEIs into centres of resilience, safety, and sustainable well-being.
>
> • Through comprehensive planning, coordinated implementation, and effective monitoring, institutions can mitigate the effects of health emergencies while ensuring the continuity of education, safety, and public trust.
>
> • These efforts align directly with SDG-3: Good Health and Well-Being, enabling HEIs to act as both first responders and long-term contributors to national health resilience and disaster preparedness frameworks.

**42. Investments: Strengthening Health Systems and Research Infrastructure under SDG-3**

Field:

Investments and Resource Mobilization

Objective:

> • To ensure that adequate financial, human, and infrastructural resources are allocated by Higher Education Institutions (HEIs), national governments, and international agencies toward achieving SDG-3: Good Health and Well-Being.
>
> • To prioritize sustained investments in public health systems, research capacity building, and disease control programs, especially in areas such as AIDS, tuberculosis (TB), and substance abuse.
>
> • To establish a resilient funding ecosystem that supports innovation, policy development, and equitable access to quality healthcare.

Key Activities

**1. Strengthening Health Systems through Domestic and Institutional Investments**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To develop robust, inclusive, and sustainable health systems that ensure universal access to affordable, quality healthcare.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Governments and HEIs should allocate dedicated budgetary provisions for improving institutional health infrastructure (campus health centres, laboratories, telemedicine units, and fitness facilities).
>
> o Promote investment in primary healthcare networks, preventive care, and community outreach programs to enhance accessibility in rural and underserved areas.
>
> o Modernize medical education infrastructure by upgrading simulation labs, digital health systems, and emergency training facilities.
>
> o Encourage private sector partnerships (PPP models) to co-finance institutional healthcare services and technology integration.
>
> o Ensure that a minimum percentage of annual HEI budgets is earmarked for health-related activities under SDG-3 compliance reporting.
>
> o Support faculty development and staff training programs in health sciences, nursing, public health management, and environmental health.
>
> o Develop a centralized health data repository within HEIs to track health outcomes, vaccination coverage, and preventive initiatives.
>
> o Promote green and sustainable health infrastructure, ensuring energy efficiency, waste management, and compliance with environmental safety standards.

**2. Strengthening Research Funding Infrastructure for Major Diseases (AIDS, Tuberculosis, Substance Abuse, etc.)**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To advance research, innovation, and policy development addressing key global and national health challenges.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Establish institutional research funds dedicated to communicable diseases such as HIV/AIDS, TB, malaria, hepatitis, and water-borne infections.
>
> o Promote multi-disciplinary research clusters connecting medicine, biotechnology, data science, and behavioral sciences.
>
> o Allocate seed grants and fellowships to young researchers and postgraduate scholars for projects on epidemiology, disease modelling, and health technology innovation.
>
> o Strengthen partnerships with national funding bodies such as the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR), Department of Biotechnology (DBT), Department of Science and Technology (DST), and National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO).
>
> o Engage international funding sources such as WHO, UNDP, USAID, The Global Fund, and the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation to co-finance research on global health threats.
>
> o Develop centres of excellence in HEIs focusing on public health research, infectious diseases, and mental health interventions.
>
> o Implement transparent research grant management systems with online tracking and periodic audits to ensure accountability and effective utilization of funds.
>
> o Encourage industry-academia collaborations for developing affordable vaccines, diagnostics, and treatment tools for marginalized populations.
>
> o Support longitudinal and community-based studies on substance abuse, alcohol dependency, and rehabilitation outcomes.
>
> o Publish policy briefs and evidence-based reports to support government decision-making in disease prevention and health promotion.

**3. Building Sustainable Financing Partnerships**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To ensure a diversified and long-term funding ecosystem that sustains health and research programs beyond short-term projects.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Establish tripartite partnerships between HEIs, government ministries, and international development agencies to co-finance health programs.
>
> o Introduce public health innovation funds within HEIs for supporting student-led startups and social enterprises working on health solutions.
>
> o Promote Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) collaborations with industries to fund awareness, vaccination, and community health drives.
>
> o Encourage philanthropic contributions and endowments for scholarships, research chairs, and infrastructure development.
>
> o Facilitate international exchange programs for research scholars and faculty to access global funding opportunities and knowledge networks.
>
> o Adopt SDG-based budgeting frameworks that align institutional spending with health impact indicators and sustainability outcomes.

Expected Outcomes:

> • Enhanced financial stability and resource efficiency within HEIs and public health systems.
>
> • Creation of world-class research facilities and innovation hubs focused on communicable and non-communicable diseases.
>
> • Strengthened national capacity for disease prevention, control, and management through evidence-based research.
>
> • Increased collaboration between academia, industry, and government agencies, fostering a culture of shared responsibility.
>
> • Improved access to healthcare, medicines, and preventive services, reducing health inequalities and mortality rates.

Broader Impact on SDG-3 and Related Goals:

> • Directly supports SDG 3.8 (Universal Health Coverage) and SDG 3.d (Strengthening Health Risk Management) through sustained investment.
>
> • Contributes to SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure) by building research and innovation capacity in the health sector.
>
> • Aligns with SDG 17 (Partnerships for the Goals) by fostering domestic and international cooperation in financing health research and systems strengthening.
>
> • Supports SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) by ensuring equitable access to healthcare and research opportunities.

Conclusion:

> • Strategic and sustained investments in health systems and research infrastructure are foundational for achieving SDG-3 targets.
>
> • By allocating sufficient domestic and international resources, HEIs and governments can transform health research into policy impact, innovation, and community well-being.
>
> • Strengthening funding mechanisms for AIDS, tuberculosis, substance abuse, and other health challenges not only improves public health outcomes but also enhances national resilience and global health security.
>
> • This financial and policy commitment will ensure that progress toward Good Health and Well-Being is inclusive, science-driven, and sustainable for generations to come.

**43. Quality of Care: Implementing WHO Standards for Respectful and Effective Patient Care in Alignment with SDG-3**

Field:

Quality of Care

Objective:

> • To ensure that all Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) and healthcare providers adhere to World Health Organization (WHO) standards of safe, respectful, and effective patient care.
>
> • To establish a continuous learning and quality improvement system that evaluates, monitors, and enhances patient experiences and clinical outcomes.
>
> • To integrate ethics, empathy, and accountability into the culture of healthcare delivery and education, in line with SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-Being) and SDG-16 (Peace, Justice, and Strong Institutions).

Key Activities

**1. Implement WHO Standards for Respectful and Effective Patient Care**

Purpose:

To align institutional health practices with global standards of patient safety, dignity, and evidence-based medicine.

Actions:

> • Adopt WHO's Quality of Care Framework, which emphasizes:
>
> o Safety: Avoiding harm to patients during the provision of care.
>
> o Effectiveness: Providing services based on scientific knowledge and best practices.
>
> o Timeliness: Reducing unnecessary delays in treatment and diagnosis.
>
> o Efficiency: Optimizing resources without compromising care quality.
>
> o Equity: Providing care that does not vary due to gender, ethnicity, geography, or socioeconomic status.
>
> o Patient-Centeredness: Respecting the values, preferences, and needs of individuals.
>
> • Ensure compliance with WHO's "Standards for Improving Quality of Maternal and Newborn Care in Health Facilities" for medical HEIs and affiliated hospitals.
>
> • Develop and display Patient Rights and Responsibilities Charters across institutional healthcare facilities.
>
> • Train healthcare staff and students on humanistic and compassionate care, focusing on communication, empathy, and ethics.
>
> • Implement safe clinical practices such as infection control, rational drug use, and standard operating procedures (SOPs) for diagnostics and treatment.
>
> • Establish feedback mechanisms for patients to report satisfaction, concerns, or grievances anonymously.
>
> • Incorporate WHO's "Five Moments for Hand Hygiene" and Patient Safety Protocols into daily practice within healthcare centres and teaching hospitals.
>
> • Integrate simulation-based learning modules in medical and nursing curricula to train students in respectful care delivery.

**2. Establish Systems for Continuous Learning and Quality Improvement**

Purpose:

To foster an institutional culture of accountability, reflection, and evidence-based improvement in patient care.

Actions:

> • Develop an institutional Quality Assurance and Patient Safety Cell (QAPSC) responsible for monitoring compliance with WHO quality standards.
>
> • Conduct periodic clinical audits and quality reviews to evaluate patient outcomes, adherence to guidelines, and staff performance.
>
> • Implement a Patient Experience Monitoring System (PEMS) that collects feedback data from patients and their families.
>
> • Encourage learning from adverse events through structured review meetings and “no-blame” error-reporting systems.
>
> • Establish continuing medical education (CME) programs and workshops for healthcare workers focusing on WHO’s best practices.
>
> • Introduce a Quality of Care Indicator Dashboard to track infection rates, readmissions, waiting times, and patient satisfaction scores.
>
> • Conduct case-based learning sessions where medical students analyze real-life healthcare challenges and propose solutions.
>
> • Collaborate with national accreditation agencies such as NABH (National Accreditation Board for Hospitals) or NAAC (for health colleges) to maintain external validation of care quality.
>
> • Create a digital knowledge-sharing repository where faculty and practitioners document innovative solutions and case studies related to patient safety and quality care.

**3. Promote Respectful Care and Ethical Standards in Clinical Practice**

Purpose:

To uphold patient dignity, confidentiality, and informed consent as core elements of healthcare delivery.

Actions:

> • Implement gender-sensitive and inclusive care practices, ensuring that marginalized populations receive equitable treatment.
>
> • Enforce ethical review processes for all clinical research, patient interactions, and experimental treatments.
>
> • Provide training in medical ethics and patient communication for all healthcare workers and students.
>
> • Encourage the adoption of WHO’s “Respectful Maternity Care Charter” and “Global Patient Safety Action Plan 2021–2030.”
>
> • Promote psychological safety for patients and staff by ensuring that care environments are non-discriminatory and supportive.
>
> • Develop standard consent forms, patient information leaflets, and grievance redressal systems that are easy to understand and multilingual.
>
> • Conduct annual workshops on empathy, ethical dilemmas, and conflict resolution for clinical staff and students.

**4. Strengthen Institutional Mechanisms for Monitoring and Evaluation**

Purpose:

To ensure transparency, accountability, and continuous improvement in patient care systems.

Actions:

> • Integrate WHO’s “Health System Building Blocks Framework” (Service Delivery, Workforce, Information Systems, Financing, Leadership, and Access to Medicines) into institutional assessment tools.
>
> • Conduct biannual external audits of healthcare units and publish Quality of Care Performance Reports.
>
> • Include Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) such as patient satisfaction rate, adverse event frequency, and compliance with clinical guidelines.
>
> • Utilize digital health technologies (Electronic Health Records, Telemedicine platforms) for accurate documentation and decision support.
>
> • Engage patients and communities in co-designing healthcare improvement initiatives, promoting participatory governance.
>
> • Establish peer-review committees to evaluate professional conduct and service standards among healthcare providers.

Expected Outcomes:

> • Enhanced trust and satisfaction among patients through dignified and ethical care.
>
