# Abstract

A Financial Operations and Management Policy in Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) serves as a comprehensive framework designed to ensure sound financial management, accountability, and long-term sustainability. Its central purpose is to provide clear and standardized guidelines for all financial activities, thereby strengthening financial controls, reducing risks of mismanagement, and enabling institutions to comply with regulatory requirements while pursuing their strategic objectives. Built upon fundamental accounting principles such as transparency, consistency, prudence, accrual, and accountability, the policy establishes a uniform basis for recording, reporting, and auditing financial transactions. This ensures that financial practices are not only reliable and accurate but also credible in the eyes of all stakeholders, including students, faculty, governing bodies, donors, and regulators.

The key objectives of the Finance Policy are multifaceted and designed to support both day-to-day operations and long-term institutional goals. One primary objective is to evolve and maintain strong financial management systems that safeguard against misuse of funds, while promoting efficiency, accountability, and effectiveness in all transactions. Equally important is the rational planning, coordination, and control of revenue inflows and expenditures, ensuring that income and expenses remain balanced, essential functions are prioritized, and allocations are aligned with the mission of the institution. These provisions extend to core areas such as academic programs, research, student and staff development, capital projects, and property maintenance. The policy also emphasizes effective financial management as a means of achieving stability, resilience, and academic excellence in an increasingly competitive higher education environment.

Resource mobilization is another vital focus of the policy, with specific guidelines established for diversifying and strengthening financial inflows. Institutions are encouraged to draw from multiple sources, including tuition and examination fees, government grants, research funding, consultancy services, alumni contributions, donations, sponsorships, and revenue-generating activities. By tapping into such diverse streams, HEIs can reduce overreliance on a single source of funding and build a more sustainable financial base. At the same time, strict compliance with legal requirements, government regulations, and accounting standards remains non-negotiable, covering areas such as taxation, employee benefits, procurement practices, and auditing protocols. The adoption of standardized accounting procedures—such as accrual-based accounting and the double-entry system—further enhances the accuracy and reliability of financial records, enabling transparent reporting and timely disclosures.

The scope of the Finance Policy is broad and encompasses all major aspects of institutional financial management. This includes the mobilization of financial resources through various channels, the allocation and utilization of funds across competing institutional priorities, and the assurance of compliance and accountability. Specific attention is paid to infrastructure development, academic enrichment, research activities, faculty development, student support services, and operational expenses. To ensure adherence to best practices, the policy mandates compliance with legal and statutory requirements, regular internal and external audits, financial reporting in accordance with national and international norms, and prompt submission of reports to governing councils and accreditation agencies.

Cash inflows detailed in the policy highlight the diverse funding opportunities available to HEIs. These include student-related income such as tuition, examination, and other prescribed fees; government and non-government grants provided for academic schemes, research, and infrastructure development; consultancy revenue generated from institutional expertise; alumni contributions and philanthropic donations; corporate sponsorships; and interest earned from deposits or investments. Conversely, cash outflows outlined in the policy cover the full spectrum of institutional needs. These range from overheads and administrative costs—including staff salaries, utilities, and operational expenses—to infrastructure development and maintenance. They also encompass academic and research-related activities such as seminars, grants, collaborations, and publications; faculty and staff development initiatives including training and higher education support; student scholarships, welfare measures, and extracurricular activities; as well as procurement of books, equipment, and other learning resources essential for quality education.

Monitoring and implementing the Finance Policy is a structured responsibility, shared across multiple layers of institutional governance. The Registrar or Principal, together with the Finance Officer, plays a central role in ensuring day-to-day compliance with the policy. Their duties include overseeing payment approvals in line with budgets, preparing and finalizing institutional budgets that integrate both short-term needs and long-term priorities, and monitoring financial performance against planned targets. The Governing Body provides supervisory oversight, particularly in approving major budget revisions or unforeseen expenditures. Meanwhile, accountability mechanisms are reinforced through rigorous internal and external audits, periodic reporting to stakeholders, and corrective actions for irregularities. The Finance Officer, in particular, has a critical role in maintaining accounts, ensuring proper revenue collection, preparing budgets, overseeing audits, and addressing financial discrepancies with diligence and transparency.

Ultimately, the Financial Operations and Management Policy in Higher Education Institutions is far more than an administrative guideline—it is a strategic instrument for institutional growth, resilience, and credibility. By embedding sound financial management practices, the policy ensures that resources are managed prudently, efficiently, and ethically in alignment with the mission of quality education and research advancement. A well-structured Finance Policy not only sustains day-to-day institutional operations but also contributes to broader national and societal objectives, ensuring that HEIs remain financially robust, academically excellent, and socially relevant in the face of evolving challenges.

# 1. Introduction

A Financial Operations and Management Policy in Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) serves as a comprehensive framework designed to ensure sound financial management, accountability, and sustainability. Its primary aim is to provide clear guidelines for handling all financial activities in a structured and transparent manner. By doing so, it strengthens financial controls, reduces the risk of mismanagement or irregularities, and helps the institution meet both regulatory requirements and long-term strategic objectives.

This policy is particularly important because universities and colleges operate in a complex environment that involves multiple financial streams—such as tuition fees, research grants, government funding, endowments, consultancy income, donations, and other revenue sources. At the same time, they must manage diverse expenditures including salaries, infrastructure development, maintenance, student services, research support, and community outreach. A well-defined Finance Policy ensures that these activities are managed with discipline, efficiency, and integrity.

The Finance Policy is built upon the application of fundamental accounting principles, such as transparency, consistency, prudence, accrual, and accountability. These principles provide a uniform basis for recording, reporting, and auditing financial transactions, which is essential for comparability and reliability in institutional financial reports. By aligning financial management with recognized accounting concepts, the institution not only ensures compliance with statutory requirements but also builds trust with stakeholders, including students, faculty, staff, donors, funding agencies, and government bodies.

The specific purpose of such a Financial Policy is to establish general guidelines that govern financial decision-making within the institution. These guidelines typically include budgeting procedures, approval hierarchies for expenditures, internal control systems, asset management protocols, and monitoring mechanisms. For example, policies may define how budgets are prepared annually, who authorizes expenses, how procurement is carried out, and what systems are in place for preventing fraud or misuse of resources.

Additionally, a Finance Policy emphasizes the importance of long-term financial planning to ensure institutional sustainability. This includes maintaining adequate reserves, monitoring cash flow, and ensuring funds are allocated to strategic priorities such as academic excellence, research, innovation, student support, and infrastructure development. Effective financial management also enables the institution to respond to unexpected challenges, such as fluctuations in student enrollment, changes in government funding policies, or emergencies requiring immediate financial interventions.

In the context of HEIs, the Finance Policy also plays an educational role by embedding financial responsibility into the institutional culture. It promotes financial literacy among administrators, faculty, and staff, ensuring that all stakeholders are aware of their roles in maintaining fiscal discipline. Furthermore, it fosters accountability and transparency, which are increasingly critical in accreditation processes (NAAC, NBA, QS I-GAUGE) and rankings, where effective governance and financial management are key evaluation parameters.

Ultimately, the Finance Policy is not just an administrative document but a strategic instrument that underpins institutional growth and sustainability. By ensuring that financial resources are managed prudently, efficiently, and ethically, it helps higher education institutions achieve their broader mission of delivering quality education, advancing research, and contributing meaningfully to society.

# 2. Objectives of the Finance Policy

- To evolve, maintain, and promote sound financial management systems, procedures, and controls

  - Establish a robust framework of financial rules, internal checks, and approval mechanisms to prevent mismanagement or misuse of funds.

  - Implement procedures that ensure accountability, efficiency, and effectiveness in financial transactions.

  - Regularly review and update financial practices to align with global standards and institutional needs.

- To plan, rationalize, co-ordinate, and control revenue inflows and expenditures of the University

  - Develop annual and long-term financial plans that balance income with expenses.

  - Rationalize expenditure to prioritize essential functions and minimize wastage.

  - Ensure alignment of financial allocation with the University’s mission and vision.

  - Specific provisions include:

    - **Academic, research, and outreach activities/programs:** Guaranteeing adequate funds for teaching, innovative research, and community engagement.

    - **Student development, welfare, and performance:** Ensuring financial support for scholarships, training, extracurricular activities, health, and counseling.

    - **Welfare and development of teaching and non-teaching staff:** Allocating resources for capacity building, training, professional growth, and welfare initiatives.

    - **Capital budgeting decisions:** Making informed financial decisions about infrastructure development, long-term investments, and expansion projects.

    - **Procurement, repair, maintenance, safety, and security of University property and assets:** Managing physical resources responsibly to maintain sustainability and operational efficiency.

- Effective Financial Management

  - Manage financial resources efficiently to support strategic objectives, long-term stability, and academic excellence.

  - Integrate financial planning with institutional growth strategies.

- Guidelines for Resource Mobilization

  - Establish clear procedures for mobilizing funds from diverse sources, including:

    - Tuition and other student fees.

    - Government grants and subsidies.

    - Research funding from public/private bodies.

    - Donations, sponsorships, and alumni contributions.

    - Revenue-generating activities such as consultancy, training programs, and intellectual property commercialization.

  - Encourage partnerships with industry, government, and NGOs for resource sharing.

- Compliance with Legal Requirements

  - Ensure strict adherence to national laws, government guidelines, UGC/AICTE directives, and accounting standards applicable to educational institutions.

  - Maintain transparency and compliance in areas such as taxation, employee benefits, statutory reporting, and audits.

- Standard Accounting Procedures

  - Maintain uniform and consistent accounting practices to ensure accurate and reliable financial records.

  - Follow accrual-based accounting, double-entry systems, and other recognized accounting standards.

  - Provide stakeholders with reliable data for decision-making and audits.

- Financial Transparency

  - Promote openness and accountability in financial operations through regular reporting, disclosures, and audits.

  - Present financial statements and budgetary performance reports to governing bodies, stakeholders, and accreditation agencies.

  - Foster trust among students, parents, staff, donors, and funding agencies by demonstrating ethical and transparent financial practices.

# 3. Scope of the Finance Policy

This policy is comprehensive in nature and applies to all financial transactions, operations, and activities carried out within Higher Education Institutions (HEIs). It covers every aspect of revenue generation, fund allocation, expenditure control, compliance, and reporting to ensure effective and transparent financial management.

- Mobilization of Financial Resources

  - Applies to all avenues through which HEIs generate income, including:

    - **Student fees:** tuition, examination, hostel, and other institutional charges.

    - **Government grants and subsidies:** funding support from central/state governments, UGC, AICTE, and other regulatory bodies.

    - **Consultancy services and sponsored projects:** income generated through faculty consultancy, industry collaborations, and externally funded research projects.

    - **Alumni contributions and donations:** philanthropic support and endowments received from alumni and well-wishers.

    - **Sponsorships:** financial support for events, conferences, research, and student activities from corporate or social organizations.

    - **Other income sources:** such as interest on deposits, investments, intellectual property commercialization, and extension activities.

- Allocation and Utilization of Funds

  - The policy governs how resources are planned, distributed, and utilized across institutional priorities, ensuring alignment with strategic objectives. Areas covered include:

    - **Infrastructure development:** construction, repair, renovation, and maintenance of academic and administrative facilities.

    - **Academic enhancements:** investment in libraries, laboratories, ICT facilities, and digital learning platforms.

    - **Faculty development:** training, workshops, conferences, research grants, and welfare measures for teaching staff.

    - **Student support and welfare:** scholarships, financial aid, extracurricular activities, health and wellness programs, and placement initiatives.

    - **Research and innovation:** funding for publications, patents, projects, and collaboration with industry or research bodies.

    - **Operational and administrative expenses:** salaries, utilities, safety, security, and general maintenance of institutional assets.

- Compliance and Accountability

  - The policy ensures adherence to legal, regulatory, and statutory requirements governing financial practices in HEIs, such as:

    - **Auditing standards:** internal audits, statutory audits, and compliance audits.

    - **Financial reporting norms:** preparation and submission of financial statements in accordance with accepted accounting standards and government guidelines.

    - **Statutory obligations:** compliance with taxation laws, employee benefit schemes, provident funds, pension contributions, and procurement rules.

  - Promotes accountability and transparency through regular disclosures, governance oversight, and timely submission of reports to governing councils, funding agencies, and accreditation bodies.

# 4. Cash Inflow

Cash inflows represent the various streams of income through which Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) generate funds to support their academic, research, infrastructure, and administrative functions. These inflows ensure financial stability and sustainability, enabling the institution to meet both its operational needs and long-term strategic objectives. The major sources include:

- 1\. Student Fees

  - **Tuition Fees:** The primary source of revenue for most HEIs, particularly from self-financing courses and professional programs.

  - **Examination Fees:** Income generated from conducting internal and external examinations.

  - **Other Prescribed Fees:** Includes charges for admission, laboratory use, library access, hostel facilities, transport, and student services.

  - **Significance:** Ensures consistent revenue to meet recurring expenses such as faculty salaries, maintenance, and student support.

- 2\. Government and Non-Government Grants

  - **Central/State Government Grants:** Funding received for specific schemes, institutional development, and academic quality enhancement.

  - **UGC, DST, ICSSR, AICTE Projects:** Competitive research and development grants provided to faculty and departments for academic advancement.

  - **Infrastructure Development Support:** Grants for construction of academic blocks, laboratories, ICT centers, and student facilities.

  - **Development Funds of MPs/MLAs:** Contributions allocated from local representatives’ development funds for infrastructure or equipment procurement.

  - **Significance:** Provides financial support for growth beyond the institution’s internal income capacity.

- 3\. Consultancy and Alumni Funding

  - **Consultancy Services:** Revenue earned through faculty expertise offered to industries, government bodies, or NGOs in the form of research, training, testing, and advisory services.

  - **Alumni Contributions:** Donations, endowments, or sponsorships from alumni to support scholarships, infrastructure, research projects, and student development activities.

  - **Significance:** Strengthens the bond between the institution, industry, and alumni while generating additional revenue streams.

- 4\. Sponsorship Grants

  - **Corporate Entities and Philanthropists:** Financial support for academic events, cultural programs, research collaborations, and campus infrastructure.

  - **Organizational Sponsorships:** Grants obtained from professional bodies, NGOs, and CSR initiatives of companies for supporting innovation, entrepreneurship, and skill-building programs.

  - **Significance:** Builds partnerships, enhances institutional visibility, and supports activities that may not be fully covered by institutional funds.

- 5\. Interest Income

  - **Bank Deposits:** Revenue earned from fixed deposits, savings accounts, and other bank instruments where surplus funds are invested.

  - **Investments:** Returns generated from endowment funds, bonds, or other approved financial instruments.

  - **Significance:** Provides a supplementary source of income, ensuring financial security and liquidity for future institutional requirements.

# 5. Cash Outflow

Cash outflows represent the expenditure patterns of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) that ensure smooth functioning, academic excellence, and institutional growth. Effective financial management requires careful allocation of resources across operational, academic, and developmental needs to balance day-to-day requirements with long-term sustainability. Major categories include:

- 1\. Overheads and Administrative Expenses

  - **Salaries and Wages:** Payment to teaching and non-teaching staff, contractual employees, and support staff.

  - **Utilities:** Expenses on electricity, water, internet, telephone, and fuel.

  - **Administrative Costs:** Stationery, office supplies, travel, insurance, and examination-related expenses.

  - **Maintenance of Records and Systems:** Costs of ERP systems, accounting software, and digital platforms.

  - **Significance:** Ensures uninterrupted day-to-day operations and institutional governance.

- 2\. Infrastructure Augmentation and Maintenance

  - **Upgradation of Facilities:** Modernization of classrooms, laboratories, libraries, and ICT-enabled learning spaces.

  - **New Construction:** Academic blocks, hostels, auditoriums, and sports complexes.

  - **Maintenance and Repairs:** Routine upkeep of buildings, furniture, campus roads, water systems, and electrical fittings.

  - **Safety and Security Systems:** Surveillance, fire safety systems, and security services.

  - **Significance:** Supports long-term campus development and sustainability.

- 3\. Academic and Research Expenses

  - **Seminars, Workshops, and Conferences:** Funding for academic events that enhance learning and global exposure.

  - **Research Grants:** Support for publications, patents, and interdisciplinary projects.

  - **Collaborations and MoUs:** Partnership expenses with national and international institutions.

  - **ICT and Digital Resources:** Investment in e-learning platforms, software licenses, and online databases.

  - **Significance:** Enhances academic reputation, research capacity, and innovation ecosystem.

- 4\. Faculty and Staff Development

  - **Training Programs:** Workshops on pedagogy, technology adoption, and subject expertise.

  - **Higher Education Support:** Sponsorships for advanced degrees, certifications, and research fellowships.

  - **Conferences and Exchange Programs:** Financial assistance for attending national and international academic events.

  - **Skill Development:** Soft skills, leadership training, and management development programs.

  - **Significance:** Builds institutional capacity and strengthens human resource quality.

- 5\. Student Support and Scholarships

  - **Merit-Based Scholarships:** Awards to academically outstanding students.

  - **Need-Based Financial Aid:** Assistance to students from disadvantaged socio-economic backgrounds.

  - **Fellowships and Grants:** Support for student research, innovation, and entrepreneurship.

  - **Emergency Aid and Welfare Funds:** Financial support for unforeseen circumstances.

  - **Significance:** Promotes inclusivity, equity, and access to education.

- 6\. Cultural and Sports Activities

  - **Cultural Programs:** Funding for festivals, exhibitions, and performing arts.

  - **Sports Infrastructure:** Development of grounds, gymnasiums, and purchase of sports equipment.

  - **Student Clubs and Societies:** Support for extracurricular initiatives such as debate clubs, innovation labs, and community service activities.

  - **Inter-University Participation:** Sponsorship for national and international competitions.

  - **Significance:** Promotes holistic development, team spirit, and leadership among students.

- 7\. Purchase of Books, Equipment, and Learning Resources

  - **Library Development:** Acquisition of textbooks, reference books, journals, and e-resources.

  - **Laboratory Equipment:** Procurement of instruments, chemicals, and materials for science and engineering labs.

  - **ICT Resources:** Computers, projectors, smart boards, and servers.

  - **Digital Learning Platforms:** Subscriptions to e-libraries, online journals, and educational databases.

  - **Significance:** Strengthens academic quality and enhances learning outcomes.

# 6. Monitoring of Policy Implementation

The effective execution of the Finance Policy requires a structured monitoring mechanism to ensure accountability, transparency, and adherence to institutional goals. The Registrar/Principal and the Finance Officer hold primary responsibility for implementing and supervising the Resource Mobilization Plan, supported by oversight from the Governing Body.

- Daily Payment Approvals

  - The Finance Officer is tasked with reviewing and authorizing day-to-day payments.

  - This ensures that all expenditures are consistent with approved budgetary allocations and comply with financial regulations, procurement norms, and internal control procedures.

  - Helps prevent unauthorized spending and maintains discipline in cash flow management.

- Budget Finalization

  - The Governing Body plays a central role in approving the annual budget, which is prepared based on projected cash inflows, anticipated revenues, and planned expenditure commitments.

  - Budgeting integrates both short-term operational needs and long-term strategic priorities of the institution.

  - This process ensures financial planning is realistic, sustainable, and aligned with institutional development goals.

- Financial Control and Monitoring

  - Continuous monitoring of financial performance against budgetary targets is conducted to assess institutional financial health.

  - Periodic reports (monthly/quarterly/annual) are prepared by the Finance Officer and submitted to the Registrar/Principal and Governing Body.

  - Deviations from budget estimates are identified early, allowing timely corrective action to maintain stability and efficiency.

  - Monitoring also includes tracking compliance with statutory requirements, grants utilization, and audit recommendations.

- Major Rectifications

  - Any significant deviations, unforeseen expenditures, or required adjustments to the financial plan must be presented to the Governing Body for approval.

  - This ensures that financial stability is safeguarded and decisions are taken with full accountability.

  - Examples include reallocating funds for new infrastructure projects, responding to unexpected crises (e.g., natural calamities, urgent repairs), or revising staff development budgets.

- Accountability and Reporting

  - Annual financial statements are audited internally and externally to validate accuracy, transparency, and compliance with accounting standards.

  - Reports are submitted to statutory authorities, funding agencies, and accreditation bodies (NAAC/NBA) as required.

  - Transparency is reinforced through disclosures to stakeholders, including faculty, students, and donors, thereby strengthening trust and institutional credibility.

# 7. Powers and Duties of the Finance Officer

**1.** Appointment and Eligibility:

- The Finance Officer (F.O.) shall be appointed according to the provisions of the institution’s regulations. The salary and allowances of the Finance Officer will be determined by the Governing Body.

**2.** Interim Appointment:

- In the event that the office of the Finance Officer is vacant, the duties of the Finance Officer will be performed by an Administrative Officer appointed by the principal.

**3.** Accounting Responsibilities:

- The Finance Officer is responsible for maintaining the accounts of the college and providing advice on all matters related to income and expenditure.

**4.** Secretary to the Finance Committee:

- The Finance Officer serves as the Secretary of the Finance Committee, facilitating the smooth operation and reporting of the committee’s activities.

**5.** Preparation of Accounts and Budget Estimates:

- The Finance Officer is responsible for:

  - Preparing the Annual Accounts for the previous year.

  - Preparing the Budget Estimates for the next financial year and the Revised Estimates for the current year.

  - Submitting these documents to the Board of Management (BOM) through the Finance Committee.

**6.** Specific Duties:

- **Budget Oversight:** Ensure that the limits set in the budget for both recurring and non-recurring expenditure are adhered to, and that funds are only used for their designated purposes, subject to the Finance Rules of the institution.

- **Monitoring Unspent Balances:** Keep track of any unspent balances under various budget heads and bring them to the notice of the principal.

- **Bank Balances and Investments:** Regularly monitor the college’s bank balances and investment status and report them to the principal.

- **Revenue Collection:** Oversee the progress of revenue collection and recommend effective methods to avoid delays or errors.

- **Audit:** Ensure that the college’s accounts are audited regularly as per the Board of Management’s prescribed procedure. The Audit Report and any audit objections must be presented to the BOM along with appropriate explanations.

- **Addressing Financial Irregularities:** Investigate unauthorized expenditure or other financial irregularities, demand explanations from employees responsible, and recommend disciplinary action if needed.

- **Information Requests:** Request financial information or returns from any office or department deemed necessary for carrying out the Finance Officer’s duties.

- **No Dues Certificates:** Issue "No Dues Certificates" to employees being relieved or retiring after verifying their financial status.

**7.** Receipt Issuance:

- The receipts issued by the Finance Officer or any employee authorized by the principal for payments made to the college shall be valid proof of the transaction and shall serve as a sufficient discharge for the payment.

**8.** Other Functions:

- The Finance Officer may perform other functions as assigned by the Governing Body, the Principal, or as specified in the Rules or Regulations of the institution.

**9.** Financial Authority:

- The Finance Officer has the authority to pass bills and issue cheques for payments on behalf of the college. In the absence of the Finance Officer, this responsibility is delegated to the Administrative Officer (AO) or any other designated officer, who may also have the power to pass bills and sign cheques.

# 8. Fee Payments by Students

**1.** Payment Deadline:

- All students are required to pay their tuition and other fees in full within one month from the beginning of each academic year.

**2.** Delayed or Part Payments:

- Delayed payments or part payments must be approved by the principal. If no approval is obtained, the fee payment will be considered as unpaid.

- The principal may permit delayed or part payments for a maximum period of 15 days only. After this period, fees will be considered overdue.

**3.** Non-Refundable Fees:

- Fees, once paid, cannot be refunded or adjusted in any manner for reasons such as:

  - Discontinuation of studies

  - Detention

  - Approved leave

  - Any other similar reason.

**4.** Annual Payment:

- Fees paid by the students will be considered for the entire academic year and cannot be adjusted or refunded for any specific semester.

**5.** Additional Fees:

- **Extra Training Fees:** Students shall pay separately for any extra training provided that enhances the skills required by the industry.

- **Transport Charges and Hostel Fees:** These will be paid separately in addition to tuition fees and any fees for extra training.

- **Caution Deposit:** Students are required to pay a caution deposit. This deposit will be refunded when the student leaves the college, after deducting charges for:

  - ID card

  - Industrial Visits

  - Alumni membership

  - Souvenir charges

  - Any outstanding dues in college labs.

# 9. Funds from External Organizations

**1.** Approach to External Funding Sources:

- External organizations such as the World Bank, AICTE, UGC, DST, CSIR and other government or international bodies are critical sources for securing financial support for the institution's growth and development.

- The institution will initiate formal applications and proposals to these organizations for obtaining funds dedicated to specific projects and needs.

**2.** Specialized Infrastructure Projects:

- **Excellence Centers:** The funds may be sought to establish or upgrade centers of excellence in specialized fields such as research, innovation, or specific academic disciplines.

# 14. Fixed Assets Policy

## Introduction

- Role of Fixed Assets

  - Fixed Assets form the backbone of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs), enabling them to generate academic, research, and administrative value while also contributing to revenue and long-term sustainability.

  - These assets include land, buildings, infrastructure, laboratory equipment, IT systems, furniture, vehicles, and other long-term resources that provide economic and service benefits over multiple years.

- Need for Standardization

  - Due to the diverse nature and applications of assets across different departments and campuses, it is critical that all business units follow a standardized set of policies and procedures.

  - Uniform processes ensure:

    - Accurate recognition, valuation, and classification of assets.

    - Consistency in accounting practices across the institution.

    - Compliance with statutory regulations and audit requirements.

- Objective of the Guide

  - This best practice guide has been developed to provide comprehensive instructions for the management and accounting treatment of fixed assets throughout their lifecycle, including:

    - Acquisition and capitalization of assets.

    - Depreciation and impairment of assets.

    - Verification, revaluation, and tagging of assets.

    - Disposal or retirement of assets.

  - The guide ensures that fixed assets are:

    - Used efficiently to maximize their economic and service potential.

    - Properly safeguarded against loss, theft, or misuse.

    - Reflected in the financial records in an accurate, reliable, and transparent manner.

- Scope of Application

  - The policy applies to all schools, departments, research centers, and administrative offices within the HEI.

  - It ensures that the Group-wide framework for fixed asset management is consistently implemented across all units.

## Objective

- The primary objective of Fixed Asset Management is to establish a systematic accounting and control process that ensures the accurate tracking, valuation, and safeguarding of all fixed assets owned by the HEI.

- Key goals include:

> 1\. Financial Accounting & Reporting

- To record and report fixed assets in accordance with applicable accounting standards, ensuring accuracy in the balance sheet and compliance with statutory and audit requirements.

> 2\. Preventive Maintenance

- To maintain assets in good working condition through timely inspections, servicing, and preventive maintenance schedules, thereby maximizing their useful life.

> 3\. Theft Deterrence & Security

- To implement tagging, inventory verification, and internal controls that safeguard assets against loss, theft, or unauthorized use.

> 4\. Efficient Utilization

- To ensure optimal deployment of assets across departments and campuses, avoiding duplication and under-utilization.

> 5\. Lifecycle Management

- To monitor assets throughout their lifecycle—from acquisition to disposal—including capitalization, depreciation, revaluation, impairment, and retirement.

> 6\. Transparency & Accountability

- To provide clear visibility of assets to management, auditors, and stakeholders through a centralized asset register and periodic reports.

## Definitions

| **Term**                            | **Definition**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Property, Plant and Equipment (PPE) | A class of tangible fixed assets used in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative, educational, or research purposes. These assets are classified as non-current assets, as they are expected to provide economic benefits for a period exceeding 12 months.                                               |
| Recognition                         | An item of PPE should be recognized as a fixed asset only if: (i) its cost can be measured reliably, and (ii) it is probable that future economic or service benefits associated with the item will flow to the institution.                                                                                                                                 |
| Cost                                | The total expenditure incurred to acquire or construct an asset, including: (i) Purchase price (net of trade discounts and rebates), (ii) Directly attributable costs (delivery, installation, site preparation, professional fees), and (iii) Any costs necessary to bring the asset to its final location and working condition for intended use.          |
| Depreciation                        | The systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of an asset over its useful life. Depreciation represents the wear and tear, usage, or obsolescence of assets and is charged periodically to the income & expenditure account, ensuring that the cost of assets is matched with the revenues they help to generate.                                      |

## Fixed Asset Register (FAR)

- Requirement to Maintain FAR

  - A Fixed Asset Register must be maintained at all institutes, departments, and the HEI Head Office where fixed assets are held.

  - The purpose of the FAR is to ensure a comprehensive and accurate record of all fixed assets, facilitating financial reporting, physical verification, depreciation calculation, and audit compliance.

- System of Maintenance

  - Best practice is to maintain the FAR within the institution’s accounting or ERP system, ensuring automation and integration with financial records.

  - Where such systems are unavailable, the FAR should be maintained in Excel or a dedicated database with proper version control, security, and backup.

- Minimum Information in FAR

Each entry in the Fixed Asset Register should include, at a minimum:

- Asset Number – Unique sequential number for identification.

- Asset Tag Reference – Barcode/RFID or physical tag affixed to the asset for verification.

- Asset Type/Category – e.g., Land, Building, IT Equipment, Furniture, Vehicle, Lab Equipment.

- Date of Acquisition – Date of purchase or commissioning.

- Asset Description – Detailed description (brand, model, specification).

- Location – Exact placement within the institute (department, building, lab, or off-site).

- Original Cost – Total acquisition cost, including directly attributable expenses.

- Useful Economic Life (Years) – Period over which the asset is expected to provide benefits.

- Accumulated Depreciation (B/F) – Depreciation charged up to the beginning of the accounting year.

- Depreciation Charge (Current Year) – Depreciation expense for the current period.

- Impairment Charge – Amount of impairment loss recognized (if any).

- Impairment Date – Date impairment was identified/recognized.

- Disposal Date – Date of sale, scrapping, or retirement.

- Disposal Proceeds – Amount realized from disposal (if applicable).

- Net Book Value (NBV) – Carrying amount of the asset after deducting depreciation and impairment.

<!-- -->

- Separable Components

  - Significant separable parts of an asset (e.g., elevator in a building, engine in a vehicle, server in a computer system) must be identified separately.

  - Costs of replacing these parts should be capitalized individually, and where appropriate, depreciated separately to reflect true asset value.

## Disposals / Sales of Asset

- Approval Process

  - The HEI must establish a formal approval mechanism for the disposal of property, plant, and equipment (PPE).

  - Disposal requests should be supported by a completed Asset Disposal Form, which must include asset details, justification for disposal, and valuation.

  - The form must be prepared, signed, and authorized in accordance with the institution’s Approval Matrix / Local Authorities & Limits Schedule before the asset can be disposed of.

- Accounting and Reconciliation

  - At the time of disposal, the following must be reconciled with the Fixed Asset Register (FAR):

> 1\. Original Cost of the asset.
>
> 2\. Accumulated Depreciation charged up to the disposal date.
>
> 3\. Net Book Value (NBV) at the time of disposal.
>
> 4\. Net Proceeds from the disposal (actual amount realized).

- These reconciliations ensure that the FAR and financial statements remain accurate and up to date.

<!-- -->

- Definition of Net Proceeds

  - Net Proceeds = Total Economic Benefit – Costs to Sell

  - Total economic benefit may include:

> 1\. Cash Consideration – money received from sale.
>
> 2\. Deferred Consideration – payments to be received at a future date.
>
> 3\. Non-Cash Consideration – e.g., receipt of a replacement asset, exchange transaction, or trade-in.

- For non-cash consideration, the value recorded should be the price the HEI would reasonably expect to pay if it had acquired the asset directly in the open market.

<!-- -->

- Additional Requirements

  - For scrapped assets (no proceeds), a disposal entry must still be passed in the FAR and accounts, showing NBV written off.

  - Any profit or loss on disposal (difference between NBV and net proceeds) should be recorded in the income & expenditure account of the HEI.

  - Assets funded by external grants must be disposed of in accordance with the funding agency’s rules, and necessary disclosures must be made.

## Depreciation

- Commencement of Depreciation

  - Depreciation begins when an asset is available for use or put to use, i.e., when it is in the location and condition necessary for operating in the intended manner.

  - Assets under construction (Capital Work-in-Progress) are not depreciated until they are completed and put into service.

- Component Depreciation

  - Each significant component of Property, Plant, and Equipment (PPE) must be depreciated separately if its cost is material in relation to the total cost of the asset.

  - **Example:** Land is not depreciated, but the building on the land is depreciated over its useful life.

- Automation of Depreciation

  - Depreciation calculations should be automated using the institution’s accounting system or ERP, ensuring consistency and reducing manual errors.

  - Depreciation must be calculated monthly and posted to the General Ledger systematically.

- Depreciation Method

  - Depreciation should reflect the pattern of economic benefit consumption.

  - BLDE (Deemed to be University) currently follows the Straight-Line Method (SLM), spreading the cost of the asset evenly over its useful life.

  - Any change in method (e.g., Written Down Value / Units of Production) must be approved by the Chief Finance Officer (CFO).

- Leasehold Improvements

  - Improvements made on leased property must be capitalized and depreciated over the shorter of their useful life or the remaining lease term.

  - If improvements can be removed without damage, they may be depreciated over their useful life.

- Indicative Useful Economic Lives (UELs)

  - The following ranges should be used as guidance for asset classification:

| **Asset Type**               | **Useful Economic Life (Years)** |
|------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Freehold Buildings           | 25–50                            |
| Plant & Equipment            | 3–20 (depending on type/usage)   |
| Motor Cars & Vans            | 4–5                              |
| Freight Vehicles             | 5                                |
| Office Furniture & Machinery | 10                               |
| Computers & IT Equipment     | 3                                |
| Tooling                      | 3–5                              |

- Factors Considered in Reviewing Useful Life

  - Expected Usage – estimated production capacity or operating workload.

  - Wear and Tear – operational intensity and maintenance history.

  - Technical/Commercial Obsolescence – due to advancements in technology, product design, or industry practices.

  - Legal or Contractual Limits – restrictions on usage from licenses, contracts, or legislation.

- Cessation of Depreciation

  - Depreciation stops only when the asset is derecognized (disposed of, sold, or scrapped).

  - Depreciation should not be halted merely because an asset is idle, retired, or temporarily out of use, unless it has been fully depreciated.

## Insurance of the Assets

- Purpose of Asset Insurance

  - Asset insurance safeguards the institution from financial losses arising from unforeseen events, ensuring business continuity and institutional stability.

  - Since HEIs invest substantial capital in buildings, equipment, vehicles, IT infrastructure, and other assets, insurance acts as a risk management tool to protect this investment.

- Scope of Coverage

Asset insurance should provide protection against:

- Physical Damage / Breakdown

  - Coverage for breakdown, accidental damage, fire, theft, or vandalism affecting assets.

- Natural Calamities

  - Loss or damage due to natural disasters such as floods, lightning, storms, earthquakes, or explosions.

- Loss of Income / Business Interruption

  - Compensation for financial losses caused by partial or complete shutdown of institutional operations due to insured events.

- Third-Party Liability

  - Protection against legal claims, lawsuits, or compensation claims arising from accidents or asset-related failures.

<!-- -->

- Assets to be Insured

  - The HEI must ensure comprehensive insurance coverage for:

    - Buildings & Infrastructure (classrooms, labs, libraries, hostels, administrative offices).

    - Machinery & Equipment (lab apparatus, research instruments, industrial equipment).

    - IT Equipment (computers, servers, networking devices, data centers).

    - Furniture & Fittings (office and classroom furnishings).

    - Vehicles (buses, vans, official cars, and other institutional transport).

- Policy Administration

  - The Finance Department, in consultation with the Registrar and Head of Administration, shall:

    - Review asset values annually for adequate coverage.

    - Ensure timely renewal of policies without any lapse.

    - Maintain an Insurance Register recording details of all insured assets, policy numbers, coverage amounts, and renewal dates.

  - Insurance claims must be processed promptly, with proper documentation of the incident, damage assessment, and claim submission to the insurer.

- Compliance & Audit

  - The insurance coverage must align with institutional risk management policies and any specific requirements from government or funding agencies.

  - Annual internal and statutory audits must review the adequacy of insurance and ensure all high-value assets are covered.

## Physical Verification of Assets

- Frequency of Verification

  - A comprehensive physical verification of all assets must be conducted annually across the HEI.

  - Certain categories of assets require more frequent verification due to their higher risk of movement, loss, or misuse:

    - Portable equipment (e.g., laptops, projectors, tablets, lab tools).

    - High-value movable items (e.g., vehicles, specialized research instruments).

  - Large, immovable, or long-life capital assets (e.g., buildings, land) may be verified annually or bi-annually.

- Asset Tagging for Identification

  - All Property, Plant, and Equipment (PPE) must be clearly tagged with asset numbers/barcodes/RFID for easy identification.

  - Tags should correspond to the details in the Fixed Asset Register (FAR), enabling quick cross-referencing during audits.

- Independence in Verification Process

  - The verification process must involve a separation of duties:

    - **Asset Selection:** The list of assets to be verified should be chosen in advance from the FAR.

    - **Verification:** The individual verifying assets must be different from the selector and not directly responsible for their safekeeping.

    - **Example:** A Factory Manager should not verify factory machinery; instead, verification should be done by an independent officer or audit team.

- Verification Methodology

  - The process should include two key checks:

    - **Existence Check:** Confirming that items recorded in the FAR physically exist.

    - **Completeness Check:** Ensuring that physical items observed on the floor are also recorded in the FAR.

- Recordkeeping of Verification

  - A verification record must be maintained for audit and compliance, capturing:

    - Asset ID and description.

    - Location of asset.

    - Person who selected the assets for verification.

    - Person who carried out the verification.

    - Date of verification.

    - Any discrepancies noted (missing assets, unrecorded assets, damaged/obsolete items).

- Action on Discrepancies

  - Discrepancies must be reported to the Registrar/Finance Officer for investigation.

  - Corrective actions may include:

    - Updating the FAR.

    - Adjusting accounting records (e.g., impairment, disposal).

    - Disciplinary action if negligence or misuse is identified.

- Any discrepancies or concerns raised by the external auditors during the audit will be reported to the Finance Committee.

- The Finance Committee will review the external audit report, evaluate the discrepancies, and then submit the findings to the Chairman for further review.

- Corrective actions will be implemented to address the discrepancies, ensuring that future financial practices are in compliance with institutional policies and legal requirements.

# 19. General Principles to Be Followed in the Finance and Accounts Section

**1.** **Maintenance of Accounts and Financial Records:**

- The Accounts and financial records of the College shall be maintained in the prescribed forms and registers.

- It is the responsibility of the Head of the Branch concerned to ensure that these records are kept properly, accurately, and are kept up-to-date.

**2.** **Financial Year Accounts:**

- The accounts of the College shall be maintained separately for each financial year.

**3.** **Changes in Forms or Registers:**

- No alteration, addition, or modification in the prescribed forms, registers, or the introduction of new forms shall be made without prior approval from the Principal.

- However, subsidiary registers may be maintained for administrative convenience, provided they do not conflict with the prescribed account books.

**4.** **Binding of Account Books:**

- All books of accounts and registers must be properly bound.

- Loose sheets or loosely bound volumes are not permissible for maintaining accounts.

**5.** **Serial Numbering of Pages:**

- The pages of all account books must be serially numbered.

- Each page must bear the stamp of the College.

- Receipt books must also be stamped before they are issued.

- The Superintendent of the section will record the total number of pages in the book on the first page before issuing it.

**6.** **Corrections in Accounts:**

- Corrections or alterations in accounts must be made neatly in red ink.

- The original entry must be crossed out with a simple line, and the correction must be attested by the dated initials of the Superintendent of the section.

**7.** **Corrections in Bills and Vouchers:**

- Any corrections or alterations in bills or vouchers should be attested by:

  - The Officer drawing the bill, or

  - The person preferring the claim.

- Similarly, corrections in pay orders must be attested by the Officer signing the orders.

**8.** **No Erasures:**

- Erasures and covering figures with fluids (e.g., white-out) are strictly prohibited.

- Any document that contains an eraser mark or fluid covering shall not be accepted.

# 20. Receipt Books Management Guidelines

**1.** **Examination and Certification of Receipt Books:**

- When the receipt books are received from the press or purchase section of the Head of the College, the total number of receipts in each book shall be examined and certified by the Accounts Officer (AO) or Co-coordinator on the last page of the book.

- The College stamp shall be affixed on each receipt at the time the books are issued.

**2.** **Serial Entry and Issuance of Receipt Books:**

- All receipt books shall be entered serially in the register.

- Each book's machine number and the number of receipts it contains shall be noted in the respective columns (3) and (4) of the register.

**3.** **Issuance of Receipt Books:**

- Only one receipt book shall be issued to the collection staff at a time unless there is an immediate need for more.

- Fresh issues of receipt books shall not be made until the completed books are returned.

- If immediate returns are not possible, the return of the receipt book after completion shall be closely monitored.

**4.** **Attestation of Receipt Book Issuances:**

- Every issuance and return of a receipt book must be recorded in the register.

- The Assistant maintaining the accounts and the Superintendent of the Section shall attest the entries for both issuance and return.

**5.** **Handling Partly Used Receipt Books:**

- Unused receipts in a partly used receipt book from the previous year must be cancelled at the close of the year.

- The Accounts Officer (AO) or Head of the Branch shall make an endorsement on the book to indicate the cancellation of unused receipts.

**6.** **Inspection and Verification of Completed Books:**

- Upon receipt of completed receipt books or during inspection, the Head of the Branch or an authorized officer shall carefully scrutinize the entries.

- The receipts in the book shall be counted to ensure that none have been removed or misused.

- A certificate confirming this verification shall be recorded on the reverse of the last receipt.

**7.** **Handling of Erasures and Corrections:**

- Erasures and corrections in receipt books must be critically examined.

- A report shall be made for any discrepancies.

- Used receipt books shall be kept in the custody of the Head of the Section for safekeeping and proper record.

# 21. Issue of Duplicate Receipts Guidelines

**1.** **No Duplicate Receipts:**

- No duplicate copy of a receipt shall be issued if the original receipt is lost. This policy ensures that no unauthorized or fraudulent duplicate receipts are generated.

**2.** **Alternative Documentation:**

- If there is a necessity for documenting the lost receipt, a certificate may be issued stating:

  - The date when the payment was received,

  - The amount received,

  - The account to which the money was credited,

  - The person from whom the payment was received.

- This certificate shall serve as an official confirmation of the payment received on that specified day.

**3.** **Verification and Inquiry:**

- Before issuing such a certificate, the Officer who is responsible for signing or countersigning the certificate must conduct a reasonable inquiry to confirm the correctness of the facts stated in the certificate.

- The Officer must ensure that the payment was genuinely received and that the information provided is accurate.

# 22. General Principles Regarding Expenditure

**1.** **Budget Provision:**

- All expenditures must be within the limits set by the sanctioned budget, ensuring that no expenses exceed the allocated amount under the respective budget heads.

**2.** **Approval and Sanctioning:**

- No expenditure should be incurred that exceeds the provision under the relevant budget head unless prior approval is obtained from the Board or Governing Body for fresh items of expenditure.

- Expenditures must conform to the relevant College Rules.

**3.** **Authority and Vigilance:**

- Every officer must exercise vigilance similar to that of a prudent individual in managing the College funds.

- No officer shall approve or sanction an expenditure that directly or indirectly benefits their personal interests.

- Officers must avoid approving travel or other allowances in such a way that recipients derive personal profit from them.

**4.** **Liability Beyond Sanction:**

- Any officer with financial delegation should not incur expenditure likely to exceed their powers of sanction.

- The principal is the only officer allowed to incur liabilities beyond the sanctioned budget with prior approval for additional funds. Officers exceeding the authorized budget will be held personally responsible.

**5.** **Funds Management:**

- Money should only be drawn from the bank when necessary for immediate disbursement. Drawing money unnecessarily and keeping it in the chest to avoid lapse of funds is not allowed.

- All sums received should be paid immediately into the bank and not used directly for departmental expenses.

- Any indisputable payable amount should never be left unpaid and should be processed promptly.

**6.** **Record Keeping and Documentation:**

- Bills, vouchers, and receipts must include full details such as the amount, the payee’s name, and the nature of the claim.

- Bills should be written clearly, with amounts in both figures and words. For amounts in rupees, ensure the term "only" is added if there are no paise.

- Corrections to amounts in bills should be made in red ink and attested by the relevant officer.

- Special orders must be quoted in the bill if they pertain to the charges incurred, and supporting sanction orders must be attached and certified.

**7.** **Voucher and Receipt Management:**

- Each voucher should include an acknowledgment of payment signed by the payee. No payment should be made without this acknowledgment.

- Rubber stamps or facsimile signatures are not acceptable for signing payments; manual signatures are required.

- In cases where vouchers are lost, a certificate of payment should be prepared and signed by the disbursing officer, with full details of the claim provided.

**8.** **Handling Lost Documents:**

- No duplicate receipts will be issued for lost documents. A certificate of payment can be issued after reasonable inquiry to verify the facts, specifying the date and amount paid.

# 23. Presentation of Bills

**1.** **Examination and Verification:**

- All bills must be submitted to the Accounts Branch for verification and payment.

- The bills will be reviewed by an officer in charge of the pre-check process to ensure:

  - The claim is admissible under College rules and budgetary provisions.

  - The authority for the claim is proper (e.g., approval from the relevant authority).

  - The signature on the bill is authentic and in proper order.

  - The receipt is a valid legal acquittance confirming the transaction.

- Once verified, an order to pay shall be issued for the bill. The order should specify the amount payable in both words and figures.

**2.** **Voucher Number and Cash Book Entry:**

- After the bill is passed for payment, it shall be assigned a voucher number.

- The bill, along with its voucher number, shall be entered into the cash book before preparing the cheque for payment. This ensures proper documentation and tracking of the payment.

# 24. Draw of Cheques

**1.** **Cheque Signing and Bill Endorsement:**

- A Cheque will be signed by the Finance Officer in favor of the party to whom the payment is due.

- After signing, an endorsement of payment will be written on the bill, indicating the cheque number and date on which the claim is paid.

**2.** **Cheque Crossing and Payee Specifications:**

- All cheques shall ordinarily be crossed, making them payable to order only.

- Each cheque shall have the notation "Under Rupees only" across it, showing the next higher amount in rupees that the cheque is made payable for.

**3.** **Cheque Signing Restrictions:**

- No cheque shall be signed unless it is required for immediate delivery to the payee or drawn in favor of a person other than the actual payee.

**4.** **Corrections and Alterations:**

- Any corrections or alterations made on cheques must be attested by the drawing officer with their full signature.

**5.** **Cash Payments via Cheques:**

- Payments will generally be made by cheques. However, in cases where claims are required to be paid in cash, a cheque may be issued in favor of the Drawing Officer to facilitate the payment.

- Any amounts drawn via cheques and the subsequent cash payments must be recorded properly in Petty Cash Books and Acquittance Registers.

**6.** **Imprest/Advance Payments:**

- For payments made from imprest/advances taken by Drawing Officers, the relevant vouchers will be examined by the Assistant Registrar in the concerned branch and admitted for payment from the imprest/advance.

# 25. Cheque Books Management

**1.** **Custody of Cheque Books:**

- Cheque books shall be kept under lock and key in the personal custody of the cheque-drawing Officer, Accounts Officer (AO), or a staff member entrusted with writing cheques.

**2.** **Stock Account Maintenance:**

- The designated officer must maintain a Stock Account for all cheque books to ensure proper tracking and accountability.

**3.** **Receipt of Cheque Books:**

- Upon receiving cheque books from the bank, the receipt must be recorded in the Stock Account.

- The cheque forms should be carefully counted, and a certificate of count must be recorded on the fly leaf of the cheque book by the drawing officer. The certificate should read:

- "Certified that I have personally counted the cheque forms, and the book contains \[Number\] Forms Nos."

**4.** **Use of Cheque Books:**

- No more than one cheque book should be brought into use at the same time unless the situation demands the issuance of cheques in excess of what a single book can accommodate.

# 26. Currency of Cheques

**1.** **Expired Cheques:**

- If the currency of a cheque expires due to it not being presented within the prescribed time, the cheque may still be re-dated upon presentation by the payee.

**2.** **Re-dating Process:**

- The cheque must be found to be in order in all other respects (e.g., proper signatures, amounts, etc.).

- The corrections in dates shall be attested by the full dated signature of the issuing authority.

**3.** **Record of Changes:**

- A note stating that the date was corrected should be recorded against the original entry in the Cash Book, ensuring proper tracking and documentation of the adjustment.

# 27. Cancelled Cheques

**1.** **Recording of Cancellation:**

- When a cheque is cancelled, the fact of cancellation, along with the number and date of the new cheque, should be recorded on the counter-foil of the cancelled cheque.

- Conversely, the number and date of the cancelled cheque should be noted on the counter-foil of the new cheque.

**2.** **Retention and Destruction:**

- The cancelled cheque should be carefully retained and attached to its counter-foil until the accounts for the relevant period have been audited.

- After the audit, the cancelled cheque can be destroyed to ensure it is not misused.

**3.** **Handling Entries in Cash Book and Registers:**

- **Before the Cash Book is Closed:**

  - If a cheque is cancelled before the Cash Book is closed, the entries in the Cash Book or any related registers must be struck off in red ink, and the action must be initialed by the issuing authority.

- **After the Cash Book is Closed:**

  - If the cheque is cancelled after the Cash Book has been closed, the amount should be adjusted with a minus entry under the corresponding expenditure head in both the Expenditure Registers and Cash Book. A note explaining this cancellation should be made in the registers where the transaction was initially recorded.

# 28. Cheques Lost/Destroyed

**1.** **Action by the Cheque Drawing Officer:**

- If a cheque is reported as lost or destroyed, the Cheque Drawing Officer must immediately contact the bank.

- The officer will request a non-payment certificate from the bank, confirming that payment will be stopped if the lost cheque is presented for payment.

**2.** **Record in the Account:**

- Once the necessary safeguards with the bank are in place, the officer shall:

  - Enter the original cheque as cancelled in the account.

  - Issue a new cheque against the original cheque in the Cash Book.

  - In the Cash Book, the entry should be marked with the remark: "Original cheque lost or destroyed".

**3.** **Note on the Counterfoil of the New Cheque:**

- A note should be made on the counterfoil of the new cheque stating: "Issued in lieu of Cheque No. \[Original Cheque Number\] Dated \[Date\] lost/destroyed."

# 29. Unpaid Cheques

**1.** **Cancellation of Unpaid Cheques:**

- A cheque that remains unpaid for 6 months or more from the date of issue shall be cancelled.

**2.** **Treatment of Cancelled Cheques:**

- For cheques related to the previous accounting year:

  - The amount of the cancelled cheque shall be classified under "Other Miscellaneous Receipts".

- For cheques related to the current accounting year:

  - The amount of the cancelled cheque shall be shown as a deduct entry under the concerned head of account.

# 30. Rounding Off of Amounts

**1.** **General Rule for Rounding Off:**

- Transactions involving fractions of a rupee shall be rounded off to the nearest rupee:

  - Amounts 50 paisa and above but less than one rupee should be rounded up to the next rupee.

  - Amounts below 50 paisa should be ignored and omitted from the accounts.

**2.** **Specific Applications:**

- **Pay and Allowances:**

  - Pay and allowances, including entitlements like HRA, DA, CCA, and deductions like house rent or loan recoveries, should be rounded off for each item of earning. Amounts 50 paisa and above should be rounded **up** to the next rupee, and amounts below 50 paisa should be omitted.

- **Recovery of Interest:**

  - Recovery of interest on loans and advances should be rounded off to whole rupees regardless of the percentage-based calculations.

- **Travelling Allowance Bills:**

  - Rounding off should be done at the last stage (after all items like railway fare, mileage, and daily allowance have been tallied) rather than for each individual item.

- **Transactions between College and Government/Other Organizations:**

  - All transactions between the College and other entities should be rounded off to the nearest rupee.

- **Bills for Recoupment of Permanent Advance/Imprest:**

  - The bill for recoupment of permanent imprest should be rounded off to the whole rupee on each occasion.

# 31. Excess and Over Payments

**1.** **Recovery of Excess Payments:**

- Any excess payments made in a bill shall be recovered either:

  - In cash, or

  - By short payment from the subsequent bill of the payee.

**2.** **Adjustment of Overpayments:**

- If the recovery is made in the same accounting year:

  - **Ordinary Adjustment:**

  - The recovery will generally be adjusted by deducting the excess from the current year's charge under the same detailed head where the overpayment occurred.

  - **Short Payment Adjustment:**

  - If the recovery is made through short payment of an item under the same detailed head as the previous overpayment, no further adjustment is needed, as the short payment will automatically offset the excess payment.

  - **Cash Recovery Adjustment:**

  - If the recovery is made in cash, the amount shall be recorded as a minus debit in the concerned expenditure head.

  - **Recovery under Different Heads:**

  - If the recovery is made via short payment of an item that is chargeable to a different detailed head than the one where the overpayment occurred:

  - The amount payable before deduction should be entered under the detailed head to which the item belongs.

  - The amount recovered should be entered as a credit under the expenditure head where the recovery relates.

- If the recovery is made in subsequent years:

  - Recoveries of overpayments, except in cases of works in progress, shall be accounted for under:

  - The relevant receipt major head, or

  - The head "Other Miscellaneous Receipts".

  - For works in progress, the recovery can be taken by reducing the expenditure under the relevant head.

# 32. Arrear Claims

The following time limits have been prescribed for presenting claims against the College, after which they will not be entertained:

**1.** **Travelling and Halting Allowance Claims:**

- Claims for travelling and halting allowances must be submitted within three months from the date on which the payment was due. Any claims presented after this period will not be entertained.

**2.** **Examination Remuneration Claims:**

- Claims for remuneration for examination must be submitted within one year from the date of publication of the examination results. Any claims presented after one year will be considered as lapsed.

**3.** **Caution Deposit Money:**

- Caution deposit money that remains unclaimed for a period of three years will be deemed to have lapsed and will no longer be available for claim.

# 33. Losses

**1.** **Discovery of Embezzlement or Misappropriation:**

- In case of any embezzlement, misappropriation of college funds, loss of money, stores, or other valuables by theft, negligence, or otherwise, an immediate enquiry shall be conducted by the Accounts Officer (AO) or any other duly authorized officer.

- The officer conducting the enquiry must immediately report the matter to the principal.

**2.** **Reporting of Loss or Embezzlement:**

- After completing the enquiry, the AO shall submit a detailed report to the principal. This report should include:

  - The total amount of money or the value of the stores or other valuables misappropriated or lost.

  - The manner in which the misappropriation or loss occurred.

  - The steps taken to recover the lost money or items and the actions to punish the offenders.

  - The measures implemented to prevent recurrence of similar losses or embezzlements in the future.

# 34. Endowments for Medals and Prizes

**1.** **Acceptance of Endowments:**

- The College accepts offers of endowments for establishing Gold Medals and Book Prizes to reward meritorious students.

**2.** **Investment and Utilization of Endowment Funds:**

- The amounts received from donors are invested in Fixed Deposits in nationalized banks.

- The annual interest generated from these Fixed Deposits is drawn and made available to the Controller of Examinations upon request.

- The Controller of Examinations receives the interest along with details of the gold medals already in possession and the advance required for purchasing additional medals for presentation at the Convocation to the awardees approved by the Governing Body.

**3.** **Account Submission and Adjustment of Advance:**

- The Controller of Examinations is required to submit an account to the Accounts Branch within one month of the Convocation. This submission must include **vouchers** for any expenditure incurred from the advance provided, in order to adjust the advance amount.

# 35. Earnest Money Deposits (EMD)

**1.** **Receipt of Earnest Money/Security Deposits:**

- Earnest Money/Security Deposits are received from contractors for various purposes such as:

  - Supply of paper and stationery.

  - Execution of works.

  - Approved printers for printing course material, etc.

- These deposits are typically furnished in the form of Demand Drafts (DDs), which are received by the concerned branches of the College and subsequently forwarded to the Accounts Branch.

**2.** **Depositing Earnest Money:**

- The amounts received are deposited into a separate account in the Bank, specifically the 'Earnest Money Deposit Account'.

- Refunds of the Earnest Money Deposits (EMD) are made by issuing a cheque from that account.

**3.** **Recording Transactions:**

- All transactions relating to the receipt and refund of EMD are recorded in the EMD Cash Book maintained for this purpose.

**4.** **Cash Book Closure and Carry Forward:**

- The EMD Cash Book **is** closed monthly.

- Any pending EMD refunds at the end of the financial year are carried forward to the subsequent year.

# 36. Establishment Procedures and Pay Bills

**1.** **Establishment Section Maintenance:**

- The Establishment Section of the College Office is responsible for maintaining an Authorized Officer (AO) Register showing the sanctioned strength of each category of posts and appointments made against such sanction.

- This register is updated with the Resolutions of the Board of Management and College Orders issued from time to time.

**2.** **Proposals for Change in Establishment:**

- Any proposed changes to the establishment must be submitted by the AO to the Principal for approval.

- The proposal should include:

  - Existing strength and cost of the affected sections.

  - Proposed changes in strength and cost.

  - Effective dates for the proposed changes.

  - Details of the post to be added or modified, including pay and other details.

  - Ability of the College to meet the additional expenditure from its normal income.

  - A financial statement showing the impact of the proposed changes.

**3.** **Pay Bills for Establishments:**

- Pay bills for establishments that are either sanctioned without a time limit or for specific periods (temporary establishments) are prepared separately.

- **Temporary establishments:** Pay bills must specify the head of account and the order sanctioning the establishment.

- **Officiating appointments:** These should be shown under the permanent establishment in the pay bills.

**4.** **Timing of Salary Disbursement:**

- No salary shall be disbursed before the first working day of the succeeding month unless in extraordinary cases.

- Salary bills for part-months can be paid before the month ends in certain cases:

  - If the employee quits service (due to dismissal, resignation, death, etc.).

  - If a temporary appointment ceases before the end of the month and there is no permanent position for the employee.

**5.** **Public Holidays and Salary Disbursement:**

- If the first two days of the month are public holidays, salaries (except for March) may be paid on the last working day before the holidays with the principal’s prior approval.

**6.** **Instructions for Salary Bills:**

- **Deductions:** All deductions (e.g., Provident Fund, Income Tax, House Rent) should be specified clearly.

- **Broken period salaries:** For salary claims based on a broken period, the period and reason must be noted.

- **First salary payment:** Made only after submission of a Health Certificate from a Medical Officer.

- **Leave/Deputation:** Employees absent on leave or deputation should be marked in the bill.

- **Salary withholding:** Reasons for withholding salary should be mentioned, and when paid later, cross-references to supplementary bills should be noted.

- **Supplementary bills:** For arrears, supplementary claims should be made through separate bills, clearly showing the month and reason for omission or delay.

- **Temporary establishment:** For temporary staff, the sanctioning orders should be quoted in the bill.

**7.** **Other Salary Bill Considerations:**

- Fixed monthly charges (like House Rent Allowance) should be included in the pay bill.

- **Drawing Officer's responsibility:** The person signing the bill is responsible for ensuring the accuracy of the amounts drawn.

- **Increments and adjustments:** Any increments or adjustments should be noted in red ink in the bill.

- **Daily wage earners:** Their wages should be drawn from supplementary bills and supported by attendance statements.

- **Recovery deductions:** Various recoveries (e.g., security deposits, loans, Provident Fund, taxes) should be deducted and documented in the salary bill.

**8.** **Recovery and Deductions Procedures:**

- Deductions for Co-operative Credit Society, Provident Fund, Income Tax, Insurance, Water and Electricity bills, and other charges should be handled carefully and entered in the relevant columns.

- **Late deductions:** If deductions are missed in one month, they should be deducted from the following month's bill.

- No advances should be sanctioned after the 30th of any month, unless in special cases, in which case, arrangements must be made for recovery from the salary.

# 40. Payroll-Salaries Policy

## Payroll Management

The Organisation recognizes payroll as one of the most critical financial functions, directly impacting employee welfare, institutional credibility, and compliance with statutory regulations. It is therefore essential to establish stringent controls to govern all payroll-related transactions and ensure transparency, accuracy, and accountability in the process.

The policy mandates that all payroll transactions and movements that may affect payroll data must be properly authorized, documented, and monitored. This includes new appointments, promotions, increments, leave adjustments, overtime, deductions, and terminations. Such controls are designed to safeguard against errors, fraud, and mismanagement while ensuring employees receive fair and timely compensation.

It is the policy of HEIs to provide appropriate remuneration strictly for time worked and services rendered, in line with employment contracts, institutional rules, and relevant labor laws. All statutory deductions—such as income tax, provident fund, professional tax, gratuity, and other regulatory contributions—will be accurately calculated and remitted within prescribed timelines to comply with national legislation and avoid penalties.

Furthermore, the organisation ensures that net pay due to employees will be directly credited into their designated bank accounts to promote efficiency, security, and financial transparency. This eliminates the risks associated with cash handling and aligns payroll practices with modern banking and governance standards.

Through this policy, the Organisation reinforces its commitment to financial discipline, regulatory compliance, and employee trust, while embedding payroll management within the larger framework of effective financial governance.

## Payroll Controls & Procedures

To ensure accuracy, compliance, and transparency in payroll management, the following controls and procedures shall apply:

- 1\. Authorization and Approvals

  - All payroll-related transactions (appointments, salary revisions, overtime, deductions, resignations, terminations) must be approved in writing by the competent authority (Registrar/Principal/HR Head).

  - No changes to payroll data shall be processed without documented approval.

- 2\. Employee Records Management

  - A comprehensive employee database shall be maintained, including appointment letters, grade details, leave balances, tax declarations, and statutory compliance information.

  - HR and Accounts departments must coordinate to ensure records are accurate and updated regularly.

- 3\. Payroll Preparation

  - Payroll shall be prepared monthly by the Finance/Accounts Department using approved payroll software or ERP systems.

  - All additions (increments, allowances) and deductions (PF, TDS, loans, insurance) must be cross-checked with supporting documentation before processing.

  - Trial payroll reports shall be reviewed by the Finance Officer before finalization.

- 4\. Statutory Deductions & Compliance

  - Deductions such as Provident Fund (PF), Professional Tax, Income Tax (TDS), Gratuity, ESI, and any other statutory requirements must be computed accurately.

  - Compliance deadlines must be strictly adhered to for timely remittance to statutory authorities.

  - Records of remittances must be retained for audit and regulatory inspections.

- 5\. Payment of Salaries

  - Net salaries shall be disbursed directly to employees’ registered bank accounts via Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT/NEFT/RTGS).

  - Salary slips shall be generated and shared electronically with employees for transparency.

  - No cash payments shall be permitted except under exceptional circumstances authorized by the Governing Body.

- 6\. Payroll Reconciliation & Monitoring

  - Monthly reconciliation of payroll against attendance records, HR data, and financial statements shall be conducted by the Finance Officer.

  - Variances, if any, must be investigated and resolved immediately.

  - Payroll costs must be tracked against budgetary allocations for effective financial control.

- 7\. Internal Control & Audit

  - Payroll shall be subject to both internal and external audits to ensure compliance, detect discrepancies, and strengthen financial governance.

  - Access to payroll systems shall be restricted to authorized personnel, with role-based permissions.

  - Audit trails of all modifications to payroll data must be maintained.

- 8\. Confidentiality & Data Security

  - Payroll data is confidential and shall not be shared with unauthorized persons.

  - Proper IT controls, including password protection, encryption, and restricted access, must be applied to payroll systems.

  - Employee financial and personal information must be handled in compliance with data protection regulations.

## Payroll Department: Roles and Responsibilities

The Payroll function, overseen by the HR Department, ensures that all employees of the University and HEIs Association are compensated accurately, timely, and in compliance with statutory regulations. The HR Administrator/Coordinator is responsible for administering payroll activities in collaboration with the Finance Department.

a\. Timesheet Submission and Payroll Processing

- All employees are required to submit their monthly timesheets by the last working day of each month.

- Timesheets must be received and verified by the HR Coordinator.

- Salaries will be processed and disbursed by the 5th working day of the following month.

- If this date coincides with a weekend or holiday, due adjustments will be made to ensure timely salary disbursement.

- TDS remittance will be carefully managed in accordance with statutory timelines.

b\. Employee Data Form and Payroll Updates

- All payroll-related changes (additions, deletions, or modifications) must be recorded through an Employee Data Form.

- This form requires the employee’s signature and submission to the HR Administrator/Coordinator.

- The form must also be signed and authorized by the HR Manager/Registrar/COO before being filed in the personnel record.

- Changes covered include:

  - Salary or wage revisions

  - Change in job title or designation

  - Modification of working status (full-time/part-time)

  - Updates to personal details such as address or name

  - Fringe benefit modifications

c\. Salary Advances and Loans

- No salary advances or loans will be issued, except as specifically provided for non-teaching staff under the institution’s Loans, Advances & Deposits Policy.

- Any payroll discrepancies will be identified and resolved in the subsequent pay cycle.

d\. Attendance Verification

- Salary calculations will be strictly based on biometric attendance records or approved timesheets.

- The HR Administrator/Coordinator of each institute must consolidate and report attendance data to the Payroll Department.

e\. Employee Responsibility for Attendance

- Employees are solely responsible for ensuring proper swipe in/out registration on biometric devices.

- Any missed swipes or attendance shortfalls must be regularized by submitting appropriate leave/regularization forms (e.g., sick leave, vacation, holiday records).

f\. Salary Disbursement

- All salaries will be transferred directly into employees’ bank accounts via electronic transfer methods (NEFT/RTGS).

- No alternative modes of payment such as cash or cheque will be permitted.

g\. New Employee Payroll Inclusion

- New employees will only be added to payroll upon submission and approval of the Employee Data Form, duly signed by the HR Manager/Registrar/COO.

- Without this documentation, payroll processing will not be initiated.

h\. Payroll System and Payslips

- Payroll activities will be processed through a computerized payroll system/ERP to ensure efficiency and accuracy.

- Payslips will be generated and distributed (digitally or physically) to employees by the second week of every month.

i\. Tax Declarations and Compliance

- Employees falling under the income tax slab are required to submit Form 12BB (statement of claims for deductions) to the HR Coordinator.

- This applies to both existing employees and new joiners, ensuring compliance with Income Tax regulations.

- HR will verify and submit declarations to the Finance Department for accurate TDS computation.

## Payroll Procedures

To ensure accuracy, compliance, and transparency, the payroll process follows a systematic procedure involving HR, Finance, and Central Payroll teams. The following steps outline the process:

a\. Validation of Timesheets

- Each HR Assistant/Coordinator at the institute level will review and validate employee timesheets for accuracy and completeness.

- This includes verifying attendance records, leave applications, overtime, and other allowances.

- All validated timesheets must be submitted to the Central Payroll Processing Team at least 1 working day before the start of the new month to allow timely salary processing.

b\. Authentication of Timesheets

- The Chief Operating Officer (COO) / Chief Administrative Officer (CAO) is responsible for authenticating all timesheets received from HR Assistants/Coordinators across different institutes.

- Only after authentication will the timesheets be forwarded to the Central Payroll Processing Team.

- This ensures an additional layer of control and accountability before payroll is finalized.

c\. Statutory and Other Deductions

- Statutory deductions such as Provident Fund (PF), Professional Tax, Employee State Insurance (ESI), and Income Tax (TDS) shall be made strictly as per Government norms.

- Other deductions (such as loans, advances, welfare fund contributions, or employee-requested deductions) will be applied only under the guidelines approved by management or upon the employee’s written consent.

d\. Discrepancy Resolution

- If an employee has any query or discrepancy regarding their salary, deductions, or allowances, they must approach the HR Department for clarification.

- HR is responsible for investigating the issue, coordinating with the Central Payroll Processing Team, and ensuring resolution in the following pay cycle.

- This ensures employees have a clear grievance redressal mechanism for payroll issues.

e\. Employee Transfers and Rotation

- As per the Transfer and Rotation Policy, when employees are transferred between campuses/institutes, their personnel files are also moved accordingly.

- The associated payroll costs will be reallocated to the respective cost center or institute budget.

- This ensures accurate financial reporting and allocation of salary expenses.

## Internal Loan Policy

The provision of Internal Loans/Internal Borrowings is applicable to both the HEI Association and the University. This policy establishes clear guidelines for the accounting, authorization, and processing of such transactions to ensure financial discipline, transparency, and accountability.

- Purpose

  - To provide short-term or medium-term financial support between the HEI Association and University.

  - To meet urgent operational, academic, or infrastructure-related financial requirements.

  - To optimize available financial resources without relying solely on external borrowings.

- Loan Request and Documentation

  - The Treasurer’s Office is responsible for receiving and processing internal loan requests.

  - Each request must be supported by a formal loan application, clearly specifying:

    - The purpose of the loan.

    - The requested amount.

    - Repayment schedule and terms.

    - Authorizing signatures from the relevant authority (Registrar/Principal/COO).

  - The Treasurer’s Office prepares the necessary loan documentation, which is filed for financial records and audit purposes.

- Review and Authorization

  - The Treasury Office reviews all loan applications to ensure completeness and compliance with institutional policies.

  - Verification includes:

    - Checking budgetary provisions.

    - Ensuring the loan is properly authorized by the designated authority.

    - Confirming repayment capability.

  - Loans shall only be approved once all necessary authorizations and documentation are verified.

- Fund Transfer

  - Once the internal loan is approved, the Central Office of the HEI Association and University will arrange for the transfer of loan proceeds.

  - Funds will be transferred only upon receipt of the approved loan application copies from the Treasurer’s Office.

  - The transfer process will be recorded in both entities’ financial systems to maintain transparency and accountability.

- Accounting and Monitoring

  - Internal loans will be recorded under “Internal Borrowings/Advances” in the books of accounts of both the lending and borrowing entity.

  - The Treasurer’s Office will maintain a register of internal loans, including:

    - Loan amount.

    - Date of issue.

    - Repayment terms.

    - Outstanding balances.

  - Regular monitoring will be undertaken to ensure timely repayment and to prevent overdue balances.

- Repayment Terms

  - Repayments must follow the schedule defined in the approved loan documentation.

  - The borrowing entity must ensure repayments are made within the agreed timelines.

  - In case of default, corrective actions may be initiated, including adjustments against future allocations or funds due.

## Loans to Employees / External People

- Definition of Loan

  - A loan is defined as a financial arrangement in which a lender provides funds to a borrower with an obligation for repayment, either in a lump sum or in installments, over an agreed period of time.

  - Loans may be short-term (repayable within one year) or long-term (repayable over more than one year).

- Restriction on Loans to Employees and External Parties

  - As a matter of policy, the Higher Education Institution (HEI) shall not provide loans of any nature (short-term or long-term) to:

    - Employees (teaching and non-teaching staff).

    - Vendors, suppliers, or contractors.

    - Alumni, students, or any external stakeholders.

  - This restriction ensures avoidance of conflicts of interest, financial risk exposure, and diversion of institutional funds away from its primary mission of education, research, and community service.

- Permissible Loans / Borrowings

  - The HEI is permitted to raise loans (short-term and long-term) from external financial institutions, banks, or government bodies to support:

    - Infrastructure development.

    - Academic and research activities.

    - Institutional expansion projects.

  - The HEI may also enter into intragroup loan arrangements with the Association, University, or related entities, subject to approval and compliance with the Finance Policy.

- Employee and Vendor Advances *(not considered as loans)*

  - While loans are prohibited, the HEI may provide:

    - Travel advances for employees undertaking official duties.

    - Festival or salary advances for non-teaching staff, strictly as per the Loans, Advances & Deposits Policy.

    - Vendor advances against contracts or purchase orders, provided these are duly approved and settled against invoices.

  - These advances must be treated separately from loans and accounted for under short-term advances in the financial statements.

- Compliance and Monitoring

  - Any violation of this restriction (e.g., extending a loan to an employee or external party) will be treated as a policy breach and subject to corrective action by the Finance Committee.

  - All borrowing activities by the HEI (internal or external) must be approved by the Governing Body and documented in financial records for audit and compliance purposes.

## Policies on Advances

### 1. Advances to Suppliers or Service Organizations:

- **Conditions for Payment:** Advances can only be paid to suppliers or service organizations if the terms are explicitly mentioned in the service contract or purchase orders.

- **Bank Guarantees Requirement:** Any advance paid to suppliers or service organizations must be secured against bank guarantees, ensuring that the institution's funds are protected.

### 2. Advances to Employees:

- **Employee Advances:** Employees can request advances for specific purposes (such as travel, project-related expenses, etc.), but they must obtain approval from the President prior to drawing such advances.

- **Approval Process:** The President must authorize the payment of advances, ensuring that the requested advance is for legitimate and authorized purposes.

### 3. Outstanding Advances Monitoring:

- **Monthly Statement Submission:** The Finance Officer is responsible for submitting a monthly statement of all outstanding advances to the President. This allows the institution to monitor the status of advances and take any necessary corrective actions.

- **Corrective Actions:** The President reviews the statement and may take corrective actions if there are unutilized or unaccounted advances, ensuring that funds are being used properly.

### 4. Settlement of Advances:

- **Timely Settlement:** Any advances drawn for approved expenditure must be settled within two weeks of being issued. If the advance is not settled within this period, it is considered overdue.

- **Salary Deduction:** Advances that are not settled within the stipulated period will be deducted from the salary of the employee who received the advance, without any further intimation. This serves as a corrective measure to ensure timely settlement.

### 5. Audit and Compliance:

- Advances are subject to regular audit checks to ensure that they are being issued for legitimate reasons and settled in a timely manner.

- The Finance Officer will also ensure that all advances are properly recorded and monitored to avoid financial discrepancies.

### 6. Documentation and Transparency:

- Proper documentation for each advance (including the purpose, approval, amount, and terms) must be maintained. This ensures transparency and accountability in the issuance and settlement of advances.

  - Used responsibly and transparently in alignment with the mission, vision, and strategic priorities of the HEI.

  - Directed towards academic, research, student welfare, and infrastructure development activities that enhance institutional quality and reputation.

  - Compliant with statutory requirements, donor stipulations, and institutional governance frameworks.

- The policy also aims to:

  - Promote trust and accountability between the institution and its donors.

  - Ensure that donations and gifts contribute to long-term sustainability and value creation for the HEI.

  - Provide a structured process for acceptance, acknowledgment, allocation, monitoring, and reporting of all contributions.

## Funds from Donators

### 1. Purpose of Donations:

- Donations can be collected for various purposes, including but not limited to:

  - **Research Funding:** Supporting research projects and academic initiatives.

  - **Infrastructure Development:** Enhancing and expanding the physical infrastructure of the institution.

  - **Development of Amenities:** Improving facilities such as libraries, labs, sports facilities, and student welfare programs.

  - **Scholarships:** Funding scholarships to support deserving students financially and encourage academic excellence.

### 2. Donation Management:

- All donations must be properly documented, specifying the purpose for which the funds are intended.

- A transparent system should be in place to manage and allocate donations in accordance with the specified objectives.

### 3. Acknowledgement of Donations:

- Donors should receive proper acknowledgment for their contributions, including receipts and recognition in relevant institutional communications (annual reports, websites, etc.).

- Special appreciation or recognition can be given to major donors, including naming rights for certain facilities or scholarships.

### 4. Utilization of Funds:

- Donations will be strictly utilized for the designated purposes as agreed upon by the institution and the donors.

- The institution should ensure proper auditing of the funds to ensure transparency and accountability in their use.

## Receipt of Cash Gifts from Non-Employees

- Eligible Donors

  - Cash gifts may be received from alumni, parents, corporations, foundations, philanthropic organizations, and individual well-wishers.

  - These contributions must be voluntary, unconditional (unless specific donor instructions are provided), and aligned with the mission and values of the HEI.

- Receiving Points

  - Gifts may be received directly by the Institute Office or through other departments across the HEI.

  - Regardless of the initial receiving point, all gifts must be reported to and processed through the Accounts Department for financial recording and compliance.

- Deposit and Recording

  - The Accounts Department shall:

    - Record all cash gifts received with complete donor details (name, address, purpose, donor’s instructions if any).

    - Deposit the funds directly into the HEI’s designated bank account under the name/reference of the donor.

    - Issue an official receipt/acknowledgment to the donor for audit and donor-relations purposes.

- Utilization of Funds

  - Gifted funds shall be utilized:

    - As per the donor’s instructions, if a specific purpose (e.g., scholarships, infrastructure, research, cultural activities) has been indicated.

    - For institutional development purposes, if the donation is unrestricted.

  - All utilizations must be authorized by the Registrar/Finance Officer, ensuring compliance with both donor intent and institutional policy.

- Transparency and Accountability

  - A separate ledger/accounting head shall be maintained for tracking cash gifts to ensure clarity in utilization and reporting.

  - Periodic reports (quarterly/annual) on donations received and utilized must be prepared for submission to the Governing Body and shared with donors, where applicable.

  - Any unutilized funds should remain in the designated account until used for the specified purpose.

## Receipt of Gifts from Retired Employees

- Source of Gifts

  - Retired employees of the HEI may voluntarily contribute cash gifts to their respective institutes as a gesture of goodwill, gratitude, or support for ongoing institutional development.

- Process of Receipt

  - Cash gifts may be received directly by the respective institutes from retired employees.

  - Immediately upon receipt, the institute must:

    - Generate an official receipt in the name of the donor.

    - Record donor details (name, employee ID/retirement details, purpose of donation, if specified).

    - Ensure that the amount is deposited without delay into the designated bank account of the institute.

- Utilization of Funds

  - Funds contributed shall be used:

    - As per the donor’s wishes, if a specific purpose is indicated (e.g., scholarships, student welfare, library enrichment, or infrastructure improvements).

    - For institutional needs and development activities, if the donation is unrestricted.

  - Utilization must be authorized by the Registrar/Finance Officer, in alignment with donor intent and institutional priorities.

- Transparency & Accountability

  - All gifts from retired employees shall be tracked in a Donations Register maintained by the Accounts Department of the institute.

  - Annual statements of such contributions and their utilization shall be reported to the University Finance Committee / Governing Body.

  - Any unutilized balance shall remain earmarked under the donation fund until it is used for the intended purpose.

## Detail Policy and Procedure

- Non-Cash Gifts Below Threshold

  - Non-cash gifts valued at less than ₹5,000 (as per prevailing market rates) are excluded from the Risk Review Process.

  - This exemption applies because such gifts are usually:

    - Donated for charitable fundraising events, or

    - Disposable/low-value items used for daily operational or advancement activities.

- Repetitive Low-Value Non-Cash Gifts

  - If multiple non-cash gifts each valued below ₹5,000 are received more than once during a financial year, the aggregate falls under the Risk Review Process.

  - This ensures that repetitive smaller donations are properly reviewed to avoid misuse or underreporting.

- Authorization & Recording Process

  - The receiving department must prepare an “Advice of Gift Received” form to authorize the preliminary acceptance of the gift.

  - If the gift is received close to the fiscal year-end:

    - A preliminary valuation is made by the receiving department.

    - The Accounts Department obtains a valuation from the donor initially, which will later be adjusted to match an independent appraisal once available.

  - The Accounts Department records the gift in the Advance Register and posts the transaction to the General Ledger (GL) through a journal entry feed.

  - Daily gift transactions must be balanced, reviewed, and reconciled with supporting documents.

- Acknowledgment & Donor Communication

  - The Accounts Department issues an official acknowledgment and receipt to the donor for all gifts received.

  - This builds transparency, strengthens donor relations, and supports compliance with audit requirements.

- Non-Cash Gifts Above Threshold (₹5,000 and above)

  - For non-cash gifts valued at more than ₹5,000 (excluding marketable securities):

    - If the HEI sells the donated item(s) within three years of receipt, the receiving department must notify the Finance Department immediately.

    - Such disclosure ensures compliance with institutional governance, donor agreements, and regulatory requirements.

## Receipt of Non-Cash Gifts – Marketable Securities

- Types of Securities Accepted

  - Gifts may be received in the form of stocks, bonds, and mutual funds.

- Acceptance & Valuation

  - The Treasurer’s Office is the designated authority to accept all gifts of securities.

  - The gift is valued as of the date the securities are irrevocably transferred into the HEI’s ownership.

  - The valuation is determined as follows:

    - **Stocks/Bonds:** Number of shares × average of the high and low price on the appropriate exchange on the date of transfer.

    - **Mutual Funds:** Net Asset Value (NAV) on the date of transfer.

- Recording the Gift

  - Once valued, the Treasurer’s Office processes the securities and records the transaction through a journal entry/CRR (Cash Receipt Record).

  - Standard entry format:

    - Investments A/c – Dr (XXXX.XX)

    - To Gifts A/c – Cr (XXXX.XX)

  - The securities are credited to the appropriate securities account under HEI’s financial records.

## Receipt of Non-Cash Gifts – Real Estate (Land & Building)

- Preliminary Review

  - When real estate is offered as a gift, the receiving department must obtain detailed information about the property.

  - The Administrative Office and Head Office assist in evaluating the feasibility and risks associated with accepting the property.

- Authorization of Acceptance

  - No valuation or accounting entry is made until the gift is formally accepted by the HEI.

  - The Vice Chancellor (VC) must provide written authorization for the acceptance of all real estate gifts.

- Valuation & Recording

  - Once accepted, the value of the real estate is determined as of the date of legal transfer of ownership.

  - The Finance Department records the gift under Capital Accounts in the General Ledger at the appraised value.

  - The Finance Department also forwards a copy of the real estate gift documentation to the Head Office for institutional records.

- Asset Management

  - The Campus Accounting Office ensures that the real estate is entered into the Asset Management System, enabling proper tracking, depreciation (if applicable), and reporting.

## Receipt of Non-Cash Gifts – Vehicle Donations

- Initial Notification & Information Gathering

  - When a donor offers a vehicle as a gift, the first individual notified of the donor’s intent must collect key details:

    - Make, model, and year of manufacture.

    - Mileage and condition of the vehicle.

    - Confirmation of a signed clear title (legal ownership).

- Authorization & Delivery

  - Acceptance of the gift must be authorized by Procurement Services.

  - The initiating department, along with Procurement Services, coordinates with the donor for:

    - Delivery of the vehicle to the campus.

    - Transfer of the signed title to the HEI.

- Valuation of Vehicle

  - The donor establishes the fair market value (FMV) of the vehicle as of the contribution date.

  - FMV must not exceed the private party sales price listed in a recognized used vehicle pricing guide (e.g., Kelley Blue Book equivalent).

  - Only qualified vehicles under the Motor Vehicle Act (automobiles, trucks, motorcycles, recreational vehicles) are accepted.

- Post-Delivery Determination

  - Procurement Services decides whether the vehicle will:

    - Be put into service for institutional operations.

    - Be auctioned/sold.

    - Be donated or sold to a needy individual at significantly below FMV.

- Donor’s Deduction & Acknowledgment

  - **If the HEI sells the vehicle:** The donor’s deduction is the lesser of the FMV at donation or the gross sales proceeds.

  - **If the HEI keeps and uses the vehicle:** The donor may deduct the full FMV on the date of contribution.

  - The HEI must issue a written acknowledgment to the donor:

    - Within 30 days of the vehicle’s sale, or

    - Within 30 days of the donation, if the HEI intends to retain and use the vehicle.

- Recordkeeping & Reporting

  - If the vehicle is sold or disposed of within 3 years, Procurement Services must notify the Head Office.

  - The Head Office records the donation in Advance and posts it to the General Ledger (GL) under capital accounts.

  - The Campus Accounting Office enters the vehicle into the Asset Management System, if retained for institutional use.

## Receipt of Non-Cash Gifts – Other Items

- Scope

  - Applies to non-cash gifts other than securities, real estate, or vehicles (e.g., artworks, equipment, furniture, collections, cultural assets).

- Donor Information

  - The Head Office should collect detailed information from the donor, including:

    - Whether the donor intends to use the gift for a charitable deduction.

    - Documentation of the item’s value (invoices, appraisals, certifications, etc.).

    - Any restrictions or conditions on usage.

- Evaluation of Gift Use

  - The receiving department or college must determine whether the item:

    - Has an institutional use (academic, operational, cultural).

    - Can be stored, maintained, or used effectively.

    - Should be declined due to high maintenance/storage costs or lack of relevance.

- Recording & Accountability

  - Once accepted, the item is recorded by the Finance Department in the appropriate asset or capital accounts.

  - For items with significant or enduring value, the Campus Accounting Office will add them to the Asset Management System.

  - If items are consumables or disposable, they may be recorded as operational contributions.

  - All grants are used strictly for the purpose for which they are sanctioned, avoiding diversion of funds.

## UGC/AICTE/DST/CSIR and Other Government Grants

- Nature of Grants

  - Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) receive grants from various government funding bodies such as:

    - University Grants Commission (UGC)

    - All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE)

    - Department of Science & Technology (DST)

    - Council of Scientific & Industrial Research (CSIR)

    - Other central and state government agencies.

  - These grants are primarily awarded for the development and strengthening of the institute, with a focus on:

    - Building and Infrastructure Development (construction of classrooms, laboratories, libraries, hostels).

    - Research and Development (R&D) (funding for projects, labs, innovation hubs, and equipment).

    - Capacity Building and Faculty Development (workshops, training, fellowships).

    - Student-Centric Initiatives (scholarships, innovation, incubation centers).

- Utilization of Grants

  - Funds must be used only for the specific purpose for which the grant has been sanctioned.

  - Diversion of funds is strictly prohibited and may lead to penalties or withdrawal of funding.

  - Each grant will have a separate ledger/account head to ensure transparent accounting.

- Authorization and Monitoring

  - Expenditure of grants requires prior approval from the Principal Investigator (for research projects), Registrar/Principal, and Finance Officer.

  - Periodic Utilization Certificates (UCs) must be prepared and submitted to the respective funding agency as per prescribed timelines.

  - Internal audits and reviews will be conducted to ensure compliance with UGC/AICTE/DST/CSIR guidelines.

- Reporting and Accountability

  - The HEI must submit periodic progress reports (financial and academic) to the funding agency.

  - Unspent balances must be reported and either carried forward with permission or refunded, depending on the funding body’s rules.

  - A compliance register will be maintained to track disbursements, expenditures, and balances for each grant.

## Bank Account for Grant Management

- Separate Bank Accounts

  - The HEI must maintain dedicated bank accounts for all grants received from government agencies such as UGC, AICTE, DST, CSIR, and other funding bodies.

  - Each major grant/project shall be credited into its own bank account, distinct from the institution’s general operating accounts.

- Purpose Restriction

  - Funds deposited in these accounts must be utilized exclusively for the specific objectives of the sanctioned grant.

  - Under no circumstances shall these funds be diverted for other institutional expenses, salaries, or unrelated projects.

- Authorization of Transactions

  - Withdrawals and disbursements from grant accounts shall require the joint authorization of the Registrar/Principal, Finance Officer, and (where applicable) the Principal Investigator (PI).

  - All payments must align with the approved budget of the grant proposal.

- Accountability & Audit

  - Each grant bank account must maintain separate books of accounts, ledgers, and supporting vouchers.

  - Monthly bank reconciliations must be conducted by the Finance Office.

  - Accounts will be subject to internal and external audits to ensure compliance with funding agency guidelines.

- Reporting

  - Regular Utilization Certificates (UCs) and Statement of Expenditure (SoE) must be prepared from the respective grant account and submitted to the funding agency within stipulated timelines.

  - The Finance Department must also provide quarterly reports to the Governing Body on the status of grant accounts.

## Criteria for Getting the Grant

- Annual Call for Applications

  - Each year, the Government of India and its funding agencies (UGC, AICTE, DST, CSIR, etc.) release calls for proposals/applications for grants.

  - Grants may cover:

    - Building Development (construction of classrooms, hostels, libraries, labs).

    - Infrastructure Development (equipment, ICT facilities, campus modernization).

    - Research & Development (R&D) (scientific projects, innovation hubs, fellowships, collaborative research).

  - HEIs must submit applications on time with all required supporting documents to be considered.

- Grant Sanction & Disbursement

  - Once a grant is sanctioned, the amount is transferred directly to the respective institute’s designated bank account.

  - The funds must only be utilized for the purpose specified in the sanction letter and under the guidelines of the funding body.

- Eligibility for Infrastructure Development Grants

  - Institutions must hold valid accreditation (NAAC/NBA or equivalent) to qualify.

  - Accreditation ensures that only institutions meeting minimum quality standards can access funds for infrastructure growth.

- Eligibility for Research & Development Grants

  - The Principal Investigator (PI) applying for R&D funding must be a permanent employee (Professor/Doctor/Lecturer) on the payroll of the HEI.

  - This ensures accountability, continuity, and compliance in project execution.

  - Collaborative proposals may also be allowed, but the lead PI must belong to the HEI.

- General Compliance Requirements

  - The HEI must have a track record of timely submission of Utilization Certificates (UCs) for past grants.

  - Institutions with pending audits or misutilization cases may be disqualified.

  - The proposed project must align with the strategic priorities of national policies (e.g., NEP 2020, R&D innovation, higher education expansion).

## Usage of R&D Grant

1\. Full Utilization of Grant

- R&D grants sanctioned by the Government must be fully utilized within the approved project duration.

- Any unspent or underutilized funds require a written explanation from the concerned institute to the Management and Funding Agency.

- Failure to utilize funds may impact eligibility for future grants.

2\. Phased Release of Funds

- Principals of institutes must not release the entire sanctioned amount at once to the researcher/Principal Investigator (PI).

- Funds should be released in installments, strictly based on actual expenses incurred and supported by bills/invoices.

- This prevents misuse, encourages accountability, and ensures funds are available throughout the project duration.

3\. Compliance with Purchase Rules

- All procurements made under the R&D Grant must comply with the purchase procedures prescribed by the funding agency (e.g., UGC, DST, AICTE).

- This includes:

  - Obtaining competitive quotations/tenders.

  - Approval of purchase committees.

  - Documented vendor selection process.

  - Adherence to agency-specific procurement ceilings.

- Non-compliance with purchase rules may lead to audit objections, recovery of funds, or cancellation of grants.

4\. Monitoring and Reporting

- Researchers/Principal Investigators must submit quarterly/annual utilization statements along with progress reports to both the institute and the funding agency.

- The Finance Office must maintain a separate ledger account for each sanctioned R&D project.

- Periodic internal audits should be conducted to ensure proper fund usage.

## R&D Grant Policy

- Institutional R&D Budget

  - In addition to the R&D grants received from Government bodies (UGC, AICTE, DST, CSIR, etc.), the HEI must create an internal budget allocation for Research & Development.

  - Each year, the HEI shall earmark 5% of its total budgeted income exclusively for R&D activities.

  - This ensures continuity of research initiatives, even in the absence of external funding.

- Utilization of R&D Budget

  - The internally allocated R&D budget must be fully utilized within the academic/financial year.

  - Funds may be applied towards:

    - Faculty and student research projects.

    - Laboratory equipment and consumables.

    - Publication and patent filing charges.

    - Research workshops, conferences, and collaborations.

    - Seed funding for innovative projects.

  - Unutilized amounts, if any, should be carried forward only with explicit approval of the Governing Body.

- Approval Matrix

  - Any expenditure from the internal R&D budget requires prior approval from:

    - Vice Chancellor (VC)

    - Registrar

    - Finance Officer (FO)

  - This multi-level approval process ensures transparency, accountability, and alignment with institutional priorities.

- Government R&D Grants

  - Each year, the Government issues calls for R&D proposals under various schemes.

  - Institutes must apply for these grants by submitting well-prepared proposals clearly stating:

    - The subject area and research problem.

    - Objectives and expected outcomes.

    - Methodology and timeline.

    - Detailed budget requirements.

  - Proposals are reviewed by the respective funding agency, and successful applications receive grants, which are then transferred to the institute’s designated grant bank account.

- Compliance and Monitoring

  - Government R&D grants must be utilized only for the sanctioned purpose and in line with the funding body’s guidelines.

  - Internal and external audits will be conducted to ensure compliance.

  - Regular reports, including Utilization Certificates (UCs) and progress updates, must be submitted to the funding agency.

## Accounting & Auditing of External Grants

- Separate Accounting of Grants

  - All external grants received from UGC, AICTE, DST, CSIR, and other funding agencies must be recorded separately in the books of accounts under distinct project/grant heads.

  - Each sanctioned grant must have an exclusive ledger account to capture receipts, expenditures, and balances, ensuring no mixing of funds with general institutional accounts.

  - Expenses must be booked only against the specific purpose sanctioned by the funding agency.

- Monthly Closure of Books

  - At the end of every month, the HEI must close the books of UGC & R&D Grant Accounts along with its general accounts.

  - This ensures:

    - Up-to-date tracking of utilization.

    - Early identification of unspent balances.

    - Timely preparation of financial reports for submission.

- Audit Requirements

  - All external grants must undergo statutory audit along with the HEI’s main financial statements.

  - The audit should confirm:

    - Proper utilization of grants as per sanctioned purpose.

    - Compliance with funding body guidelines.

    - Accuracy of financial reporting and supporting documentation.

  - In case of R&D grants, Utilization Certificates (UCs) duly certified by the statutory auditor must be submitted to the funding agency.

- Submission to Government/Funding Agencies

  - Audited statements of accounts relating to each grant, along with progress reports, must be submitted to the Government/funding body within the prescribed timelines.

  - Delay or non-compliance may lead to withholding of future grants or penalties.

- Internal Monitoring

  - The Finance Officer must conduct monthly reconciliations between the grant bank accounts, ledgers, and expenditures.

  - The Governing Body should receive quarterly reports on grant utilization for oversight.

# 49. Imprest Money Policy

*Purpose:*

- Imprest money may be issued to the Principal, HoDs, permitted faculty, or staff to meet:

  - Sudden and unforeseen expenditures.

  - Petty expenses such as postage, minor transportation, etc.

*Usage Rules:*

- Bills/receipts must be maintained and submitted for all expenses incurred from imprest money.

- Imprest money is provided as an advance from the Accounts Department.

- All imprest accounts must be settled before 31st March of every financial year.

- No carry-over of imprest advances to the next financial year is allowed.

- **Settlement Frequency:** Expenditures met from imprest money can be processed for bill passing only once a month.

- **Restrictions:** Imprest money cannot be used for routine or planned purchases.

Processing of Imprest

The imprest system is a mechanism used by the University to facilitate the smooth disbursement of small advances to staff for official activities and institutional needs. To ensure accountability, transparency, and proper utilization of funds, the following procedures and controls shall apply:

1\. Requisition and Authorization

- A staff member requiring an imprest shall complete and sign the Imprest Requisition Form at least seven (7) days in advance of the intended activity.

- The requisition must clearly specify the details of the planned activity or expense for which funds are requested.

- Only authorized staff are permitted to apply, and the requisition must be approved by the relevant departmental head before submission to the Accounts Section.

2\. Verification by Accounts Section

- The Accounts Section shall confirm that the applicant has no outstanding imprest balance before approving a new requisition.

- Records of imprests issued and accounted for shall be strictly maintained to avoid duplication or misuse.

3\. Mode of Payment

- Imprest payments shall be made either in cash or by cheque/transfer, depending on the amount involved.

- Cash payments shall not exceed Rs. 5,000/-; amounts above this threshold shall be issued through cheque or bank transfer.

- The standard processing time for imprest requests shall be five (5) working days.

4\. Conditions for Granting a New Imprest

- No further imprest shall be granted before the previous one is fully accounted for.

- Exceptions may only be made under special circumstances, subject to recommendation by the Accounts Officer and approval by the Chancellor.

5\. Recovery of Outstanding Imprest

- If an imprest remains unaccounted for, the University reserves the right to recover the outstanding amount directly through payroll deductions.

- Staff members shall be notified in writing before such deductions are implemented.

6\. Surrender and Accountability

- All imprests must be surrendered and accounted for within one (1) week after completion of the planned trip or activity.

- Supporting documents such as receipts, invoices, and reports must be attached to the settlement form.

- Unspent balances must be returned in cash to the Accounts Section at the time of surrender.

7\. Petty Cash Float

- A petty cash float of Rs. 50,000/- shall be maintained under the imprest system to meet minor office expenses.

- This float may be reviewed and adjusted from time to time with the approval of the Chancellor.

- Petty cash disbursements shall be recorded promptly, reconciled periodically, and subject to internal audit checks.

8\. Security of Funds

- All cash held on University premises shall be stored overnight in a safe under dual custody (e.g., Finance Officer and Registrar).

- Access to the safe shall require both custodians to be present, ensuring an additional layer of financial security.

# 50. Finance Committee

## Introduction

The Finance Committee is a statutory body responsible for overseeing and guiding the financial planning, budgeting, monitoring, and utilization of resources in an engineering college or university. It plays a key role in ensuring fiscal accountability, resource optimization, and financial transparency — all of which are crucial for accreditation under NBA and NAAC.

## Key Objectives of the Finance Committee

### 1. Budget Planning and Allocation:

- Develop, review, and approve the institution’s annual budget.

- Ensure that financial resources are allocated in alignment with the institution's educational and operational goals.

- Provide recommendations for funding various departments, activities, and capital expenditures.

### 2. Financial Oversight:

- Monitor the institution’s income and expenditure, ensuring that it is within the approved budget.

- Oversee the allocation and management of resources across departments and projects.

- Ensure the institution’s financial performance aligns with its objectives.

### 3. Revenue Generation:

- Identify and propose sources of revenue, such as grants, donations, tuition fees, and other financial avenues.

- Recommend measures for optimizing income generation while ensuring affordability and sustainability.

### 4. Cost Control and Efficiency:

- Identify areas for cost savings and operational efficiency.

- Monitor financial reports to ensure proper utilization of funds and prevent wastage.

- Implement strategies to reduce costs while maintaining high standards of education and services.

### 5. Compliance with Regulations:

- Ensure that the institution’s financial operations comply with governmental regulations and accreditation standards.

- Oversee audits and ensure adherence to financial policies and procedures.

### 6. Investment Planning:

- Guide the institution on safe and profitable investment options for its funds.

- Recommend policies for managing investments, ensuring financial growth without undue risk.

### 7. Financial Transparency:

- Promote transparency in all financial dealings.

- Ensure that financial reports and decisions are communicated effectively to stakeholders, including faculty, students, and governing bodies.

### 8. Sustainable Financial Management:

- Develop long-term financial strategies for growth, ensuring financial sustainability.

- Plan for future infrastructure development, technological upgrades, and academic growth through sound financial management.

### 9. Monitor Financial Health:

- Regularly review financial reports to assess the institution’s financial health and identify areas for improvement.

- Make recommendations to improve financial stability and ensure that the institution’s financial resources support its strategic objectives.

### 10. Ensure Accountability:

- Hold faculty and administrative departments accountable for the proper utilization of allocated budgets.

- Establish clear financial management guidelines and ensure adherence across all levels of the institution.

## Accountability Guidelines for the Finance Committee

**1.** Reporting to the Governing Body:

- The Finance Committee is responsible for **regularly reporting** its discussions and recommendations to the **Governing Body**. This is achieved by maintaining **detailed meeting minutes** and providing comprehensive financial reports that outline the key financial decisions, budgets, and forecasts.

- These reports should ensure that the Governing Body is **well-informed** of the institution’s financial health and any potential risks or issues that could affect its stability.

**2.** Independent Review of Financial Matters:

- The Finance Committee is empowered to conduct independent reviews of financial matters without management present, if necessary. This ensures **objectivity** and **transparency** in the review process, especially when evaluating sensitive financial topics, such as audits, budgets, or discrepancies.

**3.** Interaction with the Board Chair:

- The Chair of the Finance Committee must maintain clear communication with the Board Chair about any significant financial issues, challenges, or decisions requiring the board’s attention. This allows the board chair to be well-prepared for discussions in Governing Body meetings and ensures timely action on financial matters.

**4.** Communication with the CEO/President:

- The Board Chair should keep the CEO/President informed about financial matters and any potential concerns raised by the Finance Committee. This helps maintain a transparent and consistent flow of information at all levels of leadership.

**5.** Conflict of Interest in Financial Decisions:

- The Finance Committee is responsible for identifying and managing conflicts of **interest** among committee members or staff involved in financial decisions. If a conflict is identified, the committee may request the affected individual to **recuse themselves** from discussions or decision-making related to that matter to ensure impartiality and the integrity of financial processes.

**6.** Ensuring Financial Compliance:

- The committee must ensure that the institution’s financial activities comply with relevant laws, regulations, and **accreditation standards**. This includes reviewing financial audits, overseeing financial controls, and ensuring the integrity of financial statements.

- The Finance Committee should also oversee risk management processes, ensuring that potential financial risks (e.g., cash flow issues, regulatory non-compliance, fraud risks) are adequately mitigated.

**7.** Approval of Financial Plans:

- The Finance Committee is tasked with approving financial strategies, annual budgets, and long-term financial forecasts. All financial decisions should align with the institution's strategic objectives and ensure the effective use of resources.

- The committee is also responsible for monitoring and adjusting the institution's financial performance against the approved budget and making recommendations for any necessary adjustments.

**8.** Oversight of Internal Controls and Audits:

- The committee ensures that adequate internal controls are in place to safeguard the institution’s assets and ensure compliance with financial regulations. This includes overseeing the internal audit processes and ensuring that external audits are conducted appropriately and issues are resolved.

- The Finance Committee is responsible for reviewing audit findings and ensuring that any financial discrepancies or concerns are addressed promptly.

**9.** Transparency and Communication with Stakeholders:

- The Finance Committee ensures that there is transparency in the financial decision-making process, particularly with regard to budgeting, fundraising, and use of institutional funds. It should make the necessary financial information available to relevant stakeholders, such as faculty, staff, students, and donors, where appropriate.

**10.** Regular Review of Financial Performance:

- The committee must regularly monitor and review the institution's financial performance in relation to the approved budget and long-term financial goals. The Finance Committee is responsible for reporting significant variances and proposing corrective actions when necessary.

## Budgeting and Control-Based Policies

**1.** Advance Budget Notifications:

- The Finance Committee shall notify the financial budgets for the academic year, which must be made available to the departments six months ahead of the start of the academic year. This early notification ensures that all departments can plan and manage their finances effectively in line with institutional goals.

**2.** Budget Submission Process for Organizational Units:

- Each organizational unit (e.g., departments, research centers) requiring financial resources to meet their operational, maintenance, and development expenses must submit a comprehensive financial plan five months before the academic year starts.

- The plan should outline:

  - **Operational Expenditure:** Costs for consumables.

  - **Maintenance Expenditure:** Costs for upkeep and servicing of physical infrastructure.

  - **Development Expenditure (Capital Expenditure):** Costs for expansion, upgrades, or infrastructure improvements.

- The plan should include detailed descriptions of the physical resources required, with proper specifications for each item.

**3.** Financial Estimates for Resources:

- The Estimate Committee is tasked with preparing financial estimates for the required physical resources based on the submitted plans, ensuring that they align with budgetary constraints.

**4.** Procurement Proposals:

- Organizational units requiring the purchase of goods or services must submit a procurement proposal to the Finance Officer. The Finance Officer is responsible for:

  - Verifying that the proposal is in line with the approved budgetary provisions.

  - Confirming the availability of funds.

  - Submitting recommendations to the Finance Committee for scrutiny.

  - The Finance Committee will review the proposal and, after due consideration, seek approval from the Chairman.

- Once approved, the proposal is forwarded to the Finance Officer, the In-charge Purchase, and the respective organizational unit to proceed with the procurement process.

**5.** Procurement Process:

- The In-charge Purchase shall follow the standard procurement procedures to ensure that purchases are made in an efficient, transparent, and cost-effective manner. The Central Stores will manage the distribution of procured items, providing the necessary goods to the respective organizational units through an issue note.

**6.** Construction and Infrastructure Procurement:

- For construction projects, the In-charge Construction will prepare a detailed engineering proposal outlining the necessary materials and services required for the project.

- This proposal is submitted to the Finance Officer, who verifies the proposal with reference to budgetary provisions. The verified proposal is then forwarded to the Finance Committee, which seeks approval from the President.

- After receiving approval, the proposal is sent back to the Estimate Committee and Finance Officer for further processing.

**7.** Expenditure Control:

- All expenditure must strictly align with the approved budget. Any expenditure related to the purchase of goods, services, or conducting activities requires prior approval from the Principal/Chairman.

- The institution reserves the right to re-appropriate the budgeted expenditure as necessary, based on utility, necessity, or for other unforeseen reasons. This ensures financial flexibility and adaptability to changing circumstances during the academic year.

# 51. Financial Statements Format and Audit

## I. Financial Statements Format

### 1. Balance Sheet

- Shows the financial position as of the end of the financial year.

- Key components:

  - Assets (Fixed assets, current assets, cash & bank balances)

  - Liabilities (loans, payables, provisions)

  - Capital Fund/Corpus and Reserves

### 2. Income and Expenditure Account

- Reflects the institution’s operational performance.

- Records:

  - Revenues (fees, grants, donations, interest, etc.)

  - Expenditures (salaries, maintenance, utilities, consumables, etc.)

- Surplus or deficit is transferred to the capital fund.

### 3. Receipts and Payments Account

- Cash-based summary of all cash inflows and outflows during the year.

- Includes both capital and revenue items:

  - **Receipts:** Grants, donations, student fees, interest, etc.

  - **Payments:** Salaries, procurement, utilities, advances, etc.

### 4. Schedules & Notes to Accounts

- Detailed breakdown of each head in the financial statements.

- Includes:

  - Depreciation schedule

  - Grant utilization statement

  - Fixed asset register summary

  - Bank reconciliation details

## II. Audit of Financial Statements

### 1. Types of Audit

- Internal Audit

  - Conducted periodically to ensure compliance and internal control.

  - Focuses on adherence to institutional policies and procedures.

- Statutory Audit

  - Conducted by an external Chartered Accountant (CA) firm.

  - Ensures compliance with applicable laws (e.g., Income Tax Act, UGC/AICTE norms).

### 2. Audit Scope

- Verification of:

  - Vouchers, invoices, and supporting documents

  - Budget compliance

  - Utilization of grants/funds

  - Bank statements and reconciliations

  - Depreciation and asset records

  - Fee collection and payroll

### 3. Audit Report Components

- Auditor’s opinion on true and fair view of accounts.

- Qualified/Unqualified opinion depending on findings.

- Comments on:

  - Irregularities, if any

  - Delays in fund utilization

  - Recommendations for financial management improvements

### 4. Submission & Approval

- Final audited statements are:

  - Submitted to the Governing Body

  - Forwarded to funding agencies (AICTE, UGC, etc.)

  - Used for statutory filings and compliance
