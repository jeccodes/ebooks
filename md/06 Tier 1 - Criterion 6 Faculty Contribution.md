## Abstract

Criterion 6: Faculty Contribution is a vital component of the National Board of Accreditation (NBA) framework, carrying a total weightage of 120 marks. This criterion is designed to capture the comprehensive roles of faculty members within higher education institutions. Unlike other criteria that focus on infrastructure, governance, or student outcomes, this one directly assesses the quality, commitment, and impact of the teaching community. Faculty are considered the backbone of any institution, and NBA emphasizes that their contribution must extend far beyond classroom teaching. Instead, they must be active participants in professional development, research, consultancy, and institutional growth. To balance these dimensions, Criterion 6 is divided equally into two major sections: Professional Development Activities (6.1) and Research & Development Activities (6.2), each carrying 60 marks.

The first section, 6.1 Professional Development Activities, highlights how faculty members engage in continuous learning, knowledge sharing, and student mentoring. In today’s fast-changing academic and industrial environment, faculty members must consistently upgrade their skills and competencies to remain relevant. NBA recognizes that professional growth is essential not only for the teacher’s personal advancement but also for the institution’s overall progress. A key indicator under this section is membership in professional societies at the national or international level. Such memberships in associations like IEEE, ACM, ISTE, IETE, ASME, or the Institution of Engineers (India) reflect a faculty member’s dedication to engaging with professional networks and global academic communities. Active participation—such as attending conferences, presenting papers, or contributing to committees—is valued more than passive membership.

Another important dimension is faculty involvement in Short-Term Training Programs (STTPs) and Faculty Development Programs (FDPs). Here, NBA evaluates both roles: faculty as resource persons and faculty as participants. When faculty serve as resource persons in FDPs or STTPs, they are recognized as experts sharing their knowledge with peers across institutions, thereby contributing to the wider academic community. On the other hand, participation as learners in FDPs and STTPs ensures that faculty are continuously upgrading their knowledge, acquiring new pedagogical skills, and staying updated with technological advancements. By encouraging both participation and leadership in training programs, NBA ensures that faculty remain lifelong learners while also being recognized as domain experts. The digital revolution in higher education is also recognized through faculty contributions to MOOCs (Massive Open Online Courses) and e-content development. Faculty who create or contribute to SWAYAM, NPTEL, or other e-learning platforms bring significant value to their institutions by extending their teaching beyond the classroom and contributing to the national knowledge pool. Similarly, faculty certification through MOOCs is also rewarded. By completing certifications on platforms such as SWAYAM, NPTEL, Coursera, or edX, faculty members demonstrate their willingness to adapt to new learning environments, stay updated in their subjects, and integrate global knowledge into local teaching practices.

Equally important is the organization of FDPs and STTPs at the departmental level. Institutions that host such training programs showcase leadership, outreach, and the ability to create professional learning environments for peers. In addition, faculty mentoring of student innovative projects is another dimension. By guiding students in hackathons, start-up incubation, prototype development, or patent filing, faculty foster creativity, problem-solving, and entrepreneurship among learners. Finally, professional development is further measured by faculty internships, training, or collaborations with industry. This ensures that teachers gain practical exposure to current industrial practices, which they can transfer to their students, thereby strengthening industry—academia linkages. Taken together, Professional Development Activities reflect NBA’s focus on ensuring that faculty members remain active learners, mentors, and contributors to both academia and industry.

The second section, 6.2 Research and Development Activities, shifts the focus from professional growth to scholarly contribution and institutional research culture. Here, NBA acknowledges the critical role faculty play in advancing knowledge, generating new ideas, and solving real-world problems. One of the most fundamental measures under this section is academic research output. Faculty are expected to publish in reputed peer-reviewed journals, conference proceedings, and books. Publications indexed in Scopus, Web of Science, or UGC-CARE carry particular weight, as they signal global quality standards. The impact of these publications is measured not only by their number but also by citations, impact factors, and overall contribution to the field.

Closely linked to research productivity is Ph.D. supervision. Faculty who guide doctoral students contribute to building the next generation of researchers and academicians. Both ongoing and completed Ph.D. supervisions are considered, with greater recognition for successful completions. This sub-criterion emphasizes faculty’s academic leadership and their role in mentoring scholars beyond undergraduate and postgraduate levels.

Research and development activities also include faculty contributions to institutional development projects. This may involve designing curriculum frameworks, developing laboratories, filing patents, creating software tools, or contributing to innovative products. Marks are awarded for tangible contributions that directly enhance institutional resources and capabilities. Additionally, sponsored research projects form a significant part of this section. Faculty who attract funding from national agencies such as AICTE, UGC, DST, DRDO, DBT, ICSSR, or from international organizations demonstrate their ability to secure resources, collaborate widely, and deliver impactful research outcomes. The value of the funding, duration of projects, and research outputs all influence the assessment.

Consultancy work is another crucial aspect, recognizing faculty members who apply their expertise to solve problems for industry, government, or non-government organizations. Consultancy not only generates revenue but also bridges the gap between academia and real-world challenges. Faculty who engage in consultancy enhance the institution’s relevance, visibility, and reputation. Furthermore, NBA acknowledges the importance of institutional support for research through seed money or internal grants. Faculty who receive and utilize institutional funding for pilot projects or innovations are recognized, as such funding encourages grassroots-level research and capacity-building. Often, seed money leads to larger funded projects in the future, making it a strategic investment in institutional research culture.

Taken together, Research and Development Activities reflect an institution’s capacity to foster a vibrant research ecosystem. Faculty who are engaged in publishing, supervising Ph.D. students, leading funded projects, offering consultancy, and developing innovative products contribute directly to the institution’s reputation and ranking. More importantly, their research outputs strengthen the teaching—learning process by ensuring that faculty bring fresh insights, cutting-edge knowledge, and practical problem-solving approaches into the classroom.

When viewed holistically, Criterion 6 ensures that faculty are evaluated not only as teachers but as well-rounded professionals. The Professional Development Activities highlight their role in continuous learning, training, and student engagement, while the Research and Development Activities emphasize their contributions to knowledge creation, institutional innovation, and societal impact. By allocating equal weightage to both sections, NBA ensures that institutions do not focus on one area at the expense of the other. Instead, balanced faculty development across both domains is encouraged, leading to stronger institutions and better student outcomes.

The significance of Criterion 6 also lies in its alignment with global educational benchmarks such as the Washington Accord Graduate Attributes and the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020. Both frameworks emphasize outcome-based education, lifelong learning, research-driven teaching, and global competitiveness. Criterion 6 operationalizes these goals by ensuring that faculty are recognized and rewarded for activities that build academic quality, research capability, and industry relevance. Institutions that perform strongly in Criterion 6 demonstrate that their faculty are updated professionals, active researchers, capable mentors, and valuable contributors to society.

In conclusion, Criterion 6: Faculty Contribution is not merely an assessment of individual teacher performance but a holistic evaluation of how an institution fosters and supports its faculty to grow, innovate, and contribute. The total of 120 marks under this criterion captures the balance between professional development and research, thereby ensuring that faculty members are both continuous learners and active knowledge creators. Institutions excelling in this criterion establish themselves as environments where faculty are empowered, students are inspired, and academic excellence thrives. By focusing on faculty contributions, NBA underscores the principle that the quality of higher education depends fundamentally on the quality and commitment of its teachers.

# 1. Introduction

Criterion 6: Faculty Contribution is a vital component of the National Board of Accreditation (NBA) framework, carrying a weightage of 120 marks. It emphasizes the central role that faculty play in shaping the academic and institutional ecosystem. NBA recognizes faculty not merely as transmitters of classroom knowledge but as active contributors to professional development, research, innovation, and overall institutional growth. This criterion ensures that faculty are continuously upgrading themselves, participating in scholarly and industry networks, mentoring students, and contributing to knowledge creation through research and consultancy. By focusing on faculty activities, Criterion 6 underlines the belief that the quality of education in any institution depends significantly on the strength, competence, and contributions of its faculty members. The marks under this criterion are equally divided into two categories: Professional Development Activities (6.1 — 60 marks) and Research and Development Activities (6.2 — 60 marks).

The first section, Professional Development Activities (6.1), evaluates the extent to which faculty members enhance their professional standing, engage in continuous learning, and act as mentors for student innovation. Professional development is critical in an era of rapid technological change, where faculty must constantly adapt to evolving pedagogical approaches, digital education, and industry demands. The first sub-criterion, Memberships in Professional Societies (6.1.1), allocates five marks to faculty members who actively participate in national and international professional bodies such as IEEE, ACM, ISTE, IETE, ASME, or other relevant associations. Membership demonstrates a commitment to professional networking, staying abreast of global trends, and engaging in collaborative learning. More weight is given to active involvement, such as presenting papers, participating in committees, or organizing activities, than to passive membership.

The second sub-criterion, Faculty as Resource Persons or Participants in STTPs and FDPs (6.1.2), carries ten marks, split equally between faculty acting as resource persons and those participating as learners. Faculty who are invited as resource persons in Short-Term Training Programs (STTPs) or Faculty Development Programs (FDPs) are recognized for their expertise and contribution to broader academic communities. On the other hand, faculty participation in FDPs and STTPs reflects their commitment to continuous learning and skill enhancement. Together, these sub-criteria ensure that faculty are positioned both as contributors and beneficiaries within the larger academic ecosystem.

A further five marks are allocated under 6.1.3 for Faculty Contribution to MOOCs and E-Content, which acknowledges faculty involvement in the creation of Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) on platforms like SWAYAM or NPTEL. Faculty who design, record, or publish digital content contribute not only to their institution but also to the wider educational community. This aligns with national priorities such as NEP 2020, which emphasizes blended learning and digital access. Complementing this, 6.1.4 awards ten marks for Faculty Certification of MOOCs, where faculty members upgrade their own knowledge through certifications on platforms like SWAYAM, Coursera, or edX. These certifications reflect faculty willingness to adopt emerging trends and integrate new content into their teaching.

Organizing training and development programs is another dimension of faculty contribution. 6.1.5 awards ten marks to departments that organize FDPs and STTPs, showcasing faculty leadership, organizational capacity, and outreach. Similarly, 6.1.6, worth ten marks, acknowledges Faculty Support in Student Innovative Projects. Here, faculty mentoring of student projects, hackathons, incubation activities, start-up initiatives, and patent development is assessed. Such mentoring ensures that students are guided effectively in applying theoretical knowledge to real-world problems and developing entrepreneurial mindsets. Finally, 6.1.7, also carrying ten marks, recognizes Faculty Internships, Training, or Collaborations with Industry. Faculty who engage in industry exposure bring back practical insights that enrich classroom teaching and curriculum design. These collaborations strengthen the critical industry—academia connect and improve student employability.

The second major section of Criterion 6, Research and Development Activities (6.2), emphasizes faculty contributions to advancing knowledge, building research capacity, and strengthening institutional reputation. A total of 60 marks are allocated under this head, with several sub-criteria that assess publications, projects, consultancy, and Ph.D. supervision. 6.2.1, Academic Research, carries ten marks and evaluates faculty output in the form of high-quality publications in indexed journals such as Scopus, Web of Science, or UGC-CARE. Here, not only the number but also the quality of publications—measured through citations, impact factors, or h-index—plays a role. By encouraging strong research output, this metric ensures that faculty contribute to the global body of knowledge.

6.2.2, Ph.D. Student Guidance, provides five marks to faculty who supervise doctoral students. This contribution extends faculty influence beyond undergraduate and postgraduate levels into doctoral research, which enhances both institutional reputation and academic depth. 6.2.3, Development Activities, worth ten marks, recognizes contributions in curriculum design, laboratory development, patent filing, product development, and software tools. These activities demonstrate how faculty drive innovation and institutional improvement beyond conventional teaching roles.

A significant portion of marks, 15 under 6.2.4, is awarded for Sponsored Research Projects. Faculty who secure funding from government agencies such as AICTE, UGC, DST, DRDO, DBT, or international organizations showcase their ability to attract resources and deliver impactful research. The level of funding and measurable outcomes influence the marks awarded. Similarly, 6.2.5, Consultancy Work, also worth 15 marks, rewards faculty who provide consultancy services to industries, government departments, or NGOs. Consultancy not only generates revenue but also highlights the practical relevance of faculty expertise in solving real-world problems. Finally, 6.2.6, Institution Seed Money or Internal Grants, allocates five marks for institutions that provide faculty with seed money to initiate pilot research projects. Such internal funding mechanisms encourage grassroots-level innovation and capacity-building, which can later translate into larger externally funded projects.

Taken together, Professional Development Activities and Research & Development Activities provide a holistic view of faculty contributions. Professional development reflects the faculty’s commitment to continuous learning, networking, mentoring, and industry engagement, while research and development highlight their role in advancing knowledge, attracting funding, and contributing to innovation. The equal distribution of marks ensures that institutions do not prioritize one area at the expense of the other but instead encourage balanced growth across both dimensions.

The importance of Criterion 6 goes beyond scoring in accreditation. It reflects how faculty contributions shape institutional culture and student success. When faculty are actively engaged in professional development, students benefit from updated knowledge, new teaching methods, and enhanced mentorship. When faculty conduct quality research, institutions gain recognition, attract collaborations, and generate knowledge that benefits society. Faculty consultancy and industry collaborations bridge the gap between academia and industry, ensuring that graduates are industry-ready. Faculty involvement in innovation projects fosters entrepreneurship and creativity among students. In essence, Criterion 6 is directly tied to student outcomes, institutional reputation, and long-term sustainability.

From the NBA perspective, Criterion 6 also aligns with the Washington Accord graduate attributes and NEP 2020 mandates, which emphasize outcome-based education, research-driven teaching, global competitiveness, and digital learning. By evaluating faculty contributions across these diverse activities, NBA ensures that institutions are producing graduates who are not only academically strong but also equipped with critical thinking, innovation capacity, and industry relevance.

In conclusion, Criterion 6: Faculty Contribution is not merely a metric of faculty performance but a measure of an institution’s academic culture, research capacity, and industry relevance. With its comprehensive allocation of 120 marks, equally divided between professional development and research activities, this criterion encourages institutions to nurture faculty as continuous learners, innovators, and leaders. Institutions that perform strongly in this area create an environment where students are mentored by updated, research-active, and industry-connected faculty, which in turn leads to better graduate outcomes and stronger institutional standing. Thus, Criterion 6 serves as a cornerstone for accreditation, quality enhancement, and the continuous growth of higher education institutions.

# 2. Criterion 6: Faculty Contribution

This section evaluates how faculty members enhance their professional standing, participate in knowledge-sharing, and support students in innovation and industry linkages.

- Faculty memberships in bodies like IEEE, ACM, ISTE, ASME, ASCE, etc. are recognized.

- These memberships demonstrate professional credibility, networking, and commitment to continuous learning.

- Marks are awarded based on the number of faculty actively engaged.

- **6.1.2.1 Faculty as Resource Persons in STTPs/FDPs (05 Marks):**

  - Faculty who deliver lectures, workshops, or training in Short-Term Training Programs (STTPs) or Faculty Development Programs (FDPs).

  - Indicates recognition of faculty expertise nationally or internationally.

- **6.1.2.2 Faculty Members' Participation in STTPs/FDPs (05 Marks):**

  - Faculty who attend training programs to upgrade skills and stay current with academic and industry trends.

### 6.1.3 Faculty Contribution in Development of SWAYAM MOOCs and other E-Content (05 Marks)

- Faculty involved in designing, developing, and publishing Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) or e-learning content.

- Aligns with the national digital learning ecosystem and NEP 2020 initiatives.

### 6.1.4 Faculty Certification of MOOCs through SWAYAM, NPTEL, Coursera, etc. (10 Marks)

- Recognition of faculty completing certified online courses.

- Demonstrates commitment to upskilling and lifelong learning.

### 6.1.5 FDP/STTP Organized by the Department (10 Marks)

- Marks for departments that successfully organize FDPs/STTPs for other faculty.

- Reflects academic leadership, outreach, and contribution to knowledge dissemination.

### 6.1.6 Faculty Support in Student Innovative Projects (10 Marks)

- Faculty mentoring and guiding students in hackathons, competitions, incubation activities, and innovative product development.

- Encourages innovation, creativity, and entrepreneurial thinking.

### 6.1.7 Faculty Internship/Training/Collaboration with Industry (10 Marks)

- Faculty participating in internships, training, or collaborative research projects with industries.

- Builds industry—academia connect and enhances curriculum relevance.

## 6.2 Research and Development Activities (60 Marks)

This section emphasizes faculty’s role in advancing knowledge through research, consultancy, and sponsored projects.

- Faculty publishing in reputed journals, conferences, and books.

- Recognition given to indexed publications (Scopus, Web of Science, UGC-CARE).

### 6.2.2 Ph.D. Student Details (05 Marks)

- Faculty guiding Ph.D. scholars and contributing to doctoral research.

- Includes completed and ongoing Ph.D. supervisions.

### 6.2.3 Development Activities (10 Marks)

- Faculty contributions in curriculum design, lab development, patents, product designs, and other developmental initiatives.

- Projects funded by government agencies (DST, AICTE, DRDO, UGC, ICSSR) or international bodies.

- Marks awarded based on the quantum of funds received and project impact.

### 6.2.5 Consultancy Work (15 Marks)

- Revenue-generating consultancy services provided by faculty to industries, government, or NGOs.

- Reflects practical application of expertise and institutional impact.

### 6.2.6 Institution Seed Money or Internal Research Grant to Faculty (05 Marks)

### 6.1.2.2. Faculty Members' Participation in STTPs/FDPs (05 marks)

- **Definition:** Faculty attending FDPs/STTPs for skill upgradation.

- **Evidence:** Certificates of participation, attendance records.

- **Scope:**

  - Faculty developing online courses on SWAYAM, NPTEL, Coursera, EdX, or institutional platforms.

- **Impact:** Strengthens industry-academia linkage, ensures curriculum relevance, and enhances employability of students.

## Overall Impact of Professional Development Activities (60 marks)

- Ensures faculty remain up-to-date with emerging technologies.

| **Criterion**    | **Marks / Points** |
|------------------|--------------------|
| 2→ day FDP/STTP  | 3 points.          |
| \>5 day FDP/STTP | 5 points.          |

- **Computation Method (as per NBA guidelines):**

- **Impact:**

|-----------------------------------------|--------------------|

  - 5\. Compute RDF (Required Faculty) = DS ÷ 20 (rounded up),

    - where DS = total students in the department for that year.

  - **6. Compute Assessment Points (AP) for each year:**

    - **Formula:** AP = Sum ÷ (0.5 × RDF)

    - Cap AP at 5 maximum per year.

  - **7. Average Assessment Points (final marks):**

    - **Formula:** (AP_1 + AP_2 + AP_3) ÷ 3

    - Cap final average at 5 maximum.

# 24. FDP/STTP Organized by the Department

## 1. Context & Purpose

- This sub-criterion is part of NBA Criterion 6: Faculty Contributions.

- It evaluates the department’s active role in organizing Faculty Development Programs (FDPs) and Short-Term Training Programs (STTPs) to enhance teaching-learning and research competencies.

- Emphasis is on knowledge dissemination, skill upgradation, and collaborative academic activities at departmental level.

## 2. Scope of Consideration

- **FDPs and STTPs Organized by the department:**

  - Individually (by the department alone).

  - In collaboration with other departments of the institution.

  - In collaboration with external institutions/organizations (e.g., IITs, AICTE, ISTE, UGC, industries).

- **Minimum Duration:** 5 days (programs less than 5 days are not counted).

- **Assessment Period:** CAY, CAYm1, and CAYm2 (past 3 academic years).

## 3. Evidence Required

## For each FDP/STTP organized, the following proofs must be documented:

- Program Brochure / Schedule (with dates, topics, and resource persons).

- Attendance / Participation Records (faculty list, signed sheets, online registrations).

- Event Report / Summary (objectives, outcomes, number of participants).

- Certificates of Participation issued to attendees.

- Approval / Sponsorship Letters (if funded/supported by AICTE, ISTE, UGC, etc.).

- Photos / Screenshots of sessions and inaugural/valedictory functions.

## 4. Evaluation by NBA (Scoring Rules)

- **Scoring Formula:**

  - 2 points per FDP/STTP organized (of minimum 5 days).

  - Maximum of 4 marks per academic year (CAY, CAYm1, CAYm2).

  - Maximum of 10 marks total across 3 years.

> ***Example:***

- If the department organizes 2 FDPs in CAY (each 5 days), it earns 4 marks for that year.

- If only 1 FDP is organized in CAYm1, then 2 marks.

- If 3 FDPs are organized in CAYm2, still capped at 4 marks for that year.

- Total = (4 + 2+ 4) = 10 marks.

# 38. Programs Organized (Workshops, FDPs, STTPs, Industrial Trainings)

## 1. Purpose of this Criterion

- NBA evaluates how actively a department organizes knowledge-sharing and professional development programs.

- It reflects the institutional commitment to upgrading faculty/student skills, promoting lifelong learning, and building industry linkages.

- Marks are given based on the number, diversity, and quality of programs organized.

## 2. Types of Programs Recognized by NBA

- Workshops

  - Hands-on sessions, typically short duration (1—5 days).

  - Focused on emerging tools, techniques, or specific applications.

  - **Audience:** students, faculty, or both.

- Faculty Development Programs (FDPs)

  - Typically 5—14 days, designed to improve teaching, research, and domain expertise of faculty.

  - May be funded by AICTE, ISTE, UGC, industry sponsors.

- Short-Term Training Programs (STTPs)

  - Technical training with practical exposure, usually for faculty or students.

  - Sponsored by AICTE or industry.

- Industrial Training Programs

  - Collaborations with industry to give students/faculty exposure to real-world practices.

  - Includes internships, live projects, industrial visits, and corporate skill-building modules.

## 3. Key Documentation Required for NBA

- Program Brochure / Schedule

  - Contains program objectives, topics, speakers/resource persons, dates, and duration.

- Attendance / Participation Records

  - Faculty and student attendance sheets (signed/online records).

  - Participant registration details.

- Event Report / Summary

  - Clearly stating objectives, learning outcomes, resource persons, and number of participants.

  - Photos/screenshots (for online events) as evidence.

- Approval / Sponsorship Letters

  - If funded by AICTE, ISTE, UGC, or industry, keep approval/sanction letters.

- Certificates Issued

  - Participation/completion certificates provided to attendees.

## 4. Outcomes & NBA Relevance

- **Knowledge Enhancement:** Upgrading both faculty and students in emerging technologies (Al, IoT, cybersecurity, cloud computing, etc.).

- **Skill Building:** Practical exposure for participants, improving employability and teaching quality.

- **Industry Linkages:** Strengthens collaboration with external organizations.

- **Research & Innovation:** May result in joint projects, prototype development, or publications.

- **NBA Scoring:**

  - More programs with strong participation → higher marks.

  - Sponsored/Funded programs carry higher weight.

## 5. Scoring Guidelines (NBA)

- **Excellent (8—10 marks):**

  - Multiple FDPs/STTPs/workshops organized.

  - High participation (students + faculty).

  - Evidence of sponsorship, reports, and outcomes.

- **Moderate (4—7 marks):**

  - Some programs organized, but limited participation or documentation.

- **Low (0—3 marks):**

  - Few or no programs, weak documentation.

## What improves scoring

- Higher TRLs (≥6) with industry pilots and third-party validation.

- Awards/recognitions, patent filings linked to the prototype.

- Clear impact narrative (cost/time savings, performance vs. baseline, user adoption).

## Common pitfalls

- Listing concept notes with no working evidence.

- Missing test plans or unverifiable demo media.

- Inflated TRLs without external validation.

## Best-practice workflow

- Start with specs + test plan → build → verification → validation/pilot → iterate.

- After each milestone, update version, evidence links, and TRL in the register.

- Map each entry to faculty KPIs and student outcomes (POs/PSOs) for OBE alignment.

# 51. Sanction Letters from Funding Agencies

- Definition & Purpose

  - A Sanction Letter is the official approval document issued by the funding agency confirming that a research project has been awarded and specifying the sanctioned grant.

  - It serves as the primary evidence for NBA to verify that the institution has indeed received external sponsored research funding.

- Key Contents of a Sanction Letter

  - Project Title clearly mentioned.

  - Principal Investigator (PI) and Co-PI(s) names.

  - Department / Institution where the project is sanctioned.

  - Funding Agency Name (DST, UGC, AICTE, DBT, DRDO, CSR partner, etc.).

  - Sanctioned Amount (in INR — lakhs).

  - Duration of the Project (start and end dates).

  - Date of Sanction (year must align with CAYm1, CAYm2, or CAYm3).

  - Release Conditions (installments, utilization certification requirements).

  - Unique Reference Number / File No. (must match with project records).

  - Authorized Signatory from the funding agency.

- NBA Documentation Requirements

  - Keep scanned copies of all sanction letters in a single repository (folder) sorted by year (CAYm1, CAYm2, CAYm3).

  - **Prepare an Evidence Tracker Sheet with columns:** \| Project Title \| Funding Agency \| Sanction Letter Ref No. \| Date of Sanction \| Amount (Lakhs) \| Link to Copy (PDF) I

  - Ensure that the funding amount mentioned in sanction letters matches the figures shown in the project summary tables (CAY-wise).

  - During NBA visit, present both the Excel summary sheet and original sanction letters (hard copy or soft copy with digital signatures).

- Best Practices

  - Attach sanction letters as hyperlinks in Excel (clickable PDF links for each project).

  - **Maintain a cross-reference index:** PI → Project → Funding Agency → Sanction Letter No.

  - If multiple installments were sanctioned, maintain all related sanction letters (initial + subsequent releases).

  - Ensure infrastructure-based grants are excluded (NBA won’t count them).

  - Stamp or watermark scanned copies with "Verified — Sponsored Research" for clarity.

# 52. MoUs / Agreements with Industries or CSR Bodies

- Definition & Purpose

  - A Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) or Agreement is a formal document signed between the institution and an industry partner / corporate body / CSR division.

  - It specifies the terms of collaboration, funding support, responsibilities of both parties, and expected deliverables.

  - For NBA, these documents are evidence of industry linkage, CSR engagement, and real-world sponsored research.

- Key Elements of an MoU / Agreement

  - **Parties Involved:** Name of the Institution and the Industry/CSR body.

  - **Scope of Collaboration:** Area of research (e.g., Al in Agriculture, Green Energy, IoT for Healthcare).

  - **Funding Commitment:** Amount sanctioned (in Lakhs), mode of disbursement (installments, lump sum).

  - **Duration:** Start date and end date of collaboration/project.

  - **Responsibilities:**

    - Institution — Research execution, manpower, labs, reporting.

    - Industry/CSR Funding, equipment support, mentorship, commercialization.

  - **Intellectual Property (IP) Clauses:** Who owns patents, publications, or prototypes.

  - Termination & Renewal Clauses.

  - **Signatures:** Authorized signatories from both sides, with seal/stamp.

- NBA Documentation Requirements

  - **Keep scanned MoUs/Agreements in a separate folder (tagged by year:** CAYm1, CAYm2, CAYm3).

  - **Maintain an MoU Register or Excel Tracker with columns:** \| Project Title \| Industry/CSR Partner \| MoU/Agreement Ref. No. \| Date Signed \| Funding Amount (Lakhs) \| Duration \| Link to Copy (PDF) I

  - Ensure that the funding amount and duration match the details recorded in sponsored projects table.

  - For CSR-funded projects, ensure the CSR policy reference (like Section 135 of Indian Companies Act) is cited in the agreement.

- Best Practices for NBA Presentation

  - Provide physical signed copies during visit + digital hyperlinks in Excel tracker.

  - Highlight long-term MoUs (3+ years) with industries → shows sustainability.

  - Maintain a summary sheet of MoUs with CSR bodies (Infosys CSR, TCS Foundation, Reliance CSR, etc.) separately from government-funded projects.

  - **Mention joint outcomes in MoU annexures:** prototypes, patents, start-ups, publications.

  - Use standardized MoU templates across departments to avoid missing clauses.

# 53. Project Utilization Certificates (UCs) submitted to Funding Agencies

- Definition & Purpose

  - A Utilization Certificate (UC) is a formal document submitted by the institution to the funding agency.

  - It certifies that the sanctioned funds have been utilized strictly for the approved purpose, within the specified financial year/project duration.

  - UCs are mandatory for the release of subsequent installments or for closure of the project.

  - For NBA, UCs act as proof of actual fund usage beyond sanction letters.

- Key Contents of a UC

  - Project Title and Sanction Letter No.

  - PI and CO-PI names with department.

  - Funding Agency name.

  - Sanctioned Amount (₹ Lakhs) and Amount Released (installment-wise).

  - **Expenditure Incurred (head-wise:** equipment, manpower, travel, consumables, contingency, overheads).

  - Unspent Balance (if any).

  - **Certification Statement signed by:**

    - Head of Institution (Principal/Director).

    - Finance Officer/Chartered Accountant (with seal & date).

  - Period of Utilization (financial year/project year).

  - Date of Submission to the funding agency.

- NBA Documentation Requirements

  - **Maintain scanned copies of UCs for each sponsored project in a UC Repository (folder) year-wise:** CAYm1, CAYm2, CAYm3.

  - **Create a UC Tracker (Excel) with columns:** \| Project Title \| Funding Agency \| UC Ref. No. \| Period Covered \| Amount Utilized (₹) \| Submitted On \| Verified By \| Link to UC (PDF) I

  - Ensure that the amount utilized matches the sanctioned/received figures recorded in the project summary sheets.

  - Keep acknowledgment from the funding agency (if provided) for NBA verification.

- Best Practices

  - Cross-check UC data with sanction letters and project financial statements before NBA visit.

  - Highlight projects with 100% utilization (shows efficiency).

  - Track carry-forward/unspent balances separately.

  - Hyperlink UCs (PDF/Scanned) in Excel tracker for instant access during NBA presentation.

  - If the institution has a Finance/Accounts Office, keep an official stamp/seal on UCs.

# 54. Progress Reports / Final Reports of Completed Projects

- Definition & Purpose

  - Progress Reports → Interim reports submitted to the funding agency at regular intervals (quarterly/half-yearly/annually).

  - Final Reports → Comprehensive document submitted at the end of the project, summarizing objectives, methodology, results, outcomes, and utilization of funds.

  - NBA considers these reports as evidence of project execution and outcomes, beyond just sanction and utilization.

- Key Contents of Progress Reports

  - Project Title, PI/Co-PI details, Funding Agency.

  - Sanction details (File No., Year, Sanctioned Amount, Duration).

  - Work Completed (till date) experiments, data collected, prototypes developed.

  - Challenges/Delays (if any).

  - Funds utilized so far.

  - Next phase of work planned.

  - Submission date and signatures (PI, Head of Institution).

- Key Contents of Final Reports

  - Executive Summary.

  - Objectives and Research Questions.

  - Methodology (Tools, Techniques, Experiments).

  - Results, Findings, Innovations.

  - Outcomes Publications, Patents, Prototypes, Technology Transfers.

  - Financial Summary — Total Sanctioned vs Utilized.

  - Impact (academic, industrial, societal).

  - Certification of completion (signed by PI, Head, and Funding Agency acknowledgement if issued).

- NBA Documentation Requirements

  - Maintain scanned copies of all Progress & Final Reports in a Reports Repository, organized by year (CAYm1, CAYm2, CAYm3).

  - Completed projects must have Final Reports; ongoing ones should show at least latest Progress Reports.

- Best Practices for NBA Compliance

  - Link each project in the Projects_Details sheet to its Reports Tracker entry (cross-reference).

  - Highlight successful outcomes (patents, publications, startups) in the Final Reports.

  - Ensure funding agency acknowledgment/acceptance of reports is available (email, letter).

  - Hyperlink Progress/Final Reports in Excel for instant retrieval during NBA visit.

# 65. UCs (Utilization Certificates) submitted by faculty to institution.

- Purpose & role

  - UC (Utilization Certificate) confirms that seed money/internal grant was spent only for the sanctioned project and within the approved period.

  - Forms the primary basis for awarding the "Utilized (2 marks)" under NBA’s Institution Seed Money / IRG (5 marks).

- When to submit

  - Annually (end of financial year) and at project closure (whichever comes first).

  - Also upon grant revision/extension or additional tranche release.

- Who signs

  - PI (certifies work & spending), Head of Department, and Finance Officer/Chartered Accountant (certifies accounts).

  - Optional endorsement by Dean Research/Registrar for final closure.

- Mandatory contents (must appear)

  - **Project identifiers:** PI name, Department, Project Title, Sanction Order No./Date, Project Code/Budget Head.

  - **Financial summary (₹):** Sanctioned, Released/Received (CAY-wise if applicable), Expenditure (head-wise), Unspent balance/carry-forward.

  - **Utilization period:** start—end dates covered by the UC (e.g., 01-Apr-2024 to 31-Mar-2025).

  - **Certification text:** funds utilized as per rules and sanction terms, with true & fair view of accounts.

  - Signatures, names, designations, dates; institution seal.

- Head-wise expenditure (typical)

  - Equipment/Software, Consumables, Manpower/Student Assistantship, Travel/Field work, Contingency, Overheads (if allowed).

- Required attachments

  - Statement of Expenditure (SOE) mapped to sanction heads.

  - Ledger extract/Project account & Bank JV/credit note (internal grant transfer proof).

  - Key invoices/voucher summary, asset register entries for durable items.

  - Progress/Final report matching the UC period; stock/consumable issue register (if relevant).

- Compliance & reconciliation checklist

  - Amounts tally across Sanction Order ↔ Tracker ↔ Bank/Accounts ↔ UC.

  - Dates align with sanction validity and reporting cadence.

  - Expenditure heads do not exceed sanctioned limits; procurement norms followed.

  - Unspent shown as refunded/carry-forwarded with approval note.

- CAY mapping (for NBA tables)

  - Record actual receipts in CAYm1/CAYm2/CAYm3 (₹) and utilized (₹ Lakhs) for the same CAY as the UC period.

  - For multi-year projects, file separate annual UCs; for short projects, a single final UC is acceptable.

- How it impacts marks (built into your tracker)

  - **Received (3 marks):** based on Total Received (₹ Lakhs) over CAYm1—m3.

  - **Utilized (2 marks):** proportional to Total Utilized ÷ Total Sanctioned (→ 100% = full 2 marks).

  - Keep UCs timely and complete to evidence full utilization.

- Best practices

  - Use a standard UC template across departments; pre-fill project code & heads.

  - Maintain a UC calendar with reminders 30 days before FY end.

  - **Digitally sign PDFs; store with consistent names:** UC\_\<Year\>\_\<Dept\>\_\<PI\>\_\<ShortTitle\>.pdf.

  - Cross-reference UC number/date in your Institution_SeedMoney_Tracker.

- Common pitfalls (avoid)

  - Missing project code/budget head → weak audit trail.

  - Head misclassification (e.g., equipment claimed as consumables).

  - Late submission or period mismatch with receipts.

  - Outcomes not attached (publications/prototypes) when UC claims utilization toward those activities.

- Sample one-line certification (adapt)

> *“Certified that an amount of ₹ X has been utilized during \<period\> for \<project title\> under Sanction No. \<...\>, strictly as per terms & institutional financial rules, and the unspent balance of ₹ Y has been carried forward/refunded as applicable.”*

- How to log in your Excel (you already have the sheet)

  - **In Institution_SeedMoney_Tracker:** update Utilized Amount (₹ Lakhs) and CAY-wise receipts as per the UC period.

  - Add the UC file path in Remarks or create a "UC Link (PDF)" column to hyperlink the document.

  - **Dissertations:** title page, approval letter/notification, abstract, award date, supervisor (PI/Co-PI) mapping.

- Minimum metadata to record (map these to your tracker)

  - Outcome Type (Publication/Prototype/Dissertation).

  - Title / Short name (exact).

  - Authors/Contributors (PI/Co-PI/students; affiliation).

  - Date (acceptance/award/demo).

  - Identifiers (DOI, patent/app. no., thesis ID).

  - Link/Path to artifact (PDF/drive URL).

  - Acknowledgment line confirming seed-grant support.

  - Attribution to project code (matches sanction order & UC).

- How to log in your Excel (Institution_SeedMoney_Tracker)

  - Use Publications (No.), Prototypes (No.), Ph.D. Graduated (No.), M.E./M.Tech. Graduated (No.) columns for counts.

  - Use Outcomes (Summary) for a one-line description (e.g., "2 Scopus papers (DOI...), 1 prototype (TRL-4)").

  - **Optionally add columns:** Outcome Type, DOI/Patent/Thesis ID, Outcome Link (PDF/URL) for quick verification.

- Quality rules (NBA-friendly)

  - Title/PI/Dept must match sanction order and UC records.

  - Dates must lie within/near the project validity; explain lag if post-completion.

  - Acknowledgment statement must explicitly cite the institution seed grant no./year.

  - Prefer indexed/peer-reviewed venues; clearly tag preprints vs accepted vs published.

- Prototype documentation essentials

  - Problem & objectives, design overview, key specs, test plan & results, photos/screenshots.

  - User/industry evaluation notes (if any), readiness level (TRL), IP status, planned next steps.

- Dissertation documentation essentials

  - Program (Ph.D./M.E./M.Tech.), student name/lD, thesis title, award/approval date, supervisor (PI/Co-PI).

  - Copy of approval/notification; library handle/URL if available.

- Storage & naming (for fast retrieval during visit)

    - **Dissertations:** THESIS \<Year\>\_\<Student\>\_\<Degree\>\_\<TitIe\>.pdf

- Cross-checks before NBA

  - Counts in tracker = actual artifacts present (openable links).

  - Acknowledgment lines present and correct.

  - Utilized heads in UC align with outcomes (e.g., equipment purchase → prototype; page charges → publication).

  - No double counting with sponsored/consultancy sections.

  - **Per CAY:** total publications, prototypes, theses; utilization %; cost-to-outcome ratio.

  - **Conversion:** seed → external grant (₹), IP filings, start-up/industry engagement derived from the project.

  - Missing acknowledgment or wrong grant number.

  - Outcomes outside validity with no justification.

# 68. Disbursement Proofs (institutional accounts showing transfer to project).

- Purpose

  - Prove that the approved seed grant was actually transferred from the institution’s accounts to the project/PI cost centre.

  - Forms the bridge evidence between Sanction Order (intent) and UC (utilization), supporting "Received (3 marks)" and enabling "Utilized (2 marks)" checks.

- Accepted forms of disbursement proof

  - JV/Journal Voucher or Funds Transfer Note (internal posting to the project code).

  - Bank statement / passbook extract of the project account (credit entries highlighted).

  - ERP/Finance system ledger screenshot (GL → Project Code) showing date, amount, voucher no..

  - Cheque/EFT advice (NEFT/RTGS) or payment advice email from Finance.

  - Receipt voucher acknowledged by PI/Department (if your policy uses receipts for internal transfers).

- Mandatory details each proof must show

  - **Project identifiers:** Project Code/Cost Centre, PI name, Department, Project title.

  - **Financials:** Amount, currency (₹), date of credit, voucher/transaction ID, sanction reference.

  - **Source & destination accounts:** Institute fund head → Project/department research head.

  - Narration/reference text linking to Sanction Order No. and CAY period.

- How to map in your Excel tracker (Institution_SeedMoney_Tracker)

  - Record transfers in CAYm1/CAYm2/CAYm3 Amount Received (₹) columns (per academic year).

  - Keep Sanctioned (₹ Lakhs) and Utilized (₹ Lakhs) fields aligned with the sum of receipts and the UC.

  - Use Remarks (or add a column "Disbursement Proof Link") to hyperlink the JV/ledger PDF.

- Reconciliation workflow (simple 5-step)

  - 1\) Sanction Order → confirms who/what/how much/when.

  - 2\) Disbursement Proof → confirms money moved to the project code (date/amount).

  - 3\) Bank/ERP ledger → shows credit entry (and later, debits for spending).

  - 4\) UC+ SOE → shows head-wise utilization within validity.

  - 5\) Tracker → totals per CAY; dashboard computes marks.

- CAY tagging rules (for proofs)

  - Tag a receipt to the CAY in which it was credited to the project (academic-year basis).

  - **Multi-tranche grants:** split entries across CAYS as they are received.

- Best practices

  - **Maintain a single PDF packet per tranche:** JV/Transfer note + ledger snippet + bank credit line; annotate with highlighter.

  - Ensure narration includes Sanction No. / Project Code for easy traceability.

  - Use consistent filenames and foldering for quick retrieval during the visit (see naming below).

  - Cross-check sum(receipts) = Total Received (tracker); investigate any variance immediately.

- Common pitfalls (avoid)

  - Receipts posted to a generic departmental head instead of the specific project code.

  - Missing transaction IDs/dates, making audit trails weak.

  - Back-dating receipts across CAY boundaries (misstates marks year).

  - Discrepancy between Sanctioned and Received with no approval note for staggered release.

- Suggested repository structure & naming

  - **Folders:** Evidence/SeedMoney/Disbursements/\<CAY\>/\<PI\>\_\<Dept\>/

  - **Files:** DISB\_\<CAY\>\_\<PI\>\_\<ProjectCode\>\_\<YYYYMMDD\>\_JV.pdf, LEDGER \<CAY\>\_\<ProjectCode\>.pdf, BANK \<CAY\>\_\<ProjectCode\>.pdf

- Audit-ready checklist

  - JV/Transfer note references the Sanction Order and Project Code.

  - Amount and date match bank/ledger entry.

  - CAY column in tracker reflects the actual credit date.

  - UC/SoE totals do not exceed Total Received.

  - All proofs are hyperlinked from the tracker and openable on-site.

- Optional enhancements (l can add on request)

  - A "Disbursement \_ Tracker" sheet linked to Institution_SeedMoney_Tracker (auto-sums receipts per CAY, flags mismatches).

  - Conditional formatting to flag rows where Total Received \< Sanctioned or Utilized \> Received.

  - Pivot views (PI/Dept/CAY) of receipts with drill-down to proof links

# 69. Receipts / Invoices for expenditure.

- Purpose

  - Evidence that seed-money/internal grant was actually spent on the sanctioned project and under permitted heads.

  - Supports UC (utilization) and strengthens scoring for "Amount Utilized (2 marks)" under NBA IRG (5 marks).

- Accepted documents (any of the following, ideally all)

  - Tax invoices from vendors (GST-compliant where applicable).

  - Cash memos/receipts for small purchases (with vendor stamp/sign).

  - Proforma → Final invoice pair (if advance release was used).

  - Travel bills (tickets, boarding passes) + approved TA/DA claim.

  - Honoraria/Student assistantship vouchers with acknowledgement.

  - AMC/Software license invoices with license keys/contract number.

- Mandatory invoice fields (audit-ready)

  - **Vendor details:** legal name, address, GSTIN/PAN, bank details.

  - **Buyer details:** institute name, address, Project Code/Budget Head, PI/Dept.

  - **Document info:** invoice no., date, due date, unique serial, QR (if e-invoice).

  - **Line items:** description/specs, quantity, unit price, tax breakup (CGST/SGST/IGST), totals.

  - **Narration/reference:** Sanction Order No., PI name, Project title.

  - **Payment terms & mode:** NEFT/cheque reference or receipt acknowledgment.

- Head mapping (must match sanction & UC)

  - Equipment/Software (asset register entry if capital).

  - Consumables/Chemicals (issue register).

  - Manpower/Student stipend (attendance/acknowledgement).

  - Travel/Field work (approved tour note + tickets/bills).

  - Contingency/Services (justification note if required).

  - Ensure each invoice is tagged to one head; avoid split without approval note.

- CAY tagging (for NBA year-wise reporting)

  - Tag expenditure to the CAY in which the payment date (or booking date per finance policy) falls.

  - For multi-year projects, distribute expenses across CAYm1/m2/m3 accordingly.

- Reconciliation workflow (simple & strict)

  - Sanction Order → Disbursement Proof → Ledger Posting → Invoice/Receipt → UC/SoE.

  - **Verify:** sum(invoices by head) ≤ Total Received and matches UC/SoE totals.

  - Cross-check dates, project code, vendor, amount, tax across all documents.

- Controls & approvals

  - Prior quotation/GeM/Rate contract evidence (as per procurement rules).

  - Three-quote file (if required threshold crossed).

  - Goods Received Note (GRN)/Store entry for equipment/consumables.

  - Installation/Acceptance report for equipment/software/services.

- How to log in your existing Excel tracker

  - **Institution_SeedMoney_Tracker:** keep Utilized Amount (₹ Lakhs) aligned with head-wise totals from invoices.

  - **Add (if desired) columns:** Invoice No., Invoice Date, Vendor, Head, Amount (₹), Link (PDF).

  - Use Outcomes (Summary) to reference invoices tied to publications/prototypes (e.g., page charges, components).

- File organization & naming (fast retrieval)

  - **Folder:** Evidence/SeedMoney/lnvoices/\<CAY\>/\<Head\>/

  - **Names:** INV\_\<Vendor\>\_\<YYYYMMDD\> \<Amount\>\_\<ProjectCode\>.pdf

  - Bundle small bills into a monthly PDF pack with an index sheet.

- Quality checks (before the NBA visit)

  - Invoice to the institute (not personal), with project code and PI/Dept mentioned.

  - **Tax compliance:** valid GSTIN, correct tax rate, e-invoice IRN/QR (if applicable).

  - Bank proof (UTR/cheque no.) matches invoice amount/date.

  - No duplicate claims; mark invoices as Posted/Claimed once entered in UC.

- Common pitfalls (avoid)

  - Missing project code on invoices → weak traceability.

  - Posting expenses to wrong financial head.

  - Back-dated invoices across CAY boundary without justification.

  - Unstamped cash memos or handwritten slips without vendor details.

- Optional (l can add this to your workbook on request)

  - **Expense_Receipts_Tracker sheet with columns:** PI, Dept, Project Code, Head, Invoice No., Date, Vendor, Amount (R), CAY, Payment Ref, Link (PDF) + auto-sum by head & CAY and mismatch flags vs. UC/summary.
