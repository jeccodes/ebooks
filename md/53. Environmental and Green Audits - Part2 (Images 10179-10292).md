**Abstract**

Environmental and Green Audits in Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) have emerged as essential tools for promoting sustainability, ensuring regulatory compliance, and fostering responsible campus management. As academic institutions expand in size, infrastructure, and population, their impact on natural resources and the environment has increased significantly. This necessitates systematic evaluation mechanisms such as environmental and green audits to assess institutional performance in areas like energy, water, waste, sanitation, air quality, biodiversity, and carbon footprint. The objective of such audits is not only to ensure compliance with national and international environmental standards but also to embed sustainable practices within the academic and administrative culture of HEIs.

Green audits serve as a structured framework for identifying strengths, gaps, and opportunities in campus operations while offering practical recommendations for improvement. They align with accreditation and ranking frameworks such as NAAC, NBA, NIRF, QS I-GAUGE, and ISO 14001, and contribute to the attainment of UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly those related to clean energy, water management, sustainable cities, climate action, and responsible consumption. By integrating green audit practices, HEIs can establish clean, green, and energy-efficient campuses that not only reduce ecological footprints but also provide healthier environments for students, faculty, and staff.

The process of environmental and green auditing involves multiple stages: planning, pre-audit activities, on-site inspections, data collection, post-audit reporting, and follow-up. Each stage ensures systematic assessment of resource utilization, waste management practices, environmental policies, and compliance with statutory requirements. The outcomes of these audits include improved efficiency in resource management, cost savings through energy and waste reduction, enhanced environmental awareness, and strengthened institutional reputation as socially responsible entities. Importantly, they create a culture of sustainability among students and faculty by integrating green practices into daily operations, academic curricula, and community outreach initiatives.

This work emphasizes the dual role of environmental and green audits—as mechanisms of compliance and as instruments of transformation. On the one hand, they help institutions meet legal and accreditation requirements; on the other, they serve as catalysts for innovation in sustainability, enabling institutions to pioneer eco-friendly technologies, promote renewable energy adoption, and foster community engagement. By systematically embedding audit practices within institutional governance, HEIs can evolve into models of environmental responsibility, thereby contributing significantly to national and global sustainability agendas.

In conclusion, environmental and green audits are indispensable for HEIs seeking to balance academic excellence with environmental stewardship. They provide measurable pathways for continuous improvement, enabling higher education institutions to lead in creating sustainable campuses and resilient societies.

# **21. Energy Audits**

## **1. Meaning of Energy Audit**

- An Energy Audit is a systematic process of evaluating how a facility consumes energy and identifying opportunities to improve efficiency.

- It is a critical component of an energy management program, aimed at reducing energy costs, conserving resources, and lowering the carbon footprint.

## **2. Purpose of Energy Audits**

- To understand how energy is being used in buildings, equipment, and operations.

- To detect wastage and inefficiencies in electricity, heating, cooling, and appliances.

- To recommend cost-effective measures for saving energy and improving performance.

- To help institutions/organizations move toward sustainable and green operations.

## **3. Scope of Energy Audits**

- **Electricity Use:** Lighting systems, computers, photocopiers, printers, water coolers, and air-conditioning.

- **Heating Systems:** Type of fuel used (gas, oil, electricity) and efficiency of boilers/heaters.

- **Cooling & Ventilation:** Thermostat settings, draught prevention, insulation quality.

- **Lighting Efficiency:** Use of LED vs. incandescent or large fluorescent lamps, cleanliness of fittings, automatic switches.

- **Appliance Efficiency:** Energy-saving features in computers, monitors, photocopiers, and lab equipment.

- **Behavioral Practices:** Whether lights, computers, and appliances are switched off when not in use.

## **4. Process of Energy Audit**

1.  **Planning & Data Collection**

- Collect past utility bills and energy consumption records.

- Understand operating schedules of major energy-using equipment.

2.  **On-Site Inspection**

- Inspect heating, cooling, lighting, and electrical appliances.

- Check for leaks, poor insulation, draughts, or outdated devices.

3.  **Analysis**

- Analyze collected data to identify areas of wastage and inefficiency.

- Benchmark consumption against standards or similar institutions.

4.  **Recommendations**

- Suggest Energy Conservation Measures (ECMs) such as LED lighting, thermostat adjustments, insulation, and energy-efficient appliances.

5.  **Energy Action Plan**

- Create a structured plan prioritizing ECMs that provide the best cost-benefit ratio.

- Assign responsibilities and budget for implementation.

6.  **Implementation & Monitoring**

- Execute ECMs, monitor results, and adjust practices for continuous savings.

## **5. Benefits of Energy Audits**

- **Cost Savings:** Reduced energy bills through efficiency improvements.

- **Sustainability:** Lower greenhouse gas emissions and improved eco-footprint.

- **Improved Efficiency:** Optimized heating, cooling, lighting, and appliance use.

- **Awareness:** Encourages staff and students to adopt energy-saving practices.

- **Accreditation Support:** Provides evidence for NAAC, NBA, ISO, and sustainability rankings.

# **22. Sanitation and Hygiene Policy in the context of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs)**

## **1. Meaning of Sanitation and Hygiene Policy**

- A Sanitation and Hygiene Policy establishes institutional standards and practices for maintaining cleanliness, hygiene, and safe facilities across the campus.

- It ensures health, dignity, and comfort for students, staff, and visitors while aligning with national public health and accreditation standards.

## **2. Key Components of the Policy**

**a) Physical Appearance & Ambience**

- Maintain clean, odor-free classrooms, hostels, and public spaces.

- Promote greenery, ventilation, and a pleasant environment.

**b) Toilets & Facilities**

- Ensure adequate student/employee-toilet ratio as per guidelines.

- Provide gender-balanced facilities (male/female) and disabled-friendly toilets with proper accessibility.

- Ensure plumbing systems are functional with sufficient water taps and sanitation supplies.

**c) Drinking Water Facilities**

- Provide clean and adequate drinking water stations with regular quality checks.

- Ensure placement of RO systems, water purifiers, and coolers in strategic locations.

**d) Kitchen & Food Hygiene**

- Mandate proper apparel (aprons, gloves, head covers) for kitchen staff.

- Maintain hygiene standards in canteens and hostels, including fly-proof storage and food preparation areas.

- Regular cleaning and sanitization of cutlery, crockery, and utensils.

- Dining halls must be kept odor-free, ventilated, and hygienic.

**e) Cleaning Systems & Resources**

- Provide adequate cleaning equipment, disinfectants, and consumables.

- Ensure systematic cleaning schedules for classrooms, labs, hostels, and canteens.

- Employ trained housekeeping staff with safety and hygiene protocols.

## **3. Objectives of Sanitation and Hygiene Policy**

- To create a healthy and safe learning environment.

- To prevent diseases and infections by maintaining hygiene standards.

- To ensure gender equality and inclusivity through adequate and accessible sanitation facilities.

- To comply with national health and safety regulations.

- To support accreditation and quality frameworks (NAAC, NBA, ISO, NIRF).

## **4. Benefits of a Strong Sanitation and Hygiene Policy**

- Promotes student and staff well-being.

- Reduces absenteeism caused by illness.

- Builds institutional reputation for cleanliness and health standards.

- Supports SDG-6 (Clean Water and Sanitation) and SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-being).

- Provides evidence for accreditation, audits, and rankings.

# **23. Waste Management Policy in HEIs**

## **1. Meaning of Waste Management Policy**

- A Waste Management Policy provides a structured framework for how an institution manages, reduces, and disposes of waste responsibly.

- It ensures compliance with waste management rules, supports sustainability goals, and creates a culture of reduce–reuse–recycle within the campus.

## **2. Key Components of the Policy**

**a) Plastic-Free Campus**

- Enforce a strict “No Plastic Zone” policy, banning single-use plastics (bottles, bags, cutlery).

- Promote reusable alternatives (steel, glass, paper, cloth bags).

**b) Waste Segregation and Storage**

- Introduce two-bin or three-bin system (dry, wet, and hazardous waste).

- Ensure adequate bins across classrooms, hostels, canteens, and public areas.

- Provide storage areas for recyclable and hazardous waste until proper disposal.

**c) Solid Waste Disposal, Reuse, and Recycling**

- Establish links with authorized recycling vendors for paper, plastic, and e-waste.

- Encourage reuse of study materials, furniture, and equipment within campus.

**d) Organic Waste Management**

- Promote vermicomposting units for garden and canteen waste.

- Establish cow dung and organic manure units in agricultural or rural campuses.

- Use compost in campus gardens and landscaping.

**e) Biogas Plants**

- Install biogas plants to convert food waste into renewable energy for kitchens/hostels.

- Regularly monitor functioning and efficiency.

**f) Incinerators**

- Install incinerators for biomedical and sanitary waste where appropriate.

- Ensure compliance with pollution control norms.

**g) Food Waste Management**

- Monitor and record food waste in hostels and canteens.

- Redirect excess edible food to NGOs/food banks (where possible).

- Compost or process in biogas units to avoid landfill dumping.

- To minimize waste generation and promote recycling.

- To ensure safe and legal disposal of all waste types (solid, liquid, e-waste, biomedical).

- To move towards a zero-waste campus.

- To encourage student and staff participation in eco-friendly practices.

- To support NAAC, NBA, ISO, NIRF, and SDG-12 (Responsible Consumption & Production) compliance.

- Reduces waste sent to landfills and lowers pollution.

# **24. Air Quality Audit in HEIs**

## **1. Meaning of Air Quality Audit**

- An Air Quality Audit evaluates the quality of ambient air within and around the campus to ensure it meets prescribed safety standards.

- It monitors both natural pollutants (dust, pollen, wind-blown particles) and man-made pollutants (vehicular emissions, construction activities).

- It is an essential part of ensuring a healthy learning and working environment in academic institutions.

## **2. Importance of Air Quality Audit in HEIs**

- Protects the health of students, faculty, and staff from respiratory and related health issues.

- Ensures compliance with National Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Program (NAAQMP) and Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) standards.

- Helps institutions maintain their designation as a “silent zone”, with both air and noise levels under permissible limits.

- Supports accreditation and sustainability goals (NAAC, NIRF, NBA, ISO, SDGs).

## **3. Scope of Air Quality Audit**

- **Air Pollution Sources on Campus:**

<!-- -->

- Natural dust and pollen.

- Vehicular emissions (buses, staff vehicles, delivery vans).

- Construction activities (temporary rise in SPM and RSPM).

<!-- -->

- **Air Quality Parameters Monitored (as per CPCB/NAAQMP):**

<!-- -->

- Sulphur Dioxide (SO₂)

- Oxides of Nitrogen (NOx)

- Suspended Particulate Matter (SPM)

- Respirable Suspended Particulate Matter (RSPM/PM10, PM2.5)

<!-- -->

- **Noise Monitoring:**

<!-- -->

- Silent zones (academic institutions) must remain below 45 dB during daytime.

- Monitored especially during construction or high-traffic periods.

## **4. Methodology of Air Quality Audit**

7.  **Planning –** Identify sampling points across the campus (near hostels, canteens, classrooms, parking).

11. **Reporting –** Prepare air quality and noise audit reports, highlighting compliance status and areas for improvement.

## **5. Benefits of Air Quality Audit**

- Strengthens institutional reputation as a safe and sustainable campus.

- Provides scientific evidence for accreditations and green certifications.

# **25. Ambient Noise Monitoring in HEIs**

## **1. Meaning of Ambient Noise Monitoring**

- Ambient noise monitoring assesses sound levels in and around the campus to ensure they remain within prescribed limits for silent zones (≤45 dB during the day for academic institutions, as per CPCB norms).

- It identifies noise pollution sources and provides corrective measures for maintaining a peaceful academic environment.

## **2. Sources of Noise in HEIs**

- **Automobile noise:** From staff/student vehicles, buses, and visitors at campus entry points.

- **Human activity:** Loud conversations, crowded areas (canteens, hostels, workshops).

- **Construction and excavation:** Temporary spikes during building activities.

- **Transportation inside campus:** Use of motorbikes, autos, and service vehicles.

## **3. Monitoring Methodology**

- **Monitoring Locations:** Main gate, academic departments, workshops, canteens, and administrative building.

- **Equipment Used:** Calibrated Sound Level Meter (HTC SL-1352).

- **Measurement Scale:** Logarithmic scale in decibels (dB).

- **Procedure:**

<!-- -->

- Noise readings taken at different times of day (morning, afternoon, evening).

- Averaged and compared against CPCB silent zone standards.

- Sources of excessive noise identified (e.g., rush-hour traffic at main gate, construction near departments).

## **4. Standards for Compliance (CPCB Norms)**

- **Silent Zone (Academic institutions, hospitals):**

<!-- -->

- Daytime (6 AM – 10 PM): ≤ 50 dB (some CPCB guidelines state ≤45 dB).

- Nighttime (10 PM – 6 AM): ≤ 40 dB.

## **5. Benefits of Ambient Noise Monitoring**

- Ensures compliance with CPCB regulations for silent zones.

- Provides a healthy, peaceful environment for teaching, learning, and research.

- Identifies noise hotspots for targeted intervention.

- Helps in planning infrastructure (e.g., noise barriers, green belts, vehicle-free zones).

- Strengthens institutional accreditation and environmental responsibility credentials.

# **27. Plastic-Free Campaign in HEIs**

## **1. Meaning of Plastic-Free Campaign in HEIs**

- A Plastic-Free Campaign is an institutional initiative to completely eliminate single-use, non-biodegradable plastics from the campus.

- It addresses a global environmental concern since plastic waste pollutes soil, water, and air, while also posing risks to human health and biodiversity.

- Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) are uniquely positioned to educate, influence, and model eco-friendly practices, making their campuses leaders in sustainable development.

## **2. Need for the Campaign**

- **Environmental Concern:** Plastic waste takes hundreds of years to degrade, leading to soil infertility, water contamination, and microplastic pollution.

- **Health Risks:** Exposure to plastics and their chemical byproducts can cause respiratory and long-term health issues.

- **Institutional Responsibility:** HEIs, with large student and staff populations, generate significant plastic waste but also have the capacity to set examples of sustainable living.

- **UGC Guidelines:** University Grants Commission (UGC) mandates HEIs to adopt sustainability policies, including plastic-free initiatives.

## **3. Key Guidelines for Implementation**

- **Ban on Single-Use Plastics:** Bottles, wrappers, polystyrene cups, bags, plates, and packaging are strictly prohibited in canteens, academic blocks, labs, hostels, and administrative offices.

- **Mandatory Compliance:** Students, staff, and faculty are instructed not to bring non-biodegradable plastics to campus.

- **Promotion of Alternatives:** Encourage cloth bags, jute bags, reusable cutlery, and steel/glass bottles.

- **Awareness Campaigns:** Posters, workshops, eco-club activities, and annual “Plastic-Free Day” to instill behavioral change.

- **Monitoring and Reporting:** Include plastic-use checks in Green Audits and appoint a committee for enforcement.

## **4. Objectives**

- To completely eliminate single-use plastics from the campus.

- To create awareness among students and staff about the dangers of plastic pollution.

- To encourage eco-friendly alternatives and responsible consumption.

- To comply with sustainability regulations (UGC, NAAC, NIRF, NBA, ISO).

- To set an example of leadership in sustainability for society at large.

## **5. Expected Outcomes**

- **Environmental Benefits:** Reduced landfill waste, cleaner soil and water, and lower carbon emissions.

- **Behavioral Change:** Students and staff adopt sustainable habits such as carrying reusable bottles and bags.

- **Cost Savings:** Reduced spending on disposable plastics and increased savings from reusable alternatives.

- **Reputation & Accreditation:** Enhances the institution's image as a green campus, providing strong evidence for quality rankings.

- **Community Influence:** Practices can extend to local communities, amplifying the positive environmental impact.

# **31. Clean and Green Campus Initiatives in HEIs**

## **1. Meaning and Purpose**

- A Clean and Green Campus is a higher education institution that integrates environmental management, resource conservation, and sustainable practices into its day-to-day operations.

- The purpose is to reduce ecological footprint, promote health and hygiene, conserve biodiversity, and instill environmental values among students, staff, and faculty.

- It aligns with UGC, NAAC, NBA, NIRF, and ISO 14001 sustainability mandates, while contributing directly to UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

## **2. Key Components of Clean and Green Campus**

13. **Cleanliness and Hygiene**

- Regular cleanliness drives in classrooms, hostels, and public areas.

- Adequate sanitation facilities, clean drinking water systems, and pest-free kitchens/canteens.

- Strict waste segregation (dry, wet, hazardous) at source.

14. **Green Infrastructure**

- Tree plantation and maintenance of landscaped gardens.

- Development of herbal gardens, biodiversity parks, and green belts.

- Construction of eco-friendly, energy-efficient buildings (LEED/GRIHA certified).

15. **Resource Management**

- Rainwater harvesting and water recycling units.

- Energy conservation through LED lights, smart meters, and renewable energy (solar, wind, biogas).

- Efficient waste management: composting, recycling, and safe e-waste disposal.

16. **Sustainable Mobility**

- “No Vehicle Day” and promotion of bicycles and EVs.

- Carpooling among staff to reduce fossil fuel use.

- Pedestrian-friendly pathways inside campus.

17. **Awareness and Education**

- Student eco-clubs and green committees.

- Integration of sustainability topics into curriculum.

> Celebration of environmental events (*World Environment Day, Energy Conservation Week*).

18. **Digital and Smart Practices**

- Promotion of paperless administration through e-submissions and digital platforms.

- IoT-based monitoring of energy, water, and waste.

- Adoption of “green computing” in labs and offices.

## **3. Benefits of Clean and Green Campus**

- **Environmental Benefits:** Reduced carbon footprint, cleaner air and water, improved biodiversity.

- **Health Benefits:** Better sanitation, safe drinking water, and reduced pollution lead to improved well-being.

- **Educational Benefits:** Students gain practical exposure to sustainability, linking theory with practice.

- **Economic Benefits:** Savings on energy, water, and waste disposal costs.

- **Reputation Benefits:** Enhances institutional image, supports NAAC/NIRF/NBA rankings, and attracts collaborations.

## **4. Expected Outcomes**

- Establishment of a model eco-campus with evidence-based practices.

- Development of responsible students and staff with strong environmental ethics.

- Strengthened community engagement through outreach and extension activities.

- Contribution to national and global sustainability frameworks (e.g., SDGs, Paris Climate Agreement).
