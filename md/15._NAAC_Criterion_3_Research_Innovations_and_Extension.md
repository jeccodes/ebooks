**Abstract**

This document presents a comprehensive framework for Research, Innovation, and Extension within Higher Education Institutions (HEIs), highlighting their pivotal role in advancing knowledge, fostering innovation, and serving society. HEIs are increasingly recognized as catalysts for economic growth, social equity, and sustainable development, extending their functions beyond teaching to include cutting-edge research, entrepreneurial innovation, and structured outreach.

The framework is built upon three interdependent pillars. Research is positioned as the systematic pursuit of new knowledge, facilitated through Research and Development cells, funding schemes, advanced infrastructure, and collaborative networks. Its outcomes are reflected in peer-reviewed publications, patents, citations, and externally funded projects. Innovation is cultivated through incubation centres, entrepreneurship cells, hackathons, and industry partnerships that translate ideas into cost-effective technologies, startups, and job creation. Extension activities connect institutions with communities through programmes such as NSS, NCC, health camps, and village adoption projects, fostering civic responsibility among students while addressing pressing social challenges.

Quality assurance is ensured through mechanisms like the Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC), research ethics committees, plagiarism checks, and performance indicators such as citation counts, h-index, startups incubated, and community impact reports. Incentives for faculty and students, interdisciplinary collaboration, and alignment with national and global benchmarks further strengthen this ecosystem.

In essence, the framework demonstrates how HEIs can integrate academic excellence, practical innovation, and social responsibility into a synergistic model that prepares graduates to be globally competitive yet socially conscious citizens, capable of driving both knowledge advancement and societal transformation.

**1. Introduction**

This introduction provides an overview of the comprehensive framework for research, innovation, and extension activities within Higher Education Institutions (HEIs). HEIs are no longer confined to being centres of teaching alone; they are increasingly positioned as engines of knowledge creation, innovation, and societal transformation. This transition reflects global expectations from universities and colleges to not only impart education but also advance frontiers of knowledge, solve pressing real-world problems, and contribute directly to community development and nation-building. The framework for research, innovation, and extension thus serves as a strategic plan that ensures academic excellence, sustainable development, and social responsibility are seamlessly integrated into the functioning of institutions. It provides HEIs with a roadmap to cultivate a vibrant research culture, foster innovation ecosystems, and engage in meaningful extension activities, thereby reinforcing their role as active contributors to economic growth and social change.

The first pillar of this framework is Research, which is defined as a systematic investigation undertaken to generate new knowledge and insights. In HEIs, research is considered central to academic advancement because it stimulates inquiry-based learning, critical thinking, and creativity among faculty and students. The goals of institutional research extend beyond publications to include generating solutions for societal and industrial needs, thereby making research not only an academic exercise but also a driver of social and economic progress. To support this, institutions establish Research and Development (R&D) cells, specialized research centres, and faculty research committees. These bodies create policies, mobilize funding, and oversee the ethical conduct of research. Seed money schemes, internal grants, and access to advanced laboratories, libraries, and digital resources ensure that both faculty and students have the necessary support for their research pursuits. Outcomes are tracked rigorously through peer-reviewed publications, patents, citations, externally funded projects, and collaborative initiatives with industry and government bodies. International collaborations and participation in global research networks further strengthen the visibility and impact of HEI research. By integrating research into curriculum delivery, institutions also ensure that students experience learning through exploration and discovery, which prepares them for lifelong inquiry and problem-solving.

The second pillar, Innovation, emphasizes the creation and application of novel ideas, processes, and technologies that have a direct impact on society and industry. Innovation goes beyond traditional research outputs by ensuring that knowledge is translated into tangible products, services, and processes that improve lives and generate economic opportunities. HEIs nurture innovation through a supportive ecosystem comprising incubation centres, innovation hubs, entrepreneurship cells, and technology transfer offices. These platforms provide infrastructure, mentoring, and access to funding, helping students and faculty transform their ideas into viable solutions. Regular events such as hackathons, ideation workshops, startup competitions, and innovation festivals encourage creativity and collaboration across disciplines. Industry partnerships play a critical role by providing real-world problems for students to solve, as well as opportunities for internships, joint projects, and commercialization of innovations. The impact of innovation is measured not only through patents and intellectual property rights (IPRs) but also through the development of cost-effective technologies, creation of startups, employment generation, and technology transfer agreements. This ensures that HEIs contribute directly to building knowledge economies and strengthening national innovation systems.

The third pillar, Extension, represents the bridge between academia and society. Extension activities are designed to translate knowledge into social action by connecting institutions with the communities they serve. Through structured outreach programmes like the National Service Scheme (NSS), National Cadet Corps (NCC), Unnat Bharat Abhiyan, health awareness drives, literacy campaigns, and village adoption projects, HEIs engage in addressing pressing social issues such as poverty, health, sanitation, gender equity, and environmental sustainability. These activities instil a strong sense of social responsibility, empathy, and civic engagement in students, preparing them to become not only skilled professionals but also responsible citizens. At the same time, communities benefit from the expertise, resources, and enthusiasm of HEIs. For example, students and faculty conducting health camps improve local healthcare awareness, while technology demonstrations in rural areas help farmers and artisans adopt modern tools and practices. By embedding extension into the curriculum, institutions ensure that learning is experiential and socially contextualized, bridging the gap between academic knowledge and real-world impact.

To ensure quality, credibility, and integrity, the framework for research, innovation, and extension is supported by robust institutional mechanisms. The Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC) plays a central role in monitoring and guiding research and extension activities by establishing benchmarks, conducting periodic reviews, and facilitating continuous improvement. Research Ethics Committees and plagiarism detection systems ensure adherence to ethical standards and academic honesty. Funding utilization is closely monitored through audits and accountability mechanisms, while innovation activities are documented through incubation records, IPR filings, and commercialization reports. Extension activities are assessed through feedback collected from beneficiaries, government agencies, and community stakeholders. By relying on such rigorous quality assurance processes, HEIs maintain transparency, ensure relevance, and build credibility in their research and community engagement efforts.

Performance is monitored through specific measurable indicators that provide a clear picture of institutional achievements. In research, indicators include the h-index, citation counts, impact factors, number of publications in Scopus/UGC-CARE indexed journals, patents filed, and research grants received. For innovation, success is measured through the number of startups incubated, patents commercialized, student entrepreneurs supported, and industry collaborations established. For extension, outcomes are tracked using community feedback surveys, the number of villages adopted, people impacted through outreach, and partnerships with NGOs or local governments. These indicators are also aligned with external benchmarks such as NIRF rankings, NAAC accreditation requirements, and global ranking frameworks like QS and THE, ensuring that institutional efforts are globally relevant while remaining locally impactful.

HEIs actively promote a strong research and innovation culture by creating enabling environments that reward and recognize contributions. Incentives such as research awards, faculty appraisal points for publications, financial support for conferences, sabbatical leave, and fellowships encourage faculty and students to engage in research and innovation. Infrastructure such as digital libraries, high-speed internet, access to international journals, maker spaces, and advanced laboratories enhances the research ecosystem. Interdisciplinary collaboration is strongly encouraged, as today's complex problems—climate change, public health crises, energy sustainability—cannot be solved by single disciplines alone. Through collaborations across faculties, institutions, and even countries, HEIs amplify their capacity to innovate and extend solutions that are holistic and sustainable.

In conclusion, the comprehensive framework for research, innovation, and extension provides HEIs with a multi-dimensional strategy that aligns academic excellence with social relevance and global competitiveness. Research drives the creation of new knowledge, innovation ensures its practical application, and extension translates it into social action. Together, these three pillars create a synergistic ecosystem where HEIs function not only as centres of higher learning but also as catalysts for economic growth, social equity, and sustainable development. Supported by strong quality assurance systems and performance monitoring, this framework ensures that institutions contribute meaningfully to both national priorities and global challenges. By nurturing research aptitude, fostering innovation, and embedding social responsibility, HEIs prepare graduates who are not only employable and entrepreneurial but also empathetic and socially conscious citizens. This integrated approach underscores the transformative role of HEIs in shaping the future of knowledge societies.

> o Encourages faculty to leverage seed funding for larger external proposals.

> o Clear guidelines for project approvals, fund utilization, IP ownership, and revenue sharing.

> o Incentives for faculty securing external funding (monetary awards, recognition).

>
> o Utilization of CSR funds for socially relevant R&D.

> o Endowment funds for specific research themes or innovation centers.

• Capacity Building Workshops:

> o Training faculty on writing winning proposals and budgeting.

• Research Promotion Policies:

> o Incentivize researchers for patent filings, product development, and commercialization.

• Dedicated Office for Sponsored Projects (OSP):

> o Manages grants, reporting, and compliance for funded projects.

• Utilization Certificates and Auditing:

> o Annual financial reporting and utilization certification by Finance Officer.

• Research Progress Monitoring:

> o Periodic progress reviews, impact assessments, and outcome dissemination.

• Transparent Fund Allocation:

> o Funds distributed based on merit, departmental needs, and institutional goals.

Outcomes of Effective Resource Mobilization

• Increased number of funded projects and fellowships.

• Misuse of funds or resources

• Misconduct will be investigated by a designated committee.

• Workshops on IPR, academic writing, and responsible research conduct are conducted annually.

• The policy is reviewed every 3 years by the Ethics and Research Council.

• Updates are communicated via the institutional website and academic circulars.

| 1         | 20 – \< 30 Lakhs                                       | 3             |

*(The maximum weightage for this metric is 3 points, irrespective of the score band.)*

*3. Calculation Formula*

Average per year (Lakhs) = Total Seed Money Provided in 5 Years (Lakhs) / 5

• Step 1: Collect data for last five academic years.

• Step 2: Add total amount disbursed as seed money to faculty.

• Step 3: Divide by 5 to get the average per year.

• Step 4: Map to the scoring table above.

Example:

If institution provided ₹200 Lakhs in 5 years →

200 / 5 = 40 Lakhs per year (Average)

Score = 3 (40–50 range).

Weightage: 20 Marks

*1. Purpose of the Metric*

• To measure the quality and impact of research through the *h-index*.

• h-index definition: A researcher/institution has an *h-index = h* if *h* of their papers have been cited at least *h* times.

• Reflects both:

> o Productivity → number of significant publications.
>
> o Impact → number of citations per publication.

*2. Scoring Pattern*

|-------------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| ≥ 35                                      | 20                    |
| 25 – 35                                   | 16                    |
| 15 – 25                                   | 12                    |
| 5 – 15                                    | 8                     |
| \< 5                                      | 0                     |

*3. How to Calculate h-Index*

1\. Extract all institutional publications (last 5 years) from Scopus / Web of Science.

2\. Rank papers in descending order of citations.

3\. Find the maximum value where the number of papers (N) ≥ number of citations (C).

> o Example: If 20 papers have at least 20 citations each → h-index = 20.

4\. Institutional h-index can be generated directly from Scopus / WoS dashboard.

*4. Evidence Required*

• Bibliometric report from Scopus / Web of Science showing h-index.

• Institutional affiliation profile from databases.

• Year-wise trend of publications and citations.

• Consolidated Excel sheet with Title, DOI, Citations, and Cumulative h-index.
