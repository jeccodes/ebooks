**Abstract**

The United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG-4) — Quality Education — emphasizes inclusive, equitable, and lifelong learning opportunities for all. In the context of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs), this goal serves as a catalyst for transforming academic structures, pedagogical models, and institutional governance into systems that not only deliver knowledge but also build responsible, sustainable, and globally competent citizens. India's National Education Policy (NEP-2020) represents a strategic alignment with SDG-4, redefining educational priorities across early childhood, school, and tertiary levels to ensure access, quality, equity, and employability.

HEIs play a pivotal role in operationalizing SDG-4 by fostering human capital development, promoting research and innovation, and implementing sustainability practices within campuses and curricula. Institutions are increasingly integrating SDG-oriented courses, interdisciplinary research clusters, and experiential learning frameworks that emphasize environmental stewardship, gender equality, and social inclusion. The University Grants Commission (UGC), AICTE, NAAC, NBA, and NIRF have embedded sustainability and quality metrics into accreditation and ranking frameworks, thereby institutionalizing accountability and continuous improvement.

This paper underscores how higher education contributes to achieving SDG-4 through policy coherence, institutional capacity-building, and stakeholder engagement. It explores initiatives such as Digital Universities, Academic Bank of Credits (ABC), Vocational and Skill Integration, and Gender Inclusion Funds, which align with NEP-2020's vision of universal and transformative education. Furthermore, it highlights the importance of faculty development, e-learning infrastructure, inclusive pedagogy, and international partnerships in ensuring sustainable progress toward 2030 targets.

Ultimately, achieving SDG-4 in HEIs is not confined to curriculum reforms or infrastructural expansion but extends to nurturing a culture of innovation, equity, and ethical leadership. By positioning universities as hubs for lifelong learning, research excellence, and community empowerment, India's higher education system is emerging as a driving force in realizing both national aspirations and the global 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development.

**30. SDG-4 Concerning National Education Policy**

**Context of NEP-2020**

- Announced in 2020, a year marked by global uncertainty, the policy symbolized resilience and forward-thinking.

- It was India's first comprehensive education reform in over three decades, designed to modernize learning systems.

**Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE)**

- Recognizes the critical importance of preschool years for brain development.

- Strengthens foundational literacy and numeracy, which are the building blocks of lifelong learning.

- Introduces structured yet flexible preschool education to bridge early learning gaps.

**School Curriculum and Pedagogy Reforms**

- Shifts from rote-based learning to experiential, inquiry-driven, and skill-oriented methods.

- Encourages problem-solving, creativity, and critical thinking instead of textbook memorization.

- Integrates arts, sports, and vocational subjects into mainstream education to promote holistic growth.

**Language and Multilingualism**

- Strong emphasis on learning in the mother tongue/regional language at the foundational stage.

- Supports multilingual proficiency to improve cognitive ability and preserve India's linguistic diversity.

- Aligns language education with national integration and global opportunities.

**Higher Education Flexibility**

- Multiple entry and exit points with credit transfers through an Academic Bank of Credits.

- Allows students to pause, change streams, and rejoin without losing progress.

- Encourages lifelong learning and adaptability in rapidly changing job markets.

**Demographic Dividend**

- By aligning education with skills and career readiness, NEP-2020 prepares India's large youth population for employment and innovation.

- Creates pathways for vocational education, research, entrepreneurship, and global competitiveness.

- Ensures that human capital becomes India's strongest resource in the coming decades.

**31. Target 4.2 (Equal access to quality pre-primary education)**

**NEP-2020 and Early Childhood Care & Education (ECCE)**

- NEP-2020 places children aged 3–6 years at the centre of reform.

- Recognizes this stage as the most critical for brain development, emotional growth, and social skills.

- ECCE becomes an integral part of the education system, no longer seen as optional childcare.

**Strengthening Anganwadi Centres**

- Anganwadis (village-level child development centres) are upgraded with better infrastructure, safe learning spaces, and play-based learning tools.

- Workers receive specialized training to deliver early literacy, numeracy, and activity-based teaching.

- Anganwadis are increasingly linked with nearby primary schools for a seamless transition to Grade 1.

**Gender Inclusion Fund (GIF)**

- A dedicated fund to bridge gender gaps in education at all levels.

- Ensures that girls from disadvantaged backgrounds (SC, ST, minorities, rural and remote areas) have equal access to pre-primary and school education.

- Provides financial support to states for targeted interventions, such as safe school transport, scholarships, and community awareness programmes.

**Support for Disability-Inclusive Education**

- Ensures that children with physical or learning disabilities are not excluded from pre-primary schooling.

- Introduces barrier-free school designs, ramps, and accessible classrooms.

- Promotes use of assistive technologies like hearing aids, Braille resources, and screen readers.

- Teacher training includes modules on inclusive education practices to cater to diverse learning needs.

**Alignment with SDG-4 Target 4.2**

- The target aims for universal access to quality early childhood development and pre-primary education by 2030.

- **NEP-2020 directly contributes by:**

<!-- -->

- Expanding ECCE coverage across rural and urban India.

- Integrating nutrition, health, and education through Anganwadis.

- Guaranteeing equity through gender and disability support measures.

**Expected Long-Term Outcomes**

- All children enter Grade 1 with school readiness skills.

- Lower drop-out and repetition rates in primary school.

- Enhanced learning outcomes through strong foundational skills.

- Progress toward an inclusive and equitable education system aligned with SDG-4.

**32. Target 4.3 (Equal Access to Affordable Technical and Vocational Education)**

**NEP-2020 and Vocational Education**

- One of the strongest recommendations of NEP-2020 is to integrate vocational education into the mainstream education system.

- From Grade 6 onwards, students are introduced to vocational subjects like coding, carpentry, gardening, robotics, and digital literacy.

- The aim is to reduce the traditional stigma attached to vocational training by making it aspirational and skill-oriented.

- Students will also have access to internships and practical experiences with local industry, businesses, and artisans.

**Technical Skills for the 21st Century**

- Recognizing the importance of digital and technical literacy, NEP emphasizes early exposure to skills like coding, AI, data analysis, and robotics.

- This aligns with the global demand for future-ready skills, preparing students not just for jobs but for innovation and entrepreneurship.

- The integration of these skills makes learners more employable and adaptive to technological changes.

**National Education Technology Forum (NETF)**

- The Ministry of Education has set up NETF to act as a platform for technology policy, knowledge-sharing, and EdTech integration.

- NETF encourages schools, colleges, and universities to use digital platforms for skill training, thereby making education more accessible and affordable.

- By facilitating open-source repositories, research-sharing, and technology-driven pedagogy, NETF plays a crucial role in scaling vocational education.

**Affordability and Accessibility**

- NEP-2020 envisions making vocational and technical education affordable to all learners, including those in rural and marginalized communities.

- Partnerships with Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs), polytechnics, higher education institutions, and private sector industries will ensure access to modern labs, digital tools, and internships.

- The policy also emphasizes scholarships, online/blended learning, and financial support schemes to ensure no student is left behind.

**Alignment with SDG-4, Target 4.3**

*Target 4.3 states: “By 2030, ensure equal access for all women and men to affordable and quality technical, vocational, and tertiary education, including university.”*

NEP-2020 contributes by:

- Bringing vocational education into schools (from Grade 6).

- Building a flexible credit system (Academic Bank of Credits) for lifelong learning.

- Establishing NETF for tech-driven knowledge-sharing.

- Ensuring industry partnerships for skill-based training.

**Long-Term Benefits**

- A generation of future-ready learners with technical, vocational, and digital skills.

- Stronger industry-academia collaboration that reduces the skill gap.

- India positioned as a global skills hub, supplying talent for domestic and international markets.

- Empowerment of youth through affordable, accessible, and inclusive education pathways that lead to better employment and entrepreneurship.

**33. Target 4.4 (Increase the number of people with relevant skills for employment and entrepreneurship)**

**Shift from 10+2 to 5+3+3+4 Education Structure**

- The traditional 10+2 schooling model has been replaced by a new 5+3+3+4 structure under NEP-2020.

- This ensures stronger foundational learning in the first five years (including preschool), followed by preparatory, middle, and secondary stages.

- The structure is more aligned with global education systems, promoting age-appropriate learning and skill development.

**Multiple Entry and Exit Points in Higher Education**

- Students are no longer bound to complete a degree in one continuous stretch.

- They can enter, exit, and re-enter higher education at different stages (certificate, diploma, degree) without losing credits.

- This flexibility allows learners to gain work experience, entrepreneurship exposure, or vocational training, and return to complete their education.

- Such a model mirrors developed countries' higher education systems, where lifelong learning and career breaks are normalized.

**Improved Signalling Ability of Students**

- “Signalling ability” means how well a qualification reflects the skills and competencies a student has acquired.

- By offering flexible, recognized credentials at each stage (certificate, diploma, bachelor's degree), employers get clearer indicators of student capability.

- This makes graduates more competitive in job markets and better positioned for entrepreneurship opportunities.

**Academic Bank of Credits (ABC)**

- A digital credit repository that stores all academic credits earned by students.

- Credits can be transferred across institutions and accumulated over time toward a final degree.

- For example, a student can earn credits from one university, transfer them digitally, and complete a degree in another institution.

- Encourages lifelong learning, interdisciplinary studies, and mobility within the education system.

**Alignment with SDG-4 Target 4.4**

- The target focuses on increasing the number of people with relevant skills for employment, decent jobs, and entrepreneurship.

NEP-2020 contributes by:

- Making higher education flexible and modular.

- Building job-relevant skills through vocational exposure in school and higher education.

- Ensuring learners can adapt to changing job markets with multiple learning pathways.

- Promoting an entrepreneurial mindset by valuing diverse skill sets and practical learning experiences.

**Long-Term Impact**

- A more skilled, adaptable, and employable workforce.

- Reduction in dropout rates since students can rejoin without losing academic progress.

- Growth in entrepreneurship and innovation due to flexible pathways and interdisciplinary learning.

- India moves closer to becoming a global knowledge and skills hub, directly contributing to SDG-4 and national development goals.

**34. Target 4.5 (Eliminate Discrimination in Education)**

**Affirmative Actions by MoE & UGC**

- The Ministry of Education (MoE) and the University Grants Commission (UGC) have introduced multiple equity and inclusion policies to ensure fair access to education.

- These include reservations, scholarships, special coaching programs, inclusive admission policies, and support systems for disadvantaged communities (Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, Other Backward Classes, minorities, women, and persons with disabilities).

- The goal is to remove systemic discrimination in access, participation, and success in education at all levels.

**NEP-2020 on Inclusion and Equity**

- NEP-2020 places equity and inclusion as guiding principles for educational reform.

- It emphasizes that no child is left behind due to gender, socio-economic background, disability, or geographic location.

- Special mechanisms are proposed for socially and economically disadvantaged groups (SEDGs) to access quality schooling and higher education.

**Bridging the Digital Divide**

- The NEP proposes the creation of a dedicated unit for digital learning and online education within the Ministry of Education.

- This unit will develop internet-based e-learning platforms, digital resources, and teacher training programs for online delivery.

- The aim is to close the digital access gap that became evident during the COVID-19 pandemic, when many students in rural and remote areas could not continue their education due to lack of devices or internet connectivity.

**Infrastructure and Capacity Building**

- NEP-2020 identifies infrastructural shortages such as lack of electricity, poor internet connectivity, and inadequate digital devices as key barriers to equity.

The policy seeks to strengthen digital infrastructure, particularly in rural and underserved areas, by investing in:

- Reliable electricity supply.

- High-speed internet connectivity for schools and colleges.

- Affordable digital devices for learners.

<!-- -->

- Capacity building for teachers in digital pedagogy ensures that technology is used effectively in classrooms.

**Alignment with SDG-4 Target 4.5**

*Target 4.5 states: “By 2030, eliminate gender disparities in education and ensure equal access to all levels of education and vocational training for the vulnerable, including persons with disabilities, indigenous peoples and children in vulnerable situations.”*

India's initiatives directly align by:

- Removing systemic inequalities through affirmative action.

- Expanding digital access to prevent exclusion.

- Strengthening infrastructure to ensure that rural and marginalized learners are not left behind.

- Creating inclusive, barrier-free schools and universities.

**Long-Term Outcomes**

- A truly inclusive education system that reflects social justice.

- Reduction of dropout rates among marginalized groups.

- Digital empowerment of students from all socio-economic backgrounds.

- India's progress towards universal and equitable access to quality education in line with SDG-4.

**35. Target 4.6 (Universal Literacy and Numeracy)**

**New India Literacy Programme (2022–2027)**

- A centrally sponsored scheme launched to cover all aspects of literacy and lifelong learning.

- Goes beyond just reading and writing – it includes functional literacy, digital literacy, financial literacy, vocational skills, and critical life skills.

- Aligned with NEP-2020, it ensures education for all sections of society, especially adults who missed formal schooling.

- Seeks to eradicate illiteracy by 2027, moving towards the national goal of an educated and skilled population.

**NIPUN Bharat (2021)**

- National Initiative for Proficiency in Reading with Understanding and Numeracy launched by the Ministry of Education in July 2021.

- Designed as a national mission on foundational literacy and numeracy (FLN).

- **Target:** By 2026–27, all children at the end of Grade III should have achieved foundational literacy and numeracy.

Focuses on:

- Reading with comprehension.

- Basic arithmetic and number sense.

- Problem-solving ability.

<!-- -->

- Provides structured pedagogy, teacher training modules, age-appropriate learning materials, and monitoring systems to ensure outcomes.

**Swachh Vidyalaya Abhiyan (2014–15)**

- Launched as part of the Swachh Bharat Mission to improve hygiene and sanitation in schools.

- **Achievements:** 417,796 toilets constructed or made functional in 2,61,400 schools across states and UTs within the set deadline (August 2015).

While not directly a literacy program, it supports FLN indirectly by:

- Improving school attendance, especially of girls (reducing dropout rates due to lack of toilets).

- Providing a safe and healthy learning environment, which encourages regular schooling.

**Foundational Learning Study (FLS), March 2022**

- A large-scale national study jointly conducted by the MoE and NCERT.

- **Purpose:** To establish baseline data on learning outcomes related to literacy and numeracy.

- Helps identify gaps in student performance, teacher preparedness, and curriculum effectiveness.

- Provides a data-driven foundation for future interventions under NEP-2020 and NIPUN Bharat.

**Alignment with SDG-4, Target 4.6**

*Target 4.6 states: “By 2030, ensure that all youth and a substantial proportion of adults, both men and women, achieve literacy and numeracy.”*

India's initiatives contribute by:

- Adult literacy programmes (New India Literacy Programme).

- Foundational literacy and numeracy in early schooling (NIPUN Bharat).

- Improved school environment and attendance (Swachh Vidyalaya).

- Evidence-based policymaking (Foundational Learning Study).

**Long-Term Outcomes Expected**

- By 2026–27, India should achieve universal FLN for children up to Class III.

- Adult literacy levels will increase, narrowing the gap with developed countries.

- Improved school retention and reduced dropout rates due to supportive infrastructure.

- Stronger human capital contributing to India's knowledge economy and demographic dividend.

**38. Concept of Hub and Spoke Model in HEIs for SDGs**

The diagram on the source page illustrates the Hub and Spoke Model, where the HEI functions as the central hub coordinating "Adoption of the villages around the campus (min 5 sq km)" across five spoke categories: Education, Hygiene, Health services, Economical upliftment, and Others — each branching into specific initiatives as listed below.

**Education**

- Upliftment of schools

- Scholarship opportunities to students

- Special programs for educational problems

- Online education facilities

- Vocational trainings

- Professional courses

**Hygiene**

- Awareness programs

- Special sessions for females

**Health services**

- Primary health facilities

- Vaccination drives

- Ambulance facilities

**Economical upliftment**

- Enterpreneural skill development

- Self employment generation programs

- Thriving villages towards self sustainment

**Others**

- Legal awareness

- Imformation about rights.

- Hub (University / HEI)

<!-- -->

- Acts as the custodian and central driver of SDG implementation.

- Provides leadership, knowledge, infrastructure, and resources.

- Functions as the policy lab and knowledge hub for sustainable practices.

<!-- -->

- Spokes (Surrounding Areas / Communities within 5 km²)

<!-- -->

- The university adopts a defined geographical area (~5 km²) around its location.

- Engages with schools, local communities, industries, and civic bodies.

- Implements localized initiatives aligned to SDG-4 (Quality Education) and other SDGs (health, gender, environment, etc.).

**Roles and Responsibilities under the Model**

**1.** For Education (SDG-4)

- Provide literacy and numeracy programs for children and adults.

- Deploy MOOCs, digital classrooms, and mobile learning units for local schools.

- Train local teachers through Faculty Development Programs (FDPs).

**2.** For Research and Innovation

- Use the surrounding area as a living laboratory for SDG-related projects.

- Pilot community-based education innovations.

- Generate data and publish outcomes to inform state/national policies.

**3.** For Community Engagement

- Conduct skill development workshops (vocational training, entrepreneurship).

- Improve school infrastructure, sanitation, and digital access.

- Facilitate multi-stakeholder partnerships (NGOs, local government, private sector).

**39. Target 4.1 (Ensure free, equitable, and quality primary and secondary education)**

**1. Adopting Schools and Monitoring Attendance / Dropout Rates**

- HEIs can partner with nearby government or community schools as part of their extension and outreach activities.

- Faculty and student volunteers can regularly track attendance, identify dropout-prone students, and provide remedial support.

- This ensures that children stay enrolled in schools, reducing early dropouts and aligning with the SDG-4.1 goal of equitable education for all.

**2. Creating Awareness in the Community**

- Many parents, especially in rural and marginalized areas, are unaware of the facilities already available under government schemes (scholarships, mid-day meals, free uniforms, textbooks, bicycles for girl students, etc.).

- HEIs can conduct awareness drives, parent meetings, and community workshops to highlight the importance of schooling and how these schemes reduce the financial burden.

- This builds trust between schools and the community, leading to higher enrolment and retention.

**3. Training of Schoolteachers**

- Teachers often face challenges in making classes interactive, especially in resource-constrained schools.

- HEIs, with their expertise, can conduct teacher training workshops on:

<!-- -->

- Creative pedagogy (play-based and activity-based learning).

- Child psychology and inclusive teaching methods.

- Use of ICT tools and digital resources for blended learning.

<!-- -->

- This improves teaching quality, enhances classroom engagement, and improves student performance.

**4. Facilitating a Fun Learning Environment**

- HEIs can help transform schools into child-friendly spaces by:

<!-- -->

- Painting classrooms and walls with educational and motivational themes.

- Setting up libraries, book corners, or digital reading hubs.

- Organizing art, cultural, and sports events to make schools vibrant and engaging.

<!-- -->

- A fun, attractive, and interactive environment reduces absenteeism, motivates children to stay in school, and promotes holistic development beyond academics.

**Overall Impact on SDG-4.1**

- By adopting schools and providing support, HEIs ensure that primary and secondary education is not only accessible but also engaging and effective.

- This directly addresses quality (better learning outcomes), equity (focus on disadvantaged groups), and retention (reduced dropout rates).

- Over time, such interventions strengthen the education ecosystem in the community and prepare students for higher education, vocational training, or entrepreneurship.

**40. Target 4.2 (Equal access to quality pre-primary education) with the minimal recommended actions for HEIs**

**1. Opening of Crèches and Pre-Schools within University Campuses**

- HEIs can establish model crèches and pre-schools not only for the children of university staff but also for those from surrounding villages and low-income communities.

- This makes universities active contributors to community development and early childhood care.

- Such crèches can serve as demonstration and training centres for students of teacher-education programmes, psychology, and social work.

- These facilities embody the principle of "education for all" and showcase inclusion at the pre-primary level.

**2. Providing Transport Facilities for Local Children**

- Many rural or marginalized families cannot access pre-schools due to distance and safety concerns.

- Universities can arrange pick-and-drop transport services for children from nearby villages to the campus crèches or community pre-schools.

- This promotes equity and access, especially for children in remote or underserved areas.

- It also builds trust between the institution and the local community, encouraging long-term educational participation.

**3. Designing and Distributing Learning Materials for Pre-Primary Teachers**

- Universities with teacher education or child development departments can design age-appropriate learning aids, storybooks, and activity kits for pre-primary classes.

- These materials can be distributed to local Anganwadi centres and community schools.

- It ensures that rural teachers have creative, low-cost resources aligned with the NEP-2020 framework for Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE).

- This initiative directly contributes to improving quality and consistency of pre-primary education.

**4. Certificate Courses for Pre-Primary Educators**

- HEIs can introduce short-term certificate or diploma programmes focused on early childhood education, pedagogy, and classroom management.

- These courses will upskill existing Anganwadi and pre-primary teachers, most of whom may lack formal training.

- The outcome is a qualified, competent, and motivated ECCE workforce, capable of delivering high-quality learning experiences for children aged 3–6 years.

**5. Student Volunteer Programmes**

- University students can act as volunteers in nearby community schools or crèches.

- They can organize storytelling sessions, art workshops, games, and health awareness programs for children.

- This gives students practical experience in teaching and community engagement, while helping children learn through play and interaction.

- It also promotes the concept of "service learning", encouraging civic responsibility among university students.

**Alignment with SDG-4 Target 4.2**

- **Goal:** "Ensure that all girls and boys have access to quality early childhood development, care, and pre-primary education so that they are ready for primary education."

- **These HEI-driven initiatives directly support the target by:**

<!-- -->

- Expanding access through community-based pre-schools.

- Enhancing quality through teacher training and learning resources.

- Promoting inclusivity through free transport and open access.

- Encouraging readiness for primary education through interactive and holistic learning.

**Long-Term Impact**

- Children from the community will enter primary school with strong foundational skills in literacy, numeracy, and social interaction.

- HEIs will emerge as regional custodians of ECCE, setting benchmarks for early childhood education practices.

- The model will promote gender equality, parental participation, and community trust in formal education systems.

- Ultimately, these actions will help India move closer to achieving universal, inclusive, and high-quality early education by 2030 under SDG-4.2.

**1. Review of National Literacy and Foundational Learning Schemes**

- The Padhe Bharat Badhe Bharat initiative aimed at improving reading and comprehension among early learners must be regularly reviewed for learning outcomes, implementation gaps, and inclusivity.

**2. Curriculum Modernization and SDG Integration**

- Regular curriculum reviews ensure that teaching content reflects current global issues such as climate change, gender equality, digital literacy, and sustainability.

- Curriculum committees should include experts, educators, industry representatives, and SDG specialists for relevance and future readiness.

**3. Strengthening RTE Implementation**

- Despite legal provisions, several children remain out of school due to socio-economic or infrastructural barriers.

- The government must ensure strict compliance, infrastructure development, and targeted inclusion of girls, SC/ST, and minority communities.

**4. Community and Youth Participation**

- Active involvement of School Management Committees (SMCs), PTAs, and youth volunteers fosters local ownership of educational reforms.

- Engaging communities ensures transparency, accountability, and contextual decision-making in school improvement.

**5. Early Education in Mother Tongue**

- Implementation of multilingual education enhances foundational literacy and bridges early learning gaps, especially in rural and tribal regions.

- This policy directly supports Target 4.2 (Early Childhood Care and Education) while reinforcing linguistic inclusion.

**6. Recognition of Alternate Learning Pathways**

- Legalizing and monitoring homeschooling, online learning, and non-formal education models ensures no child is left behind.

- This approach promotes learning continuity during crises (e.g., pandemics) and supports children with disabilities or remote living conditions.

- Encourage teachers to undertake short-term attachments, fellowships, or immersion programmes in states or countries that have excelled in SDG-aligned education (e.g., Finland, Singapore, Japan).

- Develop a national Teacher Performance and Feedback Framework (TPFF) linked to student outcomes, classroom engagement, and innovation in pedagogy.

- Such mobility programmes enhance the international standing and academic capacity of Indian educators and support the global knowledge exchange vision of NEP-2020.

**Implementation Strategies**

- Establish a National Teacher Training Registry Portal to document training participation and certifications.

- Integrate teacher capacity development objectives into Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan and RUSA 2.0.

- Create a National Teacher Fellowship Scheme for short-term training abroad.

- Build partnerships with UNESCO MGIEP, British Council, and OECD Education Directorate for technical support and best practice sharing.

- Establishment of a professional, globally competent teaching community.

- Continuous improvement in pedagogical innovation and student learning outcomes.

- Increased international collaboration and research mobility.

- India emerges as a regional leader in teacher education and capacity building by 2030.
