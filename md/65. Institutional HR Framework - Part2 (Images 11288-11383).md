**17. General Conditions of Service**

**I. Purpose and Scope**

- To establish uniform and transparent conditions governing the appointment, conduct, and service obligations of all employees of the University.

- These conditions apply to all categories of employees — Teaching, Non-Teaching, Technical, and Administrative — whether permanent, temporary, probationary, or contractual.

**1. Medical Certificates and Fitness Requirements**

- Every appointment shall be subject to the condition that the appointee is certified to be in sound health and physically fit for service by the medical authority nominated by the Board of Management (BoM).

- The medical examination shall include a general physical and mental fitness assessment as prescribed by the University.

- The BoM reserves the right to relax medical requirements in special cases, subject to conditions it deems appropriate.

- At any time during service, the BoM may direct any employee to undergo a medical examination by a registered medical practitioner nominated by the University.

- The findings of the medical authority shall determine whether the employee is fit to continue in service.

- Failure to comply with such medical examination may be treated as misconduct or refusal of duty.

**2. Whole-Time Employee Status**

**(a) Definition and Commitment**

- Unless otherwise specified in the appointment order, every employee shall be deemed to be a whole-time employee of the University.

- The employee shall be available for such duties as may be assigned beyond the scheduled working hours, including holidays and Sundays, whenever required in the interest of the institution.

- The University may require services in teaching, research, administration, examination work, community engagement, or any related duties.

**(b) Exclusive Service Obligation**

- Every employee shall devote his/her full professional time and attention to University duties.

- No employee shall engage, directly or indirectly, in:

<!-- -->

- Any trade, business, or private employment, or

- Private tuition, consultancy, or paid service without prior written permission of the competent authority.

<!-- -->

- However, the prohibition shall not apply to academic or professional consultancy work, research, authorship, or training undertaken with prior permission of the competent authority, subject to prescribed conditions for remuneration and acknowledgment.

**(c) Institutional Deployment**

- The employee shall be at the disposal of the University that pays him/her, and may be assigned or transferred to any department, centre, branch, or section within the University system.

- Such transfer or redeployment shall be carried out without any change in salary, designation, or emoluments, and shall not be deemed as a punishment.

- Employees are required to cooperate with inter-departmental transfers or project assignments made in the interest of academic or administrative efficiency.

**(d) Additional Responsibilities**

- The appointing authority may, when necessary, assign an employee to duties outside their usual scope of work, including:

<!-- -->

- Special committees, examination duties, or accreditation preparation.

- Community outreach, extension, or consultancy projects.

- Supervisory, mentoring, or training responsibilities.

<!-- -->

- The employee shall perform these duties without any claim for additional remuneration, unless specifically sanctioned by the BoM.

**3. Service Integrity and Discipline**

- Every employee shall maintain absolute integrity and devotion to duty.

- They must uphold institutional reputation, comply with all rules and regulations, and observe discipline in conduct, punctuality, and cooperation.

- The Code of Conduct and Ethics Policy forms an integral part of these service conditions.

**4. Transferability and Deployment Clause**

- The University may, at its discretion, transfer any employee from one campus, department, or functional unit to another, based on institutional need.

- The transfer shall not adversely affect the employee's pay, seniority, or service continuity.

- In case of inter-campus or inter-institutional assignment, employees shall receive appropriate travel or relocation allowance as per University policy.

**5. Interpretation and Authority**

- Any question of interpretation regarding the general conditions of service shall be referred to the Registrar, whose decision shall be final, subject to ratification by the Board of Management.

- Amendments, relaxations, or exemptions from any clause shall require formal approval of the BoM.

**7. Policy Principles**

- Every appointment carries the obligation of loyalty, integrity, and dedication to institutional goals.

- Employees are given fair opportunity for improvement through mentorship and feedback.

- The process emphasizes merit, transparency, and accountability in confirming employees into permanent service.

**19. Extension of Probation**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the conditions, authority, and procedure for extending the probationary period of an employee when their performance, conduct, or overall suitability for confirmation requires further evaluation.

- This provision ensures that only those who demonstrate consistent competence and integrity are confirmed into regular service.

**1. Authority to Extend Probation**

- The Appointing Authority shall have the power to extend the period of probation of any employee for such duration as may be found necessary in the interest of the University.

- The decision shall be based on a written performance assessment report submitted by the Head of Department (HoD), Dean, or Sectional Head.

- Such extension must be recorded in writing and communicated formally to the concerned employee before the expiry of the original probation period.

**2. Grounds for Extension**

- The probation period may be extended under any of the following circumstances:

<!-- -->

- Performance not up to expected standards during the initial probation.

- Lack of consistency or reliability in work, teaching, or administration.

- Need for further observation or mentoring to assess professional aptitude.

- Extended absence due to leave, illness, or any other valid reason that prevented adequate evaluation.

- Pending disciplinary proceedings or verification of antecedents.

**3. Duration of Extension**

- Normally, the extension shall not exceed one year beyond the original probation period.

- In exceptional cases, where further evaluation is necessary, the Board of Management (BoM) may approve an additional extension with valid justification.

- No employee shall remain on probation for more than three years in total, except with BoM approval under extraordinary circumstances.

**4. Procedure for Extension**

- Performance Review:

<!-- -->

- Before the completion of the initial probation, the HoD / Dean shall submit a comprehensive Probation Evaluation Report (Form–PER–01).

<!-- -->

- Recommendation:

<!-- -->

- If the report indicates the need for extension, the same shall be forwarded to the Registrar or Appointing Authority with remarks and supporting documents.

<!-- -->

- Decision and Communication:

<!-- -->

- The Appointing Authority shall issue a formal order of extension specifying:

- Duration of the extended period.

- Specific areas for improvement.

- Conditions for successful confirmation.

<!-- -->

- Monitoring:

<!-- -->

- The HRD Cell shall conduct periodic reviews and maintain all documentation in the employee's Probation Record File until confirmation or termination.

**5. Conditions During Extension**

- The employee on extended probation shall:

<!-- -->

- Continue to perform all duties and responsibilities as assigned.

- Be entitled to the same pay and allowances unless otherwise notified.

- Undergo performance counselling or skill enhancement as recommended by the department.

- Receive continuous monitoring and written feedback from superiors.

**6. Outcome After Extension**

- At the end of the extended period, the Appointing Authority shall review the employee's performance again and decide:

<!-- -->

- \(a\) To confirm the employee in the post, or

- \(b\) To terminate the probationary service, if found unsatisfactory.

<!-- -->

- Absence of a written confirmation order shall not automatically imply confirmation.

**7. Summary Table**

| Basis for Extension            | Unsatisfactory performance, absence, or pending evaluation | HoD / Dean / HRD        |

**8. Policy Principles**

- Extension of probation is a corrective and evaluative measure, not a punitive one.

- The process shall be guided by fairness, documentation, and transparency.

- Employees shall receive timely feedback to improve performance during the extended period.

- The ultimate goal is to ensure that every confirmed employee meets the University's standards of excellence, integrity, and responsibility.

**20. Confirmation Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To outline the procedure and criteria for confirmation of employees upon successful completion of the probation or extended probation period.

- This policy ensures that only those who demonstrate competence, discipline, and alignment with institutional values are confirmed into permanent service.

**1. Eligibility for Confirmation**

- Every employee who has completed the prescribed period of probation (or any extended probation) shall be considered for confirmation in the post to which he/she was appointed.

- The probation period includes only the duration of active service, excluding periods of leave without pay, suspension, or unauthorized absence.

- The employee must have fulfilled all service requirements, performance standards, and conduct expectations as prescribed by the University.

**2. Authority for Confirmation**

- The Appointing Authority shall be responsible for evaluating and confirming the employee after the probation period.

- The final decision regarding confirmation shall rest with the Board of Management (BoM) or its delegate (Registrar / Vice-Chancellor), as specified in the Service Rules.

**3. Procedure for Confirmation**

- Performance Evaluation:

<!-- -->

- Before the expiry of the probation period, the Head of Department / Dean shall submit a Probation Assessment Report to the HRD Cell, indicating whether the employee has:

- Displayed satisfactory performance and punctuality.

- Shown good conduct, discipline, and institutional commitment.

- Met teaching, administrative, or technical output targets.

<!-- -->

- Recommendation:

<!-- -->

- The report, along with supporting feedback, shall be forwarded to the Appointing Authority with clear recommendations for:

- \(a\) Confirmation,

- \(b\) Extension of probation, or

- \(c\) Termination / non-confirmation.

<!-- -->

- Decision:

<!-- -->

- After reviewing the recommendation and verifying all records, the Appointing Authority shall decide whether the probation has been satisfactorily completed.

- If satisfied, the authority shall issue a formal written confirmation order specifying the post and effective date of confirmation.

<!-- -->

- Communication:

<!-- -->

- The confirmation order shall be communicated in writing to the employee and recorded in the Service Register and HRD Database.

**4. Conditions for Confirmation**

- The employee shall not be deemed to have been confirmed automatically merely by the lapse of the probation period.

- Confirmation shall take place only upon written approval by the Appointing Authority.

- The following shall be verified before confirmation:

<!-- -->

- Service record free from disciplinary proceedings.

- Medical fitness and satisfactory attendance record.

- Completion of mandatory training / induction programs.

- Compliance with institutional and professional ethics.

**5. Effect of Confirmation**

- Once confirmed, the employee:

<!-- -->

- Becomes a permanent member of the University staff in the respective cadre.

- Becomes eligible for annual increments, promotions, and long-term benefits (leave, gratuity, provident fund, etc.).

- Gains seniority rights within the cadre as per University regulations.

- Is bound by all service, conduct, and disciplinary rules applicable to permanent staff.

**6. Non-Confirmation or Termination**

- If the probationer's performance or conduct is found unsatisfactory:

<!-- -->

- The Appointing Authority may either extend the probation or terminate the service, with due notice and justification.

- The employee shall be informed in writing of the reasons for non-confirmation.

- Termination during or after probation shall follow the Disciplinary and Grievance Policy procedures.

**7. Summary Table**

| **Clause**            | **Provision**                                             | **Authority**                    |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Eligibility           | Completion of probation or extended probation             | Registrar / Appointing Authority |
| Basis of Confirmation | Satisfactory performance, conduct, and record             | HoD / Dean / HRD                 |
| Decision & Order      | Formal written confirmation issued                        | Appointing Authority             |
| Effective Date        | From the day following successful completion of probation | HRD / Registrar                  |
| Non-Confirmation      | Extension or termination with reasoned order              | Appointing Authority             |

**8. Policy Principles**

- Confirmation signifies the University's confidence in the employee's capabilities and integrity.

- The process shall be objective, fair, and evidence-based, with proper documentation.

- The institution retains the right to withhold or defer confirmation if performance standards are not met.

- Written confirmation is mandatory for validity — no employee shall claim permanent status without such order.

- The policy promotes accountability and reinforces a culture of excellence, responsibility, and transparency in staff management.

**21. Termination of Service Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To specify the conditions, authority, and procedures for termination of employment during probation, temporary service, or contractual tenure.

- The policy ensures that termination decisions are carried out lawfully, fairly, and transparently, maintaining institutional efficiency and accountability.

**1. Termination of Probationary Employees**

- Clause (a):

<!-- -->

- The services of a probationer may be terminated by the Appointing Authority at any time during the period of probation, or at the end of it, if the employee is found:

- Unsuitable for the post, or

- Unsatisfactory in work, discipline, or conduct.

- Such termination shall be effected by giving the employee one month's notice or one month's salary in lieu of notice.

- No formal disciplinary proceedings are required in such cases, as probation is an evaluation period, not a confirmed tenure.

- The decision of the Appointing Authority shall be final and binding, subject to the employee's right to representation or appeal as per University Grievance Policy.

**2. Termination of Temporary or Ad-hoc Employees**

- Clause (b):

<!-- -->

- The Appointing Authority shall have the power to terminate the services of any temporary or ad-hoc employee:

- Without assigning any reason, and

- Without any prior notice or compensation.

- Such appointments are made purely on a temporary or need-based basis, and carry no claim for permanent absorption or continuation.

- The termination may occur in the following instances:

- On completion of the assigned task or project.

- When the post ceases to exist or is abolished.

- If the employee's services are no longer required in the interest of the institution.

- Temporary staff are expected to acknowledge this condition at the time of appointment.

**3. Termination of Contractual / Tenure-Based Employees**

- Clause (c):

<!-- -->

- The services of an employee appointed on a contract or tenure basis shall automatically cease on the expiry of the contract period or tenure specified in the appointment order.

- The termination shall be automatic and without notice, unless the contract provides otherwise.

- Extension or renewal of contract shall be made only through a written order issued by the competent authority before the expiry date.

- The University is under no obligation to extend or renew the contract beyond its stipulated term.

- Termination before the expiry of the contract may occur:

- For violation of contract terms, misconduct, or poor performance.

- As per termination clause stated in the contract agreement.

- By mutual consent, with notice period as agreed upon.

**4. General Provisions and Conditions**

- Termination shall always be approved and recorded in writing by the competent authority (Registrar / BoM).

- All financial and administrative clearances (salary dues, leave encashment, etc.) must be settled through the HRD and Accounts Department prior to issuing the final relieving order.

- In cases involving disciplinary misconduct, separate procedures under the Grievance Redressal and Disciplinary Policy shall apply.

- Termination shall be implemented in compliance with applicable labour and employment laws where relevant.

**5. Authority and Documentation**

| **Category of Employee** | **Reason for Termination**                     | **Notice Requirement**               | **Authority**               |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Probationer              | Unsatisfactory work or non-suitability         | 1 month notice or salary in lieu     | Appointing Authority        |
| Temporary / Ad-hoc       | End of requirement or institutional discretion | No notice required                   | Appointing Authority        |
| Contract / Tenure        | Expiry of contract or tenure                   | Not applicable (automatic cessation) | Appointing Authority / BoM  |
| Project Staff            | Project completion or fund withdrawal          | As per project agreement             | Registrar / Dean (Research) |

**6. Relieving and Exit Procedure**

- Before final separation, the employee must:

<!-- -->

- Submit resignation clearance and handover certificate from concerned department.

- Return all University property (ID card, equipment, documents, etc.).

- Complete exit formalities through HRD and Finance sections.

- Receive Relieving and Service Certificate after clearance verification.

**7. Policy Principles**

- Termination shall always be guided by fairness, non-discrimination, and compliance with law.

- The probation clause enables early correction of unsuitable appointments.

- Temporary and contractual appointments remain time-bound and non-permanent, preserving flexibility in workforce management.

- Employees have the right to dignified exit and settlement of all legitimate dues.

- The University maintains the right to act in the best interest of academic and administrative efficiency, while upholding ethical and transparent practices.

**22. Non-Confirmation After Probation Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the status and service implications of an employee who has completed the prescribed probationary period but has neither been confirmed nor formally extended in writing.

- This clause ensures administrative clarity and procedural fairness in handling cases of non-confirmation by default while maintaining institutional flexibility.

**1. Status of Employee After Probation Period**

- If an employee completes the period of probation and no formal order of confirmation is issued by the Appointing Authority, and no written extension of probation is communicated, the employee shall be deemed to have been continued on a temporary basis.

- Such continuation shall not confer any permanent or substantive right to the post.

- The employee will continue to perform all duties of the post, subject to periodic review by the competent authority.

**2. Termination Under Temporary Status**

- In this deemed temporary status, the Appointing Authority shall have the power to terminate the services of the employee by giving:

<!-- -->

- One month's notice, or

- One month's salary in lieu of notice.

<!-- -->

- No specific reasons are required to be recorded for such termination, as the employee has not yet attained confirmed or permanent status.

- The termination order shall be issued in writing and duly communicated to the employee.

**3. Conditions Governing Non-Confirmation**

- The following conditions shall apply:

<!-- -->

- The employee shall continue in service on temporary basis only until a decision regarding confirmation or termination is taken.

- The University reserves the right to review performance and conduct before taking a final decision.

- The period of service under this temporary continuation shall not count as confirmed service unless regularized by an express confirmation order.

- The employee shall continue to be governed by all relevant service and conduct rules during this interim period.

**4. Authority and Procedure**

- Monitoring:

<!-- -->

- The HRD Cell shall maintain a register of probation completion dates and ensure timely initiation of confirmation proceedings.

<!-- -->

- Reporting:

<!-- -->

- The Head of Department / Dean shall submit a performance evaluation at the end of probation to facilitate the confirmation decision.

<!-- -->

- Decision Timeline:

<!-- -->

- If no decision is made within 30 days of probation expiry, HRD shall notify the Appointing Authority for necessary action to avoid procedural lapses.

<!-- -->

- Termination / Continuation Order:

<!-- -->

- Any order of continuation or termination must be signed by the Appointing Authority and recorded in the employee's service file.

**5. Summary Table**

| **Clause**                           | **Provision**                                                               | **Authority**        |
|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| Non-Confirmation                     | Employee continued on temporary basis after probation without written order | Registrar / HRD      |
| Termination Power                    | May terminate by giving one month's notice or salary in lieu                | Appointing Authority |
| Rights During Temporary Continuation | No claim to permanency or seniority                                         | Registrar            |
| Documentation                        | Written order mandatory for continuation or termination                     | HRD / BoM            |
| Performance Review                   | Continuous monitoring until decision taken                                  | HoD / Dean / HRD     |

**6. Policy Principles**

- Silence or administrative delay in confirmation shall not automatically confer permanent status.

- Continuation beyond probation without explicit confirmation is purely temporary and conditional.

- The University ensures fairness and procedural propriety while maintaining flexibility to terminate unsuitable employees.

- This provision safeguards institutional standards and prevents automatic regularization of service through oversight.

- Written documentation and timely decisions by the competent authority are essential to uphold accountability and transparency.

**23. Termination of Service on Medical Grounds Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To outline the procedure and authority for terminating the services of a permanent employee on medical grounds, ensuring both institutional efficiency and humane treatment of the affected employee.

- This provision ensures that decisions based on medical incapacity are handled with fairness, dignity, and compliance with applicable regulations.

**1. Authority for Termination**

- The Board of Management (BoM) shall have the exclusive power to terminate the services of any member of the permanent staff on medical grounds.

- Such action may be taken only after receiving a formal medical certificate issued by a medical authority nominated by the Board.

- The medical certificate must clearly state that the employee is medically unfit to continue discharging the duties of their post, either:

<!-- -->

- Permanently, or

- For an indefinite period, rendering continuation impracticable.

**2. Notice Period and Compensation**

- When termination is recommended on medical grounds, the Board shall:

<!-- -->

- Provide the employee three months' written notice, or

- Pay three months' salary in lieu of such notice.

<!-- -->

- “Salary” includes basic pay and admissible allowances last drawn by the employee.

- The payment in lieu of notice shall be made in full and final settlement of the employee's dues at the time of termination.

**3. Procedure for Termination on Medical Grounds**

- Medical Examination:

<!-- -->

- The concerned employee shall be referred to a Board-nominated medical practitioner or panel for comprehensive examination.

- The medical report must explicitly state:

- Nature of illness or disability,

- Likelihood of recovery, and

- Recommendation on fitness to continue in employment.

<!-- -->

- Communication of Findings:

<!-- -->

- The HRD Cell shall communicate the medical findings to the Board of Management through the Registrar.

<!-- -->

- Board Review:

<!-- -->

- The Board shall review the medical certificate and service record before taking a decision.

- The employee shall be given an opportunity to represent or appeal, if desired, before the final decision.

<!-- -->

- Final Order:

<!-- -->

- Upon satisfaction that the employee is medically unfit for further service, the Board shall issue a written termination order, clearly mentioning:

- Effective date of termination,

- Medical authority's certification, and

- Notice pay / compensation details.

<!-- -->

- Record Maintenance:

<!-- -->

- The HRD Cell shall file all related documents — medical certificate, Board resolution, and payment details — in the employee's service record.

**4. Post-Termination Benefits**

- Employees terminated on certified medical grounds shall be eligible for:

<!-- -->

- Gratuity, provident fund, and earned leave encashment as per University and statutory rules.

- Consideration for compassionate assistance (if applicable) under the University's welfare scheme.

<!-- -->

- Such termination shall not be treated as disciplinary action, and the employee's record shall reflect “medically relieved from service” rather than “dismissed” or “terminated.”

**5. Protection of Employee Rights**

- The process shall uphold confidentiality, dignity, and non-discrimination.

- The employee shall be informed of:

<!-- -->

- The right to submit a second medical opinion within a specified time frame (normally 15 days).

- The right to appeal to the Board of Management before final termination.

<!-- -->

- The institution shall maintain sensitivity and fairness throughout the process, recognizing that medical incapacity is not a fault but a condition beyond the employee's control.

**6. Summary Table**

| **Clause**            | **Provision**                                        | **Authority**             |
|-----------------------|------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Applicability         | Permanent employees declared medically unfit         | BoM                       |
| Medical Certification | By medical authority nominated by the Board          | Nominated Medical Officer |
| Notice Period         | 3 months' notice or 3 months' salary in lieu         | BoM / Registrar           |
| Right to Appeal       | Before final decision                                | Employee / BoM            |
| Eligible Benefits     | PF, gratuity, leave encashment                       | HRD / Accounts            |
| Record Entry          | “Medically relieved from service” (not disciplinary) | HRD Department            |

**7. Policy Principles**

- The University is committed to humane and lawful management of employment affected by medical incapacity.

- Termination on medical grounds shall be a last resort, considered only when the employee's condition makes continued service impossible.

- The decision shall be supported by certified medical evidence and approved by the highest governing body to ensure procedural integrity.

- The policy balances institutional responsibility and employee welfare, aligning with principles of fairness, compassion, and transparency.

**24. Termination of Service on Grounds of Retrenchment or Economy Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the authority, conditions, and procedures under which the University may terminate the services of a permanent employee due to retrenchment, organizational restructuring, or financial constraints.

- The policy ensures such actions are taken lawfully, transparently, and with due compensation, while maintaining institutional sustainability and fairness.

**1. Authority for Termination**

- The Board of Management (BoM) shall have the exclusive power to terminate the service of any permanent member of the staff on the following grounds:

1\. **Retrenchment** – when a post is abolished or declared surplus due to reorganization, downsizing, or redundancy.

**2. Notice and Compensation**

- The employee whose service is terminated under this clause shall be given:

<!-- -->

- Three (3) months' written notice, or

- Three (3) months' salary in lieu of such notice.

<!-- -->

- The term “salary” shall include basic pay and admissible allowances last drawn by the employee.

- Payment in lieu of notice shall be made in one lump sum at the time of issue of the termination order.

**3. Conditions Governing Retrenchment**

- Retrenchment or termination on economic grounds may be undertaken only after:

1\. Reviewing all possible alternatives (redeployment, reassignment, cost optimization).

2\. Ensuring that such action is in the best interest of the University and its long-term sustainability.

3\. Obtaining prior approval and justification in writing from the Board of Management.

4\. Providing reasonable opportunity for the affected employee to be considered for alternative employment within the University, if available.

- Identification of Redundancy:

<!-- -->

- The Head of Department / Administrative Officer shall identify posts or services that have become redundant or financially unsustainable.

<!-- -->

- Review by HRD and Finance Committee:

<!-- -->

- The proposal shall be examined jointly by the HRD Cell and Finance Committee for justification and impact assessment.

<!-- -->

- Approval by BoM:

<!-- -->

- The Board shall pass a formal resolution authorizing the termination under retrenchment or economy measures.

<!-- -->

- Notice / Compensation:

<!-- -->

- The employee shall be given three months' written notice or salary in lieu, as per the Board's decision.

<!-- -->

- Relieving and Settlement:

<!-- -->

- The employee shall complete exit formalities and receive all statutory dues including:

- Provident Fund (PF)

- Gratuity

- Leave encashment

- Any other admissible benefits

**5. Priority Consideration for Reemployment**

- If a position similar to the retrenched employee's post is reintroduced within one year, the retrenched employee shall be given first preference for reappointment, provided he/she remains eligible and medically fit.

**6. Records and Documentation**

- The HRD Department shall maintain:

<!-- -->

- Copy of the BoM resolution approving retrenchment.

**25. Termination of Service on Disciplinary Grounds Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the authority, grounds, and procedure for terminating the services of a permanent employee on disciplinary grounds such as willful insubordination, misconduct, or serious violation of institutional norms.

- This policy ensures that disciplinary terminations are executed fairly, lawfully, and in accordance with due process, protecting both institutional integrity and the principles of natural justice.

**1. Authority for Termination**

- The Board of Management (BoM) shall have the absolute power to terminate the services of any permanent employee of the University without any notice in proven cases of:

<!-- -->

- Willful insubordination or defiance of lawful orders of superiors.

- Acts of indiscipline that disrupt academic or administrative order.

- Disobedience, negligence, or dereliction of duty.

- Resort to malpractices, fraud, falsification of records, corruption, or misuse of position.

- Engagement in activities prejudicial to the interests or reputation of the University.

<!-- -->

- Such termination shall be considered a disciplinary measure of last resort, following due inquiry and formal approval of the BoM.

**2. Grounds for Disciplinary Termination**

| **Category of Misconduct**       | **Illustrative Examples**                                                                           |
|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Insubordination / Defiance       | Refusal to carry out lawful instructions of higher authority, or deliberate obstruction of work.    |
| Indiscipline / Misconduct        | Abusive behaviour, threats, or disruption of institutional harmony.                                 |
| Negligence / Dereliction         | Persistent failure to perform assigned duties, negligence in academic evaluation or administration. |
| Malpractices                     | Cheating, plagiarism, falsifying data, unauthorized disclosure of confidential information.         |
| Corruption / Misuse of Authority | Accepting bribes, misuse of funds, conflict of interest violations.                                 |
| Moral Turpitude                  | Behaviour violating ethical or moral standards expected from University employees.                  |

**3. Disciplinary Procedure**

To ensure fairness and transparency, the following due process shall be followed before imposing termination on disciplinary grounds:

- Preliminary Assessment:

<!-- -->

- Upon receipt of a complaint or report of misconduct, a Preliminary Inquiry Committee shall be constituted by the Registrar or Vice-Chancellor to determine if a prima facie case exists.

<!-- -->

- Charge Sheet and Explanation:

<!-- -->

- If prima facie evidence exists, the employee shall be served a written charge sheet outlining specific allegations and given 10 working days to submit a written explanation.

<!-- -->

- Formal Inquiry:

<!-- -->

- If the explanation is unsatisfactory, a Disciplinary Inquiry Committee shall be constituted to conduct a formal hearing.

- The committee shall include:

- One senior faculty member (Chairperson),

- One administrative officer, and

- One external or legal expert (if required).

- Both sides (employee and institution) shall have an opportunity to present evidence, witnesses, and defence.

<!-- -->

- Findings and Recommendation:

<!-- -->

- The Inquiry Committee shall submit its report and findings to the Registrar within 30 days of initiation.

- If the charges are proven, the report shall recommend an appropriate penalty, which may include termination.

<!-- -->

- Final Decision by BoM:

<!-- -->

- The Board of Management shall review the inquiry report and, if satisfied, approve the termination without notice under disciplinary grounds.

- The decision shall be final and binding.

**4. Nature of Penalties**

Depending on the gravity of misconduct, the following penalties may be imposed:

| **Category**        | **Penalty Type**                                                                        |
|---------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Minor Misconduct    | Warning, written reprimand, or withholding of increment.                                |
| Major Misconduct    | Suspension, demotion, or termination without notice.                                    |
| Grievous Misconduct | Dismissal without notice or compensation, with disqualification from future employment. |

**5. Effects of Termination on Disciplinary Grounds**

- The employee shall forfeit all rights to continuation of service, promotion, or reinstatement.

- The employee shall not be entitled to notice pay or severance benefits, except statutory dues such as provident fund or gratuity as per law.

- The termination order shall specify that it is “on disciplinary grounds” following the findings of a formal inquiry.

- Such termination shall be recorded in the Service Register and relevant HRD records.

**6. Right to Appeal**

- The terminated employee may appeal the decision of the BoM within 30 days of receipt of the termination order.

- The appeal shall be submitted to the Chancellor / Appellate Authority through the Registrar.

- The decision of the Appellate Authority shall be final and conclusive.

**7. Confidentiality and Record Maintenance**

- All proceedings, reports, and evidence related to disciplinary termination shall remain strictly confidential.

- HRD shall maintain:

<!-- -->

- Charge sheet, inquiry report, and termination order.

- Record of notices, hearings, and communications.

- Final resolution or appellate decision.

<!-- -->

- Documents shall be preserved for a minimum of 10 years for legal and audit purposes.

**8. Summary Table**

| **Clause**         | **Provision**                                                       | **Competent Authority** |
|--------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Authority          | BoM may terminate permanent employees on disciplinary grounds       | Board of Management     |
| Grounds            | Insubordination, indiscipline, malpractice, defiance, or corruption | BoM                     |
| Notice Requirement | Not applicable (termination without notice)                         | BoM                     |
| Due Process        | Preliminary inquiry, charge sheet, formal inquiry, final approval   | Registrar / BoM         |
| Right to Appeal    | Within 30 days to Chancellor or Appellate Authority                 | Terminated Employee     |
| Record Retention   | 10 years (in HRD and Legal archives)                                | HRD / Registrar         |

**9. Policy Principles**

- The University shall uphold discipline, integrity, and ethical conduct as fundamental values.

- Termination without notice shall occur only in cases of proven major misconduct, supported by formal inquiry findings.

- All disciplinary actions shall adhere to natural justice, ensuring opportunity for defence and fair hearing.

- The Board's decision shall reflect both institutional interest and procedural fairness.

- The policy reinforces the University's commitment to maintaining a professional, accountable, and corruption-free environment.

**I. Purpose**

- To define the rules, conditions, and procedures governing the resignation of University employees, ensuring that separations occur in an orderly, transparent, and responsible manner.

- This policy applies to all categories of employees — permanent, probationary, contractual, and temporary staff.

- The Appointing Authority (Registrar / Vice-Chancellor / Board of Management, as applicable) is empowered to accept or reject resignations based on institutional requirements.

- The resignation shall be submitted in writing and addressed to the competent authority through the proper channel (Head of Department → Dean → Registrar).

**2. Notice Period Requirement**

- Clause I:

<!-- -->

- A permanent employee may resign from his/her post and terminate the engagement with the University by giving:

- Three (3) months' written notice, or

<!-- -->

- The notice period ensures smooth transition, completion of responsibilities, and minimal disruption to institutional activities.

<!-- -->

- 1\. When the employee is absenting from duty in an unauthorized manner or has not reported for work without sanction of leave.

<!-- -->

- Library (for return of books),

<!-- -->

- The Registrar shall issue the Formal Relieving Order only after confirmation that all dues and handover procedures are completed.

<!-- -->

- Experience and Service Certificate:

<!-- -->

**6. Notice Pay Settlement**

- If the employee requests to be relieved before the expiry of the notice period, he/she shall pay salary equivalent to the unserved portion of the notice period.

**28. Automatic Termination Due to Unauthorized Absence Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To establish the rules and consequences for prolonged unauthorized absence from duty by any employee of the University.

- This policy ensures that institutional functioning is not disrupted by prolonged absenteeism and that accountability in service is maintained.

**1. Applicability**

- This provision applies to all categories of employees — teaching, non-teaching, technical, administrative, probationary, contractual, or permanent — who are on the rolls of the University.

- It covers cases where an employee remains absent continuously for three (3) months or more without obtaining proper prior permission or sanctioned leave.

**2. Grounds for Automatic Termination**

- Clause:

> *An employee who is continuously absent from duty for three months without proper permission shall be deemed to have been terminated from the service and shall be taken out of the rolls.*

- Such absence shall be considered voluntary abandonment of service, and the employee shall automatically forfeit his/her employment with the University.

- No separate notice or disciplinary inquiry shall be required once the condition of continuous unauthorized absence is established.

**3. Conditions and Interpretation**

- “Continuous absence” includes:

<!-- -->

- Non-reporting for duty after expiry of sanctioned leave.

- Absence without submission or approval of leave application.

- Failure to respond to official communications or reminders from the University.

<!-- -->

- The absence shall be treated as unauthorized and deliberate if:

<!-- -->

- The employee fails to provide valid justification or medical certificate within the prescribed time.

- The employee ignores written communication sent by HRD / Registrar requiring explanation or return to duty.

<!-- -->

- The University shall consider such behavior as willful neglect of duty and a breach of service discipline.

**4. Procedure Before Removal from Rolls**

To ensure fairness and record-based action, the following procedure shall be followed:

- Monitoring:

<!-- -->

- HRD shall maintain attendance and leave records for all staff.

<!-- -->

- First Reminder:

<!-- -->

- After 30 days of continuous absence, a written reminder shall be issued to the employee's last known address, directing immediate return or explanation.

<!-- -->

- Final Intimation:

<!-- -->

- If there is no response within 60 days, a final notice shall be sent via registered post or email warning of termination after the third month.

<!-- -->

- Removal from Rolls:

<!-- -->

- If the employee fails to report for duty or provide valid explanation by the end of 90 days (three months), the Registrar shall issue an order of automatic termination, citing this rule.

<!-- -->

- Intimation to Authorities:

<!-- -->

- The removal order shall be communicated to all relevant offices — Accounts, HRD, Department, and Payroll — for necessary action.

**5. Consequences of Automatic Termination**

- The employee shall cease to be in University service from the date of completion of three months' unauthorized absence.

- The name shall be struck off from the University rolls without further notice.

- The employee shall forfeit all claims to:

<!-- -->

- Reinstatement or reappointment, unless specifically approved by the Board.

- Salary, allowances, or service benefits for the unauthorized period.

- Continuity of service or seniority.

<!-- -->

- Provident Fund and Gratuity (if applicable) may be settled as per statutory provisions after due verification.

**6. Reinstatement or Reconsideration**

- Reinstatement after automatic termination may be considered only under exceptional circumstances, such as:

<!-- -->

- Proven medical emergency, accident, or calamity.

- Circumstances beyond the employee's control duly supported by evidence.

<!-- -->

- Such reinstatement shall require the explicit approval of the Board of Management, and the absence period may be treated as Extraordinary Leave without pay if permitted.

**7. Record and Documentation**

|--------------------------------------------------------|--------------------|
| Attendance and leave record                            | HRD Department     |
| Copies of reminders and final notices                  | Registrar's Office |
| Termination order and service record update            | HRD / Accounts     |
| Board or Appointing Authority approval (if reinstated) | Registrar / BoM    |

All records shall be preserved for a minimum of 10 years for legal, audit, and accreditation purposes.

**29. Application for Outside Appointments Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To regulate the conditions under which University employees may apply for or accept employment or appointments outside the University, ensuring compliance with contractual obligations, institutional integrity, and service commitments.

- This policy prevents conflicts of interest, protects University resources, and ensures that employees fulfill their agreed period of service responsibly.

**1. Applicability**

- This policy applies to all categories of employees — probationers, regular, contractual, or bond-bound — who seek employment, consultancy, or academic positions outside the University.

- It governs both applications and attendance at interviews for external employment or assignments.

**2. General Rule**

- No employee of the University shall apply for, or appear in, an interview for an outside appointment without prior written permission and a No Objection Certificate (NOC) issued by the competent authority.

- The competent authority for granting such permission shall be:

<!-- -->

- **Registrar / Vice-Chancellor –** for teaching and administrative staff.

- **Head of Department / Dean** (with Registrar's approval) – for technical and non-teaching staff.

**3. Restrictions Based on Employment Status**

**(a) Probationary Employees:**

- Employees who are on probation are not permitted to apply for any outside appointment.

- If a probationer desires to apply for an outside job, he/she must first resign from the University service and obtain formal acceptance of the resignation before applying elsewhere.

**(b) Bonded or Agreement-bound Employees:**

- Employees who have executed an agreement or service bond to serve the University for a specific period (e.g., those granted higher start in pay, sponsored for study/training, etc.) are not eligible to apply for outside appointments during the bond period.

- However, such employees may apply through proper channel if:

<!-- -->

- They obtain explicit written permission from the University, and

- The forwarding letter clearly states that if selected, the employee shall not be relieved until the agreed period of service under the bond or agreement is completed.

**(c) Regular or Confirmed Employees:**

- Regular or permanent employees may apply for external opportunities only after obtaining a NOC from the competent authority.

- Their application shall be forwarded through proper administrative channel with institutional endorsement.

**4. No Objection Certificate (NOC) Requirement**

- All employees, irrespective of category, must obtain a No Objection Certificate (NOC) before:

<!-- -->

- Applying for an external post, or

- Attending any interview or selection process outside the University.

<!-- -->

- The NOC ensures that the University is aware of and consents to the employee's participation.

- The NOC shall be valid for one specific purpose only (i.e., one post/interview).

**5. Penalties for Non-Compliance**

- If any employee applies for or attends an interview for an outside appointment without obtaining a NOC, it shall be treated as misconduct or violation of service discipline.

- The following disciplinary actions may be imposed depending on the severity of the violation:

1\. Stoppage of annual increment.

2\. Reduction in time scale or grade.

3\. Termination of service, in repeated or serious cases.

- Such actions shall be taken by the Appointing Authority after due verification and, if necessary, formal disciplinary proceedings.

**6. Procedure for Obtaining Permission or NOC**

- Submission of Request:

<!-- -->

- The employee shall submit a written application through proper channel, stating:

- Details of the external post applied for,

- Whether it is in a government, university, or private institution,

- Date and venue of the interview.

<!-- -->

- Verification of Service Conditions:

<!-- -->

- HRD shall verify whether the employee is on probation, under a service bond, or facing any disciplinary proceedings.

<!-- -->

- Approval:

<!-- -->

- If no restrictions apply, the competent authority may issue the NOC in writing, specifying the conditions of permission.

<!-- -->

- Forwarding of Application:

<!-- -->

- The University may forward the employee's application officially, indicating institutional consent and service standing.

**7. Record and Documentation**

|----------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| Employee's written request for NOC           | Department / HRD     |
| Verification of eligibility and service bond | HRD / Registrar      |

| Record of penalties (if imposed)             | Registrar / HRD File |

All such records shall be preserved for minimum 5 years for reference and audit purposes.

**30. Superannuation, Retirement, and Re-Employment Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the rules and procedures governing retirement, premature retirement, and re-employment of University employees.

- This policy ensures systematic workforce planning, provides post-retirement continuity options where necessary, and maintains institutional efficiency and fairness.

**1. Age of Retirement**

- Clause (a):

<!-- -->

- The normal age of superannuation (retirement) for all categories of University employees — teaching, non-teaching, technical, and administrative — shall be 58 years.

- An employee shall automatically cease to be in service on the last day of the month in which he/she attains the age of 58 years.

- Continuation in service beyond the age of 58 years shall be purely at the discretion of the Board of Management (BoM), based on institutional needs, health, performance, and conduct of the employee.

**2. Premature or Compulsory Retirement**

- Clause (b):

**31. Seniority Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the principles and criteria for determining the seniority of University employees within their respective posts or cadres.

- This policy ensures clarity, fairness, and uniformity in establishing inter-se seniority, which forms the basis for promotions, pay fixation, and other service-related benefits.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *The seniority of an employee in a post/cadre shall be determined by the date of joining the service in the University in the post/cadre concerned.*

- Thus, seniority shall be determined primarily by the actual date of joining duty in the particular post or cadre after due appointment through the proper selection process.

**2. Basis of Seniority Determination**

- The date of joining shall mean the date on which the employee assumes charge of the post, as recorded in the joining report accepted by the University.

- In case two or more employees join on the same date, their inter-se seniority shall be determined as follows:

1\. By the order of merit or rank assigned by the selection committee at the time of appointment.

2\. If the order of merit is not available, then by the date of birth (older person being senior).

3\. If still identical, by the alphabetical order of the employee's name.

**3. Promotional and Transfer Cases**

- When an employee is promoted to a higher post or transferred to another cadre:

<!-- -->

- The seniority in the new cadre shall be counted from the date of joining the higher post on promotion.

- Previous service in the lower post shall not be counted towards seniority in the new cadre unless specifically provided by the Board of Management (BoM).

<!-- -->

- In case of promotion through selection, the order of promotion issued by the competent authority shall determine the relative seniority among promotees.

**4. Direct Recruits and Promotees**

- When both direct recruits and promotees are appointed to a cadre during the same year:

<!-- -->

- Their inter-se seniority shall be fixed according to the rotation of vacancies between the two categories, as approved by the BoM or prescribed recruitment rules.

- In the absence of such rotation, seniority shall be determined by the chronological order of appointment letters and joining dates.

**5. Effect of Absence or Non-Joining**

- If an employee fails to join duty within the period specified in the appointment order, the seniority shall be reckoned from the actual date of joining, not from the date of appointment.

- Period of unauthorized absence or break in service shall not count for seniority unless specifically condoned by the competent authority.

**6. Seniority in Case of Re-Employment or Reinstatement**

- For employees re-employed after retirement or reinstated after termination/suspension:

<!-- -->

- Their earlier service may be counted only if specifically ordered by the BoM.

- Otherwise, they shall rank junior to all existing employees in the cadre at the time of rejoining.

**7. Record and Maintenance**

- The HRD Department shall maintain a Seniority Register for each cadre, indicating:

<!-- -->

- Name of employee,

- Designation,

- Date of appointment,

- Date of joining,

- Mode of recruitment (direct / promotion / transfer),

- Remarks on service continuity.

<!-- -->

- The register shall be updated annually and displayed for staff verification.

**8. Disputes and Final Authority**

- Any dispute regarding seniority shall be referred to the Registrar for preliminary review.

- If unresolved, it shall be placed before the Board of Management, whose decision shall be final and binding.

- The Board may, if necessary, consult the Legal Officer or External Expert Committee to ensure fairness.

**9. Summary Table**

| **Clause**                     | **Provision**                                             | **Authority**   |
|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------|
| Basic Rule                     | Seniority based on date of joining the post/cadre         | Registrar / HRD |
| Same Joining Date              | Determined by selection merit, age, or alphabetical order | HRD / BoM       |
| Promotion Cases                | Seniority from date of joining higher post                | Registrar       |
| Direct vs Promotee             | Based on rotation or appointment chronology               | HRD / BoM       |
| Non-Joining / Break in Service | Seniority from actual joining date only                   | Registrar       |
| Dispute Resolution             | Final decision by Board of Management                     | BoM             |

**10. Policy Principles**

- Seniority shall always be objective, transparent, and rule-based, avoiding personal or administrative bias.

- The date of joining remains the primary determinant, ensuring chronological fairness.

- The University's approach aligns with standard UGC, AICTE, and Government service principles.

- Annual maintenance of the Seniority Register promotes clarity, accountability, and institutional order.

- This policy supports fair opportunities for promotion, career advancement, and succession planning within the University.

**32. Inter-Se Seniority Among Candidates Selected in the Same Selection Committee Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To specify the method of determining seniority among multiple candidates selected at the same selection committee meeting for appointments within the same cadre or department.

- This policy ensures clarity, consistency, and fairness in fixing inter-se seniority, especially where appointments involve a mix of regular and temporary vacancies.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *If two or more candidates are selected for appointment for a particular cadre in the same department at one and the same selection committee meeting, the seniority of the candidates so selected shall be decided by the merit order in which they are placed by the Selection Committee, irrespective of the consideration whether they are appointed against regular or temporary vacancies.*

- Accordingly, the order of merit assigned by the Selection Committee shall be the sole determining factor for seniority among all such candidates, irrespective of:

<!-- -->

- The nature of the vacancy (regular or temporary), or

- The date of their individual joining, provided all join within the period specified in their appointment letters.

**2. Merit Order as the Basis for Seniority**

- The Selection Committee's merit list, as approved by the Board of Management (BoM) or competent authority, shall serve as the official record for fixing inter-se seniority.

- The position of candidates in the merit list shall determine their relative seniority ranking in the cadre.

- The HRD Department shall record this order in the Seniority Register immediately upon appointment notification.

**3. Appointment Against Regular and Temporary Vacancies**

- The nature of appointment — whether against a regular post or a temporary/ad-hoc post — shall not affect the initial seniority position as long as the appointments arise from the same selection process.

- However, this seniority is provisional in the case of temporary appointees until they are regularized.

**4. Effect of Reversion to Lower Post**

- Clause:

> *If, however, the candidates appointed against temporary vacancies get reverted to the lower post before they are regularized in the higher cadre, they shall not be entitled for their seniority claim when they get regularized after reversion.*

- Therefore:

<!-- -->

- Temporary appointees who are reverted to their previous posts prior to regularization shall lose their earlier seniority claim in the higher cadre.

- Upon reappointment or regularization at a later date, they shall be treated as fresh entrants in that cadre and placed below all existing regular incumbents.

- Their earlier period of temporary service will not count towards seniority or promotion eligibility in the higher cadre.

**5. Record and Documentation**

- The Registrar / HRD Department shall maintain the following records:

1\. Merit list and minutes of the Selection Committee.

2\. Approval order of the Board of Management or appointing authority.

3\. Appointment orders specifying the type of vacancy (regular or temporary).

4\. Seniority Register reflecting rank as per merit order.

5\. Subsequent reversion or regularization records, if applicable.

- Any alteration to seniority based on reversion or regularization shall be updated in the register within 30 days of such change.

**6. Summary Table**

| **Clause**                      | **Provision**                                                             | **Authority**   |
|---------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------|
| Selection Committee Merit Order | Decides inter-se seniority for all candidates selected in same meeting    | BoM / Registrar |
| Nature of Vacancy               | Regular or temporary status does not affect initial merit-based seniority | HRD Department  |
| Reversion to Lower Post         | Temporary appointees lose seniority claim after reversion                 | Registrar / HRD |
| Re-regularization               | Considered fresh entry for seniority purposes                             | BoM             |
| Record Maintenance              | Merit list, appointment orders, and updated seniority register            | HRD Department  |

**7. Policy Principles**

- Merit order determined by the Selection Committee is final and binding for fixing inter-se seniority.

- The policy maintains a clear distinction between temporary and regular appointments, ensuring fairness to confirmed employees.

- Reversion before regularization nullifies earlier seniority claims to uphold institutional discipline and transparency.

- All seniority-related actions must be documented, approved, and communicated to prevent ambiguity or disputes.

- The policy aligns with UGC and Government service standards for higher education institutions.

**35. Gratuity and Leave Encashment Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To outline the rules, eligibility, and procedures for granting Gratuity and Leave Encashment benefits to eligible employees upon cessation of service through retirement, resignation, death, or termination under approved conditions.

- This policy ensures financial security, fairness, and compliance with statutory norms under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, and institutional regulations.

**1. Gratuity Policy**

**1.1 Applicability**

- The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 shall apply to all eligible employees of the University — teaching, non-teaching, technical, and administrative — who have completed the minimum qualifying service.

- The benefit shall be extended to:

<!-- -->

- Regular and permanent staff,

- Contract employees with long-term continuous service (as decided by BoM).

<!-- -->

- Daily wage or purely temporary staff are not eligible for gratuity unless regularized.

**1.2 Eligibility Conditions**

- An employee becomes eligible for gratuity under the following conditions:

1\. Completion of at least 5 years of continuous service with the University.

2\. On superannuation or retirement.

3\. On resignation or termination, provided continuous service exceeds 5 years.

4\. On death or disablement, gratuity is payable irrespective of length of service.

**1.3 Calculation of Gratuity**

- Gratuity is calculated as per the following formula prescribed under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972:

> *Gratuity = 15 × (Last Drawn Basic Pay + D.A.) × (Completed Years of Service) ÷ 26*

- Key Points:

<!-- -->

- “15 days' wages” for each completed year of service is the standard entitlement.

- The fraction of service exceeding six months is counted as one full year.

- The maximum gratuity payable shall be as per Government of India's notified ceiling (revised from time to time — currently ₹20,00,000).

**1.4 Payment Procedure**

- On cessation of employment, the HRD Department shall initiate gratuity processing by:

1\. Verifying service records and leave balance.

2\. Calculating gratuity entitlement as per law.

3\. Forwarding the proposal to the Accounts Section for disbursement.

- Payment shall be made within 30 days from the date of superannuation or relief.

- In case of death, the gratuity shall be paid to the nominated legal heir(s).

**1.5 Forfeiture of Gratuity**

- Gratuity may be wholly or partially forfeited if the employee:

<!-- -->

- Is dismissed for gross misconduct, such as moral turpitude or corruption.

- Causes financial loss to the University by willful act or negligence.

- Leaves service without notice during the probationary period.

<!-- -->

- The decision regarding forfeiture shall rest with the Board of Management (BoM) after due inquiry.

**2. Leave Encashment Policy**

**2.1 Purpose**

- To compensate employees for earned leave accrued but not availed during service, ensuring fair monetization of leave balances at the time of separation.

**2.2 Eligibility**

- Leave encashment shall be admissible to:

<!-- -->

- All confirmed and permanent employees of the University.

- Employees retiring, resigning after completing 5 years of service, or those whose services are terminated honorably.

- In case of death, leave encashment shall be payable to the legal nominee(s).

<!-- -->

- Temporary or contractual employees shall be governed by specific terms in their appointment letter.

**2.3 Types of Leave Eligible for Encashment**

- Earned Leave (E.L.) is the only category eligible for encashment.

- Half Pay Leave (HPL), Casual Leave, or Special Leave are not encashable.

**2.4 Calculation of Leave Encashment**

- The amount payable for leave encashment shall be computed as:

> *Leave Encashment = Number of Earned Leave Days (Max. 180) × (Last Drawn Basic Pay + D.A.) ÷ 30*

- Key Conditions:

<!-- -->

- The maximum encashable leave at any time shall not exceed 180 days.

- For employees retiring or resigning mid-month, the pay of that month shall be used for calculation.

- Leave encashment is taxable, as per the Income Tax Act, 1961.

**2.5 Procedure for Leave Encashment**

1\. Verification: HRD verifies the employee's accumulated earned leave balance from the Leave Register.

2\. Calculation: Accounts Department calculates the encashment amount as per the formula.

3\. Approval: Registrar or competent authority approves the disbursement.

4\. Payment: The amount is credited to the employee's account along with final settlement.

**2.6 Encashment During Service (if permitted)**

- The Board of Management may allow encashment of earned leave once in 3 years during service, subject to institutional financial position and specific BoM approval.

- Such encashment shall be limited to maximum 15 days of earned leave at a time.

**3. Nomination Requirement**

- Every employee must file a nomination form at the time of joining, specifying legal heir(s) for receiving gratuity and leave encashment benefits.

- The nomination can be revised at any time by submitting a fresh declaration.

- Contributory Pension Scheme (CPS / NPS):

<!-- -->

- The contribution structure shall be:

- Employee Contribution: 10% of Basic Pay + Dearness Allowance.

- University Contribution: Equal 10% contribution to the employee's NPS account.

- Contributions shall be remitted monthly to the NPS Trustee Bank via the University's registered nodal office.

<!-- -->

- Defined Benefit Pension Scheme (if applicable):

<!-- -->

- Pension shall be computed based on last drawn pay and qualifying service, following Government / UGC / AICTE norms.

- The formula (where applicable):

> *Pension = (Last Drawn Pay × Qualifying Service) ÷ 66*

- Self-Contributory Retirement Benefit Scheme:

<!-- -->

- For employees not covered under EPF or NPS, the University may operate a self-funded retirement benefit account, jointly contributed by employee and employer, approved by the BoM.

**3. Qualifying Service for Pension Eligibility**

<!-- -->

<!-- -->

**40. Fixation of Pay Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the principles and procedures for pay fixation applicable to all categories of employees appointed in the University.

- The policy ensures fair, transparent, and standardized determination of initial pay, in accordance with the University's pay structure and Board of Management (BoM) regulations.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *“A member who is appointed to a post shall, in the absence of specific sanction, be eligible to draw pay at the minimum of the time-scale of pay of the post.”*

- Accordingly:

<!-- -->

- Every new appointee to a post shall commence on the minimum of the approved pay scale for that post, unless otherwise sanctioned by the competent authority.

- The time-scale of pay refers to the pay band or level approved by the Board of Management, as revised periodically following UGC / AICTE / State Government norms.

**2. Fixation of Pay on First Appointment**

- Pay on initial appointment shall be fixed at the minimum of the scale applicable to the post, except when:

> 1\. The appointee has previous experience in a recognized institution or industry that warrants higher start.
>
> 2\. The Selection Committee recommends a higher initial pay based on merit, experience, or qualifications.
>
> 3\. The Board of Management sanctions a higher start explicitly in the appointment order.

- In all other cases, the employee will draw pay at the minimum of the prescribed time scale.

**3. Fixation of Pay on Promotion**

- When an employee is promoted to a higher post, the pay shall be fixed as follows:

> 1\. The pay shall be placed at the next higher stage in the time-scale of the new post above the employee's existing basic pay.
>
> 2\. If no stage corresponds exactly, the pay shall be fixed at the next above level in the higher scale.
>
> 3\. The employee shall be entitled to a one-increment benefit in the old scale before being placed in the new scale.
>
> 4\. The date of next increment will be regulated as per University pay rules.

**4. Fixation of Pay on Transfer**

- When an employee is transferred to another post carrying the same pay scale, the pay shall remain unchanged.

- If transferred to a post with a lower pay scale, the pay shall be fixed at the maximum of the lower scale not exceeding the current pay, unless otherwise protected by special order.

**5. Fixation of Pay on Re-employment or Contract Appointment**

- When a retired employee is re-employed:

> 1\. Pay shall be fixed at the minimum of the time scale of the post or at a consolidated salary as approved by the BoM.
>
> 2\. Pension or retirement benefits shall not be duplicated; the re-employed person may draw either full pension plus reduced pay or full pay minus pension, as per BoM decision.
>
> 3\. For contract staff, remuneration shall be consolidated, based on workload, experience, and approved institutional guidelines.

**6. Fixation of Pay After Revision of Scale**

- Whenever the University adopts revised pay scales (e.g., 6th CPC / 7th CPC / AICTE / UGC recommendations):

<!-- -->

- Existing pay shall be fixed in the corresponding pay matrix level as per the approved fitment formula.

- Arrears, if any, shall be calculated from the effective date of revision and paid in accordance with BoM's financial decision.

**7. Pay Protection**

- Pay protection may be granted to an employee joining the University from:

> 1\. A Central / State Government institution,
>
> 2\. An autonomous body, or
>
> 3\. A recognized university or research organization, provided there is no break in service and pay is duly certified by the previous employer.

- Such protection requires prior approval of the Vice-Chancellor / BoM.

**8. Special Cases**

- Higher Initial Pay:

<!-- -->

- May be sanctioned to candidates possessing exceptional qualifications or specialized skills essential for the post, upon Selection Committee recommendation and BoM approval.

<!-- -->

- Stagnation Increment:

**41. Fixation of Pay on Promotion Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the rules governing pay fixation upon promotion to a higher post, ensuring uniformity, transparency, and fairness in salary progression.

- This policy outlines the process of determining the new pay level for employees who are promoted, either on a temporary, probationary, or permanent basis.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *“A member who is holding a post and is promoted to a higher post shall be entitled to draw pay in the time-scale of pay of the higher post at the stage next above his pay in the lower post after adding one increment; provided, however, so long as he holds the higher post temporarily or on probation, that if at any time his pay in the lower post is increased, his pay in the higher post shall be re-fixed at the stage next above such increased pay.”*

- This provision ensures that an employee who is promoted benefits from incremental financial recognition while maintaining consistency with institutional pay structures and career advancement norms.

**2. Eligibility**

- Applies to:

<!-- -->

- All regular and confirmed employees of the University.

- Employees promoted through the approved Selection Committee process or through Career Advancement Schemes (CAS) under UGC/AICTE guidelines.

<!-- -->

- Also applicable to employees temporarily promoted or placed on probation in a higher post.

**3. Principle of Fixation**

Upon promotion:

> 1\. The employee shall be granted one notional increment in the lower post before fixation.
>
> 2\. Thereafter, his/her pay shall be fixed at the next higher stage in the time-scale of the promoted post.
>
> 3\. If the pay in the lower post is revised or incremented later during probation, the pay in the higher post shall be re-fixed to remain one stage above the revised lower pay.

**4. Pay Fixation Formula**

New Pay (Higher Post) = (Existing Pay in Lower Post + 1 Increment) ⇒ Next Higher Stage in the Higher Scale

- Example:

<!-- -->

- Pay in lower post: ₹56,100

- Increment: ₹1,500

- Higher post scale: ₹57,700–₹1,600–₹65,000

- New Pay on Promotion: ₹57,700

**5. Temporary or Probationary Promotion**

- When promotion is temporary or on probation:

<!-- -->

- The employee shall draw pay in the time-scale of the higher post, but

- His pay shall be re-fixed each time his lower post pay increases due to increment or revision.

- This ensures continuous alignment with the next higher stage principle.

<!-- -->

- Once the employee is confirmed in the higher post, the re-fixation becomes final and increments shall accrue in the higher post scale.

**6. Re-fixation in Case of Pay Revision**

- If the pay of the lower post is revised upward (due to pay commission or BoM decision) while the employee is still on probation in the higher post:

<!-- -->

- His pay in the higher post shall be re-fixed to maintain one increment and one stage above the revised lower pay.

- No recovery shall be made for arrears caused by re-fixation.

**7. Pay Protection and Advance Increments**

- Pay protection shall apply when:

<!-- -->

- Promotion is within the same department or cadre.

- The new pay scale's minimum is lower than the pay previously drawn.

<!-- -->

- Advance increments may be granted:

<!-- -->

- For possessing Ph.D., professional certifications, or exceptional achievements.

- Subject to BoM approval based on merit and contribution.

**8. Date of Next Increment**

- The next increment in the higher post shall become due on:

<!-- -->

- The anniversary of promotion, or

- The next normal increment date, whichever is more beneficial to the employee.

<!-- -->

- The University shall ensure no loss of increment during transition between posts.

| Record Entry in Service Book   | HRD & Accounts Section          |

All pay fixation cases must be recorded, signed, and verified before payroll implementation.

**42. Increment and Annual Salary Progression Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the rules, eligibility, and conditions for granting annual increments to University employees.

- This policy ensures that salary progression reflects both length of service and performance, aligning with principles of merit-based career advancement and institutional accountability.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *“All service in a post on a time scale of pay shall count for increments in that time scale.”*

- Accordingly:

<!-- -->

- Every employee appointed to a post carrying a time-scale of pay shall earn annual increments in that scale, subject to satisfactory service and performance.

- Increments shall serve as a reward for continuous, efficient, and disciplined service rather than an automatic entitlement.

**2. Service Counting for Increments**

**(a) Service in the Same Post**

- All periods of service rendered in a post on a time scale of pay shall count for increments in that same scale.

- Increments accrue on completion of 12 months of qualifying service from the date of appointment or from the date of the last increment.

**(b) Service in a Higher Post**

*“Service in a higher post on a higher time scale of pay shall count for increments in the lower post from which the member has been promoted to the higher post.”*

- Thus, if an employee is promoted to a higher post:

<!-- -->

- The service rendered in that higher post shall also be counted towards incremental benefit in the lower post, for pension or pay protection purposes.

- This ensures continuity of service value and avoids stagnation in pay benefits during inter-cadre movement.

**(c) Effect of Leave on Increment Eligibility**

*“Leave other than extra-ordinary leave without pay shall count for increments.”*

- The following shall apply:

> 1\. All paid leave (earned, half-pay, casual, or maternity) counts towards increment accrual.
>
> 2\. Extra-ordinary leave (EOL) without pay does not count for increment unless:
>
> ▪ It is sanctioned due to illness, or
>
> ▪ Other reasons beyond the control of the employee, and
>
> ▪ The appointing authority explicitly directs that the period shall count for increment.
>
> 3\. Leave on medical certificate duly approved by the competent authority is automatically eligible for increment counting.

**3. Performance-Linked Increment System**

**(d) Annual Increment Based on Performance Appraisal**

*“Annual increment shall be based on performance appraisal.”*

- Increments shall be linked to the Annual Performance Appraisal Report (APAR) or Performance-Based Assessment (PBAS) score of the employee.

- The performance criteria may include:

<!-- -->

- Teaching/Research Output (for faculty): Publications, feedback, innovation, mentoring, etc.

- Administrative Efficiency (for staff): Timely task completion, punctuality, discipline.

- Institutional Contribution: Participation in committees, community outreach, and accreditation activities.

**4. Procedure for Grant of Increment**

> 1\. **Preparation of Increment List:**

- HRD Department prepares an annual list of employees due for increment on 1st July or 1st January (as decided by the BoM).

> 2\. **Performance Evaluation:**

- Each employee's Appraisal Form is reviewed and approved by:

> ▪ HOD / Dean for faculty, or
>
> ▪ Section Head / Registrar for non-teaching staff.
>
> 3\. **Verification and Approval:**

- The list is verified by the Accounts Section and submitted to the Registrar for approval.

- For faculty, final approval may rest with the Vice-Chancellor / BoM.

> 4\. **Increment Entry:**

- After approval, increments are entered in the Service Register and reflected in the salary bill of the succeeding month.

**5. Postponement or Withholding of Increment**

An employee's increment may be withheld, postponed, or forfeited under the following conditions:

> 1\. Unsatisfactory performance or low appraisal rating.
>
> 2\. Involvement in disciplinary proceedings or misconduct.
>
> 3\. Violation of University Code of Conduct.
>
> 4\. Unauthorized absence or habitual late attendance.

- The authority must record written reasons and communicate the decision formally.

- On improvement of performance, the increment may be restored prospectively (not retrospectively).

**6. Increment Dates and Effectivity**

- Normal date of increment shall be 1st July every year (or 1st January for certain categories).

- If an employee joins service after 1st January but before 30th June, the first increment becomes due on the following 1st July after completing 12 months of qualifying service.

- Employees joining between 1st July and 31st December will receive the first increment on next 1st January.

**43. Withholding of Increment Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the rules and procedures governing the withholding of increments as a disciplinary measure.

- This policy ensures that any action involving salary progression restrictions is implemented with due process, transparency, and fairness, in line with University disciplinary and service conduct regulations.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *“When an increment of an employee is withheld as a disciplinary measure, the authority ordering the withholding of the increment shall state the period for which the increment shall be withheld and whether it shall have the effect of postponing the future increments, i.e., with or without cumulative effect. When an increment is withheld for a certain period, this period shall be exclusive of any interval spent on leave before the period is completed.”*

- Accordingly, withholding of increment shall be treated as a formal disciplinary penalty and not a routine administrative action.

**2. Circumstances for Withholding an Increment**

- The competent authority may withhold an increment only:

> 1\. After a disciplinary proceeding under the University's Code of Conduct and Disciplinary Policy.
>
> 2\. When the employee is found guilty of:
>
> ▪ Misconduct, negligence, or insubordination.
>
> ▪ Habitual late attendance or absenteeism.
>
> ▪ Breach of confidentiality or dereliction of duty.
>
> ▪ Non-performance or violation of institutional rules.
>
> 3\. On recommendation of the Disciplinary / Inquiry Committee, duly approved by the Vice-Chancellor or Board of Management (BoM).

**3. Procedure for Imposing the Penalty**

> 1\. **Issue of Charge Memo:**

- The employee shall be informed in writing of the charge(s) and given an opportunity to explain.

> 2\. **Inquiry and Representation:**

- A Disciplinary Committee shall conduct a fair hearing and submit a report.

- The employee's written defense shall be duly considered.

> 3\. **Decision and Order:**

- Based on the inquiry findings, the competent authority may order withholding of increment.

- The order must clearly specify:

> ▪ The duration of withholding (e.g., 6 months, 1 year).
>
> ▪ Whether the penalty is with cumulative effect (affecting future increments) or without cumulative effect (increment restored after penalty period).
>
> 4\. **Communication:**

- The decision must be communicated to the employee in writing, with a copy placed in the Service Register.

**4. Types of Withholding Effects**

| **Type**                  | **Description**                                                       | **Impact on Future Increments**                                       |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Without Cumulative Effect | Increment withheld temporarily for a defined period                   | Employee resumes normal increment cycle after the penalty period ends |
| With Cumulative Effect    | Increment withheld permanently; next increments postponed accordingly | Permanent reduction in pay progression; future increments delayed     |

- The order must explicitly mention the type of effect, failing which it shall be deemed without cumulative effect.

**5. Exclusion of Leave Period**

- If an employee is on any type of leave during the penalty period:

<!-- -->

- That leave period shall not be counted toward completion of the penalty duration.

- The penalty period will resume after the employee rejoins duty and continue until the full specified duration is served.

**6. Review and Restoration of Increment**

- Upon completion of the penalty period:

<!-- -->

- The next increment shall be sanctioned only after verification of satisfactory conduct and performance post-penalty.

- In exceptional cases, based on strong performance improvement, the Vice-Chancellor may recommend early restoration of the increment (without arrears).

<!-- -->

- A record of restoration shall be updated in the Service Book and reflected in the subsequent salary bill.

**7. Record and Documentation**

|---------------------------------------------|-----------------------------|

| Increment Withholding Order                 | HRD / Accounts              |

**44. Reduction of Pay in a Time-Scale Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To establish clear rules and procedures governing the reduction of pay in a time-scale as a disciplinary measure within the University.

- This policy ensures that such actions are carried out lawfully, transparently, and proportionately, maintaining fairness in employee discipline while upholding institutional integrity.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *“When the pay of a member is reduced by a competent authority to a lower stage in time-scale, that authority shall specify in the order the period for which such reduction shall be effective, and the period shall be exclusive of any interval spent on leave.”*

- Thus, when an employee's pay is reduced, it must be done by a competent authority, with explicit mention of the duration and nature of the reduction, ensuring the employee's rights and the University's disciplinary framework remain balanced.

**2. Nature of Reduction in Pay**

- Reduction in pay in a time-scale is considered a major disciplinary penalty under the University's Code of Conduct and Disciplinary Policy.

- It involves the placement of an employee at a lower pay stage than the one previously held, within the same time-scale of pay, for a specified period.

**3. Conditions Governing Pay Reduction**

> 1\. The competent authority (Registrar, Vice-Chancellor, or BoM depending on the employee's cadre) must:

- Specify the lower stage of pay to which the employee's salary is reduced.

- Clearly mention the duration of such reduction.

- State whether, on completion of the period, the pay shall:

> ▪ Automatically revert to the original stage, or
>
> ▪ Require fresh orders for restoration.
>
> 2\. The period of reduction shall not include any interval spent on leave, ensuring that the disciplinary duration is effectively served in duty.

**4. Types of Pay Reduction**

| **Type of Reduction**                | **Description**                                                                               | **Effect on Future Increments**                  |
|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Temporary Reduction (Non-Cumulative) | Pay reduced to a lower stage for a fixed period. On expiry, original pay is restored.         | Does not affect future increments once restored. |
| Permanent Reduction (Cumulative)     | Pay reduced permanently to a lower stage, and future increments are based on the reduced pay. | Has a long-term impact on pay progression.       |

• The order must explicitly state whether the reduction is *temporary (non-cumulative)* or *permanent (cumulative)*.

**5. Procedure for Imposition of Penalty**

> 1\. **Preliminary Action:**

- Initiation of disciplinary proceedings through a show-cause notice or charge memo.

> 2\. **Inquiry Process:**

- A Disciplinary / Inquiry Committee shall conduct a fair hearing.

- The employee shall be given full opportunity to present evidence and defense.

> 3\. **Recommendation:**

- The committee submits its findings to the competent authority.

> 4\. **Decision and Order:**

- The authority, after due consideration, may order reduction of pay, specifying:

> ▪ The exact stage of reduction (e.g., from ₹56,100 to ₹54,500).
>
> ▪ The period of effectiveness (e.g., 1 year, 2 years).
>
> ▪ Whether it is with or without cumulative effect.
>
> 5\. **Communication:**

- The final order must be issued in writing, with a copy placed in the employee's Service Register and communicated to the Accounts and HRD Departments.

**6. Effect of Leave During Penalty Period**

- If the employee proceeds on any type of leave during the reduction period:

<!-- -->

- The leave duration shall not count towards completion of the penalty period.

- The reduction period resumes only after the employee rejoins duty.

**7. Restoration of Pay**

- On completion of the penalty period:

> 1\. The employee's pay shall be restored to the stage held before the reduction, unless the order states otherwise.
>
> 2\. Restoration shall take effect prospectively, without retrospective financial benefit for the penalty period.
>
> 3\. The employee's next increment shall fall due after completing 12 months of qualifying service from the date of restoration.
>
> 4\. In cases of good conduct and satisfactory performance post-penalty, the authority may recommend early restoration to the Board of Management.

**49. Explanatory Provisions on Disciplinary and Service Actions**

**I. Purpose**

- To clarify the administrative and disciplinary distinctions among actions such as withholding of increments, non-promotion, and reversion to lower posts or grades.

- These explanations ensure uniform interpretation and procedural consistency in applying service rules and penalties.

**1. Withholding of Increment**

- Explanation I:

> *“Withholding of increment of an employee may arise on account of his work being found unsatisfactory or not being of the required standard, or for failure to pass a prescribed test or examination.”*

- Key Points:

<!-- -->

- This action may be administrative or disciplinary, depending on cause.

- Reasons include:

> ▪ Persistent poor performance.
>
> ▪ Failure to achieve required productivity or academic standards.
>
> ▪ Non-clearance of mandatory exams, tests, or certifications prescribed by University / UGC / AICTE.

- The competent authority must:

> ▪ Provide written intimation,
>
> ▪ Specify whether the withholding is with or without cumulative effect, and
>
> ▪ Ensure the employee is given an opportunity to improve before finalizing the action.

- Nature:

<!-- -->

- Considered a minor penalty when imposed as part of disciplinary proceedings.

- Considered an administrative decision when linked to performance evaluation.

**2. Non-Promotion**

- Explanation II:

> *“Non-promotion may take place, whether in an officiating capacity or otherwise, of an employee to a higher post for which he may be eligible for consideration but for which he is found unsuitable after consideration of his case.”*

- Key Points:

<!-- -->

- Promotion is not a right, but an opportunity based on merit and suitability.

- Even if an employee meets eligibility criteria, promotion may be withheld if:

> ▪ Performance, conduct, or integrity reports are below standard.
>
> ▪ The employee has an adverse entry or pending disciplinary case.
>
> ▪ The Selection / Screening Committee finds the employee unsuitable for higher responsibility.

- The non-promotion decision must be recorded with clear justification in the selection minutes.

<!-- -->

- Nature:

<!-- -->

- Treated as an administrative consequence, not a penalty, unless specifically declared as a disciplinary action following misconduct.

**3. Reversion to a Lower Grade or Post (During Officiation)**

- Explanation III:

> *“Reversion to a lower grade or post, of an employee officiating in a higher grade or post may be done on the ground that he is considered, after an enquiry, to be unsuitable for such higher grade or post or on administrative grounds unconnected with his conduct.”*

- Key Points:

<!-- -->

- Applies to employees holding officiating or temporary charge of higher posts.

- Reversion may occur for:

> ▪ Unsuitability determined through a formal or departmental inquiry.
>
> ▪ Administrative reorganization, redundancy, or restructuring, not linked to misconduct.

- Such reversion does not necessarily imply punishment if unconnected to conduct.

- If imposed after an inquiry for misconduct or incompetence, it is treated as a major penalty.

<!-- -->

- Nature:

<!-- -->

- May be disciplinary (penalty) if misconduct is established.

- May be administrative (non-penal) if due to organizational restructuring or unsuitability without blame.

**4. Reversion During or After Probation**

- Explanation IV:

> *“Reversion to his previous grade or post, of an employee appointed on probation to another grade or post may be done during or at the end of the period of probation, in accordance with the terms of his appointment.”*

- Key Points:

<!-- -->

- Employees appointed on probation do not have a vested right to confirmation.

- Reversion may occur if:

> ▪ Performance during probation is unsatisfactory.
>
> ▪ The employee fails to meet expected standards or conduct requirements.
>
> ▪ The probationary assessment report recommends non-confirmation.

- Such reversion is not considered a penalty but an administrative outcome of the probationary process.

<!-- -->

- Nature:

<!-- -->

- Treated as a normal administrative decision, not a disciplinary action, provided it is in accordance with the appointment terms and review process.

| Non-Promotion                   | Screening or Selection Committee recommendation | BoM / VC                | Administrative                  |
| Reversion from Officiating Post | Formal inquiry or administrative order          | Registrar / VC / BoM    | May be Major Penalty            |

**7. Policy Principles**

**50. Termination of Services Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the rules and consequences of termination of services arising from the imposition of major disciplinary penalties such as removal or dismissal from service.

- This policy ensures that all forms of service termination are executed in accordance with due process, preserving both institutional integrity and procedural fairness.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *“When a major penalty of either removal from service or dismissal from service is imposed, the services of the employee shall stand terminated with immediate effect.”*

- Termination due to disciplinary action supersedes all other service conditions, contracts, or tenure agreements, and takes effect from the date mentioned in the final penalty order issued by the competent authority.

> ▪ Date of effect,
>
> ▪ Grounds for termination, and

> ▪ Consequences on pay, benefits, and service records.

> 5\. **Communication:**

>
