**52. Procedure for Imposition of Penalties (Enquiry and Opportunity to be Heard)**

**I. Purpose**

- To establish a uniform and fair procedure for the imposition of disciplinary penalties on University employees.

- This policy ensures that no penalty is imposed arbitrarily, and every employee is provided an adequate opportunity to defend against any charge or proposed disciplinary action.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *“Penalties specified above shall be imposed by the competent authority after an enquiry has been held and the member of the staff has been given an opportunity of showing cause against the action proposed to be taken in regard to him/her.”*

• This rule embodies the principle of natural justice — *audi alteram partem* (no one shall be condemned unheard).

- It applies to both minor and major penalties, though the procedure and extent of enquiry may vary depending on the gravity of the charge.

**2. Mandatory Procedural Requirements**

Before imposing any disciplinary penalty, the following steps must be followed:

**Step 1: Issue of Charge Sheet / Show Cause Notice**

- The employee shall be served with a written charge sheet or show cause notice clearly stating:

<!-- -->

- The specific allegations or acts of misconduct.

- The evidence or material facts relied upon.

- The proposed action or penalty (if any).

<!-- -->

- The employee shall be granted reasonable time (normally 7–15 working days) to submit a written explanation or defense.

**Step 2: Examination of Explanation**

- The competent authority shall examine the employee's reply to determine whether:

<!-- -->

- The explanation is satisfactory, or

- A formal enquiry is warranted.

<!-- -->

- If the explanation is found unsatisfactory, the authority shall record written reasons and proceed to the next stage.

**Step 3: Constitution of Enquiry Committee**

- For major penalties, a Disciplinary / Enquiry Committee shall be constituted, consisting of:

> 1\. A Chairperson (senior faculty or administrative officer).
>
> 2\. One Member Secretary (HRD / Registrar's nominee).
>
> 3\. One or more independent or external members, where necessary, to ensure impartiality.

- The Committee shall be empowered to summon witnesses, review documents, and conduct hearings.

**Step 4: Conduct of Enquiry**

- The enquiry must be fair, transparent, and documented.

- The employee shall be:

<!-- -->

- Informed of the date, time, and venue of the enquiry.

- Allowed to present oral or written defense, evidence, and witnesses.

- Permitted to cross-examine witnesses produced by the management, where appropriate.

<!-- -->

- All proceedings must be recorded in writing, signed by the Committee members, and kept in official custody.

**Step 5: Submission of Enquiry Report**

- The Enquiry Committee shall prepare a written report containing:

<!-- -->

- A summary of proceedings,

- Findings on each charge,

- Recommendations on whether the charges are proved or not.

<!-- -->

- The report shall be submitted to the competent authority (Vice-Chancellor / Registrar) for final consideration.

**Step 6: Issue of Show Cause on Proposed Penalty (If Required)**

- If the competent authority, after examining the report, intends to impose a major penalty, the employee shall be given a final opportunity to show cause against the proposed action within a specified period (usually 7 days).

**Step 7: Final Order**

- After considering:

<!-- -->

- The enquiry report,

- The employee's final explanation, and

- Relevant evidence,

> the competent authority shall issue a speaking order specifying:

- The type of penalty imposed,

- The effective date, and

- The reasons and findings leading to the decision.

<!-- -->

- The order must be communicated in writing to the employee and copied to HRD, Accounts, and Legal Sections.

**3. Distinction in Procedure**

| **Type of Penalty** | **Enquiry Requirement**                              | **Nature of Opportunity to be Given**         |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Minor Penalty       | Simplified enquiry / written explanation may suffice | Written opportunity to defend against notice  |
| Major Penalty       | Formal enquiry by a duly constituted committee       | Full opportunity for oral and written defense |

**4. Rights of the Employee**

Every employee facing disciplinary action shall have the right to:

1\. Be informed of the specific charges in writing.

2\. Access all relevant evidence or records used in framing the charges.

3\. Defend themselves, personally or through a representative.

4\. Cross-examine witnesses and produce counter-evidence.

5\. Receive a copy of the final order and be informed of their right to appeal.

**5. Principles of Natural Justice**

All enquiries and disciplinary actions shall strictly adhere to the following principles:

- No one shall be condemned unheard.

- No one shall be a judge in their own cause.

- Decisions must be based on evidence and reasoned conclusions.

- All records must be maintained in writing to ensure accountability and transparency.

**6. Documentation and Record Management**

| **Document Type**                    | **Maintained By**            |
|--------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Charge Sheet / Show Cause Notice     | Registrar / HRD              |
| Enquiry Committee Constitution Order | Registrar                    |
| Enquiry Proceedings and Minutes      | Member Secretary / HRD       |
| Enquiry Report                       | Chairperson of the Committee |
| Final Penalty Order                  | Vice-Chancellor / Registrar  |
| Appeal Record (if any)               | BoM Secretariat / Legal Cell |

All documentation shall be preserved for at least 15 years for legal, audit, and accreditation purposes.

**7. Policy Principles**

- No disciplinary penalty shall be imposed without due enquiry and opportunity to be heard.

- Every stage of the disciplinary process must be documented and approved by the competent authority.

- The University shall uphold fairness, transparency, and impartiality in all disciplinary proceedings.

- This rule ensures compliance with UGC and labor law guidelines, reinforcing institutional integrity and procedural justice.

**53. Appeal and Review Policy in Disciplinary Matters**

**I. Purpose**

- To outline the rights and procedures available to University employees for appealing or seeking review of disciplinary penalties imposed by the competent authority.

- This policy ensures that every disciplinary decision is subject to fair appellate oversight, maintaining justice, transparency, and institutional accountability.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *“A member of the staff aggrieved by any order imposing a penalty passed by the competent authority against him shall be entitled to prefer an appeal to a higher authority, i.e., the Appellate Authority. The decision of the appellate authority is final. The employee may apply for a review of the order to the same authority if there is any fresh evidence or fact that is relevant to the case.”*

- Accordingly:

<!-- -->

- Every employee has the right to appeal any disciplinary order imposing a minor or major penalty.

- After the appeal is decided, the employee may also request a review, but only if new evidence or material facts have emerged.

**2. Right of Appeal**

| **Category**       | **Right**                                                                                                                                |
|--------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Eligible Employees | All teaching, non-teaching, technical, and administrative staff of the University.                                                       |
| Scope of Appeal    | Applies to all penalties — censure, withholding of increment/promotion, reduction in rank, compulsory retirement, removal, or dismissal. |
| Grounds of Appeal  | May include errors of fact, procedural irregularity, disproportionality of punishment, or emergence of new evidence.                     |

**3. Appellate Authority Structure**

| **Nature of Penalty**                                        | **Appellate / Reviewing Authority**              |
|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Minor or Major Penalties imposed by Vice-Chancellor          | Board of Management (BoM)                        |
| Minor Penalties imposed by Registrar / Dean (Administration) | Vice-Chancellor                                  |
| Orders issued by Board of Management                         | Chancellor / Governing Authority (if applicable) |

The decision of the Appellate Authority shall be final and binding on both the employee and the University.

**4. Procedure for Appeal**

**Step 1: Filing of Appeal**

- The appeal shall be submitted in writing by the employee within 30 calendar days from the date of receipt of the penalty order.

- It shall be addressed to the Registrar, who will forward it to the Appellate Authority along with the complete disciplinary file.

- The appeal must contain:

<!-- -->

- Reference to the penalty order and date,

- Grounds of appeal,

- Any supporting documents or evidence, and

- The relief sought.

**Step 2: Preliminary Scrutiny**

- The Registrar shall verify whether:

<!-- -->

- The appeal is within the time limit,

- It is properly drafted, and

- All relevant documents are enclosed.

**Step 3: Consideration by the Appellate Authority**

- The Appellate Authority may:

<!-- -->

- Examine the records of the disciplinary case,

- Seek additional clarifications from the competent authority or the employee,

- Grant a personal hearing if deemed necessary, and

- Decide whether to uphold, modify, or set aside the penalty.

**Step 4: Decision and Communication**

- The decision of the appellate authority shall be recorded in writing, specifying:

<!-- -->

- Whether the appeal is accepted, partly allowed, or rejected, and

- The reasons for the decision.

<!-- -->

- The decision shall be communicated to the employee in writing, and a copy filed in the Service Record.

**5. Review of Appellate Order**

- After the appellate authority has passed its decision, the employee may apply for a review only if:

> 1\. Fresh evidence has come to light which could not reasonably have been produced earlier; or
>
> 2\. There are material errors of fact apparent on record that are relevant to the case.

- The review petition must be submitted to the same appellate authority within 60 days from the date of the appellate decision.

- The appellate authority shall examine the new material and may:

> 1\. Confirm the previous decision,
>
> 2\. Modify or reduce the penalty, or
>
> 3\. Reopen the case if justified by substantial new evidence.

- The decision on review shall be final and conclusive.

**6. Documentation and Record Management**

| **Document Type**            | **Maintained By**             |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Original Penalty Order       | Registrar / HRD               |
| Appeal Application           | Registrar                     |
| Appellate Decision Record    | BoM Secretariat / VC's Office |
| Review Petition and Decision | Registrar / Legal Cell        |
| Service Book Updates         | HRD Department                |

All appeal and review records must be retained for at least 15 years for audit and compliance.

**7. Summary Table**

| **Clause**                        | **Provision**                                       | **Authority**            |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Appeal Filing Right               | Employee may appeal within 30 days of penalty order | Appellate Authority      |
| Appellate Authority for VC Orders | Board of Management                                 | BoM                      |
| Review Application                | Allowed only if new evidence or facts arise         | Same Appellate Authority |
| Decision Finality                 | Appellate / Reviewing Authority decision is final   | BoM / VC                 |
| Record Maintenance                | HRD, Registrar, Legal Section                       | Registrar                |

**8. Policy Principles**

- The University guarantees every employee the right to a fair appeal and review process.

- The appellate process ensures that disciplinary actions are transparent, impartial, and proportionate.

- Appeals and reviews must be decided based on facts, evidence, and procedural fairness, not personal bias.

- The finality of appellate decisions ensures closure, accountability, and administrative efficiency.

- This framework upholds the spirit of natural justice and ensures consistency with UGC, AICTE, and labor law standards.

**54. Suspension, Subsistence Allowance, and Reinstatement Policy**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the procedures, conditions, and entitlements related to suspension of employees, subsistence allowance, and treatment of suspension period within the University.

- This policy ensures disciplinary fairness, financial propriety, and institutional transparency in all suspension-related matters.

**1. Authority for Suspension**

- The Vice-Chancellor (V.C.) is the competent authority to place any University employee under suspension in accordance with the conditions outlined below.

- Suspension may be applied in the interest of the University to ensure fair enquiry, prevent interference with investigation, or safeguard institutional reputation.

**2. Circumstances for Suspension**

| **Clause** | **Grounds for Suspension**                       | **Description**                                                                                                                                                                                            |
|------------|--------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| \(a\)      | Pending or Contemplated Disciplinary Proceedings | When disciplinary action is being contemplated or is pending against the employee.                                                                                                                         |
| \(b\)      | Criminal Investigation or Trial                  | When a case involving a criminal offence is under investigation or trial.                                                                                                                                  |
| \(c\)      | Custody Exceeding 48 Hours                       | An employee detained in custody for more than 48 hours (for any reason) shall be deemed suspended automatically from the date of detention. Suspension continues until revoked by the competent authority. |
| \(d\)      | Conviction for an Offence                        | An employee convicted of an offence and sentenced to imprisonment exceeding 48 hours shall be deemed suspended from the date of conviction.                                                                |
| \(e\)      | Reinstatement Following Appeal or Review         | If dismissal/removal is set aside on appeal and case remitted for further action, the original suspension shall continue from the date of initial dismissal until further orders.                          |
| \(f\)      | Court-Declared Void Dismissal or Re-Inquiry      | If dismissal is annulled by a court and a re-inquiry is ordered, the employee shall be deemed suspended from the original dismissal date until further orders.                                             |
| \(g\)      | Revocation of Suspension                         | Any suspension (made or deemed) may be revoked at any time by the appellate authority.                                                                                                                     |

**3. Subsistence Allowance During Suspension**

| **Provision** | **Details**                                                                                                                                      |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| \(1\)         | During the suspension period, the employee is entitled to a subsistence allowance at 50% of the basic pay drawn on the date of suspension.       |
| \(2\)         | No subsistence allowance shall be paid if the employee is arrested by police or detained under criminal charges.                                 |
| \(3\)         | Subsistence allowance is payable only if the employee does not engage in any other employment or business activity during the suspension period. |
| \(4\)         | The payment of subsistence allowance shall be reviewed every 90 days, subject to satisfactory cooperation in the enquiry.                        |

**4. Engagement in Outside Work During Suspension**

- If, during suspension, an employee is found to be engaged in employment with another organization or undertaking paid work:

<!-- -->

- His/her suspension shall automatically result in termination,

- Subsistence allowance shall cease immediately, and

- The employee's name shall be struck off the University rolls.

**5. Treatment of the Period of Suspension**

**I. When Employee is Exonerated / Found Not Guilty**

- If the employee is fully exonerated and reinstated without any penalty:

> a\) He/she shall be entitled to full pay and allowances as if not suspended, minus the subsistence already paid.

**II. When Employee is Found Guilty / Penalized but Reinstated**

- If the employee is reinstated after a penalty, the following apply:

> a\) He/she shall not receive any additional pay or allowance beyond the subsistence allowance already paid.
>
> b\) The suspension period shall be treated as absence, not as duty.
>
> c\) The service continuity during that period may be withheld depending on the nature of penalty.

**6. Reinstatement Procedure**

- The order of reinstatement shall be issued by the Vice-Chancellor or the Appointing Authority, specifying:

<!-- -->

- The effective date of reinstatement,

- The treatment of suspension period, and

- The financial adjustments (if any) to be made.

<!-- -->

- The employee shall report to duty within the time frame mentioned in the reinstatement order, failing which it may be treated as voluntary abandonment of service.

**7. Revocation and Review of Suspension**

- Suspension may be revoked by:

<!-- -->

- The Vice-Chancellor (competent authority), or

- The Appellate Authority (BoM) on review.

<!-- -->

- The authority may review the suspension at any time based on the progress of enquiry, investigation outcome, or new evidence.

**8. Interpretation and Final Authority**

- Any doubt or dispute regarding the interpretation of these rules shall be referred to the Board of Management (BoM).

- The decision of the BoM shall be final and binding on all parties.

**9. Record Management**

| **Document Type**                            | **Maintained By**       |
|----------------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Suspension Order                             | Registrar / HRD         |
| Subsistence Allowance Register               | Accounts Department     |
| Enquiry and Reinstatement Orders             | Registrar               |
| Review / Revocation Orders                   | BoM Secretariat         |
| Payroll Adjustments and Service Book Entries | HRD & Accounts Sections |

All records must be preserved for at least 15 years for institutional and legal reference.

**10. Policy Principles**

- Suspension is not a punishment, but a preventive measure to ensure fair enquiry.

- During suspension, the employee retains lien on post, unless terminated under disciplinary or criminal proceedings.

- Subsistence allowance is intended for minimum livelihood support, not a substitute for salary.

- Reinstatement is subject to the outcome of the enquiry and administrative review.

- All actions must be documented to ensure transparency, procedural fairness, and audit compliance.

**55. Exceptions to Enquiry Procedure in Disciplinary Cases**

**I. Purpose**

- To define specific exceptions under which the University may impose major disciplinary penalties (such as dismissal, removal, or reduction in rank) without conducting a formal enquiry, in situations where holding such an enquiry is impractical or unnecessary.

- This policy ensures that disciplinary powers are exercised efficiently and lawfully, while maintaining procedural integrity through recorded justification.

**1. General Rule**

- Clause:

> *“Notwithstanding the above provisions, it shall not be necessary to follow the procedure mentioned above in the following cases.”*

- This clause serves as a special provision that allows the competent authority to act decisively in exceptional circumstances, where normal enquiry procedures are not feasible or warranted.

**2. Exceptions to Enquiry Requirement**

| **Clause** | **Situation / Condition**                                                                                                            | **Description and Justification**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
|------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| \(a\)      | When an employee is dismissed, removed, or reduced in rank on grounds of conduct that led to his conviction on a criminal charge.    | If an employee has been convicted by a court of law for an offense involving moral turpitude, corruption, fraud, violence, or other serious misconduct, the University need not conduct a separate departmental enquiry. The conviction itself serves as conclusive evidence of guilt, and disciplinary action may be taken based on the judgment. The penalty (dismissal, removal, or reduction in rank) shall be imposed after recording the conviction details and approval by the competent authority.                                                                                                                                                  |
| \(b\)      | When the competent authority is satisfied that it is not practicable to give the employee a reasonable opportunity of showing cause. | In certain circumstances, holding a regular enquiry may be impossible or impractical, such as: 1. When the employee absconds or is untraceable for a prolonged period. 2. When witnesses are intimidated or unable to appear due to threats or exceptional situations. 3. When the safety, security, or reputation of the institution is at risk. In such cases, the authority empowered to dismiss or remove the employee may proceed with disciplinary action without a formal enquiry, provided that: The reasons for impracticability are recorded in writing, and The order is issued by the competent disciplinary authority (Vice-Chancellor / BoM). |

**3. Mandatory Conditions for Exercise of Exception**

1\. The competent authority must record in writing:

- The specific reasons for dispensing with the enquiry, and

- The facts or circumstances justifying the immediate disciplinary action.

2\. The decision must be made objectively and supported by documentary evidence (e.g., court conviction, police report, or institutional correspondence).

3\. The decision and supporting documents must be placed on official record for audit and legal scrutiny.

**4. Finality of Decision**

- Note (as per Rule):

> *“If any question arises whether it is practicable to give to any person a reasonable opportunity of showing cause under clause (b) above, the decision thereon of the authority empowered to dismiss or remove such person or to reduce him in rank, as the case may be, shall be final.”*

- This means:

<!-- -->

- The decision of the competent authority (e.g., Vice-Chancellor or BoM) regarding the impracticability of enquiry is final and binding.

- Such decision shall not be questioned or reopened unless proven to have been made in bad faith or without recorded justification.

**5. Competent Authority**

| **Category of Employee**               | **Authority to Impose Penalty under this Exception** | **Authority for Record Verification / Review** |
|----------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Teaching and Academic Staff            | Vice-Chancellor                                      | Board of Management                            |
| Non-Teaching and Technical Staff       | Registrar / Vice-Chancellor                          | Board of Management                            |
| Administrative / Contractual Employees | Vice-Chancellor / Appointing Authority               | BoM                                            |

**6. Record and Documentation**

| **Document Type**                                   | **Maintained By**           |
|-----------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Certified Copy of Criminal Conviction / Judgment    | Legal Section / Registrar   |
| Written Order Recording Impracticability of Enquiry | Vice-Chancellor / Registrar |
| Final Dismissal or Removal Order                    | HRD / Legal Section         |
| BoM Review Record (if applicable)                   | BoM Secretariat             |
| Service Book Closure Entry                          | HRD Department              |

All such records shall be preserved for a minimum of 15 years for audit, legal, and institutional verification.

**7. Policy Principles**

- This provision is exceptional and should be exercised only when absolutely necessary.

- The competent authority must record detailed reasons justifying the bypassing of normal enquiry procedure.

- The decision to dispense with enquiry does not exempt the authority from ensuring fairness and documentation.

- All such cases shall be reported to the Board of Management for information and post-facto review.

- The University upholds accountability, procedural integrity, and legal compliance even in exceptional disciplinary situations.

- The policy aligns with UGC, AICTE, and NEP 2020 guidelines for higher education faculty workload and performance assessment.

**1. Introduction and Objective**

- Leave Rules constitute an essential component of the institution's administrative framework to ensure:

<!-- -->

- Employee welfare through regulated time-off provisions.

- Institutional discipline and operational continuity.

- Transparency and fairness in granting and recording leave.

<!-- -->

- Objectives include:

<!-- -->

- Providing a uniform mechanism for sanctioning and monitoring leave.

- Balancing personal needs with institutional exigencies.

**2. Applicability**

- Applicable to all employees — teaching, non-teaching, administrative, and technical staff.

- Covers all employment categories — permanent, probationary, ad-hoc, and contractual.

- Applies to all types of leave:

<!-- -->

- Casual Leave (CL)

- Earned Leave (EL)

- Medical Leave (ML)

- Maternity/Paternity Leave

- Study Leave

- Duty Leave and Special Leave

**3. Fundamental Principles**

- Leave Not a Matter of Right:

<!-- -->

- Leave cannot be claimed as a matter of right.

- When exigencies of service so require, the competent authority reserves the discretion to refuse, curtail, or revoke leave of any description.

<!-- -->

- Authority Cannot Alter Nature of Leave:

<!-- -->

- The type of leave due and applied for by an employee cannot be altered at the discretion of the sanctioning authority.

- The authority may refuse or revoke leave, but not change its category.

<!-- -->

- Leave Account Maintenance:

<!-- -->

- A Leave Account shall be maintained for each employee in the prescribed institutional format.

- HR/Establishment Section shall update leave balances annually and ensure audit compliance.

<!-- -->

- Recall from Leave or Vacation:

<!-- -->

- The competent authority may recall an employee from leave or vacation in case of institutional necessity.

- The employee must return to duty at his/her own cost.

<!-- -->

- Leave During Notice Period:

<!-- -->

- Employees serving the notice period for resignation are not permitted to avail any kind of leave except casual leave.

<!-- -->

- Combination of Leave:

<!-- -->

- Any type of leave, except casual leave, may be combined or continued with other types of leave under these rules.

<!-- -->

- Leave Before Retirement:

<!-- -->

- No leave shall be granted beyond the date of compulsory retirement, except when applied well in advance and specifically sanctioned.

**4. Duty and Leave Entitlement**

- Leave is Earned by Duty Only:

<!-- -->

- Leave entitlement accrues based on periods of actual duty performed.

> o For the purpose of leave calculation, “duty” includes:
>
> a\. Any period of absence on Casual Leave and Special Casual Leave.
>
> b\. Any period of gazetted holidays, whether prefixed, suffixed, or in combination with casual leave.
>
> c\. Any period of absence during declared vacation when prefixed or suffixed to sanctioned leave.
>
> d\. Any continuous period actually spent on duty.

- Definition of Vacation:

<!-- -->

- “Vacation” refers to an officially declared recess of 7 days or more during which employees are permitted to be absent from duty with full pay and allowances.

<!-- -->

- Vacation Staff:

<!-- -->

- All regular Teaching and Non-Teaching staff (except contingency staff) in teaching departments are considered vacation staff.

**5. Holidays Prefixed and Suffixed to Leave**

- Unless otherwise directed:

> a\. If holidays are prefixed to leave, re-arrangement of pay and allowances takes effect from the first day after the holidays.
>
> b\. If holidays are suffixed to leave, re-arrangement takes effect from the day on which leave would have ended if the holidays had not been suffixed.

- No employee on leave shall take up any service or accept employment elsewhere without prior written sanction of the appointing authority.

**60. Types of Leave and Eligibility Criteria**

**1. Introduction**

- This section defines the various categories of leave, their eligibility, quantum, and rules of sanction for all categories of employees.

- The intent is to ensure uniformity, transparency, and compliance with institutional, UGC, and AICTE regulations.

- All leaves are subject to the approval of competent authorities as per the Leave Sanctioning Matrix.

**Earned Leave (EL)**

**Purpose**

- Granted to employees for rest, recuperation, or personal commitments requiring long-duration absence.

- It promotes work-life balance and ensures staff well-being without affecting institutional operations.

**Eligibility**

- Only regular staff members who are not vacation staff (both teaching and non-teaching) are eligible for earned leave.

- Vacation staff (faculty enjoying semester or annual vacations) are not ordinarily eligible for earned leave except under specific administrative orders.

**Rules Governing Earned Leave**

a\. For Employees in Teaching Service:

- Earned Leave shall accrue at the rate of one-eleventh (1/11) of the period spent on duty.

b\. For Employees in Non-Teaching Service:

- i\. One twenty-second (1/22) of the period spent on duty during the first ten years of service.

- ii\. One sixteenth (1/16) of the period spent on duty during the next ten years of service.

- iii\. One eleventh (1/11) of the period spent on duty thereafter.

**Additional Provisions**

- An employee shall cease to earn earned leave when the total accumulated leave exceeds 180 days.

- The maximum earned leave that can be granted at one time shall not exceed 180 days.

- Earned leave can be combined with other admissible leave types such as Half Pay Leave (HPL), Medical Leave (ML), or Maternity Leave, subject to prior sanction.

- Leave must be applied for in advance, and its sanction is at the discretion of the competent authority based on institutional exigencies.

- Encashment of earned leave on retirement, resignation, or separation is admissible as per institutional or government policy.

**Sanctioning Authority**

- Vice-Chancellor (V.C.) is the competent authority to sanction Earned Leave for all eligible employees.

**Summary**

| **Category**       | **Computation Formula**                    | **Accumulation Limit** | **Maximum Leave at One Time** | **Authority**   |
|--------------------|--------------------------------------------|------------------------|-------------------------------|-----------------|
| Teaching Staff     | 1/11 of period on duty                     | 180 days               | 180 days                      | Vice-Chancellor |
| Non-Teaching Staff | 1/22 → 1/16 → 1/11 (as per service period) | 180 days               | 180 days                      | Vice-Chancellor |

**Vacation Leave (VL)**

**Purpose**

- Vacation Leave is designed to provide teaching and eligible non-teaching staff an annual period of rest and recuperation during academic breaks.

- It ensures work-life balance, promotes academic rejuvenation, and allows institutional flexibility in scheduling essential duties.

**Eligibility**

- Applicable primarily to teaching faculty and academic staff who are classified as vacation staff.

- Faculty/Staff on probation are not eligible for vacation.

- Temporary, ad-hoc, contractual, or time-bound employees who have completed two years of continuous service in the University are eligible for thirty (30) days of vacation.

- The Registrar or Competent Authority retains discretionary powers to grant vacation based on institutional convenience and operational requirements.

**Rules Governing Vacation Leave**

a\. Duration:

- The maximum duration of vacation shall be limited to 60 days in any academic year.

- If a faculty or staff member is retained during the vacation period due to essential institutional work, they will be compensated with equivalent vacation at a later time, as per the convenience of the institution.

b\. Ineligibility During Probation:

- Employees on probation are not entitled to vacation benefits.

c\. Eligibility After Two Years of Service:

- Faculty/Staff (permanent or temporary) who have completed two years of continuous service become eligible for thirty (30) days of vacation in an academic year.

d\. Discretionary Approval:

- The Registrar/Competent Authority may grant or adjust vacation schedules, taking into account institutional convenience, examination duties, or administrative requirements.

e\. Combination with Other Leave:

- Vacation may be combined either as a prefix or a suffix (but not both) with any other kind of leave under these rules.

- The total duration of Vacation and Leave taken together must not exceed 120 days.

f\. Combined Limit with Other Leave Types:

- The total period combining Vacation, Earned Leave, and Commuted Leave shall not exceed 180 days in any case.

g\. Mandatory Presence Rule:

- Every employee must be present either on the day before the vacation begins or on the reopening day after the vacation.

- Absence on both these days disqualifies the employee from drawing pay and allowances for the entire vacation period.

- The competent authority may, in exceptional cases, exempt an employee from this rule based on valid justification.

**Compensation for Retention During Vacation**

- When faculty or staff are retained during the declared vacation for essential duties (e.g., admissions, examinations, audits, or training programs), they shall:

<!-- -->

- Be granted equivalent compensatory vacation leave later, or

- Receive compensatory benefits as determined by the institution.

**Sanctioning Authority**

- Registrar or other Competent Authority designated by the University.

**Half Pay Leave (HPL) and Commuted Leave (CL)**

**Purpose**

- Half Pay Leave (HPL) is intended to support permanent employees who require leave for medical treatment or personal affairs.

- It ensures that employees have access to paid leave (partially or fully) during health or family-related circumstances while maintaining service continuity.

**Eligibility**

- Applicable to all permanent employees after completion of one year of continuous service.

**a. Half Pay Leave (HPL) – Rules and Entitlement**

- i\. Each permanent employee earns 20 days of Half Pay Leave for every completed year of service.

- ii\. The maximum accumulation limit for Half Pay Leave shall be 120 days at any given time.

- iii\. Half Pay Leave may be granted on a medical certificate or for private affairs, provided the leave period is not less than three days at a stretch.

**b. Commuted Leave (on Full Pay)**

- Definition: Commuted Leave allows an employee to take full pay leave by converting eligible Half Pay Leave, based on a medical certificate.

- Conditions of Grant:

<!-- -->

- i\. Commuted Leave during the entire service shall be limited to a maximum of 180 days.

- ii\. When Commuted Leave is granted, twice the number of days shall be debited against the employee's Half Pay Leave account.

- iii\. The combined total of Earned Leave and Commuted Leave shall not exceed 180 days at one time.

- iv\. Commuted Leave shall not be granted unless the sanctioning authority has valid reasons to believe that the employee will return to duty upon expiry of such leave.

**c. Additional Provisions**

- HPL may be combined with Earned Leave (EL), Medical Leave (ML), or Maternity Leave (MTL) if duly sanctioned.

- Leave applications for medical reasons must be accompanied by a medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner.

- Upon resumption, a fitness certificate may be required by the competent authority.

**Leave Without Pay (LWP)**

**Purpose**

- Leave Without Pay (LWP) may be granted in special or unavoidable circumstances when no other type of leave is available or admissible under the rules.

- It provides employees the flexibility to attend to prolonged medical conditions or personal obligations while maintaining their employment status with the University.

**Eligibility**

- Applicable to all categories of employees (teaching and non-teaching) on permanent, probationary, or long-term contractual appointments, subject to approval by the competent authority.

**Rules Governing Leave Without Pay**

a\. On Medical Grounds:

- LWP may be sanctioned when an employee:

<!-- -->

- Has exhausted all admissible Medical Leave and Half Pay Leave, and

- Requires prolonged medical treatment or recovery period, supported by a medical certificate.

<!-- -->

- The employee must submit a formal written application along with all medical documents for verification and approval.

b\. On Personal Grounds or External Assignment:

- LWP may also be granted when an employee:

<!-- -->

- Requests such leave for personal reasons (e.g., family emergency, higher studies, relocation).

- Is invited to serve in another organization (including government, autonomous bodies, or recognized institutions) in a higher capacity with pay and allowances provided by that organization.

<!-- -->

- The authority must be satisfied with the justification before sanctioning such leave.

c\. Effect on Service:

- In both cases mentioned above:

<!-- -->

- \(i\) The employee's total service in the University shall be reduced by the length of the period spent on Leave Without Pay.

- \(ii\) Upon return, the employee's status, emoluments, and seniority shall be restored to the same level as before proceeding on LWP.

d\. LWP for Deputation to Another Organization:

- When LWP is sanctioned to depute an employee to another organization (normally a sister concern or allied institution) where:

<!-- -->

- The other organization pays salary and allowances, and

- The deputation is formally approved by the University,

<!-- -->

- The employee shall be treated as being in continuous service with the University.

- Such deputation period shall count for service, increment, and pensionary benefits, as applicable.

**Conditions and Restrictions**

- LWP will not ordinarily exceed one year at a time; any further extension requires Vice-Chancellor's approval.

- No salary, allowance, or increment shall be drawn during LWP (except in deputation cases as noted above).

- Employees on LWP are not entitled to earn leave, vacation, or service benefits during the period.

- The employee must submit a joining report immediately upon returning from LWP, along with any required documentation.

**Sanctioning Authority**

- Registrar — For regular cases of LWP on medical or personal grounds.

- Vice-Chancellor (V.C.) — For LWP exceeding one year, deputation cases, or exceptional circumstances.

| Ban on Collective Petitions | No employee shall be a signatory to any joint representation or collective petition addressed to University authorities for redressal of grievances or any other matter.                                  |
| Rationale                   | Joint representations may lead to group pressure tactics and disrupt the administrative order or discipline of the institution.                                                                           |
| Acceptable Exception        | Collective submissions may be allowed only when specifically invited by the University (e.g., institutional surveys, policy consultations, or committee nominations).                                     |
| Violation Consequence       | Signing or circulating a joint petition without authorization shall be treated as misconduct. Disciplinary actions may include censure, withholding of increments, or temporary suspension of privileges. |

**3. Grievance Redressal Channels**

- Level 1: Immediate Supervisor / Head of Department

- Level 2: Registrar (for administrative staff) / Dean (for faculty-related matters)

- Level 3: Vice Chancellor

- Level 4: Board of Management (final authority for appeals)

Employees are expected to respect the hierarchy and approach the next level only after due response or delay from the lower authority.

**4. Disciplinary Consequences**

| **Nature of Violation**      | **Illustrative Example**                                                 | **Possible Action**                                      |
|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Bypassing proper channel     | Sending a grievance directly to VC without first informing HOD/Registrar | Written warning or return of application                 |
| Sending advance copies       | Circulating duplicate copies to higher officials                         | Application invalidated / advisory note issued           |
| Joint petitioning            | Submitting group complaint letter                                        | Treated as misconduct; may invite disciplinary inquiry   |
| False or vexatious grievance | Submitting fabricated claims                                             | Censure / loss of eligibility for promotion for a period |

**5. Policy Principles**

- The University encourages fair and transparent grievance handling, but all claims must respect the institutional hierarchy.

- Grievances must be raised individually, formally, and factually, avoiding emotional, political, or collective pressures.

- Employees have a right to appeal against administrative decisions but must do so within procedural limits.

- The Registrar shall maintain a Grievance Register documenting submission dates, actions taken, and resolution outcomes.

- The Board of Management (BoM) shall be the final authority for appeal, interpretation, and enforcement of this policy.

**Policy on Willful Absence from Duty (“Dies Non” Clause)**

**I. Purpose**

- To define the administrative and financial implications of unauthorized or willful absence from duty by any employee of the University.

- To ensure that all employees maintain discipline, regularity, and accountability in attendance and performance.

- To clarify the treatment of such absence in relation to pay, leave, and service continuity.

**1. Definition and Interpretation**

| **Term**                            | **Meaning**                                                                                                                                                                                 |
|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Willful Absence                     | Absence from duty without prior permission or valid justification, including overstaying sanctioned leave, failing to report to duty after vacation, or refusing to perform assigned tasks. |
| Dies Non (Latin: “day not counted”) | A day or period during which an employee is absent from duty without authorization, resulting in no pay, no leave, and no service credit for that duration.                                 |

**2. Administrative Provision**

| **Clause**   | **Provision**                                                                                     |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| General Rule | Willful absence from duty shall be treated as “Dies Non”, which means that the period of absence: |

- Will not count for pay or allowances,

- Will not count for leave accumulation or increment, and

- Will not be treated as qualifying service for any purpose, including pension or promotion.

**Effect on Service**

The period declared as “Dies Non” shall:

- Break the continuity of service for the specific period,

- Postpone the next annual increment proportionately, and

- Be recorded in the service register of the employee.

**3. Conditions and Procedure**

| **Aspect**             | **Description**                                                                                                                                                                  |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Notice and Explanation | Before declaring a period as “Dies Non,” the competent authority shall seek a written explanation from the concerned employee regarding the cause of absence.                    |
| Authority              | The Head of Institution / Registrar / Vice Chancellor shall be the competent authority to decide and record the order of “Dies Non.”                                             |
| Documentation          | The order declaring a period as “Dies Non” must be in writing, specifying dates and reasons. A copy shall be placed in the employee's service file and reflected in pay records. |

**Review**

An employee may submit a representation for review within 15 days if they can substantiate valid grounds (medical emergency, natural calamity, etc.). The decision of the Vice Chancellor shall be final.

**4. Implications of “Dies Non”**

| **Impact Area**         | **Effect**                                                                                        |
|-------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Salary and Allowances   | No salary, allowances, or subsistence grant shall be paid for the period declared as “Dies Non.”  |
| Leave Entitlement       | The period will not be converted into any form of leave (earned, casual, or medical).             |
| Increment               | The annual increment shall be postponed by the duration of the “Dies Non” period.                 |
| Seniority and Promotion | The period shall not count for seniority or qualifying service for promotion or pension.          |
| Service Record          | Entry shall be made in red ink in the Service Register, noting “Dies Non – Unauthorized Absence.” |

**5. Policy Principles**

- Regular attendance and adherence to duty hours are integral components of professional conduct.

- Unauthorized absence constitutes misconduct and may attract disciplinary proceedings in addition to “Dies Non” entry.

- The “Dies Non” clause is punitive yet corrective, aimed at maintaining work discipline and deterring habitual absenteeism.

- The Board of Management (BoM) shall be the final authority on interpretation, appeal, and enforcement of this rule.

**Absence of Temporary Employees After Leave Expiry**

**I. Policy Statement**

- This clause governs the service status of temporary employees who remain absent from duty after availing or applying for leave to which they are not entitled.

- It ensures discipline, administrative clarity, and continuity of work in University operations by defining the automatic consequence of unauthorized absence.

**1. General Provision**

| **Clause**     | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Rule           | A temporary employee who remains absent from duty after applying for leave or extension of leave to which he/she is not entitled shall be deemed to have been discharged from service automatically. |
| Effective Date | The discharge shall take effect from the date on which the employee ceases to be entitled to leave, unless the leave or its extension is formally granted by the competent authority.                |
| Authority      | The Registrar, or any other officer duly authorized by the Vice Chancellor / Board of Management (BoM), shall be the competent authority to sanction or refuse such leave.                           |

**2. Conditions and Clarifications**

| **Condition**               | **Explanation**                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Automatic Discharge         | The employee's discharge occurs by operation of rule — no separate termination order or notice is required.                                                                           |
| Non-Entitlement to Benefits | The employee forfeits all salary, leave, and service benefits from the date of unauthorized absence.                                                                                  |
| No Claim for Reinstatement  | The employee has no right to reinstatement or continuation in service after discharge under this rule.                                                                                |
| Discretion of Authority     | The competent authority may, in exceptional cases (e.g., medical emergencies, unforeseen calamities), regularize such absence only upon adequate justification and documentary proof. |
| Documentation               | The discharge shall be recorded in the employee's service file as: “Deemed discharged from service due to unauthorized absence beyond sanctioned leave period.”                       |

**3. Procedural Steps**

| **Step**                   | **Administrative Action**                                                                                                                                             |
|----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Monitoring of Leave    | The Head of Department shall monitor the expiry date of sanctioned leave for all temporary employees.                                                                 |
| 2\. Reporting Absence      | If an employee fails to report for duty on the next working day after leave expiry, the matter shall be reported to the Registrar's office within three working days. |
| 3\. Optional Notice        | The University may issue a show-cause notice requesting the employee to justify absence within seven days.                                                            |
| 4\. Discharge Confirmation | If no valid explanation is received, the employee shall be deemed discharged from the date of unauthorized absence.                                                   |
| 5\. Record Update          | HR/Establishment Section shall update service, payroll, and attendance records accordingly.                                                                           |

**4. Effect of Discharge**

| **Area**                        | **Impact**                                                                                         |
|---------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Salary and Allowances           | Pay and allowances stop from the date of unauthorized absence.                                     |
| Leave and Continuity of Service | The employee forfeits all leave entitlement and service continuity.                                |
| Employment Status               | The person ceases to be in University employment without formal termination proceedings.           |
| Reappointment                   | If reappointed, the case will be treated as a fresh appointment, without linkage to prior service. |

**5. Policy Principles**

- Temporary employment is purely conditional upon continuous attendance and satisfactory performance.

- Unauthorized absence constitutes voluntary abandonment of service and leads to automatic discharge.

- This provision applies equally to contractual, ad-hoc, or daily-wage employees, unless explicitly exempted in their appointment order.

- The Board of Management (BoM) reserves final authority over interpretation, appeals, or exceptions under this rule.

**Policy on Applications for Outside Employment**

**I. Purpose**

- To regulate the process by which University employees may apply for employment in other institutions, organizations, or agencies.

- To maintain transparency, accountability, and institutional integrity while allowing fair professional mobility.

- To ensure that all external employment applications are processed through proper administrative channels.

**1. General Rule**

| **Clause**                        | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Official Channel Requirement      | No employee of the University shall send any application for employment under any other agency, institution, or organization except through the proper institutional channel, i.e., through the Head of the Department → Registrar → Vice Chancellor. |
| Prohibition on Direct Application | Direct submission of applications to external employers without forwarding through the University shall be treated as misconduct and may invite disciplinary action.                                                                                  |

**2. Limitation on Frequency of Applications**

| **Condition**         | **Provision**                                                                                                                                           |
|-----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Permissible Frequency | Ordinarily, every employee—whether permanent, temporary, or probationary—shall be permitted to apply for an outside post only twice in a calendar year. |
| Special Consideration | The competent authority may, in exceptional cases, allow additional applications where:                                                                 |

- The external post aligns with the University's academic or research objectives; or

- The post is a significant career advancement opportunity in a government or public institution.

**Discretion of Authority**

Permission to apply does not imply an obligation to relieve the employee if selected. The University retains discretion based on institutional needs and academic schedule.

**3. Procedure for Seeking Permission**

| **Step**                          | **Required Action**                                                                                                                                                                |
|-----------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Written Request               | The employee must submit a formal written request to the Head of the Department specifying the name of the external agency, post applied for, and relevance to career development. |
| 2\. Forwarding and Recommendation | The Head of the Department shall forward the application with comments to the Registrar and subsequently to the Vice Chancellor for approval.                                      |
| 3\. Institutional Endorsement     | Upon approval, the University shall issue a “No Objection Certificate (NOC)” or endorsement before forwarding the application to the external organization.                        |
| 4\. Record Maintenance            | The HR/Establishment section shall maintain a register of outside applications submitted by employees each year.                                                                   |

**4. Conditions and Restrictions**

| **Aspect**                   | **Provision**                                                                                                                                              |
|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Leave for Interviews         | Employees must obtain prior permission to attend interviews or selection processes; such absence will be treated as special casual leave only if approved. |
| Post-Selection Formalities   | If selected, the employee shall not assume the new post without obtaining formal relieving orders from the competent authority.                            |
| Bonded / Agreement Employees | Employees under service bond or contractual obligation must complete the stipulated service period before applying elsewhere.                              |
| Disciplinary Consequences    | Applying directly or misrepresenting University authorization will lead to censure, withholding of increment, or termination depending on severity.        |

**5. Policy Principles**

- This policy safeguards both the individual's right to career progression and the University's administrative interests.

- Applications through proper channel ensure institutional transparency and record maintenance.

- Direct or unauthorized applications reflect disrespect for institutional hierarchy and amount to violation of conduct rules.

- The Vice Chancellor's decision on granting permission or frequency relaxation shall be final and binding.

- The Board of Management (BoM) reserves the right to review or modify this policy in line with evolving institutional needs.
