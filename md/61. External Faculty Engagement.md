**Abstract**

The Comprehensive Policy Framework for External Faculty Engagement establishes a structured institutional mechanism for integrating distinguished external experts—comprising Visiting Faculty, Adjunct Faculty, Professors of Practice (PoP), and Emeritus Professors—into the academic, research, and innovation ecosystem of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs). The framework aligns with NEP 2020, UGC Guidelines (2023), and AICTE Policy on Adjunct Faculty, emphasizing the need to embed professional and industry expertise into the higher education process to ensure relevance, innovation, and global competitiveness.

This policy delineates the vision, mission, and objectives aimed at fostering a symbiotic relationship between academia, industry, and society to promote experiential, practice-oriented learning. It classifies external faculty into four categories, outlining clear eligibility criteria, tenure, and selection procedures based on merit, experience, and domain expertise rather than traditional academic qualifications. Through these appointments, HEIs can harness professional insights to modernize curricula, guide applied research, mentor students in innovation and entrepreneurship, and strengthen industry-academia linkages.

A key strength of this framework lies in its operational clarity and accountability structure, detailing specific roles and responsibilities such as course delivery, project mentorship, curriculum development, research collaboration, and institutional development activities. The inclusion of a mandatory engagement metric—a minimum of 50 hours per year—ensures consistent, measurable contribution from each external faculty member.

The policy adopts a transparent selection and evaluation process, incorporating departmental needs assessment, expert screening, structured interviews, and approval through the Academic Council or Board of Governors. Continuous performance monitoring is facilitated through Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) and student feedback mechanisms, ensuring that faculty renewal and recognition are evidence-based and outcome-driven.

Crucially, the framework integrates with NAAC, NBA, and QS I-GAUGE accreditation systems, directly contributing to institutional quality benchmarks. Under NAAC Criterion 6.3.5/6.3.6, structured engagement of external faculty can contribute up to 10 marks annually toward faculty development and innovation, while under NBA Criterion 5, it strengthens industry-academia interactions and outcome-based education metrics. The policy also supports QS I-GAUGE parameters on Teaching Quality, Employability, and Innovation by embedding professional practice into academic delivery.

The Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC) plays a pivotal role in maintaining documentation and quality compliance. It oversees record management through standardized instruments such as the Faculty Activity Log Sheet, Semester-Wise Contribution Reports, Annual KPI Evaluation Sheets, and IQAC Summary Reports for AQAR submission. This ensures traceability, transparency, and readiness for audits and peer reviews.

By linking professional engagement with institutional accreditation and continuous improvement, this policy transforms external faculty participation into a measurable driver of institutional excellence. It promotes interdisciplinary collaboration, applied research, innovation-driven education, and sustained quality enhancement, making HEIs more adaptive to emerging academic and industry trends. Ultimately, this framework establishes a national model for practice-integrated higher education, positioning external faculty engagement as a strategic lever for academic transformation, employability enhancement, and global ranking advancement.

**1. Introduction**

The policy framework for engaging external faculty—including Visiting Faculty, Adjunct Faculty, Professors of Practice (PoP), and Emeritus Professors—represents a structured, quality-assured mechanism to strengthen the academic, research, and innovation ecosystem of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs). Rooted in national educational priorities and guided by NEP 2020, UGC Guidelines (2023) for Professors of Practice, and the AICTE Policy on Adjunct Faculty, this framework aligns institutional practices with the evolving demands of academia–industry integration. It ensures compliance with the benchmarks set by NAAC, NBA, and QS I-GAUGE, positioning external faculty as catalysts for quality enhancement, experiential learning, and global competitiveness.

The vision of the policy is to establish a symbiotic ecosystem that unites higher education, industry, and society for impactful, practice-oriented learning. The mission is to attract eminent professionals, industry leaders, entrepreneurs, scientists, and policymakers into the teaching-learning process, thereby transforming classrooms into innovation-driven spaces. These engagements are designed to bridge the academic–industry gap, promote multidisciplinary learning, and strengthen research collaborations. The core objectives include: facilitating collaborative knowledge sharing between academia and professional sectors; exposing students to real-world challenges and industrial practices; integrating professional and applied content into curricula; enhancing employability and entrepreneurial capacities; and ultimately, improving institutional rankings and accreditation performance through measurable, quality-based outcomes.

The policy defines four structured categories of external engagement—each distinct in eligibility, tenure, and scope. Visiting Faculty are typically eminent academicians or researchers appointed for short durations (1 week to 6 months) to deliver specialized lectures, short-term modules, or joint research projects. They are expected to hold a Ph.D. or equivalent with at least 10 years of academic or research experience. Adjunct Faculty, generally industry experts or retired professors, serve for longer terms (6 months to 3 years) and contribute through project supervision, consultancy, and curriculum enhancement. They are required to possess a postgraduate degree and at least 10–15 years of professional experience with proven innovation or research involvement. Professors of Practice (PoP), as introduced under UGC 2023 guidelines, are senior professionals or innovators with 15+ years of domain experience or entrepreneurship leadership. A Ph.D. is not mandatory for this category, as the emphasis lies on demonstrated professional excellence. Emeritus Professors are retired academicians with outstanding service records, typically 20+ years, and significant research or publication contributions; they are appointed upon approval by the Academic Council or Board.

All appointments are made on merit and institutional need rather than academic qualifications alone. Professional integrity, excellence in domain knowledge, and measurable contribution potential are mandatory prerequisites. The policy underscores that external faculty are not temporary add-ons but integral contributors to the academic enrichment, innovation culture, and accreditation readiness of the institution.

Each external faculty category has defined roles and responsibilities aimed at institutional capacity-building. They are expected to teach at least one course or deliver structured academic sessions, particularly for senior undergraduate and postgraduate cohorts. Their responsibilities extend beyond lecturing—they include mentoring student projects, guiding dissertations, conducting seminars, workshops, and Faculty Development Programs (FDPs). They also play a crucial role in curriculum development, ensuring programs remain dynamic and aligned with current technological and industrial advancements. Furthermore, these experts actively contribute to the Institution’s Innovation Council (IIC), incubation activities, and consultancy projects, fostering entrepreneurship and applied research. A significant deliverable across all categories is the mandatory minimum contribution of 50 hours per academic year, encompassing teaching, mentoring, research, or innovation-related activities.

The appointment and evaluation process is designed to uphold transparency, fairness, and academic rigor. Departmental committees identify the need for external engagement based on curricular and research gaps. Calls for nominations or direct invitations are made, followed by screening of candidate profiles for relevance and expertise. The evaluation process includes weighted criteria: professional experience (30%), industry or research contribution (25%), teaching and mentoring potential (20%), and alignment with institutional goals (15%). A Selection Committee, chaired by the Director/Principal and including the Dean (Academics), Head of Department, a Senior Professor, and one external expert, conducts interviews or interactions. After approval by the Academic Council or Board of Governors, a formal appointment letter is issued detailing tenure, deliverables, and remuneration.

Each appointment undergoes annual performance review based on measurable Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) such as teaching hours, workshops conducted, mentorship records, research outputs, and innovation activities. A renewal is granted only if the faculty achieves at least 75% of KPI targets and attains a minimum feedback score of 4.0 out of 5 from students and peers. This performance-linked approach integrates accountability with professional recognition, ensuring sustained quality outcomes.

From a quality assurance perspective, the policy has direct accreditation and ranking significance. Under NAAC Criterion 6.3.5 / 6.3.6, the documented engagement of visiting or adjunct faculty contributes up to 10 marks annually (1 mark for policy provision and 9 marks for fulfilling 50-hour engagement benchmarks over three years). For NBA, such appointments strengthen Criterion 5 (Faculty Information and Contributions), particularly concerning faculty-industry collaboration, innovation, and outcome-based teaching. Under QS I-GAUGE, external faculty enhance scores in Teaching Quality, Employability, Research, and Innovation by bridging practical learning and industry exposure. Hence, beyond academic benefit, the engagement significantly uplifts institutional visibility and ranking performance.

A central element of the framework is its robust documentation and reporting system, led by the Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC). IQAC maintains appointment records, engagement logs, KPI evaluation sheets, and student feedback data as part of the institution’s evidence repository. Each faculty member must maintain a Faculty Activity Log Sheet (recording all teaching and mentoring hours) and submit Semester-Wise Contribution Reports detailing outcomes, publications, workshops, and collaborations. The IQAC consolidates these reports into the Annual KPI Evaluation Sheet and the IQAC Summary Report for AQAR submission, ensuring data integrity and audit readiness. Evidence such as appointment orders, feedback forms, event reports, and research outputs serve as verifiable proofs during accreditation and external reviews.

Additionally, the policy mandates semester-level reflection and evaluation to monitor academic impact. Departments are responsible for verifying contributions, while IQAC ensures integration of all records into the Accreditation Evidence Matrix and Performance Dashboard. The entire system reinforces transparency, accountability, and alignment with Outcome-Based Education (OBE) principles, which are foundational to NBA and NAAC evaluations.

In essence, the policy framework for engaging Visiting, Adjunct, PoP, and Emeritus Faculty represents a transformative model for institutional excellence. It institutionalizes structured collaboration between academia and industry, providing a steady influx of professional expertise and innovation into higher education. Through its KPI-linked evaluation, documentation rigor, and accreditation-aligned design, the framework not only enhances the quality of teaching and research but also ensures sustainability and evidence-based improvement. By fostering experiential learning, interdisciplinary engagement, and a measurable culture of innovation, this policy serves as both a strategic instrument for academic transformation and a quality benchmark for accreditation and global ranking success.

- Reinforces the academic ecosystem through knowledge sharing, skill transfer, and real-world exposure.

**4. Classification of Faculty Engagement**

| **Category**          | **Duration**         | **Profile**                                       | **Scope / Nature of Engagement**                                  |
|-----------------------|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Visiting Faculty      | 1 week – 6 months    | Eminent academicians / researchers                | Short-term teaching, seminars, workshops                          |
| Adjunct Faculty       | 6 months – 3 years   | Industry experts, retired professors, scientists  | Mentorship, consultancy, curriculum design                        |
| Professor of Practice | 3 years (extendable) | Distinguished professionals / innovators          | Practice-oriented teaching, innovation mentoring                  |
| Emeritus Professor    | As approved by Board | Retired senior professors with exceptional record | Guidance in research, publications, and institutional development |

**5. Eligibility Criteria**

- Visiting Faculty: Ph.D. or equivalent, 10+ years of research or teaching experience.

- Adjunct Faculty: Postgraduate with minimum 10 years of professional experience; recognized in field of expertise.

- Professor of Practice: 15+ years of industry or entrepreneurship experience; demonstrated innovation or leadership.

- Emeritus Professor: Retired faculty with exceptional academic contributions (publications, projects, patents).

**6. Appointment and Selection Process**

- Nomination / Invitation: Through IQAC or Dean Academics’ recommendation.

- Evaluation: Screening by Faculty Selection Committee (Dean + HoD + External Expert).

- Approval: By Academic Council / Board of Governors.

- Formalization: Appointment letter defining tenure, deliverables, and remuneration.

**7. Tenure and Renewal**

- Visiting Faculty: 1 week to 6 months, renewable.

- Adjunct Faculty: Up to 3 years, extendable annually.

- Professor of Practice: 3 years, renewable for one more term.

- Emeritus Professor: Tenure as approved by Board of Governors.

**8. Roles and Responsibilities**

- Deliver structured teaching-learning sessions, seminars, or workshops.

- Contribute to research, innovation, and consultancy.

- Mentor students in projects, internships, or startup incubation.

- Participate in curriculum review and industry collaboration.

- Conduct joint R&D and patent generation.

- Support IQAC, IIC, and accreditation-related activities.

**5. PROCEDURE FOR SELECTING VISITING FACULTY / ADJUNCT FACULTY / PROFESSOR OF PRACTICE / EMERITUS PROFESSOR**

*(Institutional Policy Framework – As per UGC & AICTE Guidelines)*

**1. Purpose and Objective**

- To establish a transparent, merit-based, and policy-driven selection procedure for engaging distinguished experts as Visiting Faculty, Adjunct Faculty, Professors of Practice, and Emeritus Professors.

- To ensure such appointments align with institutional goals, academic excellence, and NAAC/NBA accreditation criteria.

- To maintain a standardized process for scrutiny, approval, and documentation in compliance with IQAC and statutory regulations.

**2. Applicability**

- This procedure applies to all appointments made under the categories of:

<!-- -->

- Visiting Faculty

- Adjunct Faculty

- Professor of Practice (PoP)

- Emeritus Professor

<!-- -->

- It covers all schools/departments within the institution, including centres of research, incubation, innovation, and outreach programs.

**3. Source of Identification**

| **Category**          | **Source of Candidate Identification**                                                                            | **Nominating Authority**              |
|-----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Visiting Faculty      | Academic institutions, professional bodies, research labs (national/international)                                | HoD / Dean Academics                  |
| Adjunct Faculty       | Industry experts, entrepreneurs, retired scientists / administrators                                              | Dean Academics / IQAC                 |
| Professor of Practice | Senior professionals with 15+ years’ experience from engineering, science, technology, business, or public policy | Director / Principal                  |
| Emeritus Professor    | Retired professors with distinguished teaching or research record                                                 | Academic Council / Board of Governors |

**4. Invitation and Nomination Process**

- Step 1: Departments identify domain-specific needs for external expertise (through Academic Council / Board of Studies deliberations).

- Step 2: The Dean (Academics) issues an open call for nomination/invitation or considers direct nominations from reputed organizations, industries, or alumni networks.

- Step 3: Candidates may also apply directly in response to institutional announcements or formal invitations.

- Step 4: Nominations must include:

<!-- -->

- Updated CV with relevant experience and achievements.

- Statement of Purpose (SoP) outlining proposed contribution areas.

- Endorsement or recommendation from industry / academic referees (if applicable).

**5. Screening and Evaluation**

- Initial Screening: Conducted by the Departmental Faculty Committee based on:

<!-- -->

- Professional achievements and domain relevance.

- Alignment with institutional academic and innovation goals.

- Teaching, mentoring, or research aptitude.

<!-- -->

- Evaluation Parameters:

| **Criterion**                      | **Weightage** |
|------------------------------------|---------------|
| Academic / Professional Experience | 30%           |
| Industry / Research Contribution   | 25%           |
| Teaching / Mentorship Capability   | 20%           |
| Alignment with Institutional Goals | 15%           |
| Communication & Leadership Skills  | 10%           |

- Shortlisting: Candidates scoring ≥70% in the evaluation grid shall be recommended for further approval.

**6. Selection Committee Composition**

Each appointment shall be made on the recommendation of a Selection Committee constituted by the institution:

| **Designation**                                      | **Role in Committee**         |
|------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Director / Principal                                 | Chairperson                   |
| Dean (Academics)                                     | Member                        |
| Head of Department (concerned discipline)            | Member                        |
| One Senior Professor (nominated by Academic Council) | Member                        |
| One External Expert (Industry / Academia)            | Member                        |
| IQAC Coordinator                                     | Observer / Non-voting Member  |
| HR / Registrar                                       | Secretary (for documentation) |

**7. Interview / Interaction Process**

- Step 1: Shortlisted candidates are invited for an interaction / interview session, in person or through virtual mode.

- Step 2: The panel assesses the candidate’s suitability for:

<!-- -->

- Teaching and mentoring capacity.

- Industry-academia linkage potential.

- Applied research / innovation experience.

- Vision for student skill development and institutional growth.

<!-- -->

- Step 3: Evaluation is recorded in a structured Interview Assessment Form with qualitative remarks and scoring.

- Step 4: The Selection Committee’s recommendation is submitted to the Academic Council for formal approval.

**8. Approval and Appointment**

- Upon approval from the Academic Council / Board of Governors, the Registrar or HR Section issues a formal Appointment Letter (refer Annexure I–III).

- The appointment specifies:

<!-- -->

- Designation, Department, and Tenure.

- Expected duties and deliverables.

- Honorarium and funding source.

- Code of conduct, reporting structure, and evaluation mechanism.

<!-- -->

- Copy of the appointment letter is forwarded to:

<!-- -->

- Dean (Academics)

- Head of Department

- IQAC Cell

- Accounts Section

**9. Orientation and Induction**

- The newly appointed expert shall undergo an orientation session organized by IQAC to:

<!-- -->

- Understand institutional vision, policies, and academic culture.

- Familiarize with code of conduct, attendance norms, and performance metrics.

- Review templates for reporting contributions (log sheets, feedback forms, KPI sheet).

**10. Documentation and Record Maintenance**

- Each appointment shall be supported by:

<!-- -->

- Application Form / CV

- Screening and Evaluation Sheets

- Selection Committee Minutes

- Appointment Order

- Faculty Activity Log (Annexure IV)

- KPI Evaluation (Annexure VI)

- Student Feedback Reports (Annexure V)

- IQAC Summary (Annexure VII)

All documents shall be digitized and archived under the IQAC Faculty Engagement Repository for accreditation and audit purposes.

**11. Performance Review and Renewal**

- Annual Performance Review:

<!-- -->

- Conducted jointly by the Dean (Academics), HoD, and IQAC Coordinator based on KPI and student feedback.

<!-- -->

- Renewal Criteria:

<!-- -->

- Minimum 75% of KPI targets achieved.

- Positive student and faculty feedback (average ≥4.0/5).

- Submission of annual report with evidence of outcomes.

<!-- -->

- Renewal Approval:

<!-- -->

- Recommended by Academic Council and approved by the Director / Principal.

- Updated appointment order issued with revised tenure and deliverables.

**12. Accountability and Code of Conduct**

- All selected faculty are bound by the institution’s Ethics, Confidentiality, and Anti-Plagiarism Policy.

- Breach of contract, academic misconduct, or non-performance may lead to termination with due notice.

- Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) generated during engagement will be governed by the Institutional IP Policy.

**13. Reporting and Audit**

- IQAC shall prepare an annual audit summary of all appointments, contributions, and outcomes for inclusion in:

<!-- -->

- AQAR (Annual Quality Assurance Report)

- NAAC SSR Submission (Criterion 6.3.5 / 6.3.6)

- NBA Self-Assessment Report (Criterion 5)

<!-- -->

- Performance data shall also contribute to the institution’s NIRF and QS I-GAUGE submissions under faculty metrics.

**Annual Review & Renewal Decision**

**15. Institutional Oversight**

- The Dean (Academics) and IQAC Coordinator jointly oversee the policy implementation.

- The Registrar ensures documentation compliance and audit readiness.

- The Academic Council periodically reviews the quality and impact of such appointments on institutional outcomes.

- Participate in seminars, conferences, and institutional development programs as invited.

**5. Terms and Conditions:**

- The Adjunct Professor shall not engage in any work conflicting with the institution’s interests.

- Any intellectual output or consultancy outcome developed during this tenure shall be governed by the institutional IP framework.

- The appointee shall submit semester-wise performance and engagement reports for institutional records.
