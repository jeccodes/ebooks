**Abstract**

The Dean of Academic Affairs (DOAA) holds one of the most pivotal leadership positions in higher education institutions, serving as the chief custodian of academic excellence, integrity, and innovation. This book explores the multifaceted roles, responsibilities, and strategic impact of the DOAA, highlighting how this position bridges institutional leadership, faculty, students, and external quality assurance frameworks. As universities navigate the rapidly evolving landscape of higher education, the DOAA ensures that academic programs remain relevant, future-oriented, and globally competitive, while upholding national and international benchmarks such as NAAC, NBA, NIRF, and QS I-GAUGE.

The book situates the DOAA at the center of institutional governance, overseeing curriculum design, teaching–learning processes, student progression, research integration, skilling, employability, and digital transformation. With direct supervision of eight Associate Deans—covering portfolios such as Projects and Research, OBE Implementation, Student Progression, Curriculum and Feedback, Academic Registration, Skilling and VAC/CC, Time Tables and Teaching Monitoring, and MOOC Creation—the DOAA orchestrates a holistic framework for academic leadership. Each of these Associate Deans functions as a functional custodian of a vital academic pillar, collectively ensuring that the university delivers high-quality, outcome-driven, and student-centric education.

A key emphasis of the book is the alignment of institutional practices with Outcome-Based Education (OBE) and continuous improvement cycles. By embedding feedback from students, faculty, alumni, industry, and accreditation agencies, the DOAA ensures that curricula remain dynamic, teaching quality is monitored and enhanced, and student pathways are supported from admission to alumni success. The DOAA's role extends beyond administration to strategic foresight—preparing universities to adapt to digital learning, global competition, and societal challenges through innovation, research, and collaboration.

Moreover, the book underscores how the DOAA contributes to institutional credibility and reputation. By ensuring transparency in evaluation, integrity in academic records, inclusivity in student support, and excellence in teaching and research, the DOAA becomes the guardian of trust for all stakeholders. This custodianship is not only operational but symbolic, representing the values of academic rigor, accountability, and visionary leadership.

In essence, *Dean of Academic Affairs: Custodian of Academic Excellence* positions the DOAA as the anchor of academic governance in higher education. It provides a comprehensive framework for understanding how academic leadership aligns policies with practice, national mandates with global aspirations, and institutional vision with student success, ultimately ensuring that universities thrive as centres of excellence and innovation.

# **1. Introduction**

In the rapidly evolving landscape of higher education, institutions are no longer judged solely on the number of students they enroll or the infrastructure they provide. Instead, they are evaluated by their ability to maintain academic integrity, deliver quality teaching, nurture innovation, and align with national and global benchmarks. At the heart of this process lies the role of the Dean of Academic Affairs (DOAA), a senior academic leader tasked with guiding institutions through the challenges and opportunities of the twenty-first century. The DOAA functions as the chief custodian of academic excellence, balancing tradition with innovation and policy with practice. The position carries significant weight, not just administratively but symbolically, representing the institution's commitment to quality education, research, and student success. This role is essential in an era where accreditation, rankings, digital learning, student employability, and global competitiveness define the success of universities and colleges.

The DOAA is strategically positioned between the Vice-Chancellor or President, who provides the overall vision, and the Registrar and Heads of Departments, who manage operational delivery. This middle space makes the Dean a bridge between leadership and faculty, ensuring that academic strategies are effectively translated into teaching, research, and student services. In most higher education systems, the DOAA oversees curriculum development and academic programs, teaching–learning quality and faculty development, student progression, retention and employability, research integration and innovation, skilling initiatives and VAC/CC programs, digital learning through MOOCs, as well as academic records, timetabling, and evaluation processes. This breadth of responsibilities means the DOAA is not confined to a single aspect of governance but instead operates as the integrative force that unites multiple dimensions of academic life.

The word custodian captures the true essence of the Dean's role. Unlike managers who focus primarily on administration, custodians safeguard values, integrity, and trust. The DOAA is the guardian of academic standards, ensuring that programs remain aligned with the university's mission, national regulations, and global frameworks such as NAAC, NBA, NIRF, QS, and THE rankings. This custodianship involves protecting the credibility of academic records and evaluations, ensuring fairness and transparency in teaching and assessment, monitoring continuous improvement cycles in curriculum and pedagogy, and guaranteeing that students graduate with defined competencies and employability skills. By embodying this role, the Dean becomes the anchor of institutional trust for students, faculty, administrators, and external stakeholders alike.

To operationalize its vast mandate, the DOAA works through a team of Associate Deans, each serving as a functional custodian of a critical academic domain. The Associate Dean for Projects and Academic Research oversees research excellence and innovation. The Associate Dean for OBE Implementation safeguards the Outcome-Based Education framework, ensuring CO–PO–PSO attainment. The Associate Dean for Student Progression ensures student success pathways and employability readiness. The Associate Dean for Curriculum and Academic Programs, Academic Feedback, is the custodian of curricular relevance and innovation. The Associate Dean for Academic Registration ensures enrollment integrity and the reliability of academic records. The Associate Dean for Skilling and VAC/CC oversees skill-based learning and career readiness. The Associate Dean for Time Tables and Monitoring of Teaching safeguards academic discipline and teaching quality. Finally, the Associate Dean for MOOC Creation ensures digital learning and online innovation. Together, these eight Associate Deans form a custodial ecosystem under the guidance of the DOAA. This structure ensures that the University's academic mission is pursued holistically, balancing research, teaching, student development, and digital transformation.

In today's competitive academic environment, benchmarks and accreditation frameworks act as the yardstick by which institutions are measured. The DOAA, with the support of Associate Deans, ensures alignment with NBA, which emphasizes Outcome-Based Education, CO–PO–PSO attainment, employability, and continuous improvement; NAAC, which adopts a holistic approach covering curriculum, teaching, research, governance, student support, and institutional innovation; NIRF, which relies on data-driven parameters such as teaching–learning resources, research and professional practice, graduation outcomes, outreach and inclusivity, and perception; and QS I-GAUGE and QS/THE World Rankings, which apply internationally benchmarked indicators for faculty quality, employability, diversity, research impact, and digital readiness. By integrating these frameworks, the DOAA not only ensures compliance but also drives excellence and competitiveness, positioning the institution as a leader both nationally and globally.

Beyond compliance, the DOAA plays a proactive role as a strategic leader and change agent. The higher education sector is undergoing rapid transformation due to the digitalization of learning, increasing focus on employability and entrepreneurship, global competition for rankings and visibility, and the adoption of student-centric models that emphasize flexibility and lifelong learning. The DOAA must anticipate these changes, guide faculty in adapting pedagogies, promote innovation in curriculum, and support students in acquiring future-ready skills. This requires visionary leadership, adaptability, and the ability to balance long-term strategy with immediate operational needs.

Symbolically, the DOAA represents the institution's commitment to academic integrity, quality, and accountability. Practically, the DOAA coordinates people, processes, and policies to ensure smooth academic functioning. The dual nature of the role—symbolic custodian and operational leader—makes the Dean indispensable in building trust with students, parents, and employers, maintaining faculty morale and professional development, demonstrating accountability to regulators, accreditation agencies, and ranking bodies, and enhancing the institution's credibility and reputation.

This book, *Dean of Academic Affairs: Custodian of Academic Excellence*, aims to provide a comprehensive exploration of the role of DOAA and Associate Deans in higher education governance. It brings together theoretical insights, practical frameworks, and benchmark mappings to present a clear picture of how academic custodianship is operationalized. The chapters highlight each Associate Dean's portfolio in detail, mapping their responsibilities to NBA, NAAC, NIRF, and QS I-GAUGE criteria, and presenting them as functional custodians of research, OBE, student progression, curriculum, registration, skilling, teaching, and digital learning. By doing so, the book provides institutions with a framework for academic leadership that is both nationally compliant and globally competitive.

The role of the Dean of Academic Affairs extends far beyond administrative oversight; it is about custodianship, vision, and accountability. By coordinating multiple dimensions of academic life and aligning them with quality benchmarks, the DOAA ensures that institutions fulfill their mission of educating students, advancing knowledge, and contributing to society. The Dean, together with the Associate Deans, safeguards the academic values that define the identity and reputation of the university. As higher education continues to evolve, the DOAA will remain the anchor of academic excellence and innovation, ensuring that institutions are prepared not just to adapt to change but to lead it.

# **2. Dean – Academic Affairs (DOAA)**

The Dean of Academic Affairs (DOAA) is a senior-level academic leader who plays a pivotal role in shaping the intellectual and educational framework of a university or college. This position carries the primary responsibility for the strategic direction, effective administration, and quality assurance of all academic programs, courses, and policies. Acting as the bridge between the institution's top leadership and its faculty, the Dean ensures that the university's academic mission is translated into practice with integrity, innovation, and accountability. The DOAA provides oversight in critical areas such as curriculum design, teaching quality, research integration, accreditation compliance, student progression, and faculty development, making the role central to sustaining academic excellence. Beyond routine administration, the Dean sets the tone for academic culture and discipline, ensuring that programs are aligned with national and international standards (NEP-2020, NAAC, NBA, NIRF, QS rankings) and remain responsive to emerging global trends. By coordinating with Associate Deans, Heads of Departments, and Boards of Studies, the DOAA fosters collaboration across disciplines, encourages outcome-based learning, and drives digital and skill-based education initiatives. In this way, the Dean of Academic Affairs not only safeguards academic standards but also champions innovation, global competitiveness, and student success, ensuring that the institution's educational mission is continuously fulfilled and advanced.

# **4. Responsibilities of the Dean (Academics)**

### **1. Academic Leadership and Policy**

- Formulates and executes academic policies, guidelines, and strategies aligned with university vision and regulatory frameworks (NEP-2020, UGC, AICTE, NAAC, NBA, NIRF).

- Provides academic direction in consultation with Vice-Chancellor, Registrar, and Academic Council.

- Supervises Associate Deans and Heads of Departments to ensure smooth academic governance.

- Reports academic progress and outcomes to the Vice-Chancellor and governing bodies.

### **2. Curriculum Development and Programme Innovation**

- Designs, reviews, and updates curriculum frameworks in line with national and global best practices.

- Oversees the launch of new academic programmes, upgrading existing programmes, and revising course content

- Ensures curriculum integrates multidisciplinary, vocational, and skill-based learning.

- Coordinates with Boards of Studies and Academic Council for approvals.

### **3. Academic Planning and Administration**

- Decides the number of sections and batches for each theory and lab course

- Prepares and monitors the academic timetable for all courses

- Ensures adherence to academic rules by teaching faculty

- Supervises the regular and effective conduct of classes by faculty

- Coordinates academic operations with Dean (Evaluation & Academic Registration) for evaluation matters

- Maintains attendance and leave records of staff under his/her control

### **4. Quality Assurance and Evaluation**

- Implements Outcome-Based Education (OBE) framework (CO-PO-PSO mapping, attainment analysis).

- Monitors transparency in evaluation systems and ensures adherence to rules.

- Oversees continuous assessment, examinations, grading, and reporting.

- Promotes data-driven evaluation and accreditation compliance.

### **5. Student-Centric Responsibilities**

- Collects and analyses student feedback and ensures corrective actions

- Supports mentoring, remedial teaching, and student counseling systems.

- Tracks progression, retention, graduation rates, and placements.

- Promotes inclusivity and equity in academic opportunities.

### **6. Faculty Development and Human Resource Management**

- Ensures professional growth of teaching and non-teaching technical staff through training and development programs

- Organizes Faculty Development Programs (FDPs), workshops, and seminars to enhance teaching quality.

- Encourages innovative pedagogy, blended learning, and digital tools.

### **7. Research and Projects**

- Facilitates student projects, dissertations, and capstone initiatives.

- Encourages faculty-led research projects, funded proposals, patents, and publications.

- Strengthens industry–academia partnerships, consultancy, and innovation centres.

- Promotes incubation cells and IPR culture.

### **8. Skilling and Employability**

- Introduces Value-Added Courses (VACs) and Credit Courses (CCs) aligned with employability needs.

- Promotes skill development programs, MOOCs, and industry certifications.

- Encourages integration of internships, entrepreneurship, and training programs.

### **9. Budgeting and Resource Planning**

- Prepares the budget proposal for academic activities, infrastructure, and faculty development (Point XI).

# **7. Associate Dean – OBE (Outcome-Based Education) Implementation**

An Associate Dean for OBE Implementation is a senior academic administrator who plays a pivotal role in guiding a university or college through the transition to an Outcome-Based Education (OBE) framework. This position is central to ensuring that the institution's academic programs are not only designed but also delivered and assessed in alignment with clearly defined learning outcomes at the course, program, and institutional levels. Reporting to the Dean of Academic Affairs, the Associate Dean provides both academic leadership and strategic oversight in embedding OBE principles into the institution's teaching–learning ecosystem.

The responsibilities of this role extend across the design, development, monitoring, and continuous improvement of academic programs. This includes ensuring that Course Outcomes (COs) map coherently to Program Outcomes (POs) and Program-Specific Outcomes (PSOs), and that each of these outcomes is measurable, assessable, and aligned with national accreditation requirements (NBA, NAAC) as well as global quality frameworks. The Associate Dean coordinates with faculty, curriculum committees, and accreditation bodies to establish robust systems for curriculum planning, outcome mapping, attainment analysis, and data-driven decision-making.

In addition to technical expertise, the position requires strong strategic planning, academic leadership, and change management skills, as shifting to OBE demands a transformation in institutional culture. Faculty members must be trained to adopt new pedagogical approaches, design outcome-oriented assessments, and use learning analytics to evaluate student performance against desired outcomes. The Associate Dean thus also functions as a capacity builder and change agent, equipping faculty with the tools and support needed for effective OBE adoption.

By leading this transition, the Associate Dean for OBE Implementation ensures that the institution remains future-ready, accreditation-compliant, and globally competitive, while also creating a student-centered learning environment that emphasizes competency development, employability, and lifelong learning.
