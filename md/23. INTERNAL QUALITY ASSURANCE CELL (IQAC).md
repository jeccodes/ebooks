**Abstract**

The **Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC)** is a vital mechanism for post-accreditation quality sustenance and enhancement as recommended by the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC) for Higher Education Institutions (HEIs). It functions as an integral part of the institutional system, embedding quality in all academic and administrative processes rather than working as a separate body. The primary purpose of IQAC is to ensure the realization of quality enhancement and sustenance through conscious, consistent, and catalytic improvement in overall institutional performance. The **vision** of IQAC is to establish, nurture, and sustain a culture of quality, innovation, and excellence by promoting learner-centric pedagogy, advancing research and innovation, ensuring effective governance, and fostering inclusivity. Its **mission** is to develop, nurture, and institutionalize quality culture by setting benchmarks, facilitating a participative learning environment, empowering faculty, ensuring equitable access, and strengthening industry and community linkages. The **objectives** of IQAC include developing a systematic approach for continuous quality improvement across academic and administrative functions, promoting internalization of quality culture, institutionalizing best practices, and aligning the institution with national and global quality benchmarks.

IQAC achieves these goals by implementing **strategies** such as ensuring timely, efficient, and progressive performance of academic, administrative, and financial tasks; monitoring the relevance and quality of academic and research programmes; promoting equitable access to programmes; integrating modern teaching-learning methods; ensuring credibility in evaluation procedures; optimizing support structures; and facilitating networking with other institutions. Its **functions** include developing and applying quality benchmarks, creating a learner-centric ecosystem, supporting faculty development, collecting and analyzing stakeholder feedback, disseminating quality-related information, organizing workshops and seminars, documenting quality initiatives, acting as a nodal agency for quality coordination, maintaining an institutional database through MIS, preparing and submitting the Annual Quality Assurance Report (AQAR), and promoting a sustainable quality culture. The **IQAC Coordinator** plays a pivotal role as the operational head and driving force by facilitating quality culture across departments, coordinating meetings, acting as a liaison between stakeholders, managing data and MIS, and leading AQAR preparation. To ensure accountability, IQAC has a **monitoring mechanism** that involves preparation and submission of AQAR to NAAC, approval of reports by statutory bodies, website disclosure of minutes and compliance reports, peer review during NAAC visits, and implementation of recommendations for continuous improvement. In addition, IQAC conducts **Academic and Administrative Audits (AAA)** to evaluate institutional effectiveness, identify strengths and weaknesses, and recommend reforms for improvement, aligning with accreditation criteria of both NAAC and NBA.

Overall, IQAC acts as a **facilitative and participative body** that institutionalizes quality culture, enhances coordination, ensures evidence-based decision-making, and functions as a dynamic system for continuous improvement. It plays a central role in accreditation processes by ensuring adoption of Outcome-Based Education (OBE), systematic documentation, stakeholder engagement, and integration of best practices, thereby making higher education institutions more accountable, innovative, globally competitive, and socially responsible.

**1. Introduction**

> · **Background and Rationale:**
>
> o In pursuance of its Action Plan for performance evaluation, assessment, accreditation, and quality upgradation of higher education institutions, the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC), Bangalore recommends that every accredited institution establish an Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC) as a post-accreditation quality sustenance measure.
>
> o The establishment of IQAC highlights the recognition that quality enhancement is a continuous process and must be institutionalized within the system of higher education.
>
> · **Core Purpose of IQAC:**
>
> o To become an integral part of the institution’s system, ensuring the realization of quality enhancement and sustenance.
>
> o To develop a mechanism for conscious, consistent, and catalytic improvement in the overall performance of institutions.
>
> o To channelize all institutional efforts during the post-accreditation period towards promoting holistic academic excellence.
>
> · **Guidelines and Institutionalization:**
>
> o The guidelines provided by NAAC serve as a roadmap for the creation and operation of IQAC in higher education institutions.
>
> o The work of the IQAC is regarded as the first step towards internalization and institutionalization of quality enhancement initiatives.
>
> · **Participative and Facilitative Role:**
>
> o The effectiveness of IQAC depends on the sense of belongingness and participation it fosters among all stakeholders of the institution (teachers, students, management, alumni, industry partners, etc.).
>
> o IQAC is not a hierarchical structure or mere record-keeping body. Instead, it acts as a facilitative and participative voluntary system/unit/organ within the institution.
>
> · **Potential and Impact:**
>
> o IQAC has the capacity to become a vehicle for ushering in quality enhancement by devising and implementing planned intervention strategies.
>
> o These strategies focus on removing institutional deficiencies and promoting continuous improvement, similar to the “Quality Circles” in industries.
>
> o It ensures that academic, administrative, and infrastructural quality initiatives are aligned with institutional goals and accreditation standards.

**5. Strategies of IQAC**

> · **Purpose of IQAC Strategies**
>
> o The strategies framed by IQAC aim to establish a systematic framework that enhances the overall performance of institutions in academic, administrative, and research domains.
>
> o These strategies ensure sustainability, accountability, and innovation in institutional processes.

**a) Ensuring timely, efficient and progressive performance of academic, administrative and financial tasks**

> · IQAC emphasizes time-bound planning of academic calendars, administrative operations, and financial budgeting.
>
> · Ensures efficiency through digital tools like ERP systems, Learning Management Systems (LMS), and e-administration platforms.
>
> · Encourages progressive reforms such as decentralization of decision-making, automation of records, and transparent financial auditing.
>
> · Promotes continuous monitoring of task completion through periodic reviews and feedback systems.

**b) The relevance and quality of academic and research programmes**

> · IQAC ensures that academic programmes are aligned with national and global standards, industry requirements, and societal needs.
>
> · Introduces mechanisms for curriculum revision, based on feedback from stakeholders (students, alumni, employers, industry experts).
>
> · Promotes interdisciplinary and emerging areas of research (AI, data science, sustainability, etc.).
>
> · Establishes Research and Development Cells, encourages faculty research projects, and ensures quality publications.

**c) Equitable access to and affordability of academic programmes for various sections of society**

> · Implements inclusive policies like scholarships, fee waivers, and financial aid for economically weaker sections.
>
> · Ensures reservation policies and support services for marginalized and differently-abled students.
>
> · Expands open and distance learning, MOOCs, and online certifications to reach learners beyond traditional classrooms.
>
> · Promotes outreach initiatives (community learning, extension activities) to democratize access to higher education.

**d) Optimization and integration of modern methods of teaching and learning**

> · IQAC facilitates the use of technology-enabled learning such as ICT tools, smart classrooms, blended learning, and flipped classrooms.
>
> · Encourages adoption of Learning Management Systems (Moodle, Blackboard, Google Classroom) for continuous learning.
>
> · Promotes experiential learning methods – internships, fieldwork, simulations, industry projects.
>
> · Provides faculty training programs to integrate innovative pedagogy with modern digital resources.

**e) The credibility of evaluation procedures**

> · Ensures that examinations and assessments are fair, transparent, and reliable.
>
> · Introduces continuous internal assessment systems along with end-semester exams.
>
> · Promotes use of automated exam management systems to minimize human error and malpractice.
>
> · Encourages outcome-based evaluation aligned with learning objectives and programme outcomes.

**f) Ensuring the adequacy, maintenance and proper allocation of support structure and services**

> · IQAC monitors the availability and optimal utilization of infrastructure (labs, libraries, hostels, IT facilities).
>
> · Ensures maintenance of physical and digital assets through planned budgets and audits.
>
> · Allocates resources equitably among academic departments, research units, and student services.
>
> · Strengthens student support services like counselling, career guidance, placement cells, and grievance redressal systems.

**g) Sharing of research findings and networking with other institutions in India and abroad**

> · IQAC encourages faculty and student participation in national and international conferences, workshops, and seminars.
>
> · Promotes collaborative research projects with universities, industries, and government agencies.
>
> · Ensures publication and dissemination of research outputs in reputed journals, repositories, and knowledge platforms.
>
> · Facilitates MoUs and partnerships with institutions abroad for faculty exchange, student exchange, and joint academic initiatives.

**6. Functions of IQAC**

> · **Purpose of IQAC Functions**
>
> o The functions of IQAC are designed to ensure that quality assurance mechanisms are embedded into the everyday functioning of higher education institutions.
>
> o They focus on academic improvement, administrative efficiency, and stakeholder engagement, while also aligning with NAAC accreditation requirements.

**a) Development and application of quality benchmarks/parameters**

> · Establishes standards for academic and administrative activities such as teaching, research, examination, admissions, and governance.
>
> · Benchmarks may be drawn from national/international accreditation standards, industry best practices, and institutional goals.
>
> · Facilitates continuous measurement and monitoring against these benchmarks to ensure sustained quality enhancement.

**b) Facilitating a learner-centric environment & faculty maturation**

> · Promotes student-focused teaching-learning approaches, shifting away from traditional teacher-centric models.
>
> · Encourages use of participatory learning methods such as group discussions, projects, case studies, and ICT-enabled teaching.
>
> · Provides faculty training for pedagogical innovations, technology adoption, and continuous professional development.

**c) Feedback response from stakeholders**

> · Collects structured feedback from students, alumni, parents, employers, and other stakeholders on academic quality, facilities, and support systems.
>
> · Uses feedback for course corrections, curriculum improvements, and service enhancement.
>
> · Establishes feedback analysis and action-taken reports as part of transparent governance.

**d) Dissemination of information on quality parameters**

> · Ensures awareness creation among faculty, students, and staff regarding NAAC quality indicators, institutional goals, and best practices.
>
> · Publishes newsletters, circulars, reports, and digital notices on quality initiatives.
>
> · Promotes quality consciousness across all institutional levels.

**e) Organization of workshops, seminars, and promotion of quality circles**

> · Conducts inter- and intra-institutional workshops, seminars, and training programs on topics like curriculum design, research methodologies, ICT in teaching, etc.
>
> · Encourages Quality Circles (small groups of faculty/staff/students working collaboratively to solve quality issues).
>
> · Fosters a culture of peer learning and sharing of innovative practices.

**f) Documentation of programmes/activities**

> · Maintains systematic records of academic, administrative, co-curricular, and extension activities.
>
> · Ensures that documentation serves as evidence for accreditation, audits, and institutional memory.
>
> · Prepares progress reports to track institutional growth over time.

**g) Acting as a nodal agency for quality-related activities**

> · Serves as the central coordinating body for all quality-related initiatives within the institution.
>
> · Promotes adoption and institutionalization of best practices across departments and services.
>
> · Facilitates collaboration with external quality agencies, accreditation bodies, and partner institutions.

**h) Development and maintenance of institutional database through MIS**

> · Establishes a Management Information System (MIS) for collecting, storing, and analyzing institutional data.
>
> · Provides real-time insights for decision-making in admissions, examinations, placements, and research output.
>
> · Enhances data-driven planning and transparency.

**i) Development of Quality Culture**

> · Embeds quality as a core institutional value through continuous sensitization programs.
>
> · Promotes a collaborative mindset among faculty, students, administrators, and management towards improvement.
>
> · Encourages innovation, accountability, and excellence at all levels of institutional functioning.

**j) Preparation of the Annual Quality Assurance Report (AQAR)**

> · Compiles and prepares the AQAR in line with NAAC guidelines and parameters.
>
> · AQAR documents institutional performance, quality initiatives, best practices, and outcomes.
>
> · Acts as an official submission to NAAC for review, ensuring compliance with quality standards.

**8. Composition of the IQAC**

> · **Purpose of Composition**
>
> o The structure of IQAC is designed to ensure balanced representation of all major stakeholders in higher education.
>
> o It combines institutional leadership, faculty, administration, management, students, alumni, and external experts, thereby making IQAC both participative and inclusive.

1\. Chairperson: Head of the Institution

> · The Principal/Vice-Chancellor/Director acts as the Chairperson.
>
> · Provides vision, leadership, and authority for the IQAC’s functioning.
>
> · Ensures IQAC initiatives are aligned with institutional policies and goals.

2\. A few senior administrative officers

> · Includes Registrar, Deans, Controller of Examinations, Finance Officer, Librarian, etc.
>
> · Contribute expertise in administrative efficiency, governance, and resource management.
>
> · Help in integrating administrative processes with quality benchmarks.

3\. Three to eight teachers

> · Represents the academic backbone of IQAC.
>
> · Teachers contribute in curriculum development, pedagogy innovations, research promotion, and student mentoring.
>
> · Ensures that academic quality remains the central focus of IQAC activities.

4\. One member from the Management

> · A representative from the Governing Body/Trust/Board of Management.
>
> · Ensures alignment of institutional policies with governance and financial sustainability.
>
> · Provides support for policy approvals and resource allocation.

5\. One/two nominees from local society, Students and Alumni

> · Local society representative: Ensures that institutional initiatives are aligned with community needs and societal development.
>
> · Student nominee: Provides insights into the student experience, academic environment, and support services.
>
> · Alumni nominee: Brings perspectives on career readiness, employability, and institutional reputation.

6\. One/two nominees from Employers / Industrialists / Stakeholders

> · Includes representatives from industry, corporate sector, or professional organizations.
>
> · Provides inputs on employability, industry-academia linkages, internships, placements, and skill development.
>
> · Strengthens collaborations, MoUs, and consultancy opportunities.

7\. One of the senior teachers as the Coordinator/Director of IQAC

> · Acts as the operational head of IQAC.
>

>

>

>

>

>
> · Progress achieved in academic, administrative, and research domains.
>
> · Quality sustenance initiatives undertaken.
>
> o This ensures external validation of internal quality processes.
>

>
> o AQAR must be formally approved by institutional statutory bodies such as:
>
> · Governing Council.
>
> · Syndicate.
>
> · Board of Management.

> o Approval ensures that recommendations from AQAR are integrated into policy decisions and follow-up actions.

> o This enables alignment of quality measures with the institution’s strategic development plan.

>
> o IQAC should maintain an exclusive window on the institutional website:
>

**12. Mandatory Submission of AQAR**

> · **Background**
>
> o Earlier, the submission of Annual Quality Assurance Reports (AQARs) was not mandatory for Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) undergoing their 2nd and subsequent cycles of NAAC Assessment and Accreditation (A&A).
>
> o However, the Executive Committee of NAAC has revised the policy to make AQAR submission compulsory from 16th September 2016 onwards.
>
> · **Revised Pre-requisites for Submission of LOI (Letter of Intent)**

Institutions applying for 2nd and subsequent cycles of accreditation must comply with the following conditions:

1\. Functional IQAC

> · The institution must have a fully operational Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC).
>
> · IQAC should actively engage in quality monitoring, enhancement, and documentation.

2\. Minutes and Compliance Reports

> · The minutes of IQAC meetings and records of compliance with decisions must be uploaded on the institutional website.
>
> · This ensures transparency, accountability, and accessibility to stakeholders.

3\. Mandatory Submission of AQARs

> · Institutions must submit AQARs annually to NAAC without fail.
>
> · AQAR submission becomes a pre-condition for consideration in the 2nd and subsequent accreditation cycles.
>
> · Institutions failing to submit AQARs will not be eligible for reaccreditation.

4\. Public Accessibility of AQARs

> · AQARs must be uploaded on the institutional website.
>
> · Ensures that all stakeholders (students, parents, alumni, society, and regulatory bodies) can access and review institutional quality progress.
>
> · **Significance of Mandatory Submission**
>
> o Strengthens the culture of accountability and quality sustenance.
>
> o Promotes continuous monitoring and documentation of institutional activities.
>
> o Enhances credibility and transparency of higher education institutions.
>
> o Aligns institutional practices with NAAC’s mission of quality assurance and continuous improvement.

**In summary:** Since 16th September 2016, mandatory submission of AQARs, a functional IQAC, documented meeting minutes, and public disclosure of reports have become minimum institutional requirements for HEIs applying for 2nd and subsequent NAAC accreditation cycles.

**19. Academic and Administrative Audit (AAA) Committee**

**Introduction**

The Academic and Administrative Audit (AAA) is a systematic internal and external review process to evaluate the effectiveness, quality, and integrity of academic processes and administrative functions in an institution. The AAA Committee ensures that the institution aligns with its vision, mission, and quality benchmarks set by NAAC, NBA, UGC, and other regulatory/accrediting agencies.

**Objectives of the AAA Committee**

> · To monitor and assess the academic and administrative performance of departments and institutional units.
>
> · To identify strengths, weaknesses, and areas for improvement.
>
> · To promote a culture of quality enhancement, transparency, and accountability.
>
> · To provide recommendations for academic reforms, governance, and resource optimization.
>
> · To ensure compliance with NAAC/NBA accreditation criteria, UGC regulations, and institutional quality standards.

**Composition of the AAA Committee**

| **Role**                    | **Profile**                                                 |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Chairperson                 | Senior academic leader (Principal / External academician)   |
| Internal Members            | Deans, IQAC Coordinator, Senior Faculty from various Depts. |
| External Experts (Optional) | Eminent academicians/administrators from other institutions |
| NAAC/NBA Coordinators       | Ex-officio Members for documentation and process compliance |

> *In large universities/autonomous institutions, external experts are mandatory.*

**Scope of the Audit**

1\. Academic Audit

> · Curriculum design & revision aligned with Outcome-Based Education (OBE)
>
> · Academic delivery, teaching-learning process
>
> · Assessment and evaluation mechanisms
>
> · Faculty quality and research output
>
> · Student feedback & mentoring systems
>
> · Academic calendar adherence
>
> · CO-PO-PSO attainment
>
> · Industry-academia collaborations, FDPs, internships
>
> · Student performance and pass-out rates

2\. Administrative Audit

> · Governance structure & policy implementation
>
> · HR practices and faculty deployment

> · Admission, examination, and student support systems
>
> · Infrastructure and resource management
>
> · ICT usage in administration
>
> · Financial and procurement procedures
>
> · Documentation, file management, and compliance
>
> · Stakeholder grievance mechanisms

> · Conduct site visits, interact with faculty, staff, and students

> · Evaluate compliance with NAAC/NBA criteria

> · Follow up on implementation of suggestions

> · **Course-wise Feedback Reports:** Feedback collected from students regarding the quality of courses, teaching methods, and resource availability.

>
> · **Best Practices Records:** Documentation of best practices identified during audits, which can be replicated across departments and administrative units.

>
> · **Audit Procedures Manual:** A step-by-step guide on how to conduct audits, including roles and responsibilities, tools, and techniques used during the audit process.

**Best Practices for the Academic and Administrative Audit (AAA) Committee**

1\. Regular and Systematic Audits

> · **Frequency:** Ensure that both academic and administrative audits are conducted regularly—ideally once a year, but at least once every two years to assess both areas comprehensively.
>
> · **Scope:** Maintain a clear distinction between the academic audit (focused on teaching, learning, student performance, and faculty development) and the administrative audit (focused on infrastructure, financial management, and policy compliance).

2\. Well-Defined Criteria and Framework

> · **Standardized Audit Criteria:** Develop and maintain standardized audit tools that are aligned with national and international accreditation standards (such as NAAC, NBA).
>
> · **Clear Guidelines:** Prepare detailed guidelines for auditors to ensure consistency in the evaluation process and that they assess all relevant components comprehensively.

3\. Use of Technology and Data Analytics

> · **Digital Auditing:** Use digital platforms and tools for auditing processes to ensure accurate data collection, analysis, and reporting.
>
> · **Data-Driven Insights:** Incorporate data analytics to identify trends, gaps, and areas requiring improvement. This can help in making evidence-based decisions for improvement.

4\. Transparency in Processes

> · **Open Communication:** Maintain transparency during audits by ensuring that all departments, units, and stakeholders are informed well in advance about the audit procedures.
>
> · **Documentation and Reporting:** Ensure that all audit reports are clear, structured, and accessible to relevant stakeholders, including faculty, administration, and governing bodies.

5\. Feedback Mechanism

> · **Stakeholder Involvement:** Involve all key stakeholders, such as faculty members, administrative staff, and students, in the audit process by gathering feedback through surveys and discussions.
>
> · **Post-Audit Review:** After the audit, review the findings with all stakeholders and establish clear action plans to address identified issues.

6\. Development of Action Plans

> · **Action Plan Creation:** For every audit finding, develop a corrective or improvement plan with specific goals, timelines, and resources required.
>
> · **Follow-Up:** Ensure that the action plans are regularly followed up on to track the implementation and impact of changes.

7\. Continuous Improvement

> · **Benchmarking:** Compare the institution’s performance with similar institutions or national standards to gauge its relative standing.
>
> · **Continuous Professional Development (CPD):** Use audit findings to identify areas where faculty and staff need further training and development.

8\. Performance Review and Self-Assessment

> · **Self-Assessment:** Encourage departments and administrative units to conduct internal self-assessments before the audit to identify their strengths and weaknesses.
>
> · **Peer Reviews:** Involve peer departments or external experts for an objective evaluation.

9\. Ensuring Compliance

> · **Regulatory Compliance:** Regularly ensure that all academic and administrative activities comply with statutory and regulatory requirements such as UGC guidelines, accreditation requirements, and government policies.

10\. Management of Resources

> · **Resource Utilization:** Ensure that the audit evaluates the effective utilization of institutional resources, including finances, human resources, and infrastructure, and suggests improvements.

11\. Capacity Building for Auditors

> · **Training Auditors:** Provide regular training to auditors on the latest audit methodologies, standards, and tools to ensure the effectiveness of audits.

12\. Involvement of External Experts

> · **External Audit Experts:** Occasionally invite external auditors or experts to assess the institution’s practices. Their independent perspective helps ensure objectivity and credibility.

13\. Alignment with Institutional Goals

> · **Strategic Alignment:** Ensure that audit outcomes are aligned with the long-term vision and mission of the institution, especially in areas of quality assurance, student outcomes, and resource optimization.

14\. Reporting and Documentation

> · **Audit Reports:** Maintain comprehensive and well-documented audit reports that include observations, recommendations, and action plans. The reports should be reviewed by senior management and shared with the relevant committees for decision-making.

15\. Enhancing Institutional Reputation

> · **Promoting Best Practices:** Share successful audit outcomes and best practices in institutional meetings, newsletters, or on institutional websites to highlight continuous improvement efforts.

**Scope of Audit**

The audit covers student enrollment, faculty status, administrative/technical staff performance, teaching-learning processes, research activities, infrastructure use, and governance practices.

It systematically verifies:

> · Lesson plan adherence, tutorial and laboratory delivery, ERP/LMS usage, attendance automation.
>
> · Student support initiatives such as remedial classes, placement training, mentoring, and active learning sessions.
>
> · Faculty contributions through publications, projects, patents, professional memberships, training, and participation in academic/professional bodies.
>
> · Departmental governance via committee meetings, grievance redressal, stakeholder feedback, and alumni engagement.

**Monitoring Mechanisms**

> · Structured reporting on academic delivery, research productivity, and student progression.
>
> · Continuous evaluation through internal/external result analysis, quality circles, and administrative reviews.
>
> · Monthly documentation to ensure NAAC/AAA compliance and evidence-based decision-making.

**Expected Outcomes**

> · Promotes transparency, accountability, and inclusiveness in academic and administrative practices.
>
> · Ensures quality benchmarks are met in teaching, learning, research, and extension activities.
>
> · Enhances student-centric learning environments through tutorials, active learning, labs, and placement training.
>
> · Strengthens research culture and innovation by documenting projects, publications, and patents.
>
> · Builds robust stakeholder engagement with parents, alumni, employers, and society.

**Significance**

> · Functions as a quality enhancement and assurance tool to continuously improve performance.
>
> · Provides evidence for accreditation, ranking, and institutional reputation.
>
> · Establishes a culture of reflective practice, helping departments align with institutional goals.

**Conclusion**

The Academic and Administrative Audit (AAA) Committee plays a vital role in maintaining institutional accountability and continuous improvement. By incorporating these best practices, HEIs can ensure that they meet accreditation standards, improve teaching and learning outcomes, streamline administrative operations, and foster a culture of excellence and transparency.

**21. Annual Quality Assurance Report (AQAR)**

**1. Definition & Purpose**

> · The Annual Quality Assurance Report (AQAR) is a mandatory report prepared by the Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC) of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs).
>
> · It provides a systematic account of institutional activities related to teaching, learning, research, governance, student support, and quality enhancement for a particular academic year.
>
> · Serves as an evidence-based document for NAAC and as a tool for internal monitoring and continuous improvement.

**2. Objectives of AQAR**

> · To document quality-related initiatives and outcomes.
>
> · To monitor and evaluate the implementation of IQAC's Plan of Action.
>
> · To provide yearly updates to NAAC and stakeholders on institutional performance.
>
> · To promote transparency, accountability, and benchmarking.

**3. Structure of AQAR (as per NAAC guidelines)**

AQAR generally consists of the following sections:

1\. Part A – Institutional Information

> · Basic details of the institution, vision, mission, establishment of IQAC, composition, and functions.

2\. Part B – Curricular Aspects

> · Curriculum design, feedback, academic flexibility, value-added and skill-based courses.

3\. Part C – Teaching, Learning & Evaluation

> · Student enrollment, diversity, learning outcomes, evaluation reforms.

4\. Part D – Research, Innovations & Extension

> · Research projects, publications, patents, collaborations, extension activities.

5\. Part E – Infrastructure & Learning Resources

> · Library, ICT, physical facilities, labs, classrooms.

6\. Part F – Student Support & Progression

> · Scholarships, placement, progression to higher education, student participation.

7\. Part G – Governance, Leadership & Management

> · Institutional vision, leadership, e-governance, faculty development, financial support.

8\. Part H – Institutional Values & Best Practices

> · Environmental consciousness, gender equity, best practices, institutional distinctiveness.

**4. Submission of AQAR**

> · AQAR must be approved by statutory bodies (e.g., Governing Council, Syndicate) before submission.
>
> · *Submission to NAAC is done via soft copy (.doc/.docx/.pdf) through official email (capuaqar@gmail.com).*
>
> · The institution must also upload AQAR on its website for public access and stakeholder transparency.

**5. Benefits of AQAR**

> · Provides a roadmap of achievements and gaps for institutional planning.
>
> · Ensures regular internal monitoring of academic and administrative performance.
>
> · Enhances preparedness for NAAC peer team visits.
>
> · Strengthens institutional credibility, ranking, and visibility.
>
> · Acts as a self-assessment tool for continuous quality enhancement.

**22. Objectives of AQAR**

1\. Documentation of Institutional Progress

> · To systematically record the academic, administrative, and research activities of the institution on a yearly basis.
>
> · To serve as a comprehensive evidence document for internal and external quality assurance.

2\. Monitoring Quality Enhancement Measures

> · To track the implementation of the Plan of Action set by IQAC at the beginning of the year.
>
> · To measure whether the planned quality initiatives have been effectively executed and achieved.

3\. Strengthening Internal Accountability

> · To ensure transparency and accountability in institutional functioning.
>
> · To provide a structured mechanism for regular self-assessment and corrective actions.

4\. Facilitating Accreditation & Compliance

> · To act as a mandatory compliance document for NAAC accreditation cycles.
>
> · To provide evidence for peer team reviews during institutional visits.

5\. Promoting Stakeholder Participation

> · To share progress with students, faculty, alumni, employers, and society through website disclosure.
>
> · To encourage participative governance by integrating stakeholder feedback into future plans.

6\. Evidence-Based Decision-Making

> · To provide institutional leadership with data-driven insights for academic planning, resource allocation, and policy formulation.
>
> · To establish a quality database for future reference and continuous improvement.

7\. Institutional Benchmarking

> · To help the institution benchmark its performance against national and global standards.
>
> · To highlight best practices, innovations, and success stories for replication and scaling.

>

**2. Approval Process**

The draft AQAR must be reviewed and approved by the institution's statutory bodies before submission:

> · Governing Council / Syndicate.
>
> · Academic Council / Board of Management.
>
> · This ensures institutional accountability and integration of quality measures into official policy decisions.

> · AQAR must be submitted annually to NAAC.
>
> · Mode of submission:
>
> o Soft copy only in Word (.doc/.docx) or PDF format.
>

>
> · File name should include Institution Track ID and Name (e.g., *Samudra Arts and Science College, Maharashtra.doc*).
>
> · Hard copy submission is not required.
>
> · Acknowledgement of receipt will be sent by NAAC via email.

**4. Website Disclosure**

>
> · Documents should be kept year-wise in an archive section for transparency and easy stakeholder access.

**5. Mandatory Requirement**

> · Since 16th September 2016, AQAR submission is mandatory for institutions undergoing 2nd and subsequent NAAC accreditation cycles.
>
> · Non-submission may result in ineligibility for reaccreditation.

**6. Benefits of Timely Submission**

> · Ensures compliance with NAAC regulations.

**31. Policy Declaration**

The Institution hereby declares its commitment to quality assurance, enhancement, and sustenance in all its academic and administrative functions. In alignment with the framework of the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC), the Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC) is recognized as the central quality monitoring and enhancement mechanism of the institution.

Through this policy, the institution affirms that:

> · Quality is a continuous process and not a one-time exercise.
>
> · IQAC will act as a catalyst for quality improvement, ensuring that all institutional activities are aligned with the vision and mission of the institution.
>
> · All stakeholders—students, faculty, administrative staff, alumni, employers, and society—shall be active partners in the quality journey.
>
> · Regular self-evaluation, feedback collection, and monitoring shall be conducted to strengthen academic excellence, governance, and service delivery.
>
> · The institution shall ensure mandatory submission of Annual Quality Assurance Reports (AQARs) and compliance with NAAC's accreditation requirements.
>
> · Transparency, accountability, and participatory governance shall guide all IQAC-led quality initiatives.
>
> · The institution is committed to innovation, inclusivity, employability, and community engagement as integral aspects of quality culture.

This declaration reinforces our pledge to foster a vibrant academic environment, making quality a habit and culture across all levels of institutional functioning.
