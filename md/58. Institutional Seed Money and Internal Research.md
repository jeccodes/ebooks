**Abstract**

The Institutional Seed Money and Internal Research Grant Policy establishes a structured and transparent framework for promoting a strong research and innovation culture within Higher Educational Institutions (HEIs). This policy serves as an institutional mechanism to provide internal financial assistance to faculty members, researchers, and students, enabling them to initiate, pilot, or strengthen early-stage research projects. It acts as a catalyst to transform conceptual research ideas into viable, evidence-based projects capable of attracting external funding from national and international agencies such as DST, UGC, AICTE, and SERB.

The policy's primary objective is to encourage innovation, interdisciplinary collaboration, and capacity building across departments by providing initial seed funding for research activities that generate preliminary data, develop prototypes, and create proof-of-concept results. Through this mechanism, institutions can bridge the funding gap between idea incubation and large-scale sponsored projects, thereby enhancing research productivity, quality, and societal relevance. It also aims to nurture young researchers and promote student participation under faculty mentorship, fostering an ecosystem of inquiry, creativity, and experimentation.

Administratively, the policy is governed by the Research & Development (R&D) Cell under the leadership of the Dean (Research) and the Seed Money Review Committee (SMRC). The SMRC, comprising the Dean (Research), IQAC representative, Finance Officer, Subject Experts, and R&D Coordinator, is entrusted with the evaluation, monitoring, and verification of all internally funded projects. Proposals are invited annually or biannually, evaluated for novelty, feasibility, alignment with institutional research thrust areas, and their potential to attract future external funding.

The policy defines allowable expenditures under six key categories — consumables, minor equipment or software, travel, manpower support, publication and dissemination, and prototype or patent development — ensuring judicious and auditable fund utilization. It mandates the submission of Utilization Certificates (UCs), progress reports, and a final Outcome Verification Dossier, which includes documented outputs such as publications, patents, prototypes, and student research. These dossiers are verified by the SMRC and archived in the Institutional Research Repository, establishing transparency, accountability, and readiness for internal and external audits.

Importantly, the policy integrates research management with national and global quality frameworks, aligning institutional efforts with NAAC Criterion 3 (Research, Innovations and Extension), NBA Criterion 5 (Research and Development), NIRF Research and Professional Practice parameters, and QS I-GAUGE Innovation and Impact indicators. Through this alignment, the policy not only strengthens institutional performance metrics but also contributes to improved rankings, reputation, and academic excellence.

By establishing a standardized process encompassing proposal submission, evaluation, financial monitoring, and outcome verification, this policy ensures that research activities within the institution are strategically directed, ethically compliant, and outcome-oriented. It promotes interdisciplinary collaboration, enhances institutional visibility, and builds a foundation for sustainable innovation. Ultimately, the Institutional Seed Money and Internal Research Grant Policy reinforces the institution's vision of fostering a self-sustaining, accountable, and globally competitive research ecosystem that contributes meaningfully to national development goals and the advancement of knowledge in higher education.

**1. Introduction**

The institutional document titled "Institutional Seed Money and Internal Research Grant Policy in Higher Educational Institutions (HEIs)" serves as a comprehensive framework for fostering a sustainable and vibrant research culture within higher educational institutions. This policy is established to provide structured, transparent, and equitable internal financial support to faculty members, researchers, and students, enabling them to initiate, pilot, or enhance innovative research projects. The overarching purpose of the policy is to act as a catalyst for research culture, bridging the critical gap between conceptual research ideas and the stage of securing full-scale external funding. By institutionalizing a well-defined mechanism for internal research grants, the policy seeks to empower the academic community to engage in independent, interdisciplinary, and impactful research activities that contribute to knowledge creation, innovation, and societal development.

At its core, the policy is designed to stimulate intellectual curiosity and creative exploration within the institution by providing a financial foundation for early-stage research endeavors. It acknowledges that one of the major challenges faced by faculty and young researchers is the lack of seed funding necessary to generate preliminary results or establish feasibility for larger research proposals. The institutional seed money, therefore, functions as a bridge mechanism that transforms an idea into a viable research project capable of attracting external sponsorship. By offering modest but strategic financial support, the institution ensures that promising research ideas are not lost due to funding constraints but are nurtured from inception to maturity. This aligns directly with the goals of the National Education Policy (NEP 2020), which emphasizes research, innovation, and interdisciplinary collaboration as the cornerstones of higher education excellence.

The objectives of the Institutional Seed Money and Internal Research Grant Policy are multi-dimensional, targeting early-stage support, capacity building, innovation promotion, and alignment with national and global research priorities. Firstly, the policy aims to encourage early-stage research by funding projects that involve exploratory studies, pilot investigations, and feasibility assessments. Such support enables researchers to collect preliminary data, validate hypotheses, and establish methodological frameworks, thereby enhancing the scientific rigor and competitiveness of their future proposals. Secondly, it seeks to foster innovation by promoting the design and development of prototypes, models, or technological products that may lead to patentable outcomes or commercial applications. Through this, the institution contributes to the national innovation ecosystem, motivating researchers to translate academic knowledge into practical solutions that address real-world problems.

A third major objective is to enhance institutional research capacity by creating an environment that supports both independent and collaborative research. The policy encourages faculty members to mentor students and young scholars, thereby embedding research-based learning into the academic fabric of the institution. By facilitating interdepartmental and cross-disciplinary collaborations, it enables the convergence of diverse ideas and methodologies, leading to more holistic and impactful research outcomes. Furthermore, the policy aims to strengthen linkages with industries, communities, and external organizations, making institutional research more socially relevant and economically beneficial. A fourth and equally critical objective is to prepare the research community for external funding opportunities. Through seed-funded projects, researchers can produce preliminary results, build credibility, and craft competitive proposals for submission to national and international agencies such as the Department of Science and Technology (DST), University Grants Commission (UGC), All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE), and Science and Engineering Research Board (SERB).

Beyond immediate project outcomes, the policy serves as an instrument for strategic institutional growth. It is designed to align the institution's research priorities with national missions and Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), promoting research that contributes to inclusive and sustainable societal development. By linking institutional efforts with broader national and global research agendas, the policy strengthens the institution's position in competitive benchmarking and accreditation systems. Specifically, it supports documentation and performance metrics under NAAC Criterion 3 (Research, Innovations and Extension), NBA Criterion 5 (Research and Development), and the NIRF Research and Professional Practice (RP) parameters, as well as innovation and impact indicators within the QS I-GAUGE framework. This alignment not only enhances institutional reputation but also ensures that internal funding directly contributes to measurable academic excellence and external recognition.

The Institutional Seed Money and Internal Research Grant Policy in Higher Educational Institutions (HEIs) further establishes a formal, transparent structure for the administration of internal grants. The process begins with a call for proposals announced by the Research and Development (R&D) Cell or the Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC). Interested faculty and researchers are required to submit structured proposals including title, objectives, methodology, budget estimates, and expected outcomes. The proposals undergo evaluation by the Seed Money Review Committee (SMRC)—a multidisciplinary body constituted under the Dean (Research) or Director (R&D). The SMRC includes representatives from the IQAC, finance section, subject experts, and the R&D coordinator as Member Secretary. Evaluation is conducted based on predefined criteria such as novelty, feasibility, alignment with institutional thrust areas, and potential for generating external funding. Only projects that meet the standards of academic quality, ethical compliance, and financial justification are recommended for sanction.

Once projects are approved, funds are disbursed in a phased manner, and investigators are expected to submit progress reports, utilization certificates, and supporting documents to ensure accountability. The policy mandates mid-term monitoring and final outcome verification to evaluate the extent of goal achievement and fund utilization. The Outcome Verification Dossier, which includes publications, patents, prototypes, and student research outputs, serves as tangible evidence of the project's success. This dossier is reviewed by the SMRC and archived in the Institutional Research Repository for transparency, audit readiness, and accreditation purposes.

The policy also emphasizes ethical compliance and good research practices, ensuring that all projects adhere to the guidelines of the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) and the Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) policy. Any deviation, misuse of funds, or non-performance is addressed under the Compliance and Disciplinary Clause, which ensures accountability and integrity in institutional research.

In conclusion, the Institutional Seed Money and Internal Research Grant Policy in Higher Educational Institutions (HEIs) acts as a cornerstone in institutionalizing research excellence, innovation, and integrity. It not only empowers faculty and students to explore new ideas but also positions the institution as a hub for meaningful, outcome-driven research aligned with national priorities and global standards. Through its systematic structure—spanning proposal submission, evaluation, funding, monitoring, and outcome verification—the policy ensures that research becomes an integral part of institutional culture rather than a peripheral activity. By bridging the gap between concept and funded research, this policy establishes a self-sustaining research ecosystem that enhances academic productivity, fosters innovation, and strengthens the institution's standing in NAAC, NBA, NIRF, and QS I-GAUGE accreditation frameworks.

**2. Institutional Policy Document on Seed Money / Internal Research Grant**

**1. Title of the Policy**

Institutional Seed Money and Internal Research Grant Policy

**2. Policy Objective**

- To promote a strong research culture within the institution by providing initial financial support to faculty, researchers, and students.

- To encourage innovation, interdisciplinary collaboration, and the generation of preliminary data for external research funding applications.

- To align institutional research activities with national priorities, Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), and the institution's strategic plan.

**3. Scope and Applicability**

- Applicable to all permanent and full-time faculty members, research scholars, and students of the institution.

- Covers academic departments, research centres, and interdisciplinary units recognized by the Research & Development (R&D) Cell.

- Supports new and ongoing research initiatives that align with the institution's research thrust areas and national missions.

**4. Policy Goals**

- Encourage faculty to initiate independent and collaborative research projects.

- Support pilot studies that can lead to external grants from funding agencies such as DST, DBT, AICTE, UGC, SERB, ICSSR, etc.

- Facilitate prototype development, innovation, and patenting.

- Provide an opportunity for student participation in research under faculty mentorship.

- Strengthen the institution's research output, visibility, and ranking in NAAC, NIRF, and QS frameworks.

**5. Governance and Administration**

- The Institutional Research & Development (R&D) Cell will administer the scheme under the supervision of the Dean (Research) / Director (R&D).

- A Seed Money Review Committee (SMRC) will be constituted, comprising:

<!-- -->

- Dean (Research) – Chairperson

- Head of IQAC – Member

- Two Subject Experts (internal/external) – Members

- Research Coordinator – Member Secretary

<!-- -->

- The committee shall oversee proposal evaluation, fund allocation, and progress monitoring.

**6. Eligibility Criteria**

- Faculty Eligibility:

<!-- -->

- Full-time permanent or contract faculty with at least one year of service.

- Should not have an active internal grant in the same financial year.

<!-- -->

- Student Eligibility:

<!-- -->

- Final-year UG/PG students and PhD scholars working under an eligible faculty member as Co-PI.

<!-- -->

- Collaborative Projects:

<!-- -->

- Encouraged between multiple departments, centres, or institutions.

**7. Types of Grants**

| **Type**                        | **Eligible Applicants**           | **Duration** | **Funding Limit**     |
|---------------------------------|-----------------------------------|--------------|-----------------------|
| Minor Research Project          | Faculty / Research Scholar        | 1 year       | ₹25,000 – ₹2,00,000   |
| Major Internal Research Project | Faculty (with student assistants) | 2 years      | ₹2,00,000 – ₹5,00,000 |
| Student Innovation Project      | UG/PG/PhD Students                | 6–12 months  | ₹10,000 – ₹50,000     |

**8. Budget Components**

Allowable expenditures include:

- Consumables (chemicals, materials, software)

- Minor equipment purchases

- Travel (data collection, field visits)

- Student research assistants or project interns

- Publication charges (conference/journal)

- Prototype development and patent filing costs

Non-allowable expenditures include:

- Major equipment purchase (\>₹1,00,000)

- Salary of PI/Co-PI

- Infrastructure development unrelated to the project

**9. Application and Approval Process**

1\. Call for Proposals:

- Issued annually by the R&D Cell / IQAC.

2\. Proposal Submission:

- PIs submit proposals with objectives, methodology, expected outcomes, and budget justification.

3\. Review and Evaluation:

- The SMRC evaluates proposals based on novelty, feasibility, institutional alignment, and societal impact.

4\. Approval and Sanction:

- Approved proposals receive formal sanction letters and fund release orders.

5\. Disbursement:

- Funds disbursed in two phases: 70% initial + 30% after mid-term review.

**10. Monitoring and Evaluation**

- Periodic Progress Reports (half-yearly and final) to be submitted to the R&D Cell.

- Utilization Certificates (UC) to be provided at project completion.

- Projects may be subject to site inspection or audit by the SMRC.

- Non-performance or non-compliance may lead to withdrawal of funds or ineligibility for future grants.

**11. Expected Outcomes**

- Research publications in peer-reviewed journals.

- Patents or intellectual property registrations.

- Prototypes or technology solutions addressing community/societal needs.

- Development of externally funded project proposals.

- Student participation in national-level research and innovation contests.

**12. Incentives and Recognition**

- Faculty and students producing noteworthy outcomes shall be recognized during the Annual Research Day or Academic Council Meetings.

- Successful seed-funded projects may be prioritized for institutional nomination to external funding agencies.

**13. Ethical and Legal Compliance**

- All projects must comply with the Institutional Research Ethics Committee (IREC) guidelines.

- Intellectual Property (IP) generated shall be governed by the Institutional IPR Policy.

- Data and research integrity must adhere to institutional and national research conduct codes.

**Purpose of Progress Reports**

> To monitor the implementation and progress of projects funded under the *Institutional Seed Money / Internal Research Grant*.

- Submitted six months after project initiation or midway through the project duration.

- Helps determine eligibility for second installment of funds (usually 30%).

Final Progress Report (Completion Report)

- Submitted within one month of project completion.

- Includes comprehensive documentation of work done, research findings, outputs achieved, and financial utilization.

- Serves as the basis for project closure and audit by the R&D Cell.

1\. Administrative Details

- Project Title, Sanction Number, PI/Co-PI Details, Department, and Duration.

- Reiteration of project goals and brief of research methodology adopted.

- Summary of experiments, data collection, prototype development, or other activities completed.

4\. Achievements and Outputs

- Publications, patents, models, student involvement, or collaborations.

5\. Budget Utilization

- Itemized statement of expenditure vs. sanctioned amount, certified by accounts.

6\. Challenges and Corrective Measures

- Difficulties faced, delays, or deviations from the proposed plan with justification.

7\. Future Plans

- Continuation of work, proposal submission for external grants, or technology transfer possibilities.

- Remaining funds (if applicable) are released.

- Committee may recommend modification, extension, or termination of the project.

**6. Utilization Certificates (UC) – Requirement at Project Completion**

**Definition and Purpose**

- A Utilization Certificate (UC) is an official financial document submitted at the end of a project to certify that the granted seed money or internal research funds have been used strictly for the intended purposes.

- It acts as a financial accountability statement ensuring transparency, compliance, and closure of the project.

- The UC confirms that expenditure matches the sanctioned budget, following institutional financial norms and auditing standards.

**Issuing Authority**

- The UC must be prepared and signed by the Principal Investigator (PI) and verified by the institutional accounts section or Finance Officer / Registrar.

- It is then endorsed by the Head of the Department (HoD) and the Dean (Research) / Director (R&D) before submission to the R&D Cell.

**When to Submit**

- Submitted at the time of project completion, typically within one month after the final progress report.

- Mandatory for project closure, financial audit, and eligibility for future grants.

**Key Components of a Utilization Certificate**

1\. Administrative Information

- Project Title

- Sanction Letter Number and Date

- Name of PI/Co-PI, Department, and Duration

- Sanctioned Amount and Disbursed Installments

- Opening Balance (if any)

- Expenditure Details (item-wise, as per approved heads such as consumables, equipment, travel, publication, manpower, etc.)

- Total Expenditure incurred during the project

- Unspent Balance, if any, to be returned or carried forward

3\. Certification Clause

- A declaration that funds have been utilized solely for the purposes sanctioned and in accordance with institutional and funding norms.

- Statement that the audited accounts have been verified by the Finance Department.

4\. Signatures and Endorsements

- Principal Investigator (PI) – certifying correct fund use

- Head of Department (HoD) – verifying academic authenticity

- Accounts Officer / Finance Officer – validating financial accuracy

- Dean (Research) – approving on behalf of the institution

**7. Non-Performance or Non-Compliance in Institutional Seed Money / Internal Research Grants**

**Definition of Non-Performance**

- Refers to failure to achieve project objectives, deliverables, or timelines as specified in the sanctioned proposal.

Examples include:

- Minimal or no measurable progress after fund release.

- Repeated delay in submitting half-yearly or final reports.

- Inadequate research output (no prototype, paper, or result).

- Abandonment or discontinuation of project activities without approval.

**Definition of Non-Compliance**

- Refers to violation of institutional or financial norms, ethical guidelines, or policy terms governing the internal grant.

Common non-compliance cases include:

- Misuse or diversion of funds to unauthorized purposes.

- Purchase of items outside sanctioned budget heads.

- Absence of financial documentation (vouchers, bills, UC).

- Breach of research ethics (data fabrication, plagiarism, IP violations).

- Failure to respond to audit queries or inspection findings.

**Monitoring Mechanisms**

- The Research & Development (R&D) Cell and the Seed Money Review Committee (SMRC) periodically evaluate all ongoing projects.

Indicators tracked include:

- Timely submission of progress reports.

- Evidence of expenditure utilization.

- Alignment between proposed and executed activities.

- Compliance with institutional ethics and IPR policy.

**Consequences of Non-Performance / Non-Compliance**

Immediate Actions

- Written notice issued by the R&D Cell / SMRC highlighting deficiencies.

- Suspension of ongoing fund disbursement (second installment withheld).

Corrective Measures

- PI asked to provide justification or submit revised plan within a stipulated period.

- Conditional continuation only upon satisfactory explanation.

Final Actions (if unresolved)

- Withdrawal of remaining funds.

- Recovery of misused or unspent amount through Finance Office.

- Project termination with "Non-compliance" status recorded.

- Ineligibility for future internal/external grants for a specified period (usually 2–3 years).

- Reporting of case to the Institutional Ethics Committee if ethical breaches are found.

**Institutional Governance and Documentation**

- All actions and correspondence must be recorded by the R&D Cell in the Research Grant Compliance Register.

Each non-compliance case should include:

- Copy of warning letter / show-cause notice.

- Audit or inspection findings.

- PI's written explanation and committee's decision.

- Maintained for audit trail, NAAC / NBA / NIRF reporting, and internal transparency.

**Preventive Measures**

- Pre-award briefing of PIs on financial and ethical obligations.

- Workshops on financial documentation and research ethics.

**8. Outcome Verification in Institutional Seed Money / Internal Research Grant Projects**

**Purpose of Outcome Verification**

- To validate the quality, relevance, and impact of research outcomes before project closure.

**Scope of Outcome Verification**

- Conducted by the Seed Money Review Committee (SMRC) in coordination with the R&D Cell during the final evaluation or audit phase.

- Ensures that outcomes align with the approved proposal objectives, institutional research priorities, and SDG/NEP-2020 alignment.

- Documented proof of collaboration or knowledge transfer to industry or community.

- Verification through MoUs, pilot implementations, or consultancy reports.

**12. Allowable Expenditures under Institutional Seed Money / Internal Research Grant**

*(As per Institutional Research & Innovation Policy Guidelines)*

**Purpose**

- To ensure that funds allocated under the *Institutional Seed Money / Internal Research Grant* are utilized transparently, effectively, and strictly for approved research-related purposes.

- To define permissible expenditure categories that directly support research execution, innovation, and dissemination.

- To align internal funding practices with institutional finance norms and auditable standards (NAAC/NBA/NIRF compliance).

**1. Consumables**

Description:

- Includes short-term use materials, chemicals, reagents, and laboratory supplies necessary for experiments, testing, or analysis.

Examples:

- Reagents, solvents, filters, petri dishes, glassware, culture media, electrodes, data collection materials, etc.

Guidelines:

- Must be directly linked to the objectives of the approved project.

- Procurement should follow the institutional purchase procedure.

- Proper stock register entry and bill submission mandatory.

**2. Minor Equipment / Software**

Description:

- Purchase of minor tools, instruments, or licensed software directly required for the project's execution.

Examples:

- Sensors, handheld testing devices, portable analyzers, open-source software licenses, data analysis tools, or small-scale lab apparatus.

Guidelines:

- Total equipment expenditure typically should not exceed 30% of the total grant amount.

- Equipment becomes institutional property after project completion and must be tagged accordingly.

- Installation and warranty documentation must be maintained in project records.

**3. Travel**

Description:

- Covers expenses related to fieldwork, data collection, and official travel for project purposes.

Examples:

- Travel to field sites for data gathering, stakeholder consultations, or sample collection.

- Attendance at conferences/workshops only if directly linked to dissemination of project results.

Guidelines:

- Prior approval from the R&D Cell required for travel outside the state/country.

- Must include travel authorization, ticket copies, and receipts.

- TA/DA as per institutional norms.

**4. Manpower Support**

Description:

- Provision for short-term engagement of research assistants, student interns, or project fellows to assist in project execution.

Examples:

- Student stipends, part-time field investigators, or data entry operators.

Guidelines:

- Payment must be as per approved rates and on submission of attendance/assignment reports.

- No permanent employment or recurring liability on the institution.

- All payments must be processed through the Accounts Section.

**5. Publication and Dissemination**

Description:

- Funding for communicating research findings, sharing results, and enhancing project visibility.

Examples:

- Journal publication fees (preferably in Scopus/UGC-CARE-listed journals).

- Printing of research reports, posters, and conference presentations.

- Design and layout for project documentation or dissemination events.

Guidelines:

- Acknowledgment of institutional support is mandatory in all publications.

- Proof of publication (DOI/acceptance letter) must be attached with the final report.

- Predatory or unindexed journals will not be reimbursed.

**6. Prototype Development / Patenting Fees**

Description:

- Expenditure related to the design, fabrication, testing, and intellectual property protection of innovative products or processes developed during the project.

Examples:

- Prototype fabrication materials, 3D printing, simulation software, design consultancy, patent filing, and legal documentation fees.

Guidelines:

- Prototype expenditures must be approved under the sanctioned proposal.

- Patent filing should be routed through the Institutional IPR Cell.

- Receipts and filing acknowledgments must be attached for reimbursement.

**7. General Conditions for All Expenditures**

- Expenditures must:

<!-- -->

- Be directly related to the approved project activities.

- Be incurred within the project duration only.

- Follow institutional procurement, finance, and audit rules.

<!-- -->

- All financial transactions must be supported by original invoices, vouchers, and payment receipts.

- Unspent funds must be refunded or adjusted at project closure.

**14. Applicant Eligibility & Formalities**

*(Under Institutional Seed Money and Internal Research Grant Policy in Higher Educational Institutions – HEIs)*

The Institutional Seed Money and Internal Research Grant Policy defines a clear and transparent eligibility framework for faculty applicants to ensure fair participation and accountability in the research funding process. These eligibility criteria and procedural formalities are designed to identify and support deserving faculty members, especially new entrants, who demonstrate potential for academic excellence and research innovation.

**1. Eligibility Criteria**

1\. Full-Time Faculty Appointment

- The applicant must be a full-time, newly recruited faculty member appointed as Assistant Professor in the institution or any of its recognized extension centers within the last three (3) years.

- This provision ensures that early-career faculty members receive institutional support to initiate independent research and establish a research trajectory in their area of expertise.

2\. Academic Qualification

- The Principal Investigator (PI) must possess a Ph.D. degree in the relevant discipline to qualify for applying under the Seed Money Scheme.

- In exceptional interdisciplinary or practice-oriented projects, applicants without a Ph.D. may apply only with prior approval of the Dean (Research) and endorsement from the Head of Department (HoD).

3\. Re-Application after Rejection

- If a proposal is not approved by the Seed Money Review Committee (SMRC), the PI is permitted to resubmit a revised or new proposal within the same academic year.

- The revised submission must address the committee's observations and demonstrate improvements in feasibility, methodology, or expected outcomes.

4\. Continuity of the Scheme and PI Responsibility

- The Seed Money Scheme (SMS) will be discontinued automatically if the PI proceeds on unsanctioned leave exceeding 30 days.

- In such cases, the PI will be required to refund the entire amount received under the scheme to the institution.

- However, if the absence is due to unforeseen health or medical emergencies, the PI may seek special permission from the Dean (Research) for temporary suspension or continuation of the project upon return, subject to documentary proof.

5\. Resignation or Discontinuation of Service

- In the event that the PI resigns or discontinues employment before the completion of the sanctioned project, he/she shall be liable to refund the entire seed money received under the scheme to the institution.

- The project will be officially terminated, and the utilization certificate (UC) must be submitted up to the date of discontinuation for audit records.

6\. Maternity Leave and Project Extension

- Female faculty members availing maternity leave during the project tenure will be granted special permission to extend the project duration upon resuming duty.

- The PI must submit a formal request for extension prior to proceeding on maternity leave, specifying the anticipated leave duration and proposed revised completion timeline.

- The Dean (Research) and SMRC may approve the extension, ensuring continuity without penalty.

7\. Completion, Reporting, and Presentation Requirements

- Upon completion of the project, the PI must submit a comprehensive final report detailing:

- Research objectives achieved,

- Data collected or results obtained,

- Publications, patents, or innovations produced, and

- Societal or academic impact.

**UCs (Utilization Certificates) submitted by faculty to institution**

**Purpose & role**

- UC (Utilization Certificate) confirms that seed money/internal grant was spent only for the sanctioned project and within the approved period.

- Forms the primary basis for awarding the “Utilized (2 marks)” under NBA's Institution Seed Money / IRG (5 marks).

**When to submit**

- Annually (end of financial year) and at project closure (whichever comes first).

- Also upon grant revision/extension or additional tranche release.

**Who signs**

- PI (certifies work & spending), Head of Department, and Finance Officer/Chartered Accountant (certifies accounts).

- Optional endorsement by Dean Research/Registrar for final closure.

**Mandatory contents (must appear)**

- Project identifiers: PI name, Department, Project Title, Sanction Order No./Date, Project Code/Budget Head.

- Financial summary (₹): Sanctioned, Released/Received (CAY-wise if applicable), Expenditure (head-wise), Unspent balance/carry-forward.

- Utilization period: start–end dates covered by the UC (e.g., 01-Apr-2024 to 31-Mar-2025).

- Certification text: funds utilized as per rules and sanction terms, with true & fair view of accounts.

- Signatures, names, designations, dates; institution seal.

**Head-wise expenditure (typical)**

- Equipment/Software, Consumables, Manpower/Student Assistantship, Travel/Field work, Contingency, Overheads (if allowed).

**Required attachments**

- Statement of Expenditure (SoE) mapped to sanction heads.

- Ledger extract/Project account & Bank JV/credit note (internal grant transfer proof).

- Key invoices/voucher summary, asset register entries for durable items.

- Progress/Final report matching the UC period; stock/consumable issue register (if relevant).

**Compliance & reconciliation checklist**

- Amounts tally across Sanction Order ↔ Tracker ↔ Bank/Accounts ↔ UC.

- Dates align with sanction validity and reporting cadence.

- Expenditure heads do not exceed sanctioned limits; procurement norms followed.

- Unspent shown as refunded/carry-forwarded with approval note.

**CAY mapping (for NBA tables)**

- Record actual receipts in CAYm1/CAYm2/CAYm3 (₹) and utilized (₹ Lakhs) for the same CAY as the UC period.

- For multi-year projects, file separate annual UCs; for short projects, a single final UC is acceptable.

**How it impacts marks (built into your tracker)**

- Received (3 marks): based on Total Received (₹ Lakhs) over CAYm1–m3.

- Utilized (2 marks): proportional to Total Utilized ÷ Total Sanctioned (→ 100% = full 2 marks).

- Keep UCs timely and complete to evidence full utilization.

**Best practices**

- Use a standard UC template across departments; pre-fill project code & heads.

- Maintain a UC calendar with reminders 30 days before FY end.

- Digitally sign PDFs; store with consistent names: UC\_\<Year\>\_\<Dept\>\_\<PI\>\_\<ShortTitle\>.pdf.

- Cross-reference UC number/date in your Institution_SeedMoney_Tracker.

**Common pitfalls (avoid)**

- Missing project code/budget head → weak audit trail.

- Head misclassification (e.g., equipment claimed as consumables).

- Late submission or period mismatch with receipts.

- Outcomes not attached (publications/prototypes) when UC claims utilization toward those activities.

**Sample one-line certification (adapt)**

- “Certified that an amount of ₹ X has been utilized during \<period\> for \<project title\> under Sanction No. \<…\>, strictly as per terms & institutional financial rules, and the unspent balance of ₹ Y has been carried forward/refunded as applicable.”
