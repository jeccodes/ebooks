**Abstract**

The Sustainable Development Goal 3 (SDG-3) — Good Health and Well-Being — emphasizes ensuring healthy lives and promoting well-being for all at all ages. Within the framework of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs), the integration of SDG-3 reflects a transformative approach toward developing institutions as centers of health awareness, preventive education, and community engagement. HEIs serve as critical agents in promoting physical, mental, and emotional health through education, research, innovation, and social outreach.

This goal aligns with the World Health Organization’s (WHO) global mandate to strengthen health systems, combat diseases, improve maternal and child health, and ensure universal access to healthcare services. It encompasses thirteen targets addressing major global health challenges such as maternal and child mortality, infectious and non-communicable diseases, mental health, substance abuse, pollution-related illnesses, and universal health coverage. The implementation of SDG-3 within HEIs provides an opportunity to integrate wellness education, research on public health issues, and institutional policies that promote sustainable health practices across campuses.

HEIs contribute to SDG-3 through initiatives such as the establishment of yoga and meditation centers, counseling and mental health services, drug addiction monitoring systems, and collaborative research with health organizations. Through interdisciplinary teaching and partnerships with government bodies like the University Grants Commission (UGC), AICTE, ICMR, and the Ministry of AYUSH, universities are promoting a culture of health consciousness and sustainability. Moreover, HEIs play a vital role in capacity building by training healthcare professionals, organizing awareness campaigns, and implementing community-based wellness programs.

The incorporation of SDG-3 into the academic environment ensures that health and well-being are recognized as integral components of sustainable education. It enables the development of socially responsible graduates who are equipped to address global health challenges while contributing to national and international well-being agendas. By aligning institutional policies with SDG-3, HEIs not only enhance the quality of education but also foster an environment that prioritizes human health, equity, and sustainable living—ensuring that no one is left behind in the pursuit of a healthier, more resilient future.

**1. SDG-3: Ensure Healthy Lives and Promote Well-being for All at All Ages**

**SDG-3: Good Health and Well-Being**

Overview:

> • Goal 3 of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) aims to ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages.
>
> • It recognizes health as a fundamental human right and a cornerstone of sustainable development.
>
> • SDG-3 emphasizes the importance of universal health coverage (UHC), access to affordable medicines and vaccines, strengthening health systems, and addressing environmental determinants of health.

Vision:

> • To build a world where every individual enjoys physical, mental, and social well-being, free from preventable diseases, and supported by resilient healthcare systems.

Key Focus Areas of SDG-3:

> • **Maternal and Child Health:** Reduce maternal and infant mortality through safe births and neonatal care.
>
> • **Communicable Diseases:** Eradicate AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, and neglected tropical diseases.
>
> • **Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs):** Prevent and treat lifestyle-related diseases such as cancer, diabetes, and cardiovascular conditions.
>
> • **Mental Health:** Promote psychological well-being and reduce suicide rates.
>
> • **Substance Abuse:** Control drug addiction and harmful alcohol consumption through rehabilitation and awareness.
>
> • **Road Safety:** Minimize global deaths and injuries caused by traffic accidents.
>
> • **Reproductive Health:** Ensure universal access to reproductive healthcare and education.
>
> • **Pollution and Environmental Health:** Reduce illness and mortality caused by air, water, and soil pollution.
>
> • **Healthcare Access:** Achieve universal health coverage, ensuring affordability and accessibility.
>
> • **Health Workforce:** Strengthen healthcare capacity, training, and global health governance.

Targets and Indicators (Summary):

> • 3.1: Reduce maternal mortality to \<70 per 100,000 live births.
>
> • 3.2: End preventable deaths of newborns and under-five children.
>
> • 3.3: End epidemics of AIDS, malaria, TB, and neglected tropical diseases.
>
> • 3.4: Reduce premature mortality from NCDs by one-third; promote mental health.
>
> • 3.5: Prevent and treat substance abuse and alcoholism.
>
> • 3.6: Halve global deaths and injuries from road traffic accidents.
>
> • 3.7: Ensure access to reproductive healthcare and family planning.
>
> • 3.8: Achieve universal healthcare coverage and access to essential medicines.
>
> • 3.9: Reduce mortality from pollution and hazardous chemicals.
>
> • 3.A: Implement WHO's Framework Convention on Tobacco Control.
>
> • 3.B: Support R&D and access to affordable vaccines and medicines.
>
> • 3.C: Increase healthcare financing and workforce development.
>
> • 3.D: Strengthen early warning and risk management for health emergencies.

Importance of SDG-3:

> • **Economic Development:** Healthy populations contribute to higher productivity, innovation, and national growth.
>
> • **Social Stability:** Improved health reduces poverty, inequality, and social tension.
>
> • **Environmental Sustainability:** Health outcomes are directly linked to clean water, air, and safe environments.
>
> • **Human Capital Building:** Ensures that individuals live longer, healthier lives with better educational and professional potential.

Global Challenges Addressed by SDG-3:

> • Persistent health inequalities between developed and developing nations.
>
> • Shortage of medical personnel, especially in rural areas.
>
> • Rising burden of NCDs due to lifestyle and environmental factors.
>
> • Increasing mental health issues and suicide rates.
>
> • Emerging pandemics (e.g., COVID-19) revealing gaps in global health infrastructure.

Strategies to Achieve SDG-3:

> • **Strengthen Primary Healthcare Systems:** Improve accessibility and affordability.
>
> • **Invest in Public Health Infrastructure:** Expand hospitals, laboratories, and community health centers.
>
> • **Enhance Education and Awareness:** Promote healthy lifestyles, nutrition, and mental health literacy.
>
> • **Global Partnerships:** Collaborate with WHO, UNICEF, and NGOs to deliver global health initiatives.
>
> • **Research and Innovation:** Support R&D for vaccines, disease prevention, and health technology.
>
> • **Environmental Policies:** Integrate pollution control and sanitation with public health measures.
>
> • **Digital Health Initiatives:** Use telemedicine, AI, and big data for health monitoring and predictive care.

Role of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs):

> • Incorporate SDG-3 education and research into curricula.
>
> • Conduct community health outreach programs and awareness campaigns.
>
> • Partner with government health missions for real-time intervention and training.
>
> • Facilitate interdisciplinary research linking health, environment, and technology.
>
> • Establish yoga, meditation, and counseling centers to promote mental and physical well-being.

India's Commitment to SDG-3:

> • National Health Mission (NHM) and Ayushman Bharat Yojana for universal health coverage.
>
> • Mission Indradhanush for full immunization of children.
>
> • National Mental Health Programme (NMHP) for psychological well-being.
>
> • Fit India Movement and Eat Right Campaign for lifestyle improvement.
>
> • Swachh Bharat Abhiyan and Jal Jeevan Mission for clean environment and hygiene.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • Decrease in maternal, neonatal, and under-five mortality rates.
>
> • Elimination of major communicable diseases.
>
> • Controlled growth of non-communicable diseases.
>
> • Improved mental health awareness and access to treatment.
>
> • Universal access to affordable, quality healthcare for all citizens.
>
> • Stronger health infrastructure and emergency preparedness.

Conclusion:

> • SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-Being) is central to achieving all other Sustainable Development Goals because health directly influences education, poverty reduction, gender equality, and economic growth.
>
> • It calls for a holistic, inclusive, and interdisciplinary approach, integrating medical sciences, policy, environment, and technology.
>
> • Achieving SDG-3 requires the collective commitment of governments, HEIs, industries, and communities to ensure that no one is left behind in the pursuit of a healthy and sustainable world by 2030.

**2. United Nations Targets and Indicators for SDG-3: Good Health and Well-Being**

Overview:

> • Goal 3 (SDG-3): Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages.
>
> • Purpose: To create global health equity through measurable reductions in mortality, improved healthcare systems, and enhanced well-being.
>
> • Framework: The UN has defined 13 targets and 28 indicators to measure global progress toward achieving SDG-3 by 2030.
>
> • Focus Areas: Maternal and child health, communicable and non-communicable diseases, mental health, substance abuse, road safety, pollution control, healthcare access, and health workforce development.

UN Targets and Corresponding Indicators for SDG-3

**Target 3.1: Reduce Maternal Mortality**

> • Goal: By 2030, reduce the global maternal mortality ratio to less than 70 per 100,000 live births.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.1.1: Maternal mortality ratio.
>
> o 3.1.2: Proportion of births attended by skilled health personnel.
>
> • Focus: Strengthening maternal healthcare systems, training skilled midwives, and ensuring access to prenatal and postnatal care.

**Target 3.2: End Preventable Deaths of Newborns and Children**

> • Goal: By 2030, reduce neonatal mortality to ≤12 per 1,000 and under-5 mortality to ≤25 per 1,000 live births.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.2.1: Under-five mortality rate.
>
> o 3.2.2: Neonatal mortality rate.
>
> • Focus: Immunization, nutrition, hygiene, and maternal health support.

**Target 3.3: End Epidemics and Combat Communicable Diseases**

> • Goal: By 2030, end epidemics of AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, and neglected tropical diseases, and reduce hepatitis and water-borne diseases.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.3.1: New HIV infections per 1,000 uninfected population.
>
> o 3.3.2: Tuberculosis incidence per 1,000 population.
>
> o 3.3.3: Malaria incidence per 1,000 population.
>
> o 3.3.4: Hepatitis B incidence per 100,000 population.
>
> o 3.3.5: Number of people requiring interventions against neglected tropical diseases.
>
> • Focus: Vaccination, sanitation, clean water access, and disease surveillance.

**Target 3.4: Reduce Premature Mortality and Promote Mental Health**

> • Goal: By 2030, reduce premature mortality from NCDs by one-third and strengthen mental health services.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.4.1: Mortality rate from cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes, or chronic respiratory diseases.
>
> o 3.4.2: Suicide mortality rate.
>
> • Focus: Lifestyle changes, preventive screenings, mental health awareness, and early intervention.

**Target 3.5: Strengthen Prevention and Treatment of Substance Abuse**

> • Goal: Prevent and treat drug addiction and harmful use of alcohol.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.5.1: Coverage of treatment interventions for substance use disorders.
>
> o 3.5.2: Harmful use of alcohol (liters of pure alcohol per capita per year).
>
> • Focus: Awareness campaigns, rehabilitation programs, and community-based de-addiction centers.

**Target 3.6: Halve Road Traffic Deaths**

> • Goal: By 2020, halve global deaths and injuries from road traffic accidents.
>
> • Indicator:
>
> o 3.6.1: Death rate due to road traffic injuries.
>
> • Focus: Road safety education, infrastructure, and first-aid training.

**Target 3.7: Ensure Universal Access to Sexual and Reproductive Healthcare**

> • Goal: By 2030, provide universal access to reproductive health services and integrate them into national strategies.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.7.1: Proportion of women (15–49 years) whose need for family planning is met with modern methods.
>
> o 3.7.2: Adolescent birth rate (10–14 and 15–19 years) per 1,000 women.
>
> • Focus: Women's empowerment, family planning, sexual education, and adolescent health programs.

**Target 3.8: Achieve Universal Health Coverage (UHC)**

> • Goal: Ensure financial protection and access to quality healthcare, medicines, and vaccines for all.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.8.1: Coverage of essential health services.
>
> o 3.8.2: Proportion of population facing large health expenditures relative to income.
>
> • Focus: Health insurance, affordable medicine, and equitable healthcare access.

**Target 3.9: Reduce Deaths from Pollution and Hazardous Chemicals**

> • Goal: By 2030, significantly reduce deaths caused by air, water, and soil pollution.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.9.1: Mortality rate attributed to household and ambient air pollution.
>
> o 3.9.2: Mortality rate from unsafe water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH).
>
> o 3.9.3: Mortality rate from unintentional poisoning.
>

**3. Child Health and Mortality under SDG-3: Global Overview and Challenges**

Overview:

> • Child health is a central component of SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-Being), which aims to ensure every child survives and thrives beyond infancy.
>
> • Despite global advancements in healthcare and immunization, millions of preventable child deaths still occur annually, largely due to poverty, malnutrition, and limited access to medical services.
>
> • The majority of these deaths are avoidable through affordable interventions, such as improved nutrition, clean water, sanitation, and access to skilled healthcare professionals.

Key Global Statistics (as of 2018):

> • Approximately 6.2 million children and adolescents (under 15 years) died worldwide.
>
> • Around 5.3 million of these deaths occurred before the age of five, with half within the first month of life, highlighting critical neonatal vulnerability.
>
> • Sub-Saharan Africa and Southern Asia account for the highest share of child mortality — 4 out of 5 deaths under age five occur in these regions.
>
> • Children born in Sub-Saharan Africa are 15 times more likely to die before reaching age five compared to those in high-income countries.

Causes and Determinants of Child Mortality:

> • Preventable and Treatable Diseases:
>
> o Pneumonia, diarrhea, malaria, and neonatal infections remain leading killers.
>
> o Lack of timely vaccination and healthcare access exacerbates risk.
>
> • Malnutrition and Food Insecurity:
>
> o Diet-related factors contribute to nearly 45% of deaths among children under five.
>
> o Severe acute malnutrition weakens immunity, making children more susceptible to infectious diseases.
>
> o Micronutrient deficiencies (iron, vitamin A, zinc) lead to stunted growth and developmental issues.
>
> • Socioeconomic and Environmental Factors:
>
> o Poor sanitation, unsafe drinking water, and air pollution increase exposure to illness.
>
> o Children from low-income families face barriers to healthcare and education.
>
> o Armed conflicts, migration, and natural disasters further disrupt health services.
>
> • Healthcare Access Inequality:
>
> o Lack of skilled birth attendants, community health workers, and neonatal care facilities leads to preventable fatalities.
>
> o Weak health infrastructure and underfunded systems in developing regions slow progress.

Regional Disparities:

> • Sub-Saharan Africa:
>
> o Highest child mortality rate globally; inadequate immunization and nutrition remain key issues.
>
> o Limited maternal healthcare and shortage of medical professionals worsen early-life survival chances.
>
> • Southern Asia:
>
> o Progress achieved through programs like India’s *National Health Mission* and *Mission Indradhanush*, yet malnutrition and inequality persist.
>
> • High-Income Nations:
>
> o Child deaths are rare and mostly due to congenital conditions or accidents rather than infectious diseases.

Policy and Programmatic Implications under SDG-3:

> • Target 3.2: End preventable deaths of newborns and children under five by 2030.
>
> o Indicator 3.2.1: Under-five mortality rate.
>
> o Indicator 3.2.2: Neonatal mortality rate.

Key Interventions Include:

> • Scaling up immunization programs (against measles, polio, rotavirus, etc.).
>
> • Ensuring exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months.
>
> • Providing adequate nutrition and vitamin supplementation.
>
> • Improving maternal health and antenatal care to prevent preterm and low-birth-weight complications.
>
> • Expanding community-level healthcare (mobile clinics, Anganwadi, and ASHA worker programs).
>
> • Strengthening universal healthcare coverage (UHC) to make child healthcare affordable and accessible.

Recommendations for HEIs and Policymakers:

> • Research Focus:
>
> o Encourage HEIs to conduct child nutrition and public health research to inform sustainable interventions.
>
> • Awareness and Education:
>
> o Integrate child health modules into higher education courses related to medicine, nursing, and community health.
>
> • Capacity Building:
>
> o Train professionals in neonatal and pediatric emergency care through partnerships with hospitals and health departments.
>
> • Community Outreach:
>
> o Organize health camps, vaccination drives, and nutrition awareness programs in rural and low-income urban areas.
>
> • Policy Integration:
>
> o Develop evidence-based frameworks to strengthen national programs like *Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS)* and *Poshan Abhiyaan*.

Conclusion:

> • Child health remains one of the most pressing priorities within SDG-3, as millions of young lives are still lost to preventable causes.
>
> • Addressing malnutrition, inequitable access to healthcare, and poverty is critical for reducing under-five and neonatal mortality.
>
> • Through multi-sectoral collaboration, involving governments, HEIs, NGOs, and international agencies, the world can achieve the SDG-3 target of ending preventable child deaths by 2030 and ensure that every child has the right to a healthy start in life.

**4. Maternal Health and Mortality under SDG-3: Global Trends, Challenges, and Interventions**

Overview:

Maternal health is one of the most critical dimensions of SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-Being), specifically reflected in Target 3.1, which aims to “reduce the global maternal mortality ratio to less than 70 per 100,000 live births by 2030.”

> • Maternal health is a direct indicator of the strength and equity of healthcare systems, especially in developing countries where limited access to skilled medical care remains a major challenge.
>
> • Despite global progress, thousands of women still die each year from preventable pregnancy- and childbirth-related complications, making maternal health improvement a key global priority.

Key Global Statistics and Facts:

> • Healthcare Workforce Shortage:
>
> o Over 40% of nations have fewer than 10 medical doctors per 10,000 people, and over 55% have fewer than 40 nursing and midwifery staff per 10,000 people.
>
> o This severe shortage directly affects maternal care quality and emergency response.
>
> • Progress Achieved:
>
> o Maternal mortality has decreased by nearly two-thirds in Eastern Asia, Northern Africa, and Southern Asia, reflecting the impact of improved prenatal care, institutional deliveries, and midwife training.
>
> • Ongoing Concern:
>
> o In 2017, an estimated 810 women died every day from preventable pregnancy and childbirth-related causes.
>
> o Nearly 94% of all maternal deaths occurred in low- and lower-middle-income countries.
>
> o High mortality rates persist due to poor infrastructure, lack of skilled care, delayed interventions, and socio-economic inequality.
>
> • Adolescent Pregnancy Risks:
>
> o Young adolescent mothers (10–14 years) face the highest risk of obstetric complications and maternal death due to biological immaturity and limited healthcare access.
>
> o Early pregnancy also perpetuates intergenerational poverty, malnutrition, and low education outcomes.
>
> • Underdeveloped Regions:
>
> o In developing countries, the maternal mortality ratio (MMR) — the proportion of women who die from pregnancy-related causes compared to those who deliver safely — remains unacceptably high, despite advancements in healthcare infrastructure.

Major Causes of Maternal Mortality:

> • Severe bleeding (postpartum hemorrhage)
>
> • Infections (sepsis) following childbirth or unsafe abortions
>
> • High blood pressure (pre-eclampsia and eclampsia)
>
> • Complications from unsafe abortions
>
> • Obstructed labor or prolonged delivery
>
> • Underlying medical conditions, such as anemia, malaria, and HIV/AIDS, aggravated by pregnancy

Determinants of Poor Maternal Health:

> • **Socioeconomic Barriers:** Poverty, gender inequality, and lack of education restrict access to maternal care.
>
> • **Inadequate Healthcare Infrastructure:** Poorly equipped hospitals, lack of ambulances, and insufficient maternity wards.
>
> • **Shortage of Skilled Birth Attendants:** Many deliveries still occur without trained midwives or doctors.
>
> • **Cultural Practices and Stigma:** Traditional home births and delays in seeking medical help.
>
> • **Nutrition Deficiency:** Malnourished women face higher risks during pregnancy and childbirth.
>
> • **Geographical Disparities:** Rural and remote regions lack adequate health facilities and transportation.

Global and Regional Disparities:

> • Sub-Saharan Africa:
>
> o Accounts for nearly two-thirds of all maternal deaths globally.
>
> o Major contributing factors: low institutional delivery rates, under-resourced hospitals, and limited antenatal care.
>
> • Southern Asia:
>
> o Significant progress through government programs, yet regional inequalities persist in rural and marginalized areas.
>
> • High-Income Countries:
>
> o Maternal deaths are rare but can occur due to lifestyle factors, cesarean complications, or underlying medical conditions.

Policy and Programmatic Link to SDG-3 (Target 3.1):

> • Target 3.1: By 2030, reduce the global maternal mortality ratio to less than 70 per 100,000 live births.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.1.1: Maternal mortality ratio.
>
> o 3.1.2: Proportion of births attended by skilled health personnel.

Strategic Approaches Include:

> • Increasing access to antenatal, delivery, and postnatal care.
>
> • Ensuring skilled attendance at birth through trained midwives, nurses, and obstetricians.
>
> • Expanding emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) services.
>
> • Enhancing maternal nutrition programs for anemia and malnourishment prevention.
>
> • Implementing family planning initiatives to reduce adolescent and high-risk pregnancies.
>
> • Strengthening maternal health surveillance systems for early detection of complications.
>
> • Promoting community awareness campaigns for safe motherhood and women’s health rights.

Recommendations for HEIs and Policymakers:

> • Research and Data:
>
> o Encourage research in maternal mortality determinants, reproductive health systems, and rural healthcare delivery.
>
> • Skill Development:
>
> o Establish training programs for midwives, nurses, and obstetricians within HEIs and medical institutions.
>
> • Curriculum Integration:
>
> o Introduce modules on maternal and reproductive health in public health, medicine, and social work programs.
>
> • Community Outreach:
>
> o Partner with Primary Health Centres (PHCs) and Anganwadi centres for maternal health education.
>
> • Policy Collaboration:
>
> o Strengthen partnerships between HEIs, Ministry of Health, and WHO for maternal health intervention research and capacity building.
>
> • Technology Adoption:
>
> o Promote telemedicine and AI-based prenatal monitoring tools to improve maternal care accessibility in remote areas.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • Reduction of global maternal mortality ratio below 70 per 100,000 live births.
>
> • Increased proportion of births attended by skilled healthcare professionals.
>
> • Greater access to prenatal and postnatal services, especially in low-income and rural areas.
>
> • Decrease in adolescent pregnancies and related deaths.
>
> • Strengthened healthcare systems and workforce for sustainable maternal and reproductive care.

Conclusion:

> • Maternal health reflects the overall effectiveness, equity, and inclusivity of a nation’s healthcare system.
>
> • Achieving SDG-3 Target 3.1 requires a multi-sectoral approach involving healthcare reform, education, community engagement, and gender empowerment.
>
> • Through sustained investment, capacity building, and innovation, global society can ensure that no woman dies while giving life, fulfilling the promise of Good Health and Well-Being for All by 2030.

**5. HIV/AIDS, Malaria, and Other Communicable Diseases under SDG-3: Global Situation, Challenges, and Progress**

Overview:

> • Under SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-Being), Target 3.3 specifically focuses on ending the epidemics of AIDS, tuberculosis (TB), malaria, and neglected tropical diseases, and combating hepatitis, water-borne, and other communicable diseases by 2030.
>
> • Despite significant progress in treatment, awareness, and prevention, these diseases continue to cause millions of deaths, especially in low-income countries.
>
> • The global fight against infectious diseases is not only a public health necessity but also a critical determinant of social equity, gender justice, and sustainable development.

Key Global Statistics (as of 2019):

HIV/AIDS:

> • Around 38 million people were living with HIV globally.
>
> • 25.4 million people were receiving antiretroviral therapy (ART), a major success in expanding treatment access.
>
> • Approximately 1.7 million new infections were reported that year.
>
> • 690,000 deaths occurred due to AIDS-related illnesses in 2019.
>
> • Since the start of the epidemic:
>
> o 75.7 million people have been infected with HIV.
>
> o 32.7 million people have died from AIDS-related diseases.

Tuberculosis and HIV Co-infection:

> • Tuberculosis (TB) remains the leading cause of death among HIV-positive individuals, responsible for about one-third of all AIDS-related deaths.
>
> • The dual epidemic of HIV and TB highlights the need for integrated health programs addressing co-infections simultaneously.

Gender and Social Inequality:

> • Adolescent girls and young women are disproportionately affected due to gender-based inequality, stigma, violence, and lack of education.
>
> • HIV is the leading cause of death among women of reproductive age globally.
>
> • In Sub-Saharan Africa, AIDS is the leading cause of death among adolescents (10–19 years old) and the second-highest cause of death among adolescents worldwide.

Malaria: Global Progress and Current Status:

> • From 2000 to 2015, nearly 6.2 million malaria-related deaths were prevented through global interventions.
>
> • The incidence of malaria decreased by approximately 37%, while the fatality rate dropped by 58%.
>
> • The largest reductions were observed in Sub-Saharan Africa, where child mortality from malaria was historically high.
>
> • Major contributing factors include:
>
> o Insecticide-treated bed nets (ITNs) and indoor residual spraying (IRS).
>
> o Early diagnosis and access to artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACTs).
>
> o Global partnerships such as the Roll Back Malaria Partnership and the Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis, and Malaria.

Challenges and Barriers to Eradication:

1\. Healthcare Inequality and Limited Access:

> • Many developing countries still lack universal access to testing, treatment, and prevention.
>
> • Weak healthcare infrastructure and insufficient funding hinder sustained disease control.

2\. Stigma and Discrimination:

> • Persistent social stigma against HIV/AIDS patients discourages testing and treatment.
>
> • Gender inequality and discrimination further marginalize women and vulnerable populations.

3\. Drug Resistance and Treatment Gaps:

> • Emerging antimicrobial resistance (AMR) threatens the effectiveness of HIV, TB, and malaria medications.
>
> • Treatment adherence remains low in resource-limited settings due to supply interruptions and poor monitoring.

4\. Socioeconomic Determinants:

> • Poverty, illiteracy, and inadequate sanitation exacerbate the spread of communicable diseases.
>
> • Conflict zones and climate change increase population displacement, creating conditions for disease resurgence.

Policy Linkage to SDG-3 Target 3.3:

> • Goal: End epidemics of AIDS, TB, malaria, and neglected tropical diseases by 2030.
>
> • Indicators:
>
> o 3.3.1: Number of new HIV infections per 1,000 uninfected population, by sex and age.
>
> o 3.3.2: Tuberculosis incidence per 1,000 population.
>
> o 3.3.3: Malaria incidence per 1,000 population.
>
> o 3.3.4: Hepatitis B incidence per 100,000 population.
>
> o 3.3.5: Number of people requiring interventions for neglected tropical diseases.

Global and National Interventions:

1\. HIV/AIDS Control Programs:

> • **UNAIDS Global Strategy (2021–2026):** Focuses on ending inequalities and reducing new infections.
>
> • **Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) Expansion:** Widespread access through WHO's “Treat All” initiative.
>
> • **Awareness and Prevention Campaigns:** Promotion of safe sex, condom use, and HIV testing.
>
> • **Mother-to-Child Transmission (MTCT) Prevention:** Antenatal ART programs reducing vertical infection.
>
> • **Adolescent Health Interventions:** Targeted education and empowerment for young women.

2\. Tuberculosis (TB) Response:

> • WHO End TB Strategy: Aims to reduce TB deaths by 90% by 2030.
>
> • Integration of TB and HIV treatment centers to ensure early diagnosis and combined therapy.
>
> • Expansion of community-based health services and digital tracking systems for medication adherence.

3\. Malaria Elimination Programs:

> • Global Technical Strategy for Malaria 2016–2030 (GTS): Aims for a 90% reduction in global malaria incidence.
>
> • Widespread distribution of bed nets, vector control, and rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs).
>
> • Research into malaria vaccines such as *RTS,S (Mosquirix)* and ongoing trials for improved efficacy.

Recommendations for HEIs and Policymakers:

> • Education and Research Integration:
>
> o Include modules on infectious disease control, virology, and epidemiology in medical and public health curricula.
>
> • Collaborative Research:
>
> o Encourage joint projects between HEIs, WHO, and ICMR on vaccine development and behavioral health interventions.
>
> • Community Engagement:
>
> o Conduct campus and regional awareness drives on HIV prevention, stigma reduction, and malaria control.
>
> • Gender and Youth-Focused Policies:
>
> o Develop health programs targeting adolescent girls, promoting empowerment, education, and safe practices.
>
> • Technology-Driven Monitoring:
>
> o Use digital health tools, GIS mapping, and AI analytics for disease surveillance and early detection.
>
> • Cross-Sector Partnerships:
>
> o Strengthen linkages between universities, NGOs, and government agencies to advance SDG-3 implementation.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • Complete elimination of new HIV infections and reduction of AIDS-related deaths.
>
> • 90% reduction in global malaria and tuberculosis incidence.
>
> • Increased treatment coverage and gender-equitable access to healthcare.
>
> • Enhanced research collaboration and vaccine innovation for infectious diseases.
>
> • Strengthened public health infrastructure in developing nations.

Conclusion:

> • HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, and other communicable diseases remain among the most persistent barriers to achieving SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-Being).
>
> • Sustained commitment to universal access, gender equality, and innovation is essential to end these epidemics by 2030.
>
> • Through global cooperation, equitable healthcare delivery, and continuous research, the vision of a world free from preventable infectious diseases can become a reality — fulfilling the core mandate of SDG-3.

>
> • Provides ₹5,000 in financial assistance to compensate for wage loss during childbirth and childcare.
>
> • Encourages early registration of pregnancy, antenatal check-ups, and institutional delivery.

5\. Umbrella Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS):

> • Focuses on maternal nutrition, child health, and early education through Anganwadi Centres.
>
> • Provides supplementary nutrition, health check-ups, and nutrition counseling for expectant and lactating mothers.
>
> • Works in tandem with Poshan Abhiyaan (National Nutrition Mission) to combat anemia and malnutrition.

Complementary Interventions and Best Practices:

> • **LaQshya Initiative:** Improves quality of care in labor rooms and maternity operation theaters.
>
> • **Midwifery Training Program:** Certified nurse midwives deployed in rural regions to ensure safe deliveries.
>
> • **Mother and Child Tracking System (MCTS):** Digitally monitors maternal and infant health.
>
> • **Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK):** Addresses adolescent health to prevent early pregnancy risks.
>
> • **Surakshit Matritva Aashwasan (SUMAN):** Ensures respectful and free maternity services for all women.

> • Persistent regional disparities between high-performing and lagging states.
>
> • Shortage of skilled medical professionals in rural health centers.
>
> • Poor referral linkages during emergency complications.
>
> • Socioeconomic barriers such as poverty, illiteracy, and cultural stigma.
>
> • Inadequate awareness and family planning services in remote areas.

Recommendations for Strengthening Maternal Health:

> • Expand 24x7 emergency obstetric care units in all districts.
>
> • Strengthen rural ambulance services for timely referral.
>
> • Conduct maternal death audits (MDA) to identify gaps in care delivery.
>
> • Encourage public-private partnerships (PPP) for quality maternal healthcare.
>
> • Integrate mental health and postpartum care into existing maternal health programs.
>
> • Empower HEIs and medical universities to lead research on maternal mortality determinants and intervention models.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • National MMR reduced to ≤70 per 100,000 live births, meeting SDG-3 Target 3.1.
>
> • Universal access to skilled birth attendance and emergency obstetric care.
>
> • Enhanced maternal nutrition and reproductive healthcare access in rural and tribal regions.
>
> • Strengthened healthcare workforce ensuring safe motherhood and child survival.

Conclusion:

> • India’s journey toward reducing maternal mortality is a success story of policy-driven progress, though challenges remain in regional disparities and service delivery.
>
> • Continued focus on health infrastructure, capacity building, and women’s empowerment will be vital to achieving the SDG-3 goal of safe motherhood by 2030.
>
> • Through multi-sectoral efforts and inclusive health policies, India can ensure that no mother dies while giving life, realizing the broader vision of Good Health and Well-Being for All.

> o Poor maternal health, illiteracy, and inadequate sanitation.
>
> o Limited access to skilled healthcare professionals.
>
> o Gender bias and neglect of girl children in rural regions.

National Schemes and Initiatives to Reduce Under-Five Mortality:

1\. National Health Mission (NHM):

> • The flagship health program that integrates maternal, newborn, and child healthcare under the RMNCH+A (Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child, and Adolescent Health) strategy.
>
> • Key components include:
>
> o Facility-Based Newborn Care (FBNC) for neonatal complications.
>
> o Home-Based Newborn Care (HBNC) through Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHAs).
>
> o Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK): Free institutional delivery and treatment for newborns.
>
> o Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK): Health screening of children from birth to 18 years for early detection of diseases and disabilities.

2\. Mission Indradhanush:

> • Launched in 2014 by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
>
> • Aims to achieve universal immunization coverage (90%) for children under two years and pregnant women.
>
> • Vaccinates against 12 preventable diseases, including polio, tuberculosis, hepatitis B, measles, diphtheria, and pneumonia.
>
> • The initiative has substantially reduced child mortality due to vaccine-preventable diseases.
>
> • The Intensified Mission Indradhanush (IMI) further targets hard-to-reach populations.

3\. National AYUSH Mission:

> • Integrates Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy for holistic child health.
>
> • Promotes natural immunity enhancement, traditional nutrition practices, and herbal remedies for childhood illnesses.
>
> • Encourages mothers to adopt yoga and dietary interventions during pregnancy and lactation.

4\. Umbrella Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS):

> • One of the world’s largest community-based programs, providing integrated services for maternal and child health.
>
> • Offers supplementary nutrition, immunization, and preschool education through Anganwadi centers.
>
> • Focus areas:
>
> o Improving child nutrition and reducing malnutrition-related deaths.
>
> o Regular growth monitoring of children below six years.
>
> o Encouraging community engagement for child health awareness.
>
> • Works in coordination with Poshan Abhiyaan (National Nutrition Mission) to fight stunting, wasting, and anemia.

Complementary Initiatives Supporting Child Survival:

> • **Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK):** Promotes adolescent health to prevent early pregnancies.
>
> • **India Newborn Action Plan (INAP):** Focuses on ending preventable newborn deaths.
>
> • **Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA):** Ensures antenatal check-ups for pregnant women to improve neonatal survival.
>
> • **Intensified Diarrhea Control Fortnight (IDCF):** Prevents diarrhea-related child deaths through Oral Rehydration Solutions (ORS) and zinc supplementation.
>
> • **Poshan Tracker (Digital Platform):** Real-time data tracking on child nutrition and service delivery.

Challenges in Achieving SDG-3 Target 3.2:

> • Persistent regional inequalities in healthcare access and quality.
>
> • High prevalence of malnutrition and stunting in low-income states.
>
> • Insufficient immunization coverage in remote and tribal areas.
>
> • Shortage of pediatric specialists and neonatal intensive care facilities.
>
> • Lack of community awareness regarding hygiene and preventive healthcare.

Recommendations for Strengthening Child Survival Efforts:

> • Enhance neonatal intensive care units (NICUs) in rural hospitals.
>
> • Expand public health campaigns on breastfeeding, immunization, and sanitation.
>
> • Conduct periodic under-five mortality audits to identify preventable causes.
>
> • Encourage HEIs and medical universities to undertake research on early childhood health and nutrition.
>
> • Train Anganwadi and ASHA workers in emergency child healthcare and home-based neonatal care.
>
> • Promote public-private partnerships (PPP) for improved service delivery in underserved areas.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • National Under-Five Mortality Rate reduced to ≤25 per 1,000 live births.
>
> • Universal immunization coverage achieved through Mission Indradhanush.
>
> • Elimination of vaccine-preventable and nutrition-related child deaths.
>
> • Strengthened maternal and newborn health ecosystem ensuring child survival and growth.

Conclusion:

> • India has made remarkable strides in reducing under-five mortality, yet sustained focus is essential to reach the SDG-3 Target 3.2 goal by 2030.
>
> • Through continued investment in health infrastructure, immunization, nutrition, and maternal care, India can ensure that no child dies from preventable causes.
>
> • The success of programs like NHM, Mission Indradhanush, AYUSH, and ICDS demonstrates that an integrated, community-based approach is key to achieving Good Health and Well-Being for every child in India.

**8. Fighting Communicable Diseases in India: Progress, Challenges, and Key Initiatives under SDG-3**

Overview:

> • Communicable diseases such as tuberculosis (TB), HIV/AIDS, malaria, and water-borne infections continue to pose a serious public-health challenge in India.
>
> • Under SDG-3, Target 3.3, the goal is to end epidemics of AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, and neglected tropical diseases and to combat hepatitis, water-borne, and other communicable diseases by 2030.
>
> • India has achieved measurable progress through targeted national programs and inter-sectoral coordination, but high regional disparities and disease-specific challenges remain.

Current Status of Major Communicable Diseases (India, 2019):

1\. Tuberculosis (TB):

> • 2.4 million TB cases were officially notified in 2019.
>
> • National TB notification rate: 177 cases per 100,000 population.
>
> • Highest notification:
>
> o Haryana – 255 cases per 100,000 population.
>
> o Delhi – 575 cases, Chandigarh – 606 cases per 100,000 (urban density effect).
>
> • Lowest notification:
>
> o Tripura – 70 cases per 100,000 population.
>
> • India’s national objective aligns with the National Strategic Plan (NSP) for TB Elimination 2017–2025, which seeks to make India TB-free by 2025, five years ahead of the global SDG target.
>
> • Key Challenges:
>
> o Drug-resistant TB (MDR-TB) strains and incomplete treatment courses.
>
> o Poor awareness, stigma, and under-reporting in rural and slum areas.
>
> o Malnutrition and comorbidities such as diabetes heightening TB susceptibility.

2\. HIV/AIDS:

> • HIV incidence per 1,000 uninfected population: decreased from 0.07 (2017) to 0.05 (2019).
>
> • Highest incidence rates:
>
> o Mizoram – 1.18, Nagaland – 0.73, Manipur – 0.34.
>
> • Lowest incidence rates:
>
> o Himachal Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala – 0.02, nearing the global zero-incidence target.
>
> o Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh lead UTs with only 0.02 per 1,000 uninfected persons.
>
> • Key Challenges:
>
> o Gender inequality and stigma leading to under-testing among women.
>
> o Inadequate awareness in high-risk groups (migrant workers, truck drivers, sex workers).
>
> o Need for strengthened ART adherence and prevention education.

Major National Schemes & Initiatives:

1\. National Health Mission (NHM) – Flexible Pool for Communicable Diseases:

> • Integrates disease-specific programs under a common resource pool to enhance flexibility and responsiveness.
>
> • Focuses on surveillance, diagnostics, outbreak response, and capacity building at district and block levels.
>
> • Promotes convergence between TB, malaria, leprosy, and vector-borne disease control programs.

2\. National AIDS Control Programme (NACP):

> • Implemented by NACO (National AIDS Control Organisation).
>
> • Strategic objectives:
>
> o Prevent new HIV infections through awareness and behavioral interventions.
>
> o Ensure universal access to ART (antiretroviral therapy) and viral-load monitoring.
>
> o Promote mother-to-child transmission prevention (PPTCT).
>
> o Encourage community engagement and destigmatization campaigns.
>
> • India aims to achieve “End AIDS by 2030.”

3\. National AYUSH Mission:

> • Promotes the use of Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy for disease prevention and immune strengthening.
>
> • Encourages integrative healthcare models combining traditional and allopathic systems for TB, respiratory infections, and chronic disease management.

4\. Swachh Bharat Mission (SBM) – Rural & Urban:

> • Aims to eliminate open defecation and ensure universal sanitation coverage.
>
> • Improves environmental hygiene, directly reducing water-borne and vector-borne diseases such as cholera, diarrhea, and malaria.
>
> • Encourages behavioral change through community-led total sanitation (CLTS) campaigns.

5\. National Rural Drinking Water Programme (NRDWP):

> • Ensures safe and potable drinking water to rural households.
>
> • Reduces the incidence of water-borne diseases such as typhoid, cholera, and hepatitis A & E.
>
> • Emphasizes arsenic- and fluoride-free water supply systems and water-quality surveillance labs.

6\. Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation (AMRUT):

> • Focuses on urban water supply, sewage, and stormwater management in 500+ cities.
>
> • Reduces communicable diseases arising from poor waste-water handling and urban pollution.

7\. Shyama Prasad Mukherjee RURBAN Mission:

> • Integrates rural clusters into growth centers with basic healthcare, sanitation, and solid-waste management systems.
>
> • Reduces disease transmission in peri-urban and tribal areas through health awareness programs.

8\. Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana (PMUY):

> • Provides LPG connections to poor households, minimizing exposure to indoor air pollution.
>
> • Helps prevent respiratory and communicable lung infections among women and children.

9\. Pradhan Mantri Kisan SAMPADA Yojana:

> • Strengthens the food-processing supply chain to reduce contamination and food-borne diseases.
>
> • Promotes safe storage, cold-chain infrastructure, and quality assurance.

10\. Special Central Assistance to Tribal Sub-Scheme & Umbrella Programme for Development of Scheduled Tribes:

> • Targets tribal and marginalized communities with poor health access.
>
> • Establishes mobile health units, nutrition centers, and awareness programs for communicable-disease prevention.

Cross-Sectoral Impact and Integration:

> • Clean water, sanitation, nutrition, and housing programs work synergistically with health schemes to reduce disease transmission.
>
> • Inter-ministerial collaboration (Health, AYUSH, Jal Shakti, Urban Development, and Tribal Affairs) ensures holistic progress toward SDG-3.

Challenges Hindering Complete Elimination:

> • Persistent urban-rural and inter-state disparities in disease surveillance and reporting.
>
> • Drug-resistant pathogens (MDR-TB, AMR) complicate treatment.
>
> • Migration and informal settlements increase vulnerability to communicable diseases.
>
> • Limited community participation and health literacy gaps in remote regions.

Recommendations for Strengthening Disease-Control Efforts:

> • Scale up real-time digital disease-surveillance systems integrating district health data.
>
> • Expand research collaborations between HEIs, ICMR, and WHO for vaccine development.
>
> • Strengthen laboratory networks and diagnostic capacity in rural blocks.
>
> • Implement AI-enabled predictive analytics for early outbreak detection.
>
> • Promote health education campaigns through schools, HEIs, and media platforms.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • Elimination of TB, HIV, and malaria epidemics in line with SDG-3 Target 3.3.
>
> • Universal access to clean water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services.
>
> • Stronger disease-surveillance systems ensuring timely response.
>
> • Reduced morbidity and mortality from communicable diseases across all states and UTs.

Conclusion:

> • India’s integrated approach—combining medical care, sanitation, clean energy, nutrition, and education—is pivotal to eliminating communicable diseases.
>
> • Achieving SDG-3’s vision requires sustained inter-sectoral collaboration, investment in public-health infrastructure, and active participation of communities and HEIs.
>
> • With continued focus, India can become a global model in disease prevention, control, and sustainable public-health governance by 2030.

**9. Reducing Mortality from Non-Communicable Diseases and Promoting Mental Health under SDG-3**

Overview:

> • Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) have emerged as one of the most significant global health challenges, responsible for 41 million deaths annually, accounting for 71% of all global deaths.
>
> • NCDs include cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), cancers, chronic respiratory diseases, and diabetes, collectively responsible for more than 80% of premature deaths (ages 30–69 years).
>
> • SDG-3, Target 3.4 aims to reduce premature mortality from NCDs by one-third by 2030 through prevention, treatment, and the promotion of mental health and well-being.

Global and National Context:

> • 15 million people aged 30–69 years die prematurely each year due to NCDs.
>
> • 85% of these deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries, highlighting severe disparities in healthcare access.
>
> • The four major contributors are:
>
> o Cardiovascular diseases: 17.9 million deaths annually.
>
> o Cancers: 9.3 million deaths annually.
>
> o Chronic respiratory diseases: 4.1 million deaths annually.
>
> o Diabetes: 1.5 million deaths annually.
>
> • In India, NCDs account for nearly 63% of total deaths, making them the leading cause of mortality and morbidity.

Key Causes and Risk Factors:

1\. Lifestyle-related factors:

> o Unhealthy diets (high sugar, salt, trans-fat).
>
> o Physical inactivity and sedentary lifestyles.
>
> o Tobacco and alcohol use.
>
> o Obesity and stress.

2\. Environmental and Social Determinants:

> o Air pollution and occupational exposure.
>
> o Urbanization and inadequate access to green spaces.
>
> o Limited access to affordable healthcare in rural and tribal regions.
>
> o Socioeconomic inequalities affecting awareness and diagnosis.

3\. Psychological and Mental Health Dimensions:

> o Rising mental health disorders due to stress, anxiety, and depression.
>
> o Lack of awareness and stigma preventing early diagnosis and treatment.
>
> o Mental illnesses leading to increased suicide rates among youth and professionals.

National Schemes and Initiatives:

1\. NHM – Flexible Pool for Non-Communicable Diseases, Injury, and Trauma:

> • Strengthens infrastructure and service delivery for screening, prevention, and management of NCDs.
>
> • Implements National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases, and Stroke (NPCDCS).
>
> • Expands district NCD clinics and cardiac care units at district hospitals.
>
> • Integrates mental health, injury, and trauma care into district-level health planning.

2\. NHM – Human Resources for Health and Medical Education:

> • Addresses shortage of skilled professionals such as cardiologists, oncologists, and psychiatrists.
>
> • Focuses on capacity building through the establishment of new medical and nursing colleges.
>
> • Promotes continuous professional development (CPD) programs on chronic disease management and preventive care.

3\. NHM – Strengthening of State Drug Regulatory System:

> • Ensures availability of safe, effective, and quality drugs for NCD treatment.
>
> • Implements standard treatment guidelines and price regulation for essential NCD medicines.
>
> • Encourages generic drug production and distribution through Jan Aushadhi Kendras.

4\. National AYUSH Mission:

> • Promotes traditional medicine systems (Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy) for holistic management of NCDs.
>
> • Encourages Yoga-based lifestyle modification to prevent diabetes, hypertension, and anxiety.
>
> • Integrates AYUSH wellness centers with primary health care services for preventive health and mental well-being.

5\. Special Central Assistance to Tribal Sub-Scheme:

> • Focuses on tribal health infrastructure, addressing the high burden of NCDs in remote and marginalized populations.
>
> • Provides mobile medical units, screening camps, and health education for early detection of chronic diseases.

6\. Umbrella Programme for Development of Scheduled Tribes:

> • Aims to improve access to healthcare, nutrition, and mental health services in tribal regions.
>
> • Encourages collaborative projects with HEIs and NGOs for NCD prevention and community awareness.

Mental Health Integration under SDG-3:

> • Mental health disorders contribute significantly to disability and reduced productivity.
>
> • As per WHO, one in four individuals will experience a mental health issue in their lifetime.
>
> • India faces a mental health treatment gap of nearly 70–80%, especially in rural areas.

Government Initiatives:

> • **National Mental Health Programme (NMHP):** Provides community-based mental healthcare services and awareness programs.
>
> • **Manodarpan Initiative (MoE):** Supports psychological well-being of students and teachers.
>
> • **Tele-MANAS (Tele Mental Health Assistance and Networking Across States):** Offers tele-counseling services in regional languages.
>
> • **KIRAN Helpline:** 24/7 toll-free helpline for stress, depression, and suicidal tendencies.
>
> • **Mental Healthcare Act, 2017:** Ensures rights-based mental healthcare and protection from discrimination.

Role of HEIs and Health Institutions:

> • **Awareness Campaigns:** Conduct health screenings, yoga sessions, and mental wellness workshops.
>
> • **Research Initiatives:** Undertake interdisciplinary research on NCD prevention, mental health, and behavioral sciences.
>
> • **Curriculum Integration:** Include health education, stress management, and lifestyle modification modules in academic programs.
>
> • **Counseling Centres:** Establish student wellness and mental health units in every HEI.
>
> • **Community Outreach:** Partner with local PHCs to organize free NCD screening and lifestyle awareness programs.

Challenges in Combating NCDs:

> • Limited health insurance coverage for chronic diseases.
>
> • Poor awareness and late diagnosis in rural populations.
>
> • Inadequate mental health professionals (psychiatrists, counselors, clinical psychologists).
>
> • Lifestyle habits influenced by urbanization and unhealthy diets.
>
> • Insufficient data on disease prevalence and mortality.

Recommendations:

> • Strengthen primary healthcare systems for NCD screening and counseling.
>
> • Promote preventive health education through digital campaigns and HEI involvement.
>
> • Integrate mental health into national health insurance and primary care frameworks.
>
> • Encourage multi-sectoral collaboration between Health, Education, and AYUSH Ministries.
>
> • Foster AI-driven early detection systems for chronic diseases using digital health records.
>
> • Encourage corporate wellness programs through CSR initiatives.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • One-third reduction in premature NCD mortality (SDG-3 Target 3.4).
>
> • Improved access to preventive care, screening, and rehabilitation services.
>
> • Enhanced mental health awareness and community resilience.
>
> • Strengthened health infrastructure and workforce for chronic disease management.

Conclusion:

> • NCDs and mental health disorders represent a dual public health challenge—one physical, one psychological—but both preventable with early intervention.
>
> • Through sustained policy action, community participation, and integration of modern and traditional healthcare systems, India can reduce NCD mortality and foster mental well-being.
>
> • Achieving SDG-3 Target 3.4 demands a shift from treatment to prevention, focusing on healthy lifestyles, stress management, and inclusive healthcare for all.

**10. Preventing and Treating Substance Abuse: Policies, Challenges, and Initiatives under SDG-3**

Overview:

> • Substance abuse, including alcohol, tobacco, and narcotic drugs, remains one of the most pervasive public health challenges worldwide.
>
> • SDG-3, Target 3.5 specifically aims to *“strengthen the prevention and treatment of substance abuse, including narcotic drug abuse and harmful use of alcohol.”*
>
> • Despite progress in various regions, including Europe and Asia, there remains minimal advancement in reducing alcohol consumption, smoking prevalence, child obesity, and suicide mortality—all closely linked to substance abuse and mental health.
>
> • The European Public Health Association (EUPHA) and global health institutions emphasize the vital role of civil society, health institutions, and community organizations in achieving sustained progress.

Global and Regional Perspective:

> • Europe and North America:
>
> o These regions show slower-than-expected progress in reducing alcohol and tobacco consumption and associated non-communicable diseases.
>
> o Suicide and child obesity trends remain stagnant or increasing, highlighting the deep interconnection between mental health and addiction.
>
> • Asia and India:
>
> o India faces rising concerns over drug addiction among youth, especially in Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, and Northeastern states.
>
> o Alcohol consumption and tobacco use contribute significantly to domestic violence, liver disease, and premature deaths.

Current Challenges:

1\. Increasing Youth Vulnerability:

> o Peer pressure, unemployment, and social media exposure drive experimentation with drugs and alcohol.
>
> o Easy availability of substances and synthetic drugs in urban areas worsens the issue.

2\. Mental Health Linkage:

> o Substance abuse often coexists with depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation.
>
> o Lack of awareness and societal stigma prevent individuals from seeking treatment.

3\. Weak Institutional Infrastructure:

> o Insufficient rehabilitation and detoxification centers, particularly in rural and tribal areas.
>
> o Limited trained counselors, psychologists, and social workers.

4\. Inadequate Enforcement:

> o Illicit trafficking and porous borders make control of narcotics challenging.
>
> o Weak coordination among enforcement agencies delays prevention and prosecution.

5\. Data and Monitoring Gaps:

> o Under-reporting of substance abuse cases and fragmented national statistics limit effective policymaking.

Key Schemes and Initiatives:

1\. Scheme for Prevention of Alcoholism and Substance (Drugs) Abuse:

> • Implemented by the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (MoSJE).
>
> • Objectives:
>
> o To create awareness and educate the public about the ill effects of alcoholism and drug abuse.
>
> o To develop counseling, treatment, and rehabilitation infrastructure through voluntary organizations.
>
> o To support NGOs and community-based organizations in establishing Integrated Rehabilitation Centres for Addicts (IRCAs).
>
> o To promote peer-support, de-addiction camps, and community outreach activities.

Activities:

> • Financial assistance to voluntary organizations for operating de-addiction centers.
>
> • Mass awareness campaigns in schools, colleges, and workplaces.
>
> • Skill development training for rehabilitated individuals to facilitate reintegration into society.

2\. National AYUSH Mission:

> • Integrates traditional healing practices (Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, Homeopathy) to treat addiction and enhance mental resilience.
>
> • Encourages the use of herbal formulations and yogic therapies to reduce dependency and detoxify the body.
>
> • Promotes mindfulness, meditation, and lifestyle modification for stress management and relapse prevention.
>
> • AYUSH de-addiction therapies are being incorporated into integrated wellness centers at district and university levels.

3\. Police Infrastructure & Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB):

> • Strengthens the law enforcement mechanism against drug trafficking and illegal supply chains.
>
> • The Narcotics Control Bureau, under the Ministry of Home Affairs, coordinates anti-narcotics operations with state police and intelligence agencies.
>
> • Implementation of NDPS Act (Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985) ensures strict legal control over drug manufacture, sale, and possession.
>
> • Capacity-building programs for police personnel, including drug detection, community policing, and awareness drives in educational institutions.

Role of Civil Society and HEIs:

> • Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) are encouraged to establish:
>
> o Counseling and mental-wellness centers for students and staff.
>
> o Drug-free campus campaigns and regular seminars on addiction prevention.
>
> o Collaborative research on socio-psychological and behavioral patterns leading to substance abuse.
>
> • Civil society organizations and student volunteers can act as peer educators, helping early identification of at-risk individuals.
>
> • HEIs can collaborate with NCB and Health Departments for community-based awareness and outreach programs.

Best Practices and Global Learnings:

> • European Public Health Association (EUPHA):
>
> o Advocates integrating mental health services within addiction-treatment systems.
>
> o Promotes evidence-based interventions like brief counseling, harm-reduction programs, and school-based education.
>
> • WHO “SAFER” Initiative:
>
> o Global framework to reduce alcohol-related harm through policy, awareness, and enforcement.
>
> • Nordic Model:
>
> o Combines strict regulation, high taxation, and rehabilitation programs to reduce alcohol and drug consumption effectively.

Challenges in India’s Context:

> • Limited number of de-addiction centers compared to affected population.
>
> • Social stigma and lack of family support during rehabilitation.
>
> • Rural areas often lack trained counselors and long-term rehabilitation programs.
>
> • Synthetic drugs and online drug trade emerging as new threats.

Recommendations for Strengthened Action:

> • Establish state-level Addiction Monitoring and Prevention Cells (AMPCs) under HEIs.
>
> • Integrate mental-health screening into de-addiction programs.
>
> • Expand rehabilitation services under the National Health Mission to district level.
>
> • Encourage public-private partnerships (PPP) for building modern detoxification centers.
>
> • Introduce mandatory awareness programs in schools, colleges, and workplaces.
>
> • Increase funding for addiction research and behavioral interventions.
>
> • Strengthen border surveillance and law enforcement coordination under the NCB.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • Reduced prevalence of alcohol and drug abuse among youth and working populations.
>
> • Integration of mental health and addiction treatment services within the public-health framework.
>
> • Decrease in crime, road accidents, and domestic violence linked to substance abuse.
>
> • Strengthened rehabilitation ecosystem emphasizing recovery and social reintegration.

Conclusion:

> • The prevention and treatment of substance abuse demand a multidimensional, health-centered, and socially inclusive approach.
>
> • Through schemes such as the Prevention of Alcoholism and Substance Abuse Program, the National AYUSH Mission, and enhanced police and civil society engagement, India is progressing toward SDG-3 Target 3.5.
>
> • Long-term success will rely on education, awareness, and compassionate rehabilitation, ensuring that individuals not only recover but reintegrate into society with dignity—fulfilling the global commitment to Good Health and Well-Being for All.

**11. Reducing Road Injuries and Deaths under SDG-3: Policies, Progress, and Preventive Strategies**

Overview:

> • Road traffic injuries represent a major global public health concern and are a leading cause of death and disability worldwide.
>
> • Under SDG-3, Target 3.6, the objective is to *“halve the number of global deaths and injuries from road traffic accidents by 2030.”*
>
> • India, with one of the largest road networks in the world, witnesses a high burden of fatalities and injuries, predominantly affecting young adults in the productive age group.
>
> • While certain states and union territories have achieved remarkable progress, others continue to face challenges due to unsafe road behavior, inadequate infrastructure, and poor post-crash care.

Current Scenario (India, 2019):

> • As per the Accidental Deaths and Suicides in India (ADSI) Report, 2019,
>
> o The national road traffic death rate was 11.56 deaths per 100,000 population.
>
> o Goa recorded the highest fatality rate at 19.38 per 100,000 population.
>
> o Nagaland reported the lowest rate at 1.02 per 100,000 population.
>
> • States and UTs Achieving the SDG Target:
>
> o Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Nagaland among the States.
>
> o Andaman & Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep among the Union Territories.
>
> • These states have achieved the SDG-3.6 target by maintaining road traffic deaths below 6 per 100,000 population, through a combination of public awareness, strict enforcement, and road-safety interventions.

Major Causes of Road Accidents and Fatalities:

1\. Human Factors:

> o Over-speeding, distracted driving, and violation of traffic rules.
>
> o Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
>
> o Fatigue and drowsiness during long-distance driving.

2\. Vehicle Factors:

> o Poor vehicle maintenance and lack of safety features (e.g., airbags, ABS).
>
> o Overloading of goods and passengers.

3\. Infrastructure Issues:

> o Poorly designed roads, potholes, and lack of signage.
>
> o Absence of pedestrian crossings, barriers, and lighting.

4\. Emergency Response:

> o Delayed medical attention and lack of trained first responders.
>
> o Inadequate trauma care centers and ambulance networks.

High-Risk Groups:

> • Young adults (15–44 years) — the most economically active population segment.
>
> • Motorcyclists, pedestrians, and cyclists — who account for nearly 50% of total fatalities.
>
> • Children and the elderly — due to low risk awareness and slow mobility response.

National Schemes and Initiatives to Reduce Road Injuries and Deaths:

1\. Road Safety Schemes (Ministry of Road Transport and Highways – MoRTH):

> • Comprehensive programs under the National Road Safety Policy (NRSP) to enhance road safety awareness, infrastructure, and enforcement.
>
> • Key Components:
>
> o Publicity and Awareness Generation:
>
> o Nationwide campaigns promoting seat-belt and helmet use, speed limit adherence, and anti-drunk-driving messages.
>
> o Engagement of celebrities and youth ambassadors to change driver behavior.
>
> o Education in Schools and HEIs:
>
> o Inclusion of road safety education modules in school curricula.
>
> o Establishment of Road Safety Clubs in colleges and universities.

2\. National Highways Accident Relief Service Scheme (NHARSS):

> • Aims to provide immediate relief and medical assistance to accident victims on national highways.
>
> • Provisions Include:
>
> o Highway Patrol Vans equipped with first-aid kits, stretchers, and communication systems.
>
> o Tow trucks and cranes for clearing accident sites.
>
> o Emergency helpline systems (1033) for accident reporting and rapid response.
>
> • Helps minimize response time and post-crash fatalities.

3\. Institute of Driving Training and Research (IDTR):

> • Centers established in collaboration with state transport departments and automobile manufacturers.
>
> • Objectives:
>
> o Provide scientific driver training and road-safety education.
>
> o Promote use of driving simulators and skill-based modules for commercial and private drivers.
>
> o Conduct periodic re-certification and assessment of driving competency.

4\. Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019:

> • Strengthened legal framework for road safety, driver accountability, and enforcement.
>
> • Key features:
>
> o Heavy penalties for traffic violations (drunk driving, over-speeding, mobile phone use).
>

>
> o Mandatory insurance and fitness certification of vehicles.

> • Facilitate research on road engineering, driver psychology, and accident prevention technologies.

> • Introduce mandatory defensive driving courses for all new drivers.

> • Expand road safety audits and redesign accident-prone black spots.

Conclusion:

**12. Universal Access to Sexual and Reproductive Health Care, Family Planning, and Education under SDG-3**

Overview:

> • Universal access to sexual and reproductive health (SRH) is a cornerstone of both SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-Being) and SDG-5 (Gender Equality), ensuring that all individuals—especially women and adolescents—have the right to safe, informed, and affordable reproductive choices.
>
> • Target 3.7 of SDG-3 commits countries to "ensure universal access to sexual and reproductive health-care services, including family planning, information, and education, and the integration of reproductive health into national strategies and programs by 2030."
>
> • The World Health Assembly Resolution WHA57.12 (2004) established the WHO Global Reproductive Health Strategy, urging Member States to:
>
> o Integrate reproductive health into national planning and budgeting.
>
> o Strengthen health systems to achieve universal SRH coverage.
>
> o Include SRH in national monitoring frameworks for UN development goals.

Global Context:

> • Access to SRH services remains unequal across countries, with millions of women and adolescents lacking access to modern contraception, safe delivery, or reproductive health education.
>
> • Globally, nearly 200 million women have an unmet need for family planning, leading to unwanted pregnancies and unsafe abortions.
>
> • Maternal deaths, sexually transmitted infections (STIs), and adolescent pregnancies remain major global health concerns.

India's National Context:

> • India has progressively strengthened its reproductive health framework through national missions and policy reforms.
>
> • The Reproductive and Child Health (RCH) component under the National Health Mission (NHM) serves as the primary national platform for achieving universal SRH coverage.
>
> • Despite progress, rural, tribal, and low-income populations continue to face gaps in service delivery, cultural barriers, and limited education.

Major Schemes and Initiatives:

1\. National Health Mission (NHM) – Reproductive and Child Health (RCH) Flexible Pool:

> • **Core Objective:** Ensure accessible, affordable, and quality reproductive and child health services for all.
>
> • Key Components:
>
> o **Family Planning Services:** Distribution of contraceptives (condoms, oral pills, IUDs) through health workers and ASHAs.
>
> o **Antenatal and Postnatal Care:** Comprehensive check-ups, institutional deliveries, and immunizations.
>
> o **Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY):** Financial incentives to promote institutional deliveries among poor pregnant women.
>
> o **Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK):** Free maternal and neonatal care in public health facilities.
>
> o **Mission Parivar Vikas:** Focused on 145 high fertility districts across seven states to accelerate family planning initiatives.
>
> o **Menstrual Hygiene Scheme (MHS):** Promotes menstrual hygiene management among adolescent girls through sanitary napkin distribution and awareness programs.

2\. National AYUSH Mission:

> • Integrates traditional medicine systems—Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy—into reproductive and maternal healthcare.
>
> • Promotes pre-conception and prenatal care, natural childbirth methods, and stress-reduction through Yoga.
>
> • Encourages dietary and lifestyle practices that enhance reproductive health, hormonal balance, and postnatal recovery.
>
> • Establishes AYUSH Wellness Centres providing reproductive health consultations alongside modern healthcare.

3\. Umbrella Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS):

> • Operates through Anganwadi Centres under the Ministry of Women and Child Development.
>
> • Provides integrated nutrition, health, and early childhood care services to pregnant women, lactating mothers, and children under six years.
>
> • Promotes maternal nutrition awareness, breastfeeding counseling, and family planning education at the community level.
>
> • Encourages women's participation in health education programs on reproductive rights and adolescent health.

Complementary National Programs Supporting SRH:

> • **RMNCH+A Strategy:** Focuses on the continuum of care—Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child, and Adolescent Health.
>
> • **Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA):** Provides assured, quality antenatal care to pregnant women on the 9th of every month.
>
> • **Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK):** Addresses adolescent sexual and reproductive health needs through peer educators and youth-friendly clinics.
>
> • **POSHAN Abhiyaan:** Improves maternal and adolescent nutrition to reduce anemia and stunting.
>
> • **LaQshya Initiative:** Enhances quality of care in labor rooms and maternity operation theaters.

Education and Awareness Initiatives:

> • Comprehensive Sexuality Education (CSE):
>
> o Introduced in schools and HEIs to educate adolescents about puberty, safe sex, consent, and gender equality.
>
> o Promotes responsible reproductive behavior and prevention of STIs and early pregnancies.
>
> • Family Welfare Camps:
>
> o Conducted at block and district levels for contraceptive awareness and reproductive health counseling.
>
> • Public Campaigns:
>
> o Mass-media awareness drives on family planning ("Hum Do, Hamare Do"), safe motherhood, and gender equity.

Challenges in Universal SRH Access:

> • Social and cultural taboos surrounding reproductive health education.
>
> • Shortage of trained gynecologists, counselors, and midwives in rural health centers.
>
> • Adolescent reproductive health gaps, especially among school dropouts.
>
> • Limited male participation in family planning and shared reproductive responsibilities.
>
> • Inadequate integration of mental and reproductive health services.

Recommendations for Strengthening SRH Systems:

> • Integrate reproductive health education into school and university curricula.
>
> • Expand mobile reproductive health clinics to reach remote and tribal areas.
>
> • Increase public investment in contraceptive supply chains and midwifery training.
>
> • Encourage public-private partnerships (PPP) for reproductive health and awareness programs.
>
> • Strengthen data collection and monitoring systems for SRH outcomes.
>
> • Promote gender-inclusive reproductive policies, addressing male engagement and equality.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • Universal access to family planning services and contraceptive methods.
>
> • Reduction in unmet need for contraception and adolescent pregnancies.
>
> • Improvement in maternal and child health indicators through integrated SRH services.
>
> • Empowered communities with knowledge, autonomy, and reproductive rights.

Conclusion:

> • Achieving universal access to sexual and reproductive health care is fundamental to both public health and gender empowerment.
>
> • Through initiatives such as the National Health Mission, AYUSH Mission, and Umbrella ICDS, India is aligning national health strategies with the WHO Global Reproductive Health Strategy.
>
> • Ensuring inclusive education, adequate infrastructure, and culturally sensitive communication will be key to achieving SDG-3 Target 3.7—a future where every individual can make informed and safe reproductive choices, contributing to overall Good Health and Well-Being for All.

**13. Achieving Universal Health Coverage under SDG-3: Ensuring Equitable Access to Affordable and Quality Healthcare in India**

Overview:

> • Universal Health Coverage (UHC) is a foundational pillar of SDG-3 (Good Health and Well-Being), ensuring that "all people have access to the health services they need—prevention, treatment, rehabilitation, and palliation—without financial hardship."
>
> • Target 3.8 of SDG-3 specifically focuses on achieving universal health coverage, including financial risk protection, access to quality essential health-care services, and affordable essential medicines and vaccines for all.
>
> • India has made major progress in expanding access through Ayushman Bharat, NHM, and free immunization programs, but challenges remain in ensuring equitable coverage across states and population groups.

Immunization Coverage in India (2019–2020):

> • According to the Routine Immunization Programme Dashboard (Health Management Information System),
>
> o 91% of infants aged 9 to 11 months were fully immunized (received one dose of BCG, three doses of DPT and OPV, and one dose of the measles vaccine).
>
> o The national goal is to achieve 100% full immunization coverage for all eligible children.

State-wise Performance:

| **Region**      | **Coverage (%)** | **Status vs. National Target (100%)** |
|-----------------|------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Maharashtra     | 100%             | Achieved Target                       |
| Jammu & Kashmir | 100%             | Achieved Target                       |
| Ladakh          | 100%             | Achieved Target                       |
| Nagaland        | 54%              | Lowest – Needs Urgent Action          |
| Puducherry      | 54%              | Lowest – Needs Urgent Action          |

> • Observation:
>
> o High-performing regions such as Maharashtra and J&K have demonstrated strong health infrastructure and consistent outreach.
>
> o Northeastern and small UT regions face challenges due to terrain, logistics, and healthcare workforce shortages.

Major Schemes and Initiatives Supporting Universal Health Coverage:

1\. National Health Protection Scheme (Ayushman Bharat):

> • India's flagship universal health coverage initiative, launched in 2018, aims to provide comprehensive healthcare access at both primary and secondary/tertiary levels.
>
> • Components:
>
> o Ayushman Bharat – Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY):
>
> o Provides ₹5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary care hospitalization.
>
> o Covers over 10.74 crore vulnerable families (≈50 crore people) identified under the Socio-Economic Caste Census (SECC)-2011.
>
> o Ensures cashless treatment in public and empanelled private hospitals.
>
> o Ayushman Bharat – Health and Wellness Centres (HWCs):
>
> o Upgradation of 1.5 lakh Sub-centres and PHCs into HWCs providing comprehensive primary care including maternal, child, and NCD services.
>
> • Promotes preventive, promotive, curative, rehabilitative, and palliative care with special focus on the poor and rural populations.

2\. National AIDS Control Programme (NACP):

> • Implemented by the National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO).
>
> • Objectives:
>
> o Ensure universal access to HIV prevention, testing, and treatment services.
>
> o Integrate HIV care with other health programs like TB and maternal health.
>
> o Provide free ART (Antiretroviral Therapy) and counseling to all PLHIVs (People Living with HIV).
>
> • NACP-IV (2012–2017) and extended phases continue to reduce new infections and stigma through community-based interventions.

3\. Central Government Health Scheme (CGHS) and State Government Health Schemes:

> • Provide comprehensive medical care to central and state government employees, pensioners, and their families.
>
> • Services include:
>
> o Out-patient and in-patient treatment.
>
> o Reimbursement for diagnostics and hospitalization.
>
> o Empanelled hospitals and dispensaries in all major cities.
>
> • Complement national efforts toward financial protection in health spending.

4\. NHM – Human Resources for Health & Medical Education:

> • Strengthens the availability, training, and retention of skilled health professionals across India.
>
> • Focuses on:
>
> o Establishing new medical and nursing colleges in underserved regions.
>
> o Improving capacity-building for doctors, nurses, and ASHAs.
>
> o Promoting tele-medicine and digital health training for remote areas.

5\. Umbrella Programme for Development of Scheduled Tribes (STs) and Minorities:

> • Aims to improve health access and outcomes among marginalized communities.
>
> • Includes mobile health units, immunization drives, and nutritional support in tribal and minority-dominated districts.
>
> • Enhances inclusivity by ensuring no population group is left behind in healthcare access.

6\. Jan Aushadhi Scheme (Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana – PMBJP):

> • Ensures affordable access to quality generic medicines through Jan Aushadhi Kendras.
>
> • Over 10,000 Kendras operational nationwide supplying over 1,800 high-quality medicines at 50–90% lower prices than branded alternatives.
>
> • Reduces out-of-pocket expenditure (OOPE), a critical barrier to UHC in India.

Cross-Sectoral Linkages and Complementary Initiatives:

> • **Digital Health Mission (ABDM):** Creates unique health IDs to integrate patient records and enable tele-health services.
>
> • **National AYUSH Mission:** Promotes traditional and preventive healthcare models within the UHC framework.
>
> • **Mission Indradhanush:** Strengthens immunization outreach to achieve 100% coverage among children and pregnant women.
>
> • **Health Insurance Portability:** Expands private participation in PM-JAY to improve hospital network coverage.

Challenges in Achieving Universal Health Coverage:

> • Inequitable distribution of healthcare resources across regions.
>
> • High out-of-pocket spending (still ≈48% of total health expenditure).
>
> • Shortage of specialists and nurses in rural health centers.
>
> • Low health literacy leading to delayed healthcare seeking.
>
> • Fragmented data systems and limited integration between central and state schemes.

Recommendations:

> • Accelerate 100% immunization coverage through micro-planning and community engagement.
>
> • Strengthen primary healthcare as the first point of contact under HWCs.
>
> • Expand PM-JAY coverage to include outpatient and diagnostic care.
>
> • Promote health financing reforms to reduce OOPE and increase public expenditure (target ≥2.5% of GDP by 2025).
>
> • Encourage HEIs and public-private partnerships for healthcare innovation and health-system research.
>
> • Enhance monitoring and evaluation frameworks for UHC indicators at district level.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • 100% immunization coverage of infants and pregnant women.
>
> • Universal financial risk protection for secondary and tertiary healthcare.
>
> • Reduced out-of-pocket expenditure on medicines and treatment.
>
> • Strengthened primary health infrastructure reaching every household.
>
> • Improved health equity across regions and communities.

Conclusion:

> • Achieving Universal Health Coverage is central to the vision of "Health for All" and a key enabler of economic productivity and social justice.
>
> • Through schemes like Ayushman Bharat, NHM, and Jan Aushadhi, India is advancing toward inclusive, affordable, and accessible healthcare.
>
> • Continued commitment to health financing, digital health, and equitable resource distribution will ensure that by 2030, every Indian—irrespective of income, gender, or geography—can access quality healthcare without financial hardship, fulfilling SDG-3 Target 3.8: Universal Health Coverage.

**14. Reducing Illnesses and Deaths from Hazardous Chemicals and Pollution under SDG-3: Strengthening Environmental Health and Sustainable Development in India**

Overview:

> • Exposure to hazardous chemicals, toxic emissions, and environmental pollution is one of the major public-health risks identified in the United Nations' 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).
>
> • Under SDG-3, Target 3.9, the global objective is to "substantially reduce the number of deaths and illnesses from hazardous chemicals and air, water, and soil pollution and contamination by 2030."
>
> • Air pollution alone accounts for nearly 7 million premature deaths globally every year, while chemical contamination of soil and water continues to cause chronic diseases and cancers.
>
> • India faces severe challenges in this area due to rapid industrialization, urbanization, waste mismanagement, and agricultural chemical use, all of which demand strong policy integration, institutional capacity, and community awareness.

Key Health Impacts of Pollution and Chemical Exposure:

> • **Air Pollution:** Linked to cardiovascular diseases, respiratory infections, COPD, asthma, and lung cancer.
>
> • **Water Pollution:** Causes diarrheal diseases, hepatitis, arsenic- and fluoride-related disorders, and heavy-metal poisoning.
>
> • **Soil Contamination:** Leads to bio-accumulation of toxins in food chains, resulting in neurological and developmental disorders.
>
> • **Chemical Hazards:** Exposure to pesticides, lead, mercury, and industrial chemicals affects endocrine and immune systems.

Major National Schemes and Initiatives Supporting SDG-3.9

1\. Environment Protection, Management, and Sustainable Development (Pollution Abatement):

> • Implements measures for industrial emission control, hazardous-waste management, and clean-technology adoption.
>
> • Focus on:
>
> o Pollution-abatement infrastructure, such as effluent-treatment plants and air-quality monitoring stations.
>
> o Promotion of renewable energy and green-manufacturing standards.
>
> o Public awareness campaigns on chemical safety, waste segregation, and eco-friendly consumption.
>
> • Encourages states to establish State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs) with integrated data monitoring through the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP).

2\. National River Conservation Programme (NRCP):

> • **Objective:** Reduce pollution load in major rivers through wastewater treatment and riverfront development.
>
> • Activities:
>
> o Establishment of Sewage-Treatment Plants (STPs) and Common Effluent Treatment Plants (CETPs).
>
> o River-cleaning projects under Namami Gange and National River Ganga Basin Authority.
>
> o Promotes community-led river stewardship and industrial-discharge regulation.
>
> • Directly contributes to reducing water-borne diseases caused by contaminated water sources.

3\. Research & Development and Implementation of National Water Mission:

> • One of the eight National Missions under the National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC).
>
> • Focuses on integrated water-resource management for ensuring safe and sustainable water availability.
>
> • Key actions:
>
> o Water-quality monitoring, arsenic-remediation technologies, and reuse of treated wastewater.
>
> o Promotes rainwater harvesting, aquifer recharge, and efficient irrigation practices.
>
> o Research into low-cost purification methods to protect vulnerable rural communities from contamination.

4\. National Health Mission (NHM):

> • The umbrella health-system reform program comprising:
>
> o National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) and National Urban Health Mission (NUHM).
>
> • Objectives:
>
> o Attain universal access to equitable, affordable, and quality health services.
>
> o Strengthen inter-sectoral convergence to address social and environmental determinants of health.
>
> o Program components include:
>
> o Health-system strengthening in rural and urban areas.
>
> o Reproductive-Maternal-Newborn-Child-Adolescent Health (RMNCH + A).
>
> o Communicable and non-communicable disease programs addressing pollution-related illnesses.

5\. Human Resource & Capacity Development:

> • Focuses on training, recruitment, and skill development of professionals managing health and environmental risks.
>
> • Activities include:
>
> o Environmental-health modules for medical officers and community health workers.
>
> o Development of toxicology units and occupational-health specialists.
>
> o Collaboration with HEIs and research institutions for pollution-impact studies and emergency-response training.

6\. National AYUSH Mission (NAM):

> • Promotes Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy (ASU & H) for holistic health and disease prevention.
>
> • Key outcomes:
>
> o Provides low-cost, sustainable healthcare alternatives that reduce dependence on chemical drugs.
>
> o Strengthens AYUSH education and quality control of herbal medicines.
>
> o Integrates AYUSH services into public-health programs, including maternal health and nutrition schemes.
>
> o **Example:** Tamil Nadu's pilot AYUSH nutrition program showed reduced anemia and maternal deaths.

7\. Umbrella Programme for Development of Scheduled Tribes (STs) & Minorities:

> • Ensures marginalized communities gain access to education, healthcare, housing, and safe environments.
>
> • Introduces environmental-health monitoring and sanitation improvements in tribal areas exposed to industrial pollution.
>
> • Promotes clean-energy adoption and safe drinking-water access for vulnerable populations.

8\. Road Safety Schemes:

> • Linked to SDG-3.6 but also indirectly contributes to SDG-3.9 by promoting safer, cleaner transport ecosystems.
>
> • Includes:
>
> o Public awareness and publicity on road safety.
>
> o Pollution-control certification, emission-testing infrastructure, and vehicle-scrappage policies.
>
> o National Highways Accident Relief Service Project (NHARSS) to improve emergency medical response.

9\. National AIDS Control Programme (NACP):

> • Though primarily aimed at HIV/AIDS prevention, NACP also builds laboratory and biosafety infrastructure ensuring safe handling of biomedical waste and chemicals.
>
> • Enhances public-health resilience by integrating infection-control and hazardous-waste management within hospitals and clinics.

Challenges in Reducing Pollution-Related Deaths:

> • High urban air-pollution levels and vehicular emissions in megacities.
>
> • Lack of waste segregation and hazardous-chemical management in industries.
>
> • Rural-industrial conflict zones lacking monitoring and remediation mechanisms.
>
> • Data gaps in correlating disease burden to pollution exposure.
>
> • Inadequate coordination between environment, health, and industry ministries.

Recommendations:

> • Strengthen environmental-health surveillance systems across states.
>
> • Mandate pollution-impact health audits for major industrial projects.
>
> • Expand real-time air- and water-quality monitoring networks to all districts.
>
> • Promote clean-energy transitions (LPG, EVs, renewables) and enforce waste-to-energy technologies.
>
> • Enhance community participation in pollution-control planning through local governance and HEIs.
>
> • Foster cross-disciplinary research in toxicology, public health, and environmental sciences.

Expected Outcomes by 2030:

> • Substantial reduction in morbidity and mortality related to air, water, and soil pollution.
>
> • Strengthened environmental-health infrastructure at district and national levels.
>
> • Universal access to safe drinking water, sanitation, and clean energy.
>
> • Increased capacity of the workforce to manage chemical hazards and environmental emergencies.
>
> • Improved public awareness on sustainable living and eco-friendly practices.

Conclusion:

> • Reducing illnesses and deaths from hazardous chemicals and pollution is central to India's health and sustainability agenda.
>
> • Through initiatives such as the Environment Protection Programme, National River Conservation, National Water Mission, and the National Health Mission, India is moving toward a health-centered environmental governance model.
>
> • Achieving SDG-3.9 will require inter-ministerial collaboration, scientific innovation, and community engagement to ensure a cleaner, safer, and healthier environment for present and future generations—realizing the essence of Good Health and Well-Being for All.

**16. Building Infrastructure for Yoga, Meditation, and Counselling**

> • The recommendation encourages Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) to build infrastructure dedicated to yoga, meditation, and counselling.
>
> • The aim is to improve the physical and mental well-being of students, faculty, and surrounding communities — which directly supports United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 3 (SDG-3): Good Health and Well-Being.

Why It Is Needed:

> • Students and faculty in higher education often face academic pressure, anxiety, and lifestyle-related health issues.
>
> • Creating safe and supportive spaces for relaxation, self-reflection, and professional mental health care helps reduce stress, burnout, and depression.
>
> • It ensures a balanced environment where learning, emotional stability, and physical health coexist.

Yoga Infrastructure:

> • Yoga is proven to enhance physical strength, flexibility, and concentration while reducing stress.
>
> • HEIs should:
>
> o Construct yoga halls or open-air wellness zones.
>
> o Hire qualified yoga trainers to conduct regular sessions for students and staff.
>
> o Make yoga a part of orientation programs or daily schedules.
>
> o Collaborate with organizations such as the Ministry of AYUSH for expert guidance.

>
> • HEIs should establish quiet meditation rooms within hostels or academic blocks.
>
> • Conduct mindfulness workshops and short meditation breaks before exams or stressful periods.

> • These spaces promote emotional regulation and a peaceful learning environment.

Counselling and Mental Health Centres:

>
> • Institutions should set up counselling centres staffed with trained psychologists and counsellors.
>
> • Services can include stress management, career guidance, crisis intervention, and therapy sessions.

> • The findings led to evidence-based recommendations focusing on safe rice cultivation and consumption practices.
>
> • Suggested interventions included:
>
> o Promoting the use of low-arsenic rice varieties and safe irrigation sources.
>
> o Encouraging specific rice preparation methods, such as parboiling or rinsing, to reduce arsenic content in cooked rice.
>
> o Raising awareness among farmers about the risks of groundwater irrigation in arsenic-prone areas.
>
> • These research outcomes contribute directly to SDG-3 targets on reducing illness from hazardous chemicals and environmental pollution, as well as SDG-6 (Clean Water and Sanitation) through better water management practices.

Capacity Building and Public Health Training in Indonesia:

>
> • A multidisciplinary team conducted training programs for local health workers focused on cardiovascular disease prevention, risk factor management, and digital health literacy.

>
> • Through this program, 48,000 people benefited from improved access to healthcare information and preventive screening.

Innovation through Digital Health Solutions:

> • The SMART Health app provided real-time data collection and analysis, enabling healthcare workers to make informed decisions about patient management.

> o To protect employees from financial hardship caused by medical expenses and ensure timely access to healthcare facilities.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Implement a comprehensive health insurance policy that covers all categories of staff—academic, administrative, technical, and contractual employees.
>
> o Offer cashless medical facilities through partnerships with recognized hospitals and healthcare providers.
>
> o Cover essential medical services such as:
>
> o Outpatient and inpatient care
>
> o Maternity and child healthcare
>
> o Emergency and surgical treatments
>
> o Preventive check-ups and vaccinations
>
> o Ensure full or partial premium payment by the institution for lower-income or contractual employees to guarantee equitable access.
>
> o Extend family coverage to include dependents (spouse, children, and parents), enhancing social protection.
>
> o Regularly review and update insurance policies to include new medical conditions, telemedicine options, and mental health consultations.
>
> o Collaborate with public sector health schemes such as *Ayushman Bharat*, *Employees' State Insurance (ESI)*, or *Central Government Health Scheme (CGHS)* where applicable.
>
> o Establish a dedicated health benefit committee to manage claims, monitor service quality, and ensure transparency in implementation.

**2. Establish Institutional Health and Wellness Centres**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To complement insurance coverage with accessible on-campus healthcare services for employees.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Set up a campus health centre equipped with general physicians, nurses, and first-aid facilities.
>
> o Offer free annual health check-ups and preventive screenings (blood pressure, sugar levels, cholesterol, etc.) for employees.
>
> o Maintain digital health records for easy access and follow-up care.
>
> o Provide wellness programs, including yoga sessions, stress management workshops, and lifestyle modification guidance.
>
> o Establish tie-ups with local diagnostic centres and hospitals for advanced investigations at negotiated institutional rates.

**3. Promote Preventive and Mental Health Care for Employees**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To address the holistic well-being of employees by including mental and emotional health in institutional health strategies.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Organize mental health counselling sessions, meditation programs, and awareness talks on work-life balance and emotional resilience.
>
> o Offer Employee Assistance Programs (EAPs) providing confidential counselling for stress, burnout, or family-related issues.
>
> o Encourage routine health awareness drives on nutrition, exercise, and lifestyle diseases.
>
> o Observe World Health Day, World Mental Health Day, and National Wellness Week through health education campaigns.

**4. Integrate Health Benefits into Institutional Policy Frameworks**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To institutionalize healthcare as a fundamental employee right rather than a discretionary benefit.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Incorporate health cover provisions into HR manuals, service rules, and employment contracts.
>
> o Make health coverage a mandatory inclusion during recruitment or contract renewal processes.
>
> o Allocate annual budgetary provisions under the institutional welfare fund for healthcare initiatives.
>
> o Monitor annual health utilization reports to assess employee health trends and improve policy responsiveness.
>
> o Recognize and reward departments that actively promote employee well-being and safety.

Expected Outcomes:

> • Guaranteed financial risk protection and reduced out-of-pocket expenditure for all employees.
>
> • Improved employee morale, productivity, and retention due to enhanced workplace well-being.
>
> • Increased preventive healthcare adoption, leading to early diagnosis and reduced disease burden.
>
> • Stronger institutional reputation as a health-promoting and employee-friendly organization.
>
> • Greater alignment with national health policies and international sustainable development frameworks.

Broader Impact on SDG-3 and Related Goals:

> • Directly contributes to SDG 3.8 by providing universal health coverage at the institutional level.
>
> • Supports SDG 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth) by promoting safe, healthy, and sustainable working conditions.
>
> • Aligns with SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) by ensuring equal health access for all categories of staff.
>
> • Strengthens SDG 17 (Partnerships for the Goals) through collaborations with healthcare providers and public health agencies.

Conclusion:

> • By providing free or subsidized health cover, HEIs can serve as models of social responsibility and sustainability, ensuring that every employee enjoys access to quality healthcare without financial strain.
>
> • These measures transform campuses into healthy, equitable, and productive ecosystems, directly advancing the global agenda of SDG-3: Good Health and Well-Being.
>
> • In doing so, HEIs not only safeguard their workforce but also demonstrate leadership in building inclusive and health-secure institutions that uphold the well-being of all stakeholders.

**38. SDG-3 Target 3.9 and Minimal Recommended Actions for Higher Education Institutions (HEIs): Ensuring Healthy Environments through Cleanliness, Waste Management, and Pollution Control**

SDG-3 Target 3.9:

*"By 2030, substantially reduce the number of deaths and illnesses from hazardous chemicals and air, water, and soil pollution and contamination."*

Objective:

> • To promote environmental health, cleanliness, and pollution-free campuses as an integral part of institutional responsibility toward achieving SDG-3: Good Health and Well-Being.
>
> • To ensure that HEIs adopt scientifically sound waste disposal systems and embed the principles of Swachh Bharat Mission (SBM) into academic, social, and outreach activities.
>
> • To build a culture of environmental consciousness and preventive health through student-led social participation and safe laboratory practices.

Minimal Recommended Actions for HEIs

**1. Implement 'Swachh Bharat Mission' as a Social Activity in Each Degree Program**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To integrate cleanliness, sanitation, and community hygiene into the core educational experience, promoting behavioral transformation and civic responsibility among students.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Introduce "Swachh Bharat Mission (SBM)" activities as part of the curriculum or social engagement component for every degree program (undergraduate and postgraduate).
>
> o Encourage students to participate in campus and community cleanliness drives, including waste segregation, plastic reduction, and beautification initiatives.
>
> o Conduct awareness campaigns in surrounding villages, schools, and urban localities to promote cleanliness, hygiene, and safe sanitation practices.
>
> o Partner with local municipal bodies, NGOs, and Swachh Bharat Abhiyan offices to organize regular clean-up events and environmental workshops.
>
> o Include service-learning credits or marks for participation in SBM-related projects.
>
> o Celebrate Gandhi Jayanti (October 2) and World Environment Day (June 5) by conducting mass cleanliness campaigns across the campus.
>
> o Establish student-led Green and Swachh Clubs to maintain ongoing hygiene and waste management initiatives throughout the academic year.

**2. Ensure Proper Chemical Waste Disposal Systems in All HEIs**

> • Purpose:
>
> o To safeguard human health and the environment from chemical exposure and contamination resulting from laboratory and research activities.
>
> • Actions:
>
> o Develop and implement a Chemical Waste Management Policy adhering to Environment, Health, and Safety (EH&S) guidelines.
>
> o Classify and label hazardous and non-hazardous wastes generated from teaching laboratories, research units, and maintenance departments.
>
> o Provide separate containment and collection units for chemical waste, biological waste, and e-waste across laboratories.
>
> o Establish designated storage areas for temporary holding of hazardous materials before disposal or treatment.
>
> o Collaborate with authorized hazardous waste management agencies and local environmental boards for safe transportation and disposal.
>
> o Train faculty, lab technicians, and students in safe handling, segregation, and disposal protocols.
>
> o Introduce eco-friendly laboratory practices, such as the use of green solvents, micro-scale experiments, and waste minimization techniques.
>
> o Install chemical fume hoods and emergency wash stations in all laboratories to ensure occupational safety.
>
> o Conduct biannual safety audits and maintain compliance with national environmental safety regulations.
>
> o Display safety signage, Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS), and disposal instructions prominently in all laboratory spaces.

Expected Outcomes:

> • Enhanced student participation in national cleanliness and hygiene missions, leading to sustained community engagement.
>
> • Creation of clean, pollution-free, and health-promoting campus environments.
>
> • Reduction in chemical and laboratory-related health risks through proper waste segregation and disposal.
>
> • Strengthened compliance with environmental and health safety standards, ensuring institutional accountability.
>
> • Development of a generation of eco-conscious graduates committed to public health, sustainability, and environmental stewardship.

Broader Impact on SDG-3 and Related Goals:

> • Directly supports SDG 3.9 by minimizing pollution-related health hazards and exposure to hazardous chemicals.
>
> • Contributes to SDG 6.3 (Clean Water and Sanitation) through proper wastewater and chemical waste management.
>
> • Aligns with SDG 11.6 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) by promoting waste reduction and sanitation awareness.
>
> • Strengthens SDG 12.4 (Responsible Consumption and Production) through the adoption of environmentally sound waste management practices.
>
> • Advances SDG 13 (Climate Action) by reducing environmental degradation and promoting sustainability-oriented behaviors.

Conclusion:

> • By institutionalizing Swachh Bharat Mission activities and implementing robust chemical waste disposal systems, HEIs can act as epicentres of environmental and public health transformation.
>
> • These initiatives not only ensure compliance with EH&S standards but also instill in students a deep sense of social responsibility and environmental ethics.
>
> • Through education, awareness, and sustainable action, HEIs contribute significantly to the vision of a cleaner, healthier, and more sustainable India, aligning directly with SDG-3: Good Health and Well-Being and its allied sustainability goals.

**39. Proposed Research Agenda for Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) and Governmental Bodies under SDG-3: Good Health and Well-Being**

Objective:

> • To guide Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) and governmental agencies in developing a focused, evidence-based research roadmap aligned with Sustainable Development Goal 3 (SDG-3).
>
> • To promote innovation, interdisciplinary collaboration, and policy-relevant studies aimed at improving public health systems, healthcare access, environmental health, and preventive medicine.
>
> • To encourage the integration of SDG principles into curriculum design, academic research, and institutional strategy for sustainable societal transformation.

Proposed Research Areas for HEIs

**1. Public Health Systems and Preventive Healthcare**

> • Conduct research on the effectiveness of preventive health interventions such as vaccination programs, maternal care, and community health awareness campaigns.
>
> • Evaluate primary healthcare delivery models, focusing on affordability, accessibility, and quality of services in rural and urban regions.
>
> • Study disease surveillance systems and their integration with technology for early detection and outbreak management.
>
> • Develop community-based health indicators for assessing local and regional well-being outcomes.
>
> • Promote comparative studies on traditional medicine and modern healthcare systems to enhance integrative health policy design.

**2. Mental Health, Well-Being, and Youth Resilience**

> • Investigate the prevalence, causes, and coping strategies for mental health issues among adolescents and higher education students.
>
> • Assess the impact of digital learning, social media, and urban stressors on mental wellness.
>
> • Develop evidence-based frameworks for counselling, peer support, and institutional mental health policies.
>
> • Explore the role of yoga, meditation, and mindfulness practices in improving emotional resilience and cognitive performance.
>
> • Study long-term effects of lifestyle habits such as sleep, nutrition, and exercise on youth well-being.

**3. Health Education, Awareness, and SDG Integration in Curriculum**

> • Redesign undergraduate and postgraduate curricula to include Health Education, Sustainable Development, and Social Responsibility modules.
>
> • Conduct studies on the impact of curriculum-based SDG education on student awareness, behavior, and career choices.
>
> • Evaluate MOOCs and digital learning platforms as tools for promoting health literacy and SDG-focused education.
>
> • Develop teaching frameworks and evaluation rubrics for integrating SDG-3 concepts into classroom learning across disciplines.
>
> • Research the pedagogical outcomes of community-based learning initiatives such as fieldwork, health camps, and social internships.

**4. Environmental and Occupational Health**

> • Study the relationship between environmental degradation (air, water, soil pollution) and public health outcomes.
>
> • Research sustainable models for chemical waste management, clean water access, and eco-campus practices.
>
> • Evaluate occupational health and safety conditions for workers in educational, industrial, and agricultural settings.
>
> • Develop early-warning systems for pollution-related diseases and climate-induced health risks.
>
> • Examine the long-term health impacts of urbanization and industrialization through data-driven studies.

**5. Technology and Digital Health Innovation**

> • Explore Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML), and Big Data Analytics applications for disease prediction, telemedicine, and personalized healthcare.
>
> • Conduct studies on the effectiveness of e-health platforms in improving access to healthcare in underserved areas.
>
> • Research the adoption and user behavior of mobile health (mHealth) applications among students and rural populations.
>
> • Promote interdisciplinary collaborations between computer science, public health, and biomedical engineering departments to design scalable digital health tools.
>
> • Develop prototypes for affordable wearable health monitoring devices and conduct pilot implementation studies.

**6. Gender and Reproductive Health Research**

> • Conduct gender-sensitive studies focusing on female reproductive health, menstrual hygiene, and maternal health challenges.
>
> • Analyze policy gaps and implementation barriers in access to sexual and reproductive healthcare services.
>
> • Research the role of education, awareness, and socio-cultural practices in influencing women's health behaviors.
>
> • Encourage collaborative projects between HEIs and public health departments to improve health outcomes among women and marginalized communities.

**7. Nutrition, Lifestyle, and Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs)**

> • Study the dietary patterns and nutrition-related risk factors contributing to obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular diseases among youth.
>
> • Evaluate the effectiveness of Eat Right Campus initiatives in improving nutritional habits.
>
> • Conduct research on food safety, sustainable agriculture, and micronutrient deficiencies.
>
> • Promote studies linking physical activity, lifestyle management, and long-term health outcomes.
>
> • Design and test institutional wellness models that combine diet, physical activity, and stress management programs.

Proposed Research Collaborations for Government and HEIs

> • Governmental Bodies:
>
> o Collaborate with Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW), ICMR, NITI Aayog, AICTE, UGC, and AYUSH for policy-linked research programs.
>
> o Utilize funding under National Health Mission (NHM) and Rashtriya Uchchatar Shiksha Abhiyan (RUSA) to support SDG-based research in universities.
>
> o Develop joint data-sharing frameworks for health research, enabling real-time data collection and policy evaluation.
>
> o Create National Health Research Networks linking HEIs, hospitals, and local governments for collaborative projects.
>
> • Inter-HEI and Global Collaborations:
>
> o Partner with international universities through UNESCO Chairs, WHO Collaborating Centres, and SDG Academies.
>
> o Encourage joint publications, global conferences, and online courses on SDG-3 themes.
>
> o Promote faculty exchange programs for global knowledge sharing in public health, environmental health, and sustainable education.

Expected Outcomes:

> • Strengthened evidence base for health policy and community-based interventions.
>
> • Integration of SDG-3 principles into higher education curricula and research priorities.
>
> • Increased interdisciplinary and inter-institutional collaboration in health and sustainability research.
>
> • Empowerment of students as health-conscious, socially responsible, and sustainability-oriented citizens.
>
> • Tangible contribution to national and global health indicators through research-driven policy innovations.

Conclusion:

> • The alignment of academic research with SDG-3 targets ensures that HEIs not only generate knowledge but also translate it into social impact.
>
> • By redesigning curricula, fostering innovation, and strengthening partnerships, HEIs and governmental bodies can co-create a research ecosystem focused on sustainable health, equity, and well-being.
>
> • This integrated research agenda represents a critical step toward realizing the 2030 Vision for Good Health and Well-Being, reinforcing the role of education and research as the foundation of sustainable development.
