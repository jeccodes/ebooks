**1. Introduction**

This guide provides a robust framework for Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) to excel in NAAC's Criterion 5, which focuses on Student Support and Progression. It elaborates on the policies, practices, and institutional mechanisms designed to foster students’ academic, personal, and professional growth throughout their higher education journey. The central goal of this framework is to improve student retention, graduation rates, employability, and overall well-being, which are critical to institutional excellence and accreditation success. By integrating structured student support services with quality assurance systems, HEIs can create an inclusive and nurturing ecosystem that prepares learners to thrive in a competitive global environment.

A core dimension of Criterion 5 is **student support**, which emphasizes financial assistance, skill development, grievance redressal, and personal counseling. Financial support plays a pivotal role in ensuring equity and inclusivity. Institutions are expected to provide scholarships and freeships funded by the government, non-governmental bodies, industries, philanthropists, or the institution itself. NAAC requires meticulous record-keeping of all beneficiaries, including sanction letters and year-wise lists, and mandates institutions to make this information publicly accessible through their official websites. The benchmark for the highest score under this metric is when at least 60% or more of the total student population benefits from such schemes. This practice not only supports students from marginalized backgrounds but also demonstrates institutional commitment to social responsibility and inclusivity.

Beyond financial support, capacity and skill development initiatives are critical to preparing students for employability and lifelong learning. Institutions are expected to conduct structured programs in four essential areas: soft skills, language and communication skills, life skills, and awareness of technology trends. Soft skills training fosters leadership, teamwork, and problem-solving capabilities, while communication programs strengthen students’ fluency in English and regional languages to compete globally. Life skills initiatives typically focus on yoga, health, hygiene, and entrepreneurial abilities, ensuring students are physically and mentally prepared for challenges. Finally, awareness of technology trends like artificial intelligence, data science, cybersecurity, and digital literacy ensures students remain relevant in rapidly evolving industries. NAAC benchmarks institutions based on the number of these four categories covered annually, with coverage across all four earning the highest grade.

A safe and inclusive campus environment is ensured through effective grievance redressal mechanisms. HEIs must implement University Grants Commission (UGC) guidelines on anti-ragging and sexual harassment by forming statutory committees like the Student Grievance Redressal Committee (SGRC), Internal Complaints Committee (ICC), and Anti-Ragging Committee (ARC). Institutions are also expected to foster awareness campaigns to build a culture of zero tolerance, provide accessible mechanisms for grievance submission (both online and offline), and ensure timely resolution through committee action. NAAC awards the top score only when all four elements—policy guidelines, widespread awareness, grievance submission platforms, and timely redressal—are fully implemented and documented. These efforts create a safer learning environment, encouraging students to focus on their academic and personal growth.

Another vital dimension is **career and personal counseling**, which provides structured support for students in academic planning, career exploration, and personal well-being. Institutions are expected to organize one-to-one counseling sessions, workshops on competitive examinations like UPSC, GATE, NET, or GRE, and career fairs with industry partners. Professional counselors must be available to address student concerns related to stress, anxiety, and mental health. The use of online platforms for e-counseling and digital career guidance tools ensures accessibility for all students, even beyond the campus. Counseling initiatives serve to strengthen student confidence, align career aspirations with academic choices, and enhance employability outcomes.

The second dimension of Criterion 5 focuses on student progression, which measures how effectively an institution supports students’ transition to higher education or employment. NAAC closely evaluates institutional performance in placement, higher education progression, competitive exam qualifications, and alumni engagement. In terms of placement and higher education, institutions must maintain detailed, year-wise data on placed students with proof such as offer letters, compensation details, and company names. Similarly, for higher education progression, evidence such as admission letters or enrollment records from universities is required. Transparency is key, and institutions are encouraged to publish this data annually on their official websites. NAAC considers placement rates of 70% or more to be excellent, while higher education progression rates of 40% or more indicate strong institutional support for academic advancement.

The success of students in competitive examinations like NET, SLET, UPSC, GRE, or GATE serves as an indicator of academic rigor and institutional preparation. Institutions must maintain year-wise lists of qualified students along with copies of certificates or scorecards as verifiable evidence. Even a qualification rate of 1.0% or higher among outgoing students meets NAAC’s top benchmark, reflecting the institution’s contribution to national and global talent pools. To support this outcome, HEIs are encouraged to provide special coaching classes, mentoring schemes, and access to preparatory resources for these competitive exams.

Another essential component of Criterion 5 is alumni engagement, which reflects the strength of the institution’s long-term impact. Alumni play a dual role by contributing financially through scholarships, endowments, and infrastructure development, and non-financially through mentoring, curriculum development, and professional networking opportunities. NAAC specifically evaluates the total financial contribution made by alumni associations during the five-year accreditation period, with contributions of ₹100 lakhs or more receiving the highest score. More importantly, strong alumni networks enrich the current student experience, provide career guidance, and help institutions stay aligned with industry trends.

Underlying all of these dimensions is the principle of documentation and continuous improvement. For every metric, NAAC requires institutions to maintain year-wise data in prescribed formats and provide verifiable evidence such as sanction letters, committee minutes, placement orders, audited financial statements, and photographs with captions for events. Institutions must also ensure that this evidence is available in the public domain, usually through dedicated pages on their official websites. Robust feedback mechanisms from students, alumni, employers, and community stakeholders must be integrated to assess the effectiveness of student support services. Regular audits and reviews conducted by the Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC) help in identifying gaps, implementing corrective measures, and aligning activities with institutional strategic goals.

In conclusion, Criterion 5 of NAAC emphasizes that student support and progression are central to institutional quality and accreditation success. By strengthening financial assistance, skill development, counseling, and grievance redressal, institutions ensure inclusivity and well-being. By promoting placements, higher education, competitive exam preparation, and alumni engagement, they guarantee long-term impact on student careers and societal development. Transparent documentation and continuous quality improvement serve as the backbone of these efforts, enabling institutions to showcase their achievements effectively to accreditation agencies. Ultimately, this framework ensures that Higher Education Institutions fulfill their responsibility of nurturing students not just as graduates, but as empowered global citizens capable of driving positive change in society.

**2. Student Support and Progression**

**Definition**

> • Refers to institutional mechanisms, policies, and practices designed to ensure students’ academic, personal, and professional growth during their tenure in an institution.
>
> • Aims to promote retention, progression, and graduation rates while enhancing student well-being and employability.

**Key Objectives**

> • Facilitate academic success through targeted support systems.
>
> • Provide career guidance and skill development opportunities.
>
> • Encourage student participation in co-curricular and extracurricular activities.
>
> • Address psychological, social, and financial challenges that may hinder student progression.

> • **Academic Support:** Remedial classes, tutoring, mentoring, bridge courses, peer-assisted learning.
>
> • **Career Development:** Placement cells, internship programs, career counselling, skill training workshops.
>
> • **Personal Counselling:** Psychological counselling, stress management programs, peer support groups.

> • **Financial Support:** Scholarships, fee waivers, financial aid, part-time job facilitation.
>
> • **Skill & Talent Development:** Leadership training, entrepreneurship development, communication skills enhancement.
>
> • **Student Grievance Redressal:** Ombudsman committees, anti-ragging cells, sexual harassment prevention cells.

>
> • Maintain an active alumni network for guidance and networking.
>
> • Integrate feedback mechanisms to continuously improve services.

> • Collaborate with industry and NGOs for internships, projects, and training.

>
> • Enhanced academic performance and employability.
>

**6. Efforts Taken by the Institution to Provide Career Counselling, E-Counselling, and Guidance for Competitive Examinations**

The institution recognises that career guidance, timely counselling, and structured preparatory support for competitive examinations are essential components of student progression and employability. Over the last five years, a dedicated Career Guidance and Counselling Cell (CGCC) has been functioning under the Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC) to provide holistic career support services, combining in-person and e-counselling modes to ensure accessibility for all students.

**1. Career Counselling Initiatives**

> • **One-to-One Counselling:** Faculty mentors and professional counsellors conduct personalised sessions to help students identify career goals aligned with their strengths, interests, and academic performance.
>
> • **Career Seminars & Workshops:** Regular sessions on emerging career opportunities in engineering, management, humanities, and sciences, conducted in collaboration with industry experts and alumni.
>
> • **E-Counselling Platform:** An institutional Learning Management System (LMS) integrated with video-conferencing tools enables online career guidance, especially during pandemic restrictions, ensuring continuity of services.
>
> • **Soft Skills Development:** Modules on communication skills, problem-solving, leadership, and time management are embedded into training schedules to improve employability.

**2. Guidance for Competitive Examinations**

> • **Coaching Programs:** Special coaching classes for UPSC, SSC, GATE, NET, CAT, and other competitive exams, facilitated by experienced faculty and external subject experts.
>
> • **Mock Tests & Practice Sessions:** Regular practice tests and full-length mock exams to familiarise students with exam patterns and time management strategies.

**13. Percentage of graduated students who have progressed to higher education in the last five years, calculated year-wise.**

**Metric Description**

> • *5.2.2.1*: Percentage of graduated students who have progressed to higher education in the last five years, calculated year-wise.
>
> • Focuses on tracking students who, after graduation, enrolled in higher studies (PG, diploma, certificate courses, etc.).

**Data to be Collected (Year-wise)**

> • Year – Academic year of graduation (e.g., 2019–20, 2020–21, etc.).
>
> • Number – Total number of outgoing students progressing to higher education.

**Formula for Calculation**

*Percentage = Total Number of outgoing students progressing to higher education / \[(1.2 × Total final year outgoing students) – (5.2.1.1: Total number of Students placed)\] × 100*

> *Numerator*: Students enrolled in higher education after graduation.
>
> *Denominator*: Adjusted total final-year outgoing students after excluding those placed in jobs.

**42. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)**

**Metric 5.1.1: Percentage of students benefited by scholarships and freeships provided by the institution, Government and non-government bodies, industries, individuals, philanthropists during the last five years**

**Focus of the Metric**

> • To assess the extent of financial support provided to students to ensure inclusivity and access to education.
>
> • Captures all forms of scholarships and freeships:
>
> ○ Institutional support (merit scholarships, need-based fee waivers, sports/cultural concessions, etc.)
>
> ○ Government schemes (state/central scholarships, SC/ST/OBC/EWS, minority scholarships, etc.)
>
> ○ Non-governmental/NGO/Industry support (corporate scholarships, CSR-based initiatives, alumni donations, philanthropists, etc.).

**Documents Needed**

1\. Sanction Letters of scholarships/freeships:

> ○ From government, institutional, and non-government agencies.
>
> ○ Must be in English (or translated copies, if originally in regional language).

2\. Policy Document of HEI:

> ○ Outlines eligibility criteria, process of selection, amount, and monitoring mechanism for scholarships/freeships.

3\. Year-wise Beneficiary List:

> ○ Student name, roll number, scheme name, amount received, year.
>
> ○ Signed and certified by the competent authority (Principal/Registrar).

**Specific Instructions to HEIs**

> • Report year-wise data (last five years) in the NAAC Data Template.
>
> • Include all scholarships/freeships sanctioned by institution, government, industries, NGOs, philanthropists.
>
> • During DVV Clarification, HEI should be ready with supporting documents for randomly selected students.
>
> • Ensure that the total number of beneficiaries ≤ Total student strength (EP 1.1).

**Avoid the Following While Uploading Data**

> • Reporting without sanction letters or proof of disbursement.
>
> • Submitting only a list of students without authority signatures.
>
> • Inflating beneficiary numbers beyond the actual student strength.
>
> • Including stipends for internships/assistantships (not counted as scholarships/freeships).
>
> • Uploading unsigned/unauthenticated lists.

**Metric 5.1.3: Capacity Development and Skills Enhancement Initiatives**

**Focus of the Metric**

> • Evaluates whether the institution provides structured opportunities to students for holistic development beyond the regular curriculum.
>
> • Covers four broad areas of capacity development:

1\. Soft skills (leadership, teamwork, problem-solving, time management, personality development).

2\. Language & communication skills (English proficiency, foreign languages, business communication, public speaking).

3\. Life skills (Yoga, meditation, physical fitness, health awareness, hygiene, entrepreneurial skills, self-employment).

4\. Awareness of technology trends (ICT skills, coding, data analytics, AI/ML basics, digital literacy, cybersecurity awareness).

**Documents Needed (Mandatory Evidence)**

1\. Program Reports:

> ○ With objectives, outcomes, and schedule.
>
> ○ Must be year-wise and signed by competent authority.

2\. Photographic Evidence:

> ○ Geo-tagged and date-stamped photos/videos of the sessions.
>
> ○ Must include faculty/trainers and students as participants.

3\. Attendance Records:

> ○ List of students enrolled/attended with signatures or digital attendance system reports.

4\. Certificates (if issued):

> ○ Copies of completion certificates for selected programs.

5\. Annual Consolidated Report:

> ○ Summary of all initiatives conducted under the four categories.

**Specific Instructions to HEIs**

> • Consider all students enrolled in various initiatives (not only those who completed).
>
> • Submit year-wise quantitative data in the NAAC Data Template.
>
> • Ensure programmes are conducted periodically (not one-time events).
>
> • Be ready with sample certificates and attendance proofs during DVV Clarification.

**Avoid the Following While Uploading Data**

> • Mere circulars/notices announcing the program (not acceptable).
>
> • Only lists of students without signatures/attendance evidence.
>
> • Programs that are part of the regular curriculum/credit courses (not counted).
>
> • Uploading generic workshops (must be skill/capacity enhancement-specific).
>
> • Claiming online awareness emails/notices as initiatives.

**Metric 5.1.4: Redressal of Student Grievances (including Sexual Harassment and Ragging Cases)**

**Focus of the Metric**

> • To evaluate whether the institution has robust, transparent, and effective mechanisms for addressing student grievances.
>
> • Covers four critical components:

1\. Implementation of statutory/regulatory guidelines (UGC, AICTE, MHRD, State Acts).

2\. Organisation-wide awareness and undertakings (Zero tolerance to sexual harassment, ragging, discrimination).

3\. Accessible mechanisms for grievance submission (online/offline).

4\. Timely redressal of grievances through designated committees (ICC, Anti-Ragging, Grievance Redressal).

**Documents Needed (Mandatory Evidence)**

1\. Committee Details:

> ○ Orders/notifications establishing committees such as:
>
> ○ Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) for sexual harassment (as per UGC regulations, 2015).
>
> ○ Anti-Ragging Committee (as per UGC & AICTE guidelines).
>
> ○ Grievance Redressal Cell/Committee.
>
> ○ Must be published on institutional website with membership list.

2\. Implementation Proof:

> ○ Circulars, notices, and policy documents on compliance with statutory guidelines.
>
> ○ Institutional policies with zero tolerance statements.

3\. Awareness Activities:

> ○ Reports, circulars, campaigns, workshops on gender sensitization, anti-ragging, inclusivity.
>
> ○ Photos, posters, media coverage of awareness programmes.

4\. Grievance Submission Mechanism:

> ○ Screenshots of online grievance portal/email system.
>
> ○ Copy of grievance forms/register for offline submissions.

5\. Committee Records:

> ○ Annual reports of committees with summary of grievances received and resolved.
>
> ○ Minutes of meetings where grievance cases were reviewed.

**Specific Instructions to HEIs**

> • Ensure website link of grievance redressal cell, ICC, and Anti-Ragging Cell is functional.
>
> • Upload year-wise evidence (awareness programmes, meeting reports).
>
> • Show timeliness of redressal in committee minutes (dates of receipt vs. resolution).
>
> • For DVV Clarification, HEIs should be ready with sample grievance case files (anonymized).

**Avoid the Following While Uploading Data**

> • Merely listing committee names without official notification orders.
>
> • Uploading only policy documents but not proof of implementation.
>
> • Reporting awareness programmes without evidence (photos, reports).
>
> • Showing inactive grievance portals or broken web links.
>
> • Submitting lists of complaints without minutes of meetings or resolution status.

**Metric 5.2.1: Percentage of Placement of Outgoing Students During the Last Five Years**

**Focus of the Metric**

> • Evaluates the effectiveness of institutional career support systems in securing job placements for outgoing students.
>
> • Reflects the employability of graduates and the strength of the industry-academia interface.

**Documents Needed (Mandatory Evidence)**

1\. Placement List (Year-wise, Last Five Years):

> ○ Student name, roll number/ID, program studied.
>
> ○ Placement details: Company name, position offered, compensation package.
>
> ○ Link to placement data hosted on institutional website.

2\. Appointment/Offer Letters:

> ○ Copies (with sensitive details masked if needed).
>
> ○ Must correspond to the list provided.

3\. Placement Drive Reports:

> ○ Internal reports of placement drives conducted.
>
> ○ Photos, brochures, and records of recruiters visiting campus.

4\. Supporting Website Link:

> ○ Institution’s placement cell page showing placement statistics, recruiter logos, and testimonials.

**Specific Instructions to HEIs**

> • Include placements through:
>
> ○ On-campus drives.
>
> ○ Pool campus recruitment drives hosted by other HEIs (with proof).
>
> • Multiple offers to the same student → counted as one placement only.
>
> • Maintain consistency in student data with examination records (outgoing students must match EP 1.1 data).
>
> • If data volume is high, NAAC DVV partners will ask for random sample of appointment orders for verification.

**Avoid the Following While Uploading Data**

> • Inflating placement numbers by counting multiple offers as separate placements.
>
> • Uploading unsigned/unverified lists without official attestation.
>
> • Claiming internships, part-time jobs, or self-employment as placements (unless documented separately).
>
> • Placement statistics not supported by appointment/offer letters.
>
> • Broken website links or outdated placement records.

**Metric 5.2.2: Percentage of Graduated Students Who Have Progressed to Higher Education Year-Wise During the Last Five Years**

**Focus of the Metric**

> • Measures the academic progression of graduates into higher education (postgraduate, doctoral, or professional degrees).
>
> • Highlights how effectively the HEI motivates and prepares students to pursue advanced studies.

**Documents Needed (Mandatory Evidence)**

1\. Year-wise List of Students Progressing to Higher Education:

> ○ Student name, roll number/ID, program graduated.
>
> ○ Details of the program joined (degree/diploma/doctoral).
>
> ○ Institution/University name of enrollment.
>
> ○ Link to data hosted on institutional website.

2\. Proof of Enrollment/Continuation in Higher Education:

> ○ Admission letters of selected students.
>
> ○ Student identity cards, enrollment records, or registration certificates from the admitting institution.

3\. Consolidated Report:

> ○ Year-wise count of graduates vs. number progressing to higher education.
>
> ○ Summary attested by the Head of Institution/Registrar.

**Specific Instructions to HEIs**

> • Data must be year-wise for the last five years.
>
> • Ensure only students admitted to higher education are counted.
>
> • Appearing/passing entrance exams (e.g., GATE, CAT, GRE) cannot be considered unless admission is secured.
>
> • Placement, self-employment, or competitive exams without admission fall under other metrics, not here.
>
> • If the data is large, NAAC DVV partners will seek admission letters or ID cards of randomly selected students for verification.

**Avoid the Following While Uploading Data**

> • Claiming students only preparing for higher education (not admitted).
>
> • Uploading lists without proof of enrollment.
>
> • Providing unsigned lists without authentication by competent authority.
>
> • Including duplicate students across multiple years.
>
> • Giving links on institutional website that are inactive or non-functional.

**Metric 5.2.3: Percentage of Students Qualifying in State/National/International Level Examinations Out of Graduated Students During the Last Five Years**

**Focus of the Metric**

> • Evaluates the academic competitiveness and preparedness of students for state, national, and international examinations.
>
> • Includes qualifying in exams such as:
>
> ○ State Level Eligibility Test (SLET)
>
> ○ National Eligibility Test (NET)
>
> ○ Civil Services Exams (UPSC, State PSC)
>
> ○ GATE, GMAT, GRE, TOEFL, IELTS, CAT, JAM, JRF, or other recognized tests conducted by State/Central Govt. or international bodies.

**Documents Needed (Mandatory Evidence)**

1\. Year-wise List of Students Qualified (last five years):

> ○ Student name, roll number/ID, program graduated.
>
> ○ Exam name, category (state/national/international), year of qualification.
>
> ○ Link to supporting certificates or result notifications uploaded on institutional website.

2\. Qualifying Certificates:

> ○ Scanned copies of exam qualification certificates.
>
> ○ Or, official scorecards for exams such as NET, GATE, UPSC prelims/mains, GRE/TOEFL.

3\. Supporting Documents (if applicable):

> ○ Letters/emails from exam authorities confirming results.
>
> ○ Institution’s consolidated report signed by competent authority.

**Specific Instructions to HEIs**

> • Only students who have graduated from the HEI and qualified examinations can be considered.
>
> • Job recruitment tests (e.g., bank clerical exams, company-specific tests) are not eligible.
>
> • NAAC DVV partners may request certificates of randomly selected students for verification.
>
> • Appearing/writing exams without qualification should not be included.
>
> • Ensure exam qualification records are linked on the institutional website for transparency.

**Avoid the Following While Uploading Data**

> • Claims without qualifying certificates → not considered.
>
> • Listing students who only appeared but did not qualify.
>
> • Including recruitment/job selection tests not recognized under this metric.
>
> • Uploading unauthenticated or unsigned lists.
>
> • Certificates not clearly showing student name + exam + year.

**Metric 5.3.1: Number of Awards/Medals Won by Students for Outstanding Performance in Sports/Cultural Activities at Inter-University, State, National, or International Events (Last Five Years)**

**Focus of the Metric**

> • Evaluates the achievements of students in sports and cultural activities beyond the institutional level.
>
> • Only considers awards/medals at inter-university, state, national, and international events.
>
> • Team event awards are counted as one award (not multiple for each participant).

**Documents Needed (Mandatory Evidence)**

1\. Year-wise List of Awardees:

> ○ Student name, roll number/ID.
>
> ○ Event name, level (inter-university/state/national/international).
>
> ○ Award/medal title (e.g., Gold Medal, Best Speaker, Silver Medal in Athletics).
>
> ○ Year of achievement.
>
> ○ Link to institutional website displaying achievements.

2\. Award Letters/Certificates:

> ○ E-copies of official award certificates or letters.
>
> ○ For NCC students, participation in Republic Day Parade can be considered.

3\. Event Details:

> ○ Brochures, press releases, media coverage, or event reports confirming participation and award.

**Specific Instructions to HEIs**

> • Consider only inter-university, state, national, and international level awards.
>
> • Team awards → counted as one, even if multiple students are involved.
>
> • Maintain year-wise documentation in the NAAC Data Template.
>
> • Ensure evidence is uploaded and linked on the institutional website.
>
> • NCC Republic Day Parade participation can be counted as recognition.

**Avoid the Following While Uploading Data**

> • Intra-institutional or departmental-level awards (not valid).
>
> • Local/regional/community awards outside university/state/national/international framework.
>
> • Participation certificates without awards/medals (not considered).
>
> • Duplicate counting of team event awards.
>
> • Awards with no verifiable documentation (press notes, certificates, etc.).

**Metric 5.3.3: The Institution Conducts/Organizes Sports, Cultural, Technical/Academic Fests, and Other Events Through Active Clubs and Forums**

**Focus of the Metric**

> • Assesses the vibrancy of campus life by tracking the number and variety of events organized by the HEI.
>
> • Focuses on four broad categories of activities:

1\. Sports competitions/events (intra-college tournaments, annual sports meets).

2\. Cultural competitions/events (music, dance, theatre, debates, literary festivals).

3\. Technical/academic fests (hackathons, paper presentations, quiz contests, exhibitions).

4\. Other events through clubs/forums (eco-club, NSS/NCC special drives, entrepreneurship cell, photography club, drama club, etc.).

**Documents Needed (Mandatory Evidence)**

1\. Event Reports:

> ○ Detailed report of each event with objectives, participation details, and outcomes.
>
> ○ Year-wise compilation.

2\. Photographic Evidence:

> ○ Geo-tagged and date-stamped photos/videos.
>
> ○ Captions clearly stating event, date, and activity details.

3\. Circulars/Brochures/Invitations:

> ○ Proof of event announcement/organization.
>
> ○ Copies of event schedules/programs.

4\. Student Participation List:

> ○ Year-wise, event-wise participant list with student details.
>
> ○ Must be signed and verified by the Head of Institution/competent authority.

**Specific Instructions to HEIs**

> • Only events organized by the HEI should be considered (not external events students attended).
>
> • Maintain year-wise documentation in the NAAC Data Template.
>
> • Photos and reports must clearly link to organized events, not informal gatherings.
>
> • Encourage active clubs and forums to record their activities systematically.

**Avoid the Following While Uploading Data**

> • Including external events organized by other institutions.
>
> • Submitting undated or uncategorized photos.
>
> • Providing lists of students without signatures/verification.
>
> • Counting routine academic classes or lectures as events.
>
> • Claiming informal student gatherings as official events.

**Metric 5.4.1: Alumni Contribution During the Last Five Years (INR in Lakhs) to the University Through Registered Alumni Association**

**Focus of the Metric**

> • Measures the financial support extended by alumni towards institutional development, student support, scholarships, or infrastructure.
>
> • Recognizes the strength of the alumni network and their commitment to giving back to the institution.
>
> • Only contributions made through a formally registered Alumni Association are considered.

**Documents Needed (Mandatory Evidence)**

1\. Annual Audited Statements of Accounts:

> ○ Must highlight alumni contributions separately.
>
> ○ Certified by a Chartered Accountant (CA) or Finance Officer.

2\. Year-wise List of Contributors:

> ○ Names of alumni (or anonymized codes if confidentiality is required).
>
> ○ Amount contributed per alumnus.
>
> ○ Consolidated total for each year.

3\. Proof of Registered Alumni Association:

> ○ Registration certificate of Alumni Association/Society/Trust.
>
> ○ Constitution and by-laws (if applicable).

**Specific Instructions to HEIs**

> • Report contributions year-wise for the last five years.
>
> • Only include donations routed through Alumni Association bank accounts, not informal individual contributions.
>
> • Contributions in kind (equipment, books, furniture) should not be monetized unless formally reflected in audited accounts.
>
> • Ensure alumni association is registered; unregistered groups’ contributions will not be considered.

**Avoid the Following While Uploading Data**

> • Contributions without audited proof.
>
> • Listing alumni names without contribution records.
>
> • Including informal donations not routed through Alumni Association.
>
> • Counting CSR/industry donations under alumni contributions.
>
> • Submitting unsigned or uncertified financial statements.
