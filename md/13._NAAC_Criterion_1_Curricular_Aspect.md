## Abstract

The Curricular Aspects dimension under **Criterion 1 of NAAC Accreditation** evaluates how Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) design, implement, and enhance their academic programs to ensure relevance, flexibility, and quality. It emphasizes participatory curriculum design involving statutory bodies, faculty, industry experts, alumni, and students, with regular revisions to incorporate emerging disciplines and national priorities such as NEP 2020, Academic Bank of Credits (ABC), and multidisciplinary approaches. Academic flexibility is promoted through the Choice-Based Credit System (CBCS), open electives, interdisciplinary offerings, and credit transfer for MOOCs, enabling diverse learning pathways. Curriculum enrichment integrates cross-cutting issues including ethics, gender sensitivity, sustainability, and social responsibility, supported by experiential learning through projects, internships, and community engagement. Employability, entrepreneurship, and skill development are fostered via industry collaborations, incubation centers, and value-added programs. The framework also highlights Outcome-Based Education (OBE), mapping POs, PSOs, and COs to Bloom's Taxonomy, with continuous monitoring of attainment levels. Quality assurance is institutionalized through IQAC-led academic audits, structured feedback systems, and Action Taken Reports (ATRs) for transparency and accountability. Collectively, these processes ensure that HEIs balance national priorities with global trends, producing graduates equipped for professional success and responsible citizenship.

# 1. Introduction

The institution adopts a comprehensive and systematic approach to curriculum design, delivery, and quality assurance, ensuring alignment with the objectives of the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 and the benchmarks of accreditation bodies such as NAAC. Curriculum design is a participatory exercise led by the Board of Studies and the Academic Council, with active contributions from subject experts, industry professionals, alumni, and other stakeholders. This inclusive process ensures that programmes remain relevant, skill-oriented, and research-focused, while also upholding the institution's vision and values. The curriculum is reviewed and revised periodically, typically every two to five years, to keep pace with emerging global trends and national priorities. In recent years, cutting-edge domains such as Artificial Intelligence, Data Science, Internet of Things, Blockchain, Cybersecurity, Climate Change, and Entrepreneurship have been integrated into various courses, thereby strengthening both employability and research orientation.

Academic flexibility is a core institutional principle, providing students with diverse programme options across undergraduate, postgraduate, and doctoral levels. The institution has effectively implemented the Choice-Based Credit System (CBCS), allowing learners to customize their academic pathways by choosing from electives, interdisciplinary courses, skill-based modules, and ability enhancement courses. Credit transfer provisions, particularly for online courses offered through SWAYAM, NPTEL, and similar platforms, further expand the learning ecosystem and ensure global exposure. This flexibility empowers students to pursue individual learning interests while achieving broader academic outcomes.

Curriculum enrichment focuses on embedding cross-cutting issues such as ethics, gender sensitivity, environmental sustainability, human rights, and social responsibility into the teaching–learning framework. Beyond theoretical learning, the institution prioritizes experiential learning by integrating field projects, research projects, community service, and internships into the curriculum. Value-added certificate and diploma courses, along with MOOCs, enhance employability and entrepreneurship skills while promoting lifelong learning.

Employability and entrepreneurship form the backbone of the institution's curriculum strategy. Industry readiness is fostered through structured final-year projects, mandatory internships, industry-mentored courses, and collaboration with corporate partners like Infosys, Wipro, and TCS, as well as national bodies such as the NSDC. Entrepreneurship development is nurtured through hackathons, pitch fests, incubation centres, and the Institutional Innovation Council, enabling students to translate innovative ideas into viable business ventures. Hands-on training, skill labs, and value-added courses further strengthen the employability profile of graduates.

The institution has fully embraced Outcome-Based Education (OBE), systematically defining and mapping Programme Outcomes (POs), Programme Specific Outcomes (PSOs), and Course Outcomes (COs). Learning outcomes are aligned with Bloom's Taxonomy levels and are monitored through continuous assessments, end-semester evaluations, viva-voce examinations, and student exit surveys. Data on outcome attainment is regularly analyzed, and gaps are addressed through targeted interventions in curriculum delivery, teaching methodologies, and resource allocation, ensuring a cycle of continuous improvement.

Feedback plays a central role in curriculum evaluation and revision. Structured mechanisms are in place for collecting and analyzing feedback from students, faculty, alumni, employers, and academic peers on the relevance and effectiveness of the curriculum. This feedback is consolidated into Action Taken Reports (ATRs), and outcomes are documented and disseminated on the institution's website to uphold transparency and accountability.

The institution integrates Information and Communication Technology (ICT) into all aspects of curriculum delivery. Learning Management Systems (LMS), e-content repositories, and blended learning practices ensure access to digital resources and interactive teaching. Platforms such as SWAYAM, NPTEL, Moodle, and Google Classroom are actively used to supplement classroom teaching, while innovative pedagogical approaches like flipped classrooms, blended modes, and simulation-based learning enhance student engagement and understanding.

Quality assurance is institutionalized through the Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC), which monitors compliance with national frameworks and accreditation standards. Regular academic audits, peer reviews, and self-assessment exercises are conducted to maintain academic excellence. The institution has also developed structured data management systems, including Excel trackers and templates aligned with NAAC's Quantitative Metrics (QnM), covering areas such as curriculum design, academic flexibility, enrichment activities, feedback systems, and experiential learning. These tools provide ready-to-use frameworks for data collection, calculation of scores, and preparation of supporting evidence, such as minutes of BoS/Academic Council meetings, course brochures, MoUs with industry, project reports, and completion certificates.

Overall, the institution's academic ecosystem reflects a balance between tradition and innovation, theory and practice, and national priorities and global trends. By embedding NEP 2020 principles of flexibility, multidisciplinarity, and holistic development, while also adhering to NAAC's emphasis on evidence-based quality assurance, the institution ensures that its graduates are well-equipped with knowledge, skills, values, and competencies required for professional success and responsible citizenship.

# 32. NAAC Metric 1.2.1 – Percentage of New Courses Introduced (QnM)

- Definition: Measures the extent of curriculum innovation by tracking the percentage of new courses introduced in the last five years out of the total number of courses offered.

- Weightage: 30 points

Scoring Bands (Quantitative Matrix)

- 4 points (Maximum) → ≥ 20% new courses introduced

- 3 points → 15% – \<20% new courses introduced

- 2 points → 10% – \<15% new courses introduced

- 1 point → 5% – \<10% new courses introduced

- 0 points (Minimum) → \<5% new courses introduced

Formula for Calculation

Percentage of New Courses Introduced = (Number of New Courses Introduced in Last 5 Years / Total Number of Courses Offered in Last 5 Years) × 100

Data Required

- List of all programmes and courses offered during the last 5 years

- Identification of new courses introduced year-wise

- Approval documents / curriculum revision records

Evidence to Maintain

- Minutes of Academic Council / BoS meetings approving new courses

- Curriculum revision documents (syllabus copies)

- Institutional website showing updated curriculum

# 40. STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE (SOP) FOR NAAC CRITERION 1 – CURRICULAR ASPECTS

1\. Purpose

- To establish a systematic process for curriculum design, development, implementation, and review.

- To ensure alignment with NEP 2020, UGC/AICTE guidelines, and NAAC accreditation standards.

- To promote continuous improvement, quality assurance, and outcome-based education.

2\. Scope

- Applicable to all Undergraduate, Postgraduate, and Doctoral programs of the institution.

- Covers processes from curriculum planning to delivery, enrichment, and feedback incorporation.

3\. Responsibilities

- Academic Council (AC): Final approval of curriculum, academic regulations, and revisions.

- Board of Studies (BoS): Drafting, reviewing, and recommending syllabi, incorporating expert and industry inputs.

- IQAC (Internal Quality Assurance Cell): Ensures quality standards, maintains documentation, and coordinates NAAC-related evidence.

- Departments / Faculty: Implement curriculum, integrate ICT-enabled pedagogy, and collect feedback.

- External Experts / Industry Partners: Provide inputs on employability, emerging technologies, and industry readiness.

4\. Standard Process Flow

### 4.1 Curriculum Design and Development

- Establish BoS with subject experts, industry representatives, and alumni.

- Benchmark programs with national frameworks (NEP 2020, UGC, AICTE) and reputed institutions.

- Integrate emerging domains (AI, IoT, Climate Change, Cybersecurity, etc.) into syllabi.

- Emphasize value-based education, skill enhancement, and research orientation.

- Revise curriculum every 2–5 years with documented approval (BoS & Academic Council minutes).

### 4.2 Academic Flexibility

- Implement Choice-Based Credit System (CBCS).

- Offer interdisciplinary, multidisciplinary, and elective courses.

- Facilitate credit transfer and MOOCs (SWAYAM, NPTEL, Coursera) integration.

### 4.3 Curriculum Enrichment

- Embed cross-cutting issues: ethics, gender sensitivity, environmental sustainability, human values.

- Provide field projects, internships, service-learning, and mini-projects.

- Offer value-added/certificate courses beyond the core syllabus.

### 4.4 Employability, Entrepreneurship, and Skill Development

- Integrate industry-oriented courses, final-year projects, and internships.

- Collaborate with industry partners (Infosys, Wipro, TCS, NSDC, etc.).

- Encourage entrepreneurship via IIC (Institution's Innovation Council), hackathons, pitch fests.

### 4.5 Outcome-Based Education (OBE)

- Define and map Programme Outcomes (POs), Course Outcomes (COs), Programme Specific Outcomes (PSOs).

- Use rubrics and assessment tools to measure attainment.

- Document and monitor outcome achievements annually.

### 4.6 Feedback System

- Collect structured feedback from students, faculty, alumni, and employers.

- Analyze data systematically through IQAC.

- Prepare Action Taken Reports (ATR).

- Publish ATR on the institution's website for transparency.

### 4.7 ICT and Curriculum Delivery

- Adopt Learning Management System (LMS) and digital repositories.

- Use ICT-enabled pedagogy: smart classrooms, e-content, blended learning.

- Provide faculty development for innovative teaching practices.

5\. Documentation & Evidence (for NAAC)

- BoS & Academic Council Minutes (for curriculum approval/revision).

- Curriculum framework and syllabi copies.

- Records of new courses introduced (NAAC Metric 1.2.1).

- List of certificate/value-added courses (NAAC Metric 1.3.2).

- Internship/MoU records (NAAC Metric 1.3.3).

- Feedback records and ATR with website link (NAAC Metric 1.4.1).

- Student enrollment and outgoing student data (NAAC Metric 1.1, 1.2).

6\. Review and Continuous Improvement

- IQAC conducts an annual curriculum audit.

- External academic and industry experts review relevance and employability.

- Feedback loops ensure dynamic curriculum enrichment.

- Action plans updated every academic year.

## Metric 1.2.1: Percentage of New Courses Introduced (Last 5 Years)

Formula:

Percentage = Number of New Courses Introduced in Last 5 Years / Total Number of Courses Across All Programmes × 100

1\. Documents Needed

- Minutes of Board of Studies (BoS) Meetings

<!-- -->

- Clearly specify syllabus approval of new courses.

- Should include course code, title, credits, and syllabus details.

<!-- -->

- Academic Council (AC) Meeting Extracts

<!-- -->

- Endorsement of BoS decisions regarding new course introduction.

- Acts as the final academic approval.

<!-- -->

- Additional Supporting Documents (if available)

<!-- -->

- Notifications/circulars of new course introduction.

- Curriculum revision reports.

- Course structure booklet / revised syllabus copy.

- Website links (for public access).

2\. Specific Instructions to HEI

- Ensure that "Course" vs "Programme" distinction is clear (as per NAAC glossary):

<!-- -->

- Course → A paper/subject in a programme. Example: Data Structures in B.Tech.

- Programme → Degree awarded. Example: B.Tech CSE.

<!-- -->

- Provide year-wise list of new courses introduced across programmes.

- Upload authenticated minutes and approvals (BoS + AC).

- Maintain a summary sheet with:

<!-- -->

- Academic year

- Programme name

- Course title

- Nature of course (new/modified)

- Date of approval

3\. Avoid the Following

- Uploading only syllabi without BoS/AC approval documents.

- Uploading programmes introduced instead of courses (misinterpretation).

- Submitting draft documents without official signatures.

- Mixing backlog of previous approvals with the 5-year SSR window.

## Metric 1.3.2: Number of Certificate / Value-Added Courses

Definition:

Counts the number of certificate, value-added, diploma programmes, and recognized online courses (MOOCs, SWAYAM, NPTEL, e-Pathshala, etc.) offered by the institution and successfully completed by students in the last five years.

1\. Documents Needed

- Institutional Programme Brochure / Notice

<!-- -->

- Must specify course modules, objectives, and outcomes.

<!-- -->

- List of Students with Attendance Sheets

<!-- -->

- To prove actual participation in certificate/value-added courses.

<!-- -->

- Evidence of Course Completion

<!-- -->

- Certificates of completion (issued by institution or platform like SWAYAM/NPTEL).

- Digital completion reports (MOOC dashboards).

2\. Specific Instructions to HEI

- Minimum duration of 30 contact hours for each course (mandatory).

- Courses should be outside the regular curriculum, optional, and value-adding.

- If the same add-on/certificate/value-added course is repeated every year, it should be counted as ONE course only (not multiple).

- Only courses offered by the institution (or through formal MoU/collaboration with MOOC platforms) should be considered.

- Student-wise completion certificates should be collected and archived.

3\. Avoid the Following

- Counting online courses taken independently by students (without institutional offering/collaboration).

- Repeating the same course multiple times across years as separate entries.

- Including short-term workshops/seminars with less than 30 hours duration.

- Uploading attendance lists without completion certificates.

5\. Best Practice for Compliance

- Maintain a repository of course materials, brochures, attendance sheets, and certificates year-wise.

- Use an Excel tracker to summarize courses and completion data across 5 years.

- Upload scanned certificates and enrolment lists as supporting proof.

## Metric 1.3.3: Percentage of Programmes with Field Projects / Research / Internships

Definition:

- This metric measures the extent of experiential learning embedded into programmes through field projects, research projects, or internships in the last five years.

- Focus is on programmes (not individual students) that formally integrate such components with credits, evaluation, and documentation.

1\. Documents Needed

- Programme and Course Contents

<!-- -->

- BOS-approved syllabi explicitly mentioning field projects / internships / research with credit allocation.

<!-- -->

- Internship Completion Letters

<!-- -->

- Issued by host institutions/organizations to confirm student participation.

<!-- -->

- Evaluated Project Reports / Field Work Reports

<!-- -->

- Signed and evaluated by faculty/examiners.

<!-- -->

- Internship / Research Completion Certificates

<!-- -->

- Provided by host institutions, to be matched with specific student lists.

2\. Specific Instructions to HEI

- Only consider programmes with BOS-approved curriculum components (not voluntary/extra activities).

- Internship = inclusive of Industry Immersion practices, short-term training, and research collaborations.

- Ensure that credits are assigned in the curriculum for these activities.

- Maintain a programme-wise list of students along with completion certificates.

- Evidence should show academic linkage (not just external training).

3\. Avoid the Following

- Uploading only lists of students without completion certificates or reports.

- Including voluntary/uncredited internships not part of official programme structure.

- Counting seminars/workshops as internships or field projects.

- Submitting incomplete documentation (only BOS approval without completion records).

## 4. NAAC Metric 1.4.1 – Structured Feedback for Curriculum

1\. Metric Description

- Objective: To evaluate how systematically the institution collects, analyses, and acts on curriculum-related feedback from all stakeholders.

- Stakeholders considered:

<!-- -->

- Students

- Teachers

- Employers

- Alumni

- Academic peers

<!-- -->

- Scoring Classification (A–E):

<!-- -->

- A (Score 4): Feedback collected, analysed, action taken & communicated, and hosted on the institutional website.

- B (Score 3): Feedback collected, analysed, action taken, and communicated to relevant body.

- C (Score 2): Feedback collected and analysed.

- D (Score 1): Feedback collected only.

- E (Score 0): Feedback not collected.

2\. Essential Documents Required

- Filled-in feedback forms: At least 4 samples per stakeholder type (Students, Teachers, Employers, Alumni, Academic peers).

- Feedback analysis reports: Consolidated reports submitted to the relevant committee.

- Action Taken Reports (ATR): Document showing the actions implemented based on the feedback.

- Web link: Institution's website page hosting feedback, analysis, and ATR (publicly visible).

3\. Specific Instructions to HEI

- Collect year-wise feedback across all 5 years.

- Ensure feedback is strictly related to curriculum design & review.

- Document communication of actions taken (meeting minutes, circulars, website updates).

- Maintain stakeholder category-wise feedback evidence.

- Host comprehensive feedback reports & ATR on the HEI website (other platforms not valid).

4\. Avoid the Following (NAAC Restrictions)

- Feedback not related to curriculum development.

- Reports hosted on platforms other than the official HEI website.

- Incomplete or sample-only feedback forms (must be filled-in and authentic).

- Counting fewer than 4 filled-in forms per stakeholder type.

- Submitting student-only feedback (must cover all stakeholder categories).
