**Abstract**

A systematic and transparent feedback mechanism is an essential pillar of governance and academic excellence in higher education institutions (HEIs). It provides a structured process to collect, analyze, and act upon inputs from multiple stakeholders, thereby ensuring Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) and aligning institutional performance with the requirements of accreditation frameworks such as NAAC and NBA. Feedback is defined as a structured mechanism to capture opinions, perceptions, and suggestions from stakeholders on various dimensions of academic and administrative functioning. Its core purposes include assessing teaching–learning effectiveness, evaluating curriculum relevance to industry needs and employability, identifying infrastructure and resource gaps, enhancing student outcomes and satisfaction, and serving as documented evidence for accreditation and quality assurance.

A comprehensive mechanism involves diverse stakeholders, each contributing unique perspectives: students provide feedback on curriculum, teaching, evaluation, facilities, and support services; faculty share insights on curriculum design, workload balance, pedagogy, and resource adequacy; alumni evaluate program relevance to careers, graduate preparedness, and industry applicability; employers and industry partners assess graduate competency, skill gaps, employability, and collaboration opportunities; and parents highlight institutional support, student development, placements, and overall student experience.

The feedback cycle follows a systematic CQI model: Feedback → Analysis → Action Taken → Disclosure → Impact. Data is collected through surveys, structured forms, interviews, and suggestion boxes, followed by both quantitative (percentages, indices, NPWI scores) and qualitative (comments, grievances) analysis. Corrective and preventive measures are then initiated by HoDs, IQAC, or Management, with Action Taken Reports (ATRs) published on institutional platforms to ensure transparency. The cycle culminates in measurable impact on curriculum, pedagogy, infrastructure, facilities, and student success.

To ensure inclusiveness, multiple feedback tools are deployed, such as structured online/offline forms, Student Satisfaction Surveys (SSS), exit/graduate surveys, alumni and employer surveys, focus groups, interviews, suggestion boxes, and grievance mechanisms. These tools capture inputs across critical areas including curriculum (industry relevance, PO/CO alignment, practical exposure), teaching–learning and faculty (pedagogy, subject knowledge, interaction, mentoring), infrastructure and learning resources (classrooms, laboratories, libraries, ICT, hostels), support services (career guidance, counseling, placement, administrative efficiency), and overall student experience (inclusivity, campus culture, grievance redressal).

Feedback plays a crucial role in accreditation. For NAAC, it provides evidence under multiple criteria, including Curriculum Design & Development (Criterion 1), Teaching–Learning and Evaluation (Criterion 2), Student Support & Progression (Criterion 5), and Governance, Leadership & Management (Criterion 6). For NBA, feedback is integral to the Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) cycle, mapping to Program Outcomes (POs) and Course Outcomes (COs) within the Outcome-Based Education (OBE) framework.

Reporting and action taken are carried out through standardized templates and formats, covering student, faculty, alumni, parent, and employer surveys, as well as special feedback forms for guest lectures, FDPs, STTPs, workshops, industrial visits, central/digital library, examination cell, registrar office, sports & yoga, medical facilities, hostel services, and extension activities. Reports include quantitative analysis (percentages, weighted indices, 3D graphs), qualitative insights (strengths, gaps, suggestions), Action Taken Reports (ATRs) that demonstrate responsiveness, and public disclosure of ATRs via institutional websites to build trust.

In conclusion, a systematic feedback mechanism is not merely a compliance exercise but a strategic quality tool. By ensuring transparency, stakeholder involvement, and evidence-based corrective actions, institutions can continuously enhance their academic delivery, facilities, and student outcomes. Moreover, it strengthens their NAAC/NBA accreditation standing by providing robust, verifiable, and transparent documentation of CQI practices.

**1. Introduction**

Feedback in higher education is defined as a systematic mechanism for collecting opinions, experiences, and suggestions from various stakeholders, including students, faculty, alumni, employers, parents, and industry representatives. Its fundamental purpose is to assess institutional performance and drive continuous quality improvement (CQI) across academic, administrative, and infrastructural domains. The core objectives of feedback collection are multifaceted. Firstly, it evaluates the effectiveness of teaching and learning, the relevance of the curriculum, and the adequacy of institutional facilities. Secondly, it helps identify gaps in academic delivery, infrastructure, and student support services, thereby guiding corrective action. Thirdly, it ensures alignment with industry needs to enhance student employability and professional readiness. Feedback also provides crucial evidence for national accreditation bodies such as NAAC and NBA, particularly in criteria linked to Teaching-Learning, Curriculum Design, Student Support, and Governance. Furthermore, the process fosters a culture of accountability, transparency, and continuous improvement, while promoting self-reflection among students and professional development among faculty.

A wide range of stakeholders contribute to the institutional feedback system, each bringing unique perspectives. Students evaluate teaching quality, curriculum design, fairness of assessments, facilities, and mentoring effectiveness. Faculty provide input on curriculum adequacy, pedagogy tools, and assessment methods. Alumni highlight the relevance of programs to their careers, acquired competencies, and networking opportunities. Employers/Industry assess graduates’ readiness, domain knowledge, and soft skills. Parents offer valuable observations regarding institutional support, placements, and holistic student development. Together, these inputs provide a holistic assessment of institutional effectiveness.

The feedback mechanism follows a structured cycle: Feedback → Analysis → Action Taken → Disclosure → Impact. This ensures that collected feedback is not only documented but also analyzed systematically to identify improvement areas. The outcomes are acted upon through corrective and preventive measures, followed by transparent disclosure of Action Taken Reports (ATRs) to stakeholders. This cycle closes with measuring the impact of improvements, thereby reinforcing continuous quality enhancement.

Various tools and methods are employed to collect and report feedback, including structured online/offline forms, Student Satisfaction Surveys (SSS), exit/graduate surveys, alumni and employer surveys, suggestion boxes, focus group discussions, and personal interviews. Reporting formats generally include percentage analysis in tabular form alongside graphical representations such as bar charts, pie charts, radar diagrams, and 3D graphs. Transparency is further enhanced through Action Taken Reports (ATRs) and impact statements, which provide visible evidence of CQI. To streamline this process, institutions develop standardized feedback templates covering curriculum, faculty, facilities, alumni, parents, employers, graduate exit surveys, and specific academic/administrative areas such as guest lectures, faculty development programs (FDPs), short-term training programs (STTPs), workshops, industrial visits, library services, examination section, registrar office, sports and yoga, medical facilities, hostel facilities, and extension activities.

Importantly, feedback plays a significant role in national accreditation frameworks. For NAAC, feedback mechanisms demonstrate student-centric learning environments, quality enhancement measures, and transparency, with the Student Satisfaction Survey (SSS) directly impacting institutional grading. For NBA, Course Outcomes (COs) and Program Outcomes (POs) attainment are mapped and improved through systematic stakeholder feedback, providing direct and indirect evidence of quality in teaching-learning and curriculum design.

In essence, feedback serves as the backbone of quality assurance in higher education institutions. It not only ensures that institutional practices are responsive and adaptive to stakeholder needs but also builds a culture of accountability, transparency, and continuous improvement. By embedding a systematic feedback mechanism into governance and academic processes, institutions can strengthen their academic outcomes, student experience, accreditation readiness, and societal impact.

**2. About Feedback in Higher Education**

**1. Definition**

Feedback is a systematic mechanism of collecting opinions, experiences, and suggestions from stakeholders (students, faculty, alumni, employers, parents, industry) to assess institutional performance and implement continuous quality improvement (CQI).

**2. Purpose of Feedback**

• To evaluate teaching–learning effectiveness, curriculum relevance, and institutional facilities.

• To identify gaps in academic delivery, infrastructure, and student support.

• To enhance employability by aligning with industry requirements.

• To serve as evidence for accreditation (NAAC/NBA) under Teaching-Learning, Curriculum Design, and Governance.

**3. Stakeholders**

• Students → Teaching quality, curriculum, evaluation fairness, facilities, mentoring.

• Faculty → Curriculum design, assessment pattern, resources, pedagogy tools.

• Alumni → Relevance of program to career, skills, networking.

• Employers/Industry → Competency of graduates, industry readiness, soft skills.

• Parents → Institutional support, placements, overall student development.

**4. Feedback Collection Tools**

• Structured feedback forms (online/offline).

• Student Satisfaction Survey (SSS) as per NAAC guidelines.

• Exit/Graduate surveys.

• Alumni/employer surveys.

• Suggestion boxes, focus groups, and interviews.

**5. Feedback Cycle**

Feedback → Analysis → Action Taken → Disclosure → Impact

• Feedback: Collect structured data (Likert scale).

• Analysis: Quantitative (percentages, averages) + Qualitative (comments, suggestions).

• Action Taken: Corrective and preventive measures (e.g., new electives, lab upgrades).

• Disclosure: Publish ATR (Action Taken Report) on website / notice board.

• Impact: Improved curriculum, teaching, infrastructure, and student outcomes.

**6. Reporting Format**

• Tabular % Analysis (Above Expectation, Met Expectation, Needs Improvement).

• Graphical Representation (Bar, Pie, Radar charts).

• Action Taken Report (ATR) for transparency.

• Impact Statement to show CQI.

In Accreditation Context (NAAC/NBA):

• NAAC Criterion 1, 2, 5, 6 use feedback as key evidence.

• SSS (2.7.1) directly contributes to institutional grade.

• Employers/Alumni feedback strengthens Outcome-Based Education (OBE).

**3. Purpose**

**Establish a Structured Feedback Regime**

• To create a systematic, transparent, and continuous feedback process within the Institute.

• Ensures feedback is collected regularly, analyzed rigorously, and acted upon effectively.

• Builds a culture of accountability and continuous improvement in teaching, learning, and administration.

**Promote Student Learning and Academic Outcomes**

• Provide students with meaningful, timely, and constructive feedback on their performance.

• Encourage self-reflection, identify strengths and weaknesses, and set clear directions for academic progress.

• Align feedback with Course Outcomes (COs) and Program Outcomes (POs) to measure achievement.

• Enhance overall student outcomes by using feedback as a tool for remedial support, mentoring, and skill-building.

**Monitor and Improve the Student Experience**

• Assess student satisfaction with teaching methods, curriculum delivery, facilities, and support services.

• Detect learning gaps, stress points, or grievances early and address them proactively.

• Provide insights into student engagement, inclusivity, and campus life for holistic development.

• Benchmark the Institute’s performance against national/international standards and peer institutions.

**Assist in Staff Development and Institutional Growth**

• Provide faculty with actionable insights into their teaching effectiveness and student perception.

• Encourage continuous professional development through training, workshops, peer mentoring, and reflective practices.

• Use feedback to design workplace improvement plans, enhancing teaching methodologies, administrative efficiency, and support mechanisms.

• Promote a culture of collaborative growth where both staff and students benefit from a feedback-driven environment.

**Support Policy and Strategic Decision-Making**

• Serve as evidence for quality assurance, accreditation, and ranking processes (e.g., NAAC, QS I-GAUGE, NIRF).

• Guide management in making informed decisions regarding curriculum design, resource allocation, and infrastructure development.

• Strengthen institutional credibility and transparency by publishing feedback-based improvements.

**4. Objective of feedbacks**

**Formation of a Working Group for Student Input**

• Establish a dedicated committee or working group comprising faculty, administrators, and student representatives.

• Examine recurring issues raised through student feedback and provide actionable recommendations.

• Ensure a structured and inclusive mechanism for addressing concerns related to teaching, curriculum, infrastructure, and support services.

• Create a feedback-to-action loop, where student input is systematically reviewed and integrated into institutional decision-making.

**Student Participation in Quality Evaluation**

• Provide students with a formal opportunity to share their experiences regarding teaching methods, curriculum relevance, and support facilities.

• Make feedback an integral part of both formative (during the course) and summative (end of course) evaluation processes.

• Empower students to contribute actively to institutional quality assurance processes, thereby enhancing transparency and accountability.

• Encourage a sense of ownership among students towards their learning journey and the institution’s growth.

**Evaluation of Academic Offerings Against Expectations**

• Measure how well academic programs meet the initial expectations and aspirations of students.

• Assess effectiveness of teaching delivery, curriculum design, assessment strategies, and academic support.

• Identify gaps between intended learning outcomes and student experiences, ensuring corrective action where necessary.

• Provide evidence-based inputs to faculty and curriculum designers for continuous program improvement.

**Improving Delivery and Content of Student Units**

• Use feedback to refine course content, teaching strategies, and pedagogical tools for better engagement.

• Introduce innovative teaching methods such as flipped classrooms, blended learning, and problem-based learning in response to student needs.

• Enhance the overall delivery by aligning course material with industry relevance, skill requirements, and global standards.

• Foster a culture of continuous curriculum renewal, ensuring that students receive a high-quality and future-ready education.

**5. Parts of Feedback Collected**

**Curriculum Feedback**

• Relevance of curriculum to current industry standards and global trends.

• Alignment of course content with Program Outcomes (POs) and Course Outcomes (COs).

• Adequacy of electives, interdisciplinary options, and skill-based components.

• Scope for practical exposure, research orientation, and innovation.

**Teaching–Learning Process Feedback**

• Effectiveness of teaching methodologies and pedagogical innovations.

• Quality of classroom interactions, faculty engagement, and accessibility.

• Use of ICT-enabled tools, e-learning resources, and blended learning methods.

• Timeliness and fairness of assessments, internal evaluation, and constructive feedback provided by faculty.

**Faculty Feedback**

• Subject knowledge, preparedness, and clarity of delivery.

• Approachability, mentoring support, and encouragement for student participation.

• Ability to integrate theoretical concepts with practical applications.

• Contribution to overall academic growth and personal development of students.

**Infrastructure and Learning Resources Feedback**

• Adequacy of classrooms, laboratories, libraries, and digital resources.

• Availability and accessibility of learning management systems (LMS).

• Quality of hostel, sports, cultural, and recreational facilities.

• Efficiency of IT support, internet access, and campus facilities.

**Support Services Feedback**

• Effectiveness of academic advising, counselling, and career guidance.

• Responsiveness of administrative staff in addressing student concerns.

• Availability of scholarships, financial aid, and student welfare schemes.

• Efficiency of placement services, internships, and industry collaborations.

**Overall Student Experience Feedback**

• Level of satisfaction with institutional culture, inclusivity, and student engagement.

• Opportunities for co-curricular and extracurricular development.

• Perception of fairness in grievance redressal mechanisms.

• Overall rating of academic and administrative quality of the institution.

**Alumni & Employer Feedback (Extended Parts)**

• Alumni: Relevance of the education received in professional careers and higher studies.

• Employers: Competency, employability skills, and industry readiness of graduates.

• Both perspectives guide curriculum updates, skill training modules, and placement strategies.

The feedback framework is organized into two parts: Part A covers feedback collected from the students on the curriculum and teaching learning process, while Part B covers feedback analysis from the employers, faculty, parents and alumni.

**6. Collection of Feedback**

Feedback of Stakeholders encompasses inputs from Students, Parents, Academic Experts, Industry Experts, Alumni, and Faculty.

• Stakeholders Involved

> o Students: Current students provide course-wise, semester-wise, and overall institutional feedback.
>
> o Faculty: Feedback on curriculum, teaching–learning environment, and institutional support.
>
> o Alumni: Reflections on employability, curriculum relevance, and higher education preparedness.
>
> o Employers/Industry: Input on graduate attributes, skills, and industry readiness.
>
> o Parents (Optional in some frameworks): Observations on academic quality, student support, and institutional culture.

• Timing of Collection

> o Course-Level Feedback: Mid-semester (formative) and end-semester (summative).
>
> o Programme-Level Feedback: At the end of each academic year.
>
> o Exit Feedback: From final-year students during graduation/convocation process.
>
> o Alumni Feedback: During alumni meets, online surveys, or structured outreach.
>
> o Employer Feedback: Collected annually or during placement drives.

• Modes of Collection

> o Online Platforms: Institutional Learning Management System (LMS), ERP portals, or Google Forms.
>
> o Offline Methods: Structured questionnaires, feedback boxes, or written surveys.
>
> o Direct Interactions: Focus group discussions, class committee meetings, and town-hall style student forums.

• Feedback Instruments (Tools)

> o Questionnaires/Surveys: Designed with Likert scale ratings (e.g., 1–5) and open-ended questions.
>
> o Course Outcome Mapping: Linking feedback questions directly with COs and POs.
>
> o Qualitative Inputs: Open comments, suggestions, and observations for richer insights.
>
> o Quantitative Data: Statistical summaries, percentages of satisfaction, and trend analysis across semesters.

• Process of Collection

> o Step 1: Design and validate structured feedback forms based on NAAC/NBA criteria.
>
> o Step 2: Disseminate to all stakeholders through institutional channels.
>
> o Step 3: Ensure anonymity and confidentiality to encourage honest responses.
>
> o Step 4: Collect responses systematically and maintain records for accreditation and quality audits.
>
> o Step 5: Forward compiled data to the Feedback Working Group/Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC/NBA CQI team) for analysis.

• Purpose (Accreditation-Oriented)

> o NAAC: Demonstrates student-centric learning, quality enhancement, and transparency.
>
> o NBA: Validates Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) cycle by showing evidence of stakeholder participation in curriculum and outcome-based education review.

**7. Stepwise process for Feedback Collection and Action**

**Feedback Collection & Action Cycle**

1\. Design of Feedback Tools

> o Prepare structured feedback forms/questionnaires (Course, Programme, Faculty, Infrastructure, Support Services).
>
> o Align questions with COs, POs, PEOs (NBA) and quality parameters (NAAC).

2\. Dissemination of Feedback Forms

> o Distribute to stakeholders (Students, Faculty, Alumni, Employers, Parents if applicable).
>
> o Conduct through LMS/ERP, Google Forms, or offline surveys.

3\. Collection of Responses

> o Collect feedback at scheduled intervals (mid-semester, end-semester, annually, exit-level).
>
> o Ensure anonymity & confidentiality to encourage honest responses.

4\. Compilation & Data Entry

> o Gather all responses in a central database.
>
> o Prepare statistical summaries (satisfaction %, averages, trends).
>
> o Extract qualitative remarks for deeper insights.

5\. Analysis of Feedback

> o Conduct quantitative analysis (ratings, percentages, CO-PO attainment mapping).
>
> o The Working Group/IQAC/NBA CQI Committee reviews patterns and identifies gaps.
>
> o Categorize issues: curriculum, teaching methods, infrastructure, support services.

6\. Action Plan Formulation

> o Draft corrective and enhancement measures.
>
> o Assign responsibilities (faculty, HoD, administration, IQAC).
>
> o Set timelines for implementation.

7\. Implementation of Actions

> o Modify curriculum (if required) through Board of Studies.
>
> o Introduce faculty training, ICT-based teaching, or remedial classes.
>
> o Upgrade infrastructure and support services.

8\. Communication of Actions Taken

> o Publish “Action Taken Reports (ATRs)” on institutional website/notice boards.
>
> o Inform students and stakeholders about changes implemented.
>
> o Ensure transparency and accountability.

9\. Review & Continuous Improvement

> o Monitor effectiveness of actions through follow-up feedback.
>
> o Integrate improvements into CQI cycle (NBA) and Quality Assurance practices (NAAC).
>
> o Repeat cycle for sustained enhancement.

In summary:

Feedback Collection → Compilation → Analysis → Action Plan → Implementation → Communication → Review → Continuous Improvement

**8. Feedback on Curriculum Process**

1\. Collection of Curriculum Feedback

> o Stakeholders: Students, Faculty, Alumni, Employers, Industry Experts, Parents (optional).
>
> o Timing:
>
> o At the end of every academic year (students).
>
> o During curriculum review cycles (faculty, industry, alumni).
>
> o At exit level (final year students) and after placements (employers).
>
> o Modes: Online surveys, structured questionnaires, Board of Studies (BoS) meetings, academic council discussions.

2\. Evaluation and Compilation

> o Curriculum feedback compiled by the IQAC / Department Academic Committee.
>
> o Quantitative analysis (ratings, averages, % satisfaction).
>
> o Qualitative analysis (suggestions for new courses, relevance to industry, global competitiveness).
>
> o Mapping with Programme Educational Objectives (PEOs), Programme Outcomes (POs), and Course Outcomes (COs) as per NBA framework.

3\. Analysis by Curriculum Committee/BoS

> o Review gaps between existing curriculum and stakeholder expectations.
>
> o Identify needs for:
>
> o New electives and interdisciplinary courses.
>
> o Emerging technologies (AI, Data Analytics, IoT, Sustainability, etc.).
>
> o Skill-based, industry-oriented modules.
>
> o Enhanced practical/laboratory components.

4\. Recommendations and Action Plan

> o Curriculum Committee formulates proposals for revision.
>
> o Inputs forwarded to Board of Studies (BoS) for formal consideration.
>
> o Action Taken Report (ATR) prepared with timeline for changes.

5\. Implementation of Curriculum Changes

> o Approved modifications integrated into syllabi, course structures, and teaching plans.
>
> o Faculty sensitized through workshops/orientation sessions.
>
> o Updated curriculum uploaded to institutional website and communicated to students.

6\. Review and Continuous Improvement

> o Effectiveness of changes monitored through subsequent feedback cycles.
>
> o Alignment with NBA Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) process.
>
> o Provides documentary evidence for NAAC SSR Criterion 1 (Curricular Aspects) and NBA SAR reporting.

In summary:

Feedback Collection → Compilation → Analysis → Recommendations → Implementation → Review (CQI Cycle)

**9. Internal Stakeholders**

**Management**

• Coordinate and oversee the activities of academic programmes to ensure alignment with institutional vision and mission.

• Provide strategic direction, policy support, and resources for curriculum design, teaching–learning, and quality assurance.

• Monitor the achievement of Programme Educational Objectives (PEOs) and overall institutional goals.

• Ensure compliance with accreditation standards (NAAC/NBA) and drive Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI).

**Faculty**

• Act as primary channels for achieving institutional mission and programme outcomes.

• Deliver curriculum effectively through innovative pedagogy, ICT-enabled methods, and outcome-based education practices.

• Mentor and guide students for academic success, research orientation, and career progression.

• Participate in curriculum development, feedback analysis, and accreditation-related processes.

• Contribute to achieving Course Outcomes (COs) and mapping them to Programme Outcomes (POs).

**Students**

• Actively adapt and internalize the institution’s vision, mission, and PEOs for their academic and professional growth.

• Provide constructive feedback on curriculum, teaching–learning, infrastructure, and support services.

• Engage in co-curricular, extracurricular, and extension activities to develop holistic competencies.

• Strive for self-development by achieving desired graduate attributes such as communication skills, teamwork, problem-solving, and ethical responsibility.

• Serve as the primary beneficiaries and also as contributors to the institution’s quality assurance framework.

**10. External Stakeholders**

**Parents**

• Serve as ambassadors who provide constructive suggestions for the smooth functioning of the institution.

• Offer feedback on student welfare, academic environment, and institutional support mechanisms.

• Contribute to strengthening trust, transparency, and parent–institution collaboration.

**Alumni**

• Act as brand ambassadors by carrying forward the mission and values of the institution in their professional lives.

• Provide feedback on the relevance of curriculum in career advancement and higher education.

• Assist in mentoring, networking, fundraising, and creating industry linkages.

• Play a critical role in validating the attainment of Programme Educational Objectives (PEOs).

**Professional Bodies (e.g., IEEE, BMESI, SEEE, ISTE)**

• Promote excellence in education through awards, recognitions, certifications, and knowledge exchange.

• Support professional development of students and faculty via memberships, conferences, and seminars.

• Ensure programmes stay updated with global standards and practices.

**Industry Representatives (e.g., Siemens Healthineers, GE Healthcare, Philips Healthcare, Medtronic, NI, KMCH, Texas Instruments)**

• Facilitate training programs, industrial visits, internships, and real-world projects.

• Provide valuable feedback to bridge the gap between academia and industry requirements.

**Industry Representatives (continued)**

• Support curriculum design with insights into emerging technologies and skill demands.

• Strengthen placement opportunities and research collaborations.

**Faculty from Renowned Institutions (IIT, IISc, IIST, Tier I Institutions, Foreign Universities)**

• Offer guidance and mentorship for delivering high-quality education.

• Provide expert suggestions on pedagogy, curriculum benchmarking, and research integration.

• Collaborate in joint research, guest lectures, and exchange programmes.

**Employers**

• Represent major end users of graduates, providing insights into workplace competencies and employability skills.

• Offer feedback on graduates’ performance in technical, managerial, and soft skills.

• Help institutions overcome the gap between program outcomes and industry expectations.

• Support curriculum revision to enhance Programme Outcomes (POs) such as teamwork, communication, and problem-solving.

**Accrediting Authorities (NAAC, NBA, International Bodies)**

• Function as recognized agencies for quality assurance, evaluating institutional services and academic operations.

• Ensure compliance with standards of Outcome-Based Education (OBE) and continuous quality improvement.

**12. Objectives of Feedback**

The feedback collection process is aimed at continual improvement of academic quality and administrative facilities within the campus. The primary objectives are:

**Feedback in Learning Process**

• To help students maximize their potential at different stages of their academic journey.

• To raise awareness among learners about their strengths and areas requiring improvement.

• To provide constructive inputs that guide students in setting academic and professional goals.

• To encourage reflective learning, active participation, and self-directed improvement.

• To identify specific actions and remedial measures that enhance performance and learning outcomes.

**Feedback in Assessment Process**

• To provide clarity on subject understanding and areas where students can strengthen their knowledge.

• To ensure students receive clear and actionable guidance on how to improve performance in future assessments.

• To promote transparency and fairness in the evaluation system.

• To enhance the effectiveness of internal and external assessments by aligning them with Course Outcomes (COs) and Programme Outcomes (POs).

• To support Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) by using assessment feedback to refine teaching strategies and evaluation methods.

**13. Feedback Mechanism**

**Collection of Feedback**

• Feedback is obtained periodically from multiple stakeholders, including students, faculty, alumni, parents, employers, and industry experts.

• Structured questionnaires, online surveys, and direct interactions (class committee meetings, BoS meetings, alumni forums) are used as instruments.

• Both quantitative (ratings) and qualitative (open comments) data are collected to ensure comprehensive insights.

**Analysis of Feedback**

• The collected feedback is compiled and analyzed by the respective Departments / Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC).

• Trends, gaps, and recurring issues are identified through statistical analysis and qualitative review.

• Course-level, programme-level, and institutional-level concerns are categorized for targeted action.

**Corrective Measures / Improvements**

• The Head of Department (HoD) and Principal initiate corrective measures based on stakeholder inputs.

• Improvements may include:

> o Revision of teaching–learning strategies.
>
> o Curriculum enrichment through BoS and Academic Council.
>
> o Faculty development programmes and workshops.
>
> o Enhancement of infrastructure and student support services.

• If necessary, feedback reports are escalated to Management for policy-level decisions, additional resources, or strategic reforms.

**Action Taken and Communication**

• Action Taken Reports (ATRs) are prepared and documented as part of quality assurance records.

• The actions implemented are communicated to stakeholders via institutional website, circulars, or feedback review meetings.

• Transparency in “feedback–action–review” loop builds trust and motivates stakeholders to participate actively.

**Impact of Feedback Mechanism**

• Significant improvement in academic activities, teaching methodologies, and student engagement.

• Effective utilization of teaching–learning resources and ICT-enabled tools.

• Enhanced professional growth, employability, and career advancement of students.

• Strengthened alignment with Outcome-Based Education (OBE) and Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) cycles required by NAAC/NBA.

**16. Students’ Feedback on Faculty**

> o Quality of teaching
>
> o Student–faculty interactions
>
> o Clearance of doubts
>
> o Communication effectiveness
>
> o Fairness of evaluation
>
> o Subject knowledge and expertise
>
> o Punctuality and professionalism
>
> o Laboratory sessions and practical delivery (where applicable)

**Mode of Collection**

• Anonymous online survey conducted to encourage honest and unbiased responses.

• All students are required to participate at designated intervals (usually end of course/semester).

• Ensures transparency and eliminates fear of identification or bias.

**Processing and Analysis**

• Data collected is compiled and analyzed statistically.

• Ratings are given on a 1–5 scale (Likert scale: 1 = Poor, 5 = Excellent).

• A Net Performance Weighted Index (NPWI) is calculated for each dimension, including theory and laboratory courses.

• NPWI helps in quantifying faculty effectiveness and comparing across programmes/departments.

**Review and Corrective Action**

• The analyzed results are forwarded to the Head of Department (HoD).

• HoDs discuss outcomes with the concerned faculty for reflective improvement.

• If significant issues arise, reports are escalated to the Principal and Management for policy-level intervention.

• Faculty are encouraged to attend Faculty Development Programs (FDPs), workshops, or mentoring sessions as corrective measures.

**Impact of Feedback**

• Ensures continuous improvement in faculty teaching quality and student engagement.

• Helps align faculty practices with institutional mission, POs, and COs.

• Enhances student satisfaction, learning outcomes, and professional readiness.

• Provides documented evidence for NAAC Criterion 2 (Teaching–Learning and Evaluation) and NBA CQI cycle.

> o Advised to adopt modern teaching strategies (ICT tools, blended learning, case studies, peer learning).
>
> o Encouraged to participate in Faculty Development Programs (FDPs), workshops, and mentoring sessions.
>
> o Progress monitored in the following feedback cycle to ensure improvement.

Middle Range (70%–90%) (Optional to mention if needed)

• Faculty are encouraged to sustain performance with minor improvements.

• Additional training/resources provided if certain parameters show lower scores.

• Promotes a culture of excellence and accountability in teaching.

• Strengthens alignment with NAAC Criterion 2 (Teaching–Learning and Evaluation) and NBA Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) cycle.

**28. Feedback → Analysis → Action Taken (CQI Cycle)**

1\. Feedback

> o Collected from stakeholders (Students, Faculty, Alumni, Employers, Parents, Industry).
>
> o Tools: Online surveys, structured forms, suggestion boxes, PTMs, exit surveys.
>
> o Covers: Curriculum, Teaching–Learning, Faculty, Facilities, Support Services.

2\. Analysis

> o Data compiled by IQAC / HoDs / Committees.
>
> o Quantitative Analysis: Percentages, NPWI scores, satisfaction ratings.
>
> o Qualitative Analysis: Comments, suggestions, grievances.
>
> o Findings categorized (Curricular, Co-Curricular, Infrastructure, Faculty).

3\. Action Taken

> o Corrective & preventive measures initiated.
>
> o Examples:
>
> o Curriculum gaps → New electives introduced via BoS.
>
> o Faculty improvement → FDPs, mentoring, ICT adoption.
>
> o Facility gaps → Lab upgrades, Wi-Fi expansion, new library resources.
>
> o Student support → Placement training, counseling sessions.
>
> o Action Taken Reports (ATRs) documented and shared with stakeholders (website/public disclosure).

4\. Impact (Outcome)

> o Improved student satisfaction, teaching quality, employability, and infrastructure utilization.
>
> o Evidence of Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) for NAAC/NBA accreditation.

Summary Formula:

Feedback (Input) → Analysis (Review) → Action Taken (Improvement) → Impact (CQI Loop)

**38. Accreditation Framework - Student Satisfaction Survey**

**Guidelines for Student Satisfaction Survey (SSS)**

• Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) have to strictly upload data of at least 50% of currently enrolled students as per data template format of excel sheet given in portal.

• Data will be accepted in text format only as per the template given by NAAC.

• Column names (case sensitive) and order should not be altered. No column/cell should be left blank

• Repetition of Name, e-mail address, Mobile number is not allowed.

• There are two separate columns for Student ID and Enrollment ID. In the absence of separate Ids the institutions can repeat same Id in the two columns.

• Total entries should not be greater than the students marked in Institutional Information for Quality Assessment (IIQA).

• **SSS will be administered to institutions simultaneously with Data Validation and Verification (DVV) process.**

• The SSS questionnaire (20 objective & 01 subjective) which is available in NAAC website and will also be e-mailed to students and the following rules will be applied for processing the responses.

a\) Maximum of Two survey attempts will be initiated to reach the desired level of response as per requirements mentioned below

> o i. For Colleges – (UG/PG and Autonomous) responses should be received from at least 10% of the student population or 100, whichever is lesser.
>
> o ii. For Universities – 10% of the student population or 500, whichever is lesser.

b\) If the response rate is lower than the limits mentioned by NAAC, the metric will not be taken up for evaluation.

c\) SSS will be completed within 30 days simultaneously with Data Validation and Verification (DVV) process.

d\) As soon as survey is initiated by the coordinator of NAAC it has to be completed within 10 days.

e\) The institute can encourage students to participate in survey and guide them about survey to make the SSS possible within the given time period of 10 days.

f\) SSS questionnaire is in English. NAAC website will have both English and Hindi version available. If needed HEIs can make local language translation available for information of students before they take the survey.

**Context:**

From 2017 onwards the NAAC (National Assessment and Accreditation Council) has endeavoured to conduct a Student Experience Survey the results of which will go into the accreditation process. The Survey will capture student responses through the list of students provided by the Higher education institutes (HEIs). The students will remain anonymous throughout the process. The institution is supposed to send a list of total student strength, with details of their student ID number, Aadhaar ID number (Any other Valid ID No. in the absence of Aadhaar), degree programme, email id and mobile number. NAAC will send online link to the survey to the email address/mobile no of the student, and the student will have to fill the survey before a stipulated date.

**About questionnaire:**

The questionnaire will be based on the Likert type scale, that means the responses are scaled on a scale of 0 to 4, with the most positive response being rated as 4 and the most negative response being rated as 0. The score emerging out of the survey is part of the second criterion on Teaching—Learning and Evaluation, out of the seven NAAC criteria. The questionnaire consists of several facets of the teaching learning process. Questions vary from specific teaching skills of the teacher, to his overall approach to the educational process. Specific skills of the teacher like, subject knowledge, communication skills, class preparation, and use of ICT tools are part of the questionnaire.

The overall approach of the teacher and institution with respect to providing the right environment, motivation, interpersonal relationships, feedback etc. forms the second major component of the questionnaire. Twenty of the twenty one questions are objective in nature, while one question is open ended to elicit observations and suggestions for improvements providing an opportunity to the student to give suggestions and criticisms in their own words. Analysis of the survey would be done using software which will aggregate the responses and generate the score. The score will range from a minimum of 0 to a maximum of 4 on a five point scale and would affect the overall score of second criteria on Teaching-Learning and evaluation. Responses to the open ended question would also be aggregated to find out the most common suggestion and criticisms emerging out of the survey.

**Process:**

The survey analysis score will be used as a key component of accreditation. A predetermined weightage is also assigned to this key indicator in this accreditation framework. A stratified random sample of students will be chosen for the survey. Response rate below 10% will not be considered. In sample, students would be spread evenly across different classes, year of enrolment and gender as far as possible. The HEIs are required to submit data of all students. (Class wise with name, e-mail & mobile number and Aadhaar number) The institution, teachers; should not by any way try to influence the students with respect to the survey. This will ensure genuine feedback for the institute to improve further.

**Analysis of questionnaire:**

There are twenty objective questions in the questionnaire and one open ended question.

**Analysis of objective questions:**

There are twenty objective questions and students will respond on a scale of 4 to 0, with the most positive response rated as 4 and most negative response rated as 0. The mean score for each question will be calculated and the overall mean will be arrived at. This figure will range from 4 to 0 and will give the mean satisfaction level of the students for the particular institute. > o Feedback forms, reflection reports, group presentations, and viva assessments post-visit provide measurable metrics → can be scored in % and linked directly to CO attainment.
