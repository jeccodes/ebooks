# Abstract

The National Board of Accreditation (NBA) framework for Tier-1 institutions outlines a comprehensive approach to developing and assessing an Outcome-Based Curriculum (OBC) carrying 120 marks across five criteria. The first criterion, Vision, Mission, and Program Educational Objectives (PEOs) (35 marks), focuses on institutional aspirations and commitments, with 3–5 broad PEOs defined through stakeholder consultations and validated by statutory bodies, disseminated widely, and supported with correlation matrices and justification notes. The second criterion, Curriculum Structure and Features (30 marks), emphasizes systematic curriculum design and revision aligned with NBA Program Outcomes (POs), Washington Accord Knowledge Profiles, AICTE/UGC model curricula, and NEP 2020 reforms. It includes semester-wise credit allocation under the National Credit Framework, grouping of courses across domains, and innovations such as Academic Bank of Credits (ABC), APAAR, MOOCs, industry-driven courses, and multidisciplinary electives. The third criterion, POs, PSOs, and Mapping with Courses (20 marks), mandates articulation of the 12 graduate attributes and up to three department-specific outcomes, documented through course-to-PO/PSO mapping matrices with correlation levels and justification notes. The fourth criterion, Course Outcomes (COs) and Course Articulation Matrix (30 marks), requires defining 4–6 measurable COs per course using Bloom’s Taxonomy verbs, mapping them to POs and PSOs with correlation levels (–, 1, 2, 3), and providing justification for strong links while ensuring alignment of assessment tools with COs. The fifth criterion, Program Articulation Matrix (5 marks), consolidates all course-level contributions to demonstrate comprehensive coverage of POs and PSOs at the program level. Together, these criteria emphasize systematic documentation, stakeholder involvement, global alignment with Washington Accord standards, and continuous improvement cycles, ensuring that Tier-1 institutions maintain curriculum relevance, graduate employability, quality assurance, and international recognition.

# 1. Introduction

The National Board of Accreditation (NBA) framework for Tier-1 institutions provides a comprehensive guide for the development, implementation, and assessment of an Outcome-Based Curriculum (OBC), which is central to ensuring the quality, relevance, and international recognition of engineering education. The framework is evaluated across five key criteria totaling 120 marks and aims to establish systematic processes, requirements, and evidence that reflect an institution’s commitment to outcome-based education. The first criterion, Vision, Mission, and Program Educational Objectives (PEOs) with 35 marks, requires institutions to define long-term aspirations, action-oriented commitments, and career goals for graduates. These PEOs are framed with stakeholder input, validated by statutory bodies such as DAC, BOS, and Academic Council, disseminated widely, and logically mapped to the institutional mission using correlation matrices and justification notes. The second criterion, Curriculum Structure and Features with 30 marks, emphasizes systematic curriculum design and revision aligned with NBA’s Program Outcomes (POs), Washington Accord Knowledge Profiles (WKs), AICTE/UGC guidelines, and NEP 2020 reforms. It mandates clear credit distribution under the L–T–P–S system with 30 learning hours equal to 1 credit as per the National Credit Framework, proper grouping of courses across categories, and adoption of innovations such as multidisciplinary electives, Academic Bank of Credits (ABC), APAAR, MOOCs, and industry collaborations. The third criterion, Program Outcomes (POs), Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs), and Mapping with Courses worth 20 marks, focuses on defining the 12 NBA graduate attributes and up to three PSOs, supported by a consolidated course-to-PO/PSO mapping matrix that assigns correlation levels and provides justification for strong links. The fourth criterion, Course Outcomes (COs) and Course Articulation Matrix with 30 marks, requires every course to define 4–6 measurable outcomes using Bloom’s Taxonomy action verbs and to map these COs to POs and PSOs through a matrix that records the strength of correlation (3 = High, 2 = Medium, 1 = Low, – = None) with justification notes and alignment to relevant assessment tools. The fifth criterion, Program Articulation Matrix with 5 marks, demands a program-level consolidated matrix that demonstrates how all courses, through their COs, collectively ensure comprehensive coverage of all 12 POs and the department’s PSOs.

Across all criteria, NBA stresses the importance of systematic documentation, stakeholder involvement, logical alignment of Vision–Mission–PEOs–POs–COs, and continuous quality improvement. Institutions are expected to maintain evidence such as lesson plans, BOS/DAC minutes, industry feedback, surveys, and attainment analyses to validate compliance with NBA’s Outcome-Based Education framework, thereby strengthening graduate employability, institutional credibility, and global recognition.

# 2. Outcome-Based Curriculum (120 Marks)

## A. Vision, Mission and Program Educational Objectives (PEOs) – 35 Marks

### 1. State the Vision and Mission of the Institute and the Department (05 Marks)

- Vision and Mission must be formally defined, approved (BoS/AC), and published.

- **Vision:** Long-term aspiration (e.g., “To be a globally recognized center of excellence in engineering education, research, and innovation.”).

- **Mission:** Action-oriented statements reflecting teaching, research, industry, and societal contributions.

### 2. State PEOs of the Program (05 Marks)

- PEOs are broad program-level goals, achievable 4–5 years after graduation.

- Typically 3–5 PEOs, aligned with Vision/Mission.

- Example for Computer Science:

<!-- -->

- **PEO1:** Excel in professional career using computing fundamentals.

- **PEO2:** Demonstrate research and innovation in emerging technologies.

- **PEO3:** Exhibit ethics, leadership, and lifelong learning.

### 3. Process of Defining Vision, Mission and PEOs (10 Marks)

- **Inputs:** Stakeholder feedback (faculty, alumni, industry, employers, students).

- **Validation:** Department Advisory Committee (DAC), Board of Studies (BoS), Academic Council (AC).

- **Outcome:** Documented process flow (Feedback → DAC → BoS → AC).

### 4. Dissemination of Vision, Mission and PEOs (05 Marks)

- Displayed on:

<!-- -->

- Institute website, department website.

- Student/Faculty handbooks.

- Classrooms, laboratories, program brochures.

<!-- -->

- Awareness created through induction programs, meetings, workshops.

### 5. Mapping of PEOs with Mission (10 Marks)

- Use a Mission ↔ PEO mapping matrix (Low/Medium/High correlation).

- Provide justification notes for each mapping.

- Example: Mission element on “Research & Innovation” strongly correlates with PEO2.

## B. Curriculum Structure and Features – 30 Marks

### 1. State the Process for Developing/Revising the Program Curriculum (10 Marks)

- **Process flow:** Stakeholder Feedback → DAC Draft → BoS Review → AC Approval → Implementation.

- Alignment with NBA POs/WKs, AICTE/UGC model curricula, NEP 2020 guidelines.

- **Evidence:** BoS minutes, IQAC reports, curriculum revision documents.

### 2. Curriculum Structure (10 Marks)

- Semester-wise listing of courses with L–T–P–SL → Credit conversion (30 hrs = 1 credit as per NCF).

- Clear separation of Core, Electives, Labs, Projects, Internships.

### 3. Components of Curriculum (05 Marks)

- Grouping by categories:

<!-- -->

- Basic Sciences, Engineering Sciences, Humanities/Social Sciences, Program Core, Electives, Projects, Internships.

<!-- -->

- Show credit distribution (%) across categories.

### 4. Strategies for Education Reforms (05 Marks)

- Multidisciplinary courses.

- Open electives, minor/major degree pathways.

- Academic Bank of Credits (ABC), APAAR integration.

- MOOCs/NPTEL adoption.

- Interdisciplinary projects, industry collaborations.

## C. POs, PSOs and their Mapping with Courses – 20 Marks

### 1. POs and PSOs (05 Marks)

- **POs:** 12 graduate attributes defined by NBA (Engineering Knowledge, Problem Analysis, Design, Ethics, Communication, etc.).

- **PSOs:** Program-specific outcomes (max 3) framed by department.

- Example (EEE):

<!-- -->

- **PSO1:** Design and analyze power systems.

- **PSO2:** Apply renewable energy technologies.

- **PSO3:** Demonstrate ethical and professional practice in industry.

### 2. Mapping between Courses and POs/PSOs (15 Marks)

- Consolidated Course ↔ POs/PSOs Matrix.

- Example:

<!-- -->

- Data Structures → PO1, PO2, PO3, PSO1.

- Ethics → PO6, PO7, PO8, PSO3.

- Capstone Project → All POs + all PSOs.

<!-- -->

- **Evidence:** Course–PO/PSO mapping sheets, justification notes.

## D. Course Outcomes and Course Articulation Matrix – 30 Marks

### 1. Course Outcomes (Semester Wise) (15 Marks)

- Define 4–6 measurable COs per course.

- Provide for two core courses per semester (I–VIII).

- Example (Data Structures, Sem III):

<!-- -->

- **CO1:** Explain linear and nonlinear data structures.

- **CO2:** Apply DS for problem solving.

- **CO3:** Implement using modern tools.

- **CO4:** Evaluate algorithm efficiency.

### 2. Course Articulation Matrix (15 Marks)

- Rows = COs, Columns = POs + PSOs.

- Fill correlation (1–2–3/–).

- Example (Data Structures):

<!-- -->

- CO1 → PO1(3), PO2(3), PSO1(3).

- CO2 → PO2(3), PO3(3), PSO1(3).

- CO3 → PO3(3), PO5(3), PSO2(2).

## E. Program Articulation Matrix – 05 Marks

- Consolidated Course ↔ POs/PSOs table (program-wide).

- Demonstrates how all courses together cover all outcomes.

- Example:

<!-- -->

- Math → PO1, PO2.

- Ethics → PO6, PO7, PO8.

- Projects → PO3, PO4, PO5, PO9, PO10, PO11, PO12 + PSOs.

## 2. Vision & Mission (05 Marks)

- **Vision:** Long-term aspirations of the institute/department (future destination).

<!-- -->

- Should be *clear, inspiring, forward-looking*.

- Example: “*To be a globally recognized centre of excellence in engineering education and research for sustainable development.*”

<!-- -->

- **Mission:** Immediate commitments and strategies to achieve the vision.

<!-- -->

- Should focus on *teaching-learning, research, ethics, industry collaboration, and societal development*.

- Example: “*To impart quality technical education, foster innovation, and nurture professionals with ethical values and leadership skills.*”

<!-- -->

- **NBA Evaluation:**

<!-- -->

- Clarity, relevance, and alignment of statements.

- Visibility on website, classrooms, curriculum.

- Consistency with Program Outcomes (POs) and PEOs.

## 3. Program Educational Objectives (PEOs) (05 Marks)

- **Definition:** Broad career/professional achievements expected of graduates within 3–5 years after graduation.

- **Features:**

<!-- -->

- 3–5 statements, concise yet comprehensive.

- Should cover employability, higher education, research, entrepreneurship, ethics, and societal contribution.

<!-- -->

- **Example PEOs:**

<!-- -->

- Graduates will excel in professional careers by applying engineering knowledge and modern tools.

- Graduates will pursue higher education, research, and lifelong learning.

- Graduates will demonstrate innovation, leadership, and entrepreneurship.

- Graduates will practice professional ethics and contribute responsibly to society.
