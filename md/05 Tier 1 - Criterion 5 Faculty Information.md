## Abstract

Criterion 5, *Faculty Information*, is one of the most vital elements of the National Board of Accreditation (NBA) framework, contributing 100 marks to the overall evaluation of a department or program. This criterion underscores the centrality of faculty in shaping the academic, research, and developmental ecosystem of higher education institutions. The purpose of this assessment is not only to verify that sufficient faculty are available to deliver programs but also to evaluate whether the faculty pool is appropriately qualified, balanced across hierarchies, retained over time, and supplemented with external expertise when needed. Faculty are the backbone of any academic program, responsible not only for teaching but also for mentoring students, advancing research, collaborating with industry, and ensuring accreditation standards are met. By allocating 100 marks to faculty information, NBA highlights that the human resources of a department are as critical as its infrastructure, curriculum, and outcomes.

The first sub-criterion, Student–Faculty Ratio (SFR), accounts for 30 marks and is perhaps the most direct reflection of whether an institution has adequate staffing relative to its student body. The SFR ensures that each student receives sufficient academic attention, guidance, and mentoring, and that faculty workloads remain manageable to allow quality teaching and research. The ratio is calculated using the formula S/(F – FF), where S represents the eligible number of students (undergraduate and postgraduate, excluding supernumerary quotas, and capped at 110% of sanctioned intake), F denotes full-time faculty engaged in teaching, and FF excludes first-year-only faculty members. The evaluation framework is rigorous, requiring institutions to report averages over the Current Academic Year (CAY) and the two preceding years (CAYm1 and CAYm2) to ensure consistency rather than a one-year spike. Marks are awarded on a sliding scale: programs with a ratio below 15:1 receive the full 30 marks, while those with ratios at or above 25:1 receive none. This metric incentivizes institutions to maintain healthy faculty recruitment and distribution, as smaller class sizes and lower faculty– student ratios have been empirically linked to better academic outcomes, stronger mentoring, and greater student satisfaction. Departments scoring high on SFR demonstrate their ability to balance student demand with adequate human capital, ensuring that teaching quality is not compromised.

The second sub-criterion, Faculty Qualification Index (FQI), carries 25 marks and focuses on the quality and academic depth of the faculty pool. Here, the emphasis is on advanced qualifications, particularly the presence of Ph.D.-qualified faculty who bring with them expertise in research, innovation, and curriculum development. The formula used is 2.5 × (10X + 4Y) / RF, where X denotes the number of Ph.D. faculty, Y represents the number of M.Tech./M.E. faculty, and RF refers to the Required Faculty calculated as S/20. This weighting system heavily rewards the presence of doctoral faculty while still giving due credit to those with master’s qualifications. The maximum score achievable is 25 marks. By incorporating this measure, NBA aligns its standards with global accreditation systems that emphasize the value of research-oriented faculty in promoting knowledge creation, publications, and intellectual property development. A higher FQI is an indicator of an academically strong department where faculty possess advanced knowledge, enabling them to teach complex concepts, mentor research students, and engage in cutting-edge investigations. Institutions that perform well in this sub-criterion illustrate their commitment to hiring and retaining highly qualified individuals who elevate both teaching and research standards.

The third sub-criterion, Faculty Cadre Proportion, also carries 25 marks and evaluates the distribution of faculty across hierarchical levels: Professors, Associate Professors, and Assistant Professors. The NBA prescribes a normative ratio of 1:2:6, representing one Professor for every two Associate Professors and six Assistant Professors. This balance ensures continuity of leadership, mentoring chains, and succession planning within the department. The formula involves comparing the Required Faculty (RF) for each cadre (RF1, RF2, RF3) with the actual faculty distribution, ensuring that all tiers are appropriately represented. A department skewed toward junior faculty without sufficient senior leadership risks gaps in academic vision, research supervision, and policy implementation. Conversely, departments with the proper balance reflect academic maturity, mentorship culture, and sustainability. This sub-criterion, therefore, promotes a healthy academic pyramid where experienced professors guide mid-level faculty, who in turn mentor early-career academics, creating a cohesive structure that sustains quality over time. Institutions that score well here demonstrate their ability to attract senior academics, nurture mid-level scholars, and create opportunities for young faculty, resulting in a robust academic ecosystem.

The fourth sub-criterion, Visiting/Adjunct Faculty/Professor of Practice, is assigned 10 marks.

Recognizing that no institution can exist in isolation, this metric rewards programs that establish strong linkages with external experts. These may include distinguished academics from other universities, researchers from specialized institutes, or industry professionals who bring real-world perspectives into the classroom. Marks are allocated based on both system establishment and hours of contribution: one mark is awarded for having a formalized system to engage external faculty, while nine marks are awarded for actual contributions amounting to at least 50 hours per year across three consecutive years (3 marks each year). This component is particularly significant in bridging the gap between theory and practice, enhancing employability, and keeping students updated with the latest industrial trends.

Professors of Practice, for instance, enrich learning by bringing live case studies, applied projects, and industry insights. Such engagements also foster industry–academia collaborations, internships, and joint projects, which directly benefit students' career readiness. A department that systematically integrates adjunct faculty not only enhances teaching quality but also expands the professional horizons of its students.

The fifth sub-criterion, Faculty Retention, contributes 10 marks and addresses the stability of faculty resources within an institution. High retention rates are evidence of effective HR practices, supportive work environments, and institutional stability. Retention ensures continuity in teaching, research, mentoring, and curriculum development, while also building institutional memory. NBA allows evaluation through either a simple calculation of the percentage of faculty retained for at least three years or an advanced Faculty Retention Ratio (FR) method, which assigns greater weightage to longer-serving faculty. Departments that score high in this sub-criterion demonstrate not only strong recruitment but also effective retention strategies such as transparent appraisal systems, opportunities for professional development, research support, and work–life balance. Faculty stability enhances student learning experiences and builds trust among stakeholders, including parents, alumni, and accreditation agencies. On the other hand, frequent faculty turnover disrupts continuity and adversely impacts both teaching quality and student outcomes.

Taken together, these five sub-criteria form a comprehensive evaluation of faculty resources within a department. Student–Faculty Ratio (30 marks) ensures sufficiency in numbers, Faculty Qualification (25 marks) emphasizes academic depth, Faculty Cadre Proportion (25 marks) promotes balance across hierarchical levels, Adjunct/Visiting Faculty (10 marks) integrates external expertise, and Faculty Retention (10 marks) highlights institutional stability. The criterion as a whole paints a detailed picture of how an institution recruits, retains, and supplements its faculty resources to sustain academic quality. It evaluates faculty not merely as employees but as essential pillars of institutional excellence.

Beyond scoring, Criterion 5 has profound implications for higher education institutions. It compels them to prioritize recruitment of highly qualified faculty, maintain balanced cadre structures, foster retention through strong HR policies, and build connections with industry and other academic bodies. Departments that consistently perform well in this criterion gain reputational advantages, attract better student intakes, and enhance their ability to secure research funding and industry collaborations. At a macro level, this criterion ensures that institutions align with global benchmarks of faculty quality and academic leadership.

In conclusion, Criterion 5: Faculty Information is a multifaceted assessment that goes far beyond counting faculty numbers. By evaluating sufficiency, qualifications, hierarchical distribution, external engagement, and retention, it ensures that faculty resources are aligned with both institutional and student needs. The allocation of 100 marks to this criterion underscores the NBA’s recognition of faculty as the single most critical determinant of academic quality. Departments that excel in Criterion 5 demonstrate not only compliance with accreditation requirements but also a deep commitment to nurturing academic excellence, fostering innovation, and sustaining long-term institutional growth.

# 1. Introduction

Criterion 5, titled *Faculty Information*, is a cornerstone of the National Board of Accreditation (NBA) framework, carrying a weightage of 100 marks. It directly addresses the quality and sufficiency of faculty resources available within a department, recognizing that faculty are the backbone of higher education institutions. Faculty members are not only responsible for classroom teaching but also for mentoring, research guidance, and fostering innovation. This criterion seeks to ensure that programs are staffed adequately, with faculty members who are not just sufficient in number but also qualified, experienced, and balanced across hierarchical ranks. A strong faculty structure promotes academic excellence, research productivity, industry collaboration, and long-term institutional growth. Criterion 5 is thus a crucial indicator of an institution’s ability to provide students with a high-quality learning environment.

The first sub-criterion under this framework is the Student–Faculty Ratio (SFR), which is allotted 30 marks. The SFR reflects whether the department maintains an appropriate ratio of students to faculty members, thereby ensuring that teaching, mentoring, and research guidance are conducted effectively. This ratio is calculated by considering the number of faculty members available against the student strength, including both undergraduate and postgraduate students. Importantly, students from allied clusters or related programs are also factored into this calculation. A lower SFR indicates fewer students per faculty, which is desirable as it allows for more personalized attention, effective academic mentoring, and improved student outcomes. For example, a ratio closer to 1:15 or better is often associated with high-quality education delivery. Departments with strong SFR values can demonstrate their commitment to student success, thereby earning higher marks in this category.

The second sub-criterion is Faculty Qualification (FQI), which carries 25 marks. This aspect highlights the academic depth and quality of the teaching staff, encouraging departments to employ highly qualified faculty members, especially those holding doctoral degrees. Faculty members with Ph.D. qualifications contribute not only to teaching excellence but also to research output, innovation, and consultancy. Even those with M.Tech./M.E. qualifications bring advanced knowledge to the classroom and strengthen applied learning. The FQI calculation considers the weighted qualifications of all core teaching faculty, ensuring that departments with a strong base of research-active and well-qualified faculty are recognized.

High scores in this category signal that the institution values intellectual capital and academic rigor. By promoting the recruitment and retention of doctoral faculty, Criterion 5 aligns with global standards of higher education.

The third sub-criterion, Faculty Cadre Proportion, is also assigned 25 marks. It focuses on the distribution of faculty members across three major ranks: Professors, Associate Professors, and Assistant Professors. The NBA prescribes a normative ratio of 1:2:6, which ensures that there is a healthy balance of senior, mid-level, and junior faculty in the department.

Professors bring leadership, experience, and research acumen; Associate Professors contribute mentorship and advanced teaching; while Assistant Professors inject energy, new ideas, and innovation into the academic culture. A department that achieves the recommended cadre ratio demonstrates both vertical and horizontal balance, enabling effective mentoring chains, research collaborations, and administrative continuity. In contrast, institutions with a skewed distribution–such as very few professors and an overwhelming number of junior faculty–may struggle with academic leadership and governance. This sub-criterion, therefore, ensures sustainability and succession planning within academic departments.

The fourth sub-criterion is Visiting/Adjunct Faculty/Professor of Practice, which carries 10 marks. It acknowledges the importance of bridging the gap between academia and industry by engaging external experts. Visiting faculty may include academicians from other reputed institutions, researchers, or professionals with substantial industry experience. Professors of Practice, in particular, bring real-world insights, case studies, and professional practices that enhance the employability of students. Marks are awarded based on the existence of a structured system for inviting and engaging such faculty, as well as the number of hours they contribute. Their involvement enriches the curriculum, offers students an industry perspective, and strengthens the academic–industry ecosystem. This provision is especially relevant in a rapidly changing global economy, where students need to be exposed to practical skills alongside theoretical knowledge. Institutions with robust adjunct faculty systems demonstrate their commitment to preparing students for careers beyond academics.

The fifth and final sub-criterion is Faculty Retention, which is allocated 10 marks. This dimension evaluates the stability of faculty strength over time and serves as a proxy for institutional health and HR practices. Retaining faculty for three or more years indicates that the institution has supportive policies, transparent appraisal systems, and a healthy academic environment. Faculty retention ensures continuity in teaching, mentoring, and research projects, thereby enhancing student outcomes. Furthermore, long-term retention fosters institutional loyalty and builds a stronger sense of community within the department. The Faculty Retention Ratio (FRR), which measures the percentage of faculty retained over a given period, provides quantitative evidence of institutional effectiveness in managing its workforce. Institutions with high retention not only secure higher marks in this criterion but also benefit from the accumulated experience and institutional memory of their staff.

Together, these five sub-criteria form a comprehensive framework for assessing faculty resources. Student–Faculty Ratio (30 marks) ensures that departments are adequately staffed relative to student strength. Faculty Qualification (25 marks) rewards departments with highly qualified faculty, particularly those with doctoral degrees. Faculty Cadre Proportion (25 marks) emphasizes the importance of hierarchical balance and leadership in departments.

Visiting/Adjunct Faculty (10 marks) integrates external expertise into the academic process, while Faculty Retention (10 marks) highlights the stability and sustainability of institutional HR practices. This multidimensional evaluation ensures that faculty are assessed not just in terms of numbers but also in quality, diversity, stability, and external engagement.

Criterion 5 also plays a strategic role in aligning institutions with broader accreditation goals.

A well-staffed, balanced, and highly qualified faculty body directly impacts student performance (Criterion 4), research productivity (Criterion 6), and governance and leadership (Criterion 8). It also strengthens compliance with regulatory and accreditation requirements under NAAC, NIRF, QS I-GAUGE, and global standards. Faculty information is therefore not an isolated metric but a central determinant of institutional excellence across multiple domains.

From a student perspective, Criterion 5 ensures that they are taught and mentored by experienced, qualified, and diverse faculty. From an institutional perspective, it pushes management to prioritize faculty recruitment, retention, and professional development. For accreditation bodies, it provides clear evidence that an institution is investing in its most critical asset–its human capital. Thus, Criterion 5 represents a comprehensive approach to ensuring that higher education institutions meet global benchmarks in faculty quality and quantity.

In conclusion, Criterion 5: Faculty Information of the NBA accreditation process goes far beyond headcounts. It provides a nuanced, evidence-based framework for evaluating the sufficiency, qualification, balance, engagement, and retention of faculty in higher education institutions. By carrying 100 marks, this criterion underscores the pivotal role of faculty in shaping academic quality, research innovation, student success, and institutional reputation.

Strong performance under Criterion 5 not only enhances accreditation outcomes but also contributes to the long-term sustainability and excellence of the institution.

# 2. Criterion 5: Faculty Information

### 5.1. Student–Faculty Ratio (SFR) – 30 Marks

- **Definition:** Ratio of students (UG + PG in the department + allied clusters) to full-time faculty members (excluding first-year-only faculty).

- **Formula:**

SFR=S/F-FF

- S = Students (Sanctioned Intake + Lateral Entry, max 110% of SA; exclude SNQ/EWS/TFWS).

- F = Full-time faculty (regular/contractual, department + allied).

- FF = First-year-only faculty (100% load in FY).

| \< 21:1                   | 18 marks  |
| \< 23:1                   | 14 marks  |
| \< 25:1                   | 10 marks  |
| \> 25:1                   | 0 marks   |

- **Purpose:** Ensures departments are adequately staffed.

### 5.2. Faculty Qualification – 25 Marks

- **Index (FQI):**

FQI=2.5×(10X+4Y)/RF

- X = Ph.D. faculty (as per AICTE/UGC norms).

- Y = M.Tech./M.E. faculty.

- RF = Required Faculty (S/20).

- Max = 25 marks.

- **Purpose:** Encourages highly qualified faculty (Ph.D./PG) in core teaching.

- **Evidence:** Ph.D. degrees, approvals, AICTE/UGC compliance.

### 5.3. Faculty Cadre Proportion – 25 Marks

- **Normative Ratio:**

**1(Professor):**2(AssociateProf.):6(AssistantProf.)1 (Professor) : 2 (Associate Prof.) : 6 (Assistant Prof.)1(Professor):2(AssociateProf.):6(AssistantProf.)

- RF1 = Professors required = (1/9 × RF)

- RF2 = Associate Professors required = (2/9 × RF)

- RF3 = Assistant Professors required = (6/9 × RF)

- **Marks Formula:**

Marks=(AF1/RF1+AF2/RF2×0.6+AF3/RF3×0.4)×12.5

- Max 25 marks, capped if exceeded.

- **Purpose:** Ensures a healthy distribution across senior, mid-level, and junior faculty ranks.

- **Evidence:** Appointment letters, approvals, university records.

|-----------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Provision of system         | 1 mark.                     |

### 5.5. Faculty Retention – 10 Marks

**Two evaluation methods:**

**1. Retention % (simple method):**

Retention%=Faculty retained ≥3 yrs/RF×100

| **Retention %** | **Marks** |
|-----------------|-----------|
| ≥90%            | 10 marks  |
| ≥75%            | 8         |
| ≥60%            | 6         |
| ≥50%            | 4         |
| \<50%           | 0         |

**2. Faculty Retention Ratio (FR advanced method):**

FR=((0A+1B+2C+3D+4E)/RF)×2.5

- **A:** \<1 year, B: ≥1 & \<2, C: ≥2 & \<3, D: ≥3 & \<4, E: ≥4 years.

- Average FR across 3 years, capped at 10.

- **Purpose:** Evaluates institutional stability and HR practices.

- **Evidence:** Faculty joining/relieving records, payroll continuity.

# 3. Student–Faculty Ratio (SFR)

- Purpose of SFR

  - Evaluates whether a department has an adequate number of faculty members relative to its student strength.

  - Ensures quality of teaching, mentoring, and research guidance.

  - A lower ratio (fewer students per faculty) reflects better academic standards.

- Scope of Calculation

  - Calculated at the Department level.

  - **Must include:**

    - All Undergraduate (UG) programs under the department and allied clusters.

    - All Postgraduate (PG) programs under the department and allied clusters.

  - Faculty engaged exclusively in First Year teaching are excluded.

- UG Program Contribution

  - **For each UG program (UG1 ... UGn):**

    - B = No. of students in 2nd year.

    - C = No. of students in 3rd year.

    - D = No. of students in 4th year.

  - First year students are excluded because their faculty is also excluded from count.

- PG Program Contribution

  - **For each PG program (PG1 ... PGm):**

    - A = No. of students in 1st year.

    - B = No. of students in 2nd year.

- Student Count (S)

  - S = Total eligible students of UG (2nd–4th year) + PG (1st–2nd year) across all programs.

  - **Student strength (ST) is computed as:**

ST = Sanctioned Intake (SA) + Lateral Entry students (L up to 10% of SA)

- **Exclusions from S:**

  - Students admitted under supernumerary quotas (SNQ, EWS, TFWS, etc.).

- Faculty Count (F)

  - **Includes:**

    - Full-time regular faculty.

    - Full-time contractual faculty (with full workload).

    - Faculty from allied departments/clusters contributing to the program.

  - **Excludes:**

    - Faculty teaching only First Year courses (100% workload in 1st year).

- Formula for SFR

  - SFR = S ÷ F

  - **Where:**

    - S = Total eligible student strength.

    - F = Total eligible faculty strength.

# 4. Marks calculation logic for NBA SFR

## 1. Context

- NBA evaluates Student–Faculty Ratio (SFR) over 3 years (CAY, CAYm1, CAYm2).

- After computing SFR1, SFR2, SFR3, we take the Average SFR = (SFR1 + SFR2 + SFR3) / 3.

- Marks are then awarded out of 30 based on this Average SFR.

## 2. Marking Criteria (as per your note)

| **Marking Criteria (as per your note)** | **Marks**              |
|-----------------------------------------|------------------------|
| SFR \< 15                               | 30 Marks (full marks). |
| 15 ≤ SFR \< 17                          | 26 Marks.              |
| 17 ≤ SFR \< 19                          | 22 Marks.              |
| 19 ≤ SFR \< 21                          | 18 Marks.              |
| 21 ≤ SFR \< 23                          | 14 Marks.              |
| 23 ≤ SFR \< 25                          | 10 Marks.              |
| SFR ≥ 25                                | 0 Marks (no marks).    |

## 3. Mathematical Expression

- The rule between SFR = 15 and 25 is linear with slope –2 marks per +1 in SFR.

- **Formula:**

Marks=30–2×(SFR–15)

- **Boundaries:**

  - If SFR \< 15 → cap at 30.

  - If SFR \> 25 → floor at 0.

## 4. Example Mapping

| **Average SFR** | **Marks Awarded** |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| 14.5            | 30                |
| 16.0            | 26                |
| 18.0            | 22                |
| 20.0            | 18                |
| 22.0            | 14                |
| 24.0            | 10                |
| 26.0            | 0                 |

## 5. Excel Formula Used

**Inside the Excel file (cell just below Average SFR row, CAY column):**

=IF(B\[last_avg_row\]="","",  
IF(B\[last_avg_row\]\<15,30,  
IF(B\[last_avg_row\]\<=25, 30 - 2\*(B\[last_avg_row\]-15), 0)  
)  
)

- B\[last_avg_row\] → the cell containing Average SFR (CAY column).

- **Checks in sequence:**

  - If blank → return blank.

  - If \< 15 → return 30.

  - If between 15 and 25 → apply linear formula.

  - If \> 25 → return 0.

- **Label:** “Marks (out of 30) – linear 15→25; 30 if \<15; 0 if \>25”.

- **Value:** Computed automatically.

# 14. Faculty Qualification

## 1. Purpose of this Criterion

- This sub-criterion evaluates the academic quality of faculty in a department/cluster.

- It rewards departments that maintain a good proportion of Ph.D.-qualified and M.Tech/ME-qualified faculty.

- The metric used is the Faculty Qualification Index (FQI), which links faculty qualifications with required strength (RF).

## 2. Formula for Faculty Qualification Index (FQI)

FQI=2.5×(10X+4Y)/RF

- X = Number of faculty members with Ph.D. (or equivalent) degree.

- Y = Number of faculty members with M.Tech/ME (or equivalent) degree.

- RF = Required Faculty = S/20

  - Where S = Number of students in department (including allied programs).

  - 20:1 is the NBA-mandated Student–Faculty Ratio (from Criterion 5.1).

## 3. Meaning of Each Term

- 10X → Each Ph.D. faculty gets 10 points weight.

- 4Y → Each M.Tech faculty gets 4 points weight.

- RF → Denominator ensures faculty quality is measured relative to the faculty required (not just those available).

- 2.5 multiplier → Normalization to bring maximum score to 25 marks.

## 4. Step-by-Step Calculation Procedure

**1. Count students (S):**

- From approved admissions (SA + lateral entries ≤110% SA, excluding SNQ/EWS).

- Include students of all UG & PG programs in department + allied departments.

**2. Calculate RF:**

RF=S/20

> ***Example:** If total students S=600S = 600S=600, then RF=600/20=30*

**3. Count faculty:**

- X = Faculty with Ph.D. (approved, full-time, counted in SFR).

- Y = Faculty with M.Tech/ME.

- (Exclude first-year only faculty (FF), visiting, guest, part-time).

**4. Compute Weighted Faculty Value:**

10X+4Y

**5. Apply Formula:**

FQI=2.5×(10X+4Y)/RF

**6. Cap maximum:**

- If FQI \> 25, award full 25 marks only.

## 5. Example Calculation

- Total students (S) = 600

- Required Faculty (RF) = 600/20 = 30

- Faculty with Ph.D. (X) = 12

- Faculty with M.Tech (Y) = 18

Step 1: Weighted faculty value = (10×12 + 4×18) = 120 + 72 = 192

Step 2: Divide by RF = 192/30 = 6.4

Step 3: Multiply by 2.5 = 16.0

FQI = 16.0 marks (out of 25)

## 6. Key NBA Compliance Points

- Faculty considered must be AICTE/UGC approved and full-time.

- Allied department faculty are included in X, Y, and RF.

- If RF is higher than available faculty, it still applies (to prevent artificially inflated FQI).

- First-year faculty (100% load) excluded from X & Y.

## 7. Documentation Required

- Faculty Qualification Proofs

  - Ph.D./M.Tech degree certificates.

  - University/AICTE faculty approvals.

- Faculty List (mapped with qualification, designation, DOJ, dept).

- Student Strength Records (admissions, registers) to justify S.

- Cross-verification with SFR records (Criterion 5.1).

## 8. Marking

- FQI directly converts to marks (capped at 25).

- If FQI ≥ 25, department secures full 25 marks.

- If FQI \< 25, proportional marks as per calculation.

# 15. Faculty Cadre Proportion

## 1. What is being assessed?

- NBA checks whether the faculty distribution across cadres (Professor, Associate Professor, Assistant Professor) in a department matches the ideal ratio prescribed by AICTE/UGC.

- **The ideal proportion is:**

1:2:6

- 1 Professor

- 2 Associate Professors

- 6 Assistant Professors (for every 9 faculty required to satisfy the 20:1 student–faculty ratio).

## 2. Formula for Required Faculty (RF)

- **First calculate total faculty required based on student strength:**

RF=S/20

where S = total number of students in the department + allied departments (UG + PG, as per section 5.1).

- **Then split RF into cadre requirements:**

  - RF1 (Professors required) = 1/9 × RF

  - RF2 (Associates required) = 2/9 × RF

  - RF3 (Assistants required) = 6/9 × RF

## 3. How to Evaluate Actual Faculty vs. Required Faculty

- **Count actual faculty in each cadre:**

  - AF1 = Actual Professors

  - AF2 = Actual Associate Professors

  - AF3 = Actual Assistant Professors

- Compare with required values (RF1, RF2, RF3).

## 4. Marking Scheme (Faculty Cadre Proportion Index – FCPI)

- **NBA generally uses the following formula to assign marks:**

FCPI=(AF1/RF1+AF2/RF2+AF3/RF3)÷3×25

- **Conditions:**

  - Each ratio is capped at 1.0 (i.e., excess faculty in one cadre cannot compensate for shortage in another).

  - Maximum marks = 25.

## 5. Step-by-Step Example

Suppose a department has S = 720 students.

**1. Compute RF (Required Faculty):**

RF=720/20=36

2\. Distribute as per 1:2:6:

- RF1 (Professors) = 36 × (1/9) = 4

- RF2 (Associates) = 36 × (2/9) = 8

- RF3 (Assistants) = 36 × (6/9) = 24

**3. Actual Faculty (assume):**

- AF1 = 3 Professors

- AF2 = 6 Associates

- AF3 = 28 Assistants

**4. Compute ratios:**

- AF1/RF1 = 3/4 = 0.75

- AF2/RF2 = 6/8 = 0.75

- AF3/RF3 = 28/24 = 1.16 → capped at 1.0

**5. Compute FCPI:**

FCPI=(0.75+0.75+1.0)/3×25=20.83

Marks obtained = 20.83 / 25

## 6. Key Notes for Compliance

- Faculty qualifications & experience must meet AICTE/UGC norms for cadre designation.

  - Professor = Ph.D. + minimum experience.

  - Associate Professor = Ph.D./M.Tech + prescribed experience.

  - Assistant Professor = M.Tech/ME or equivalent.

- **Exclusions:** Do not count visiting/adjunct/part-time faculty.

- **Allied departments:** Their faculty must be included if their students are counted in S.

## 7. Documentation Required

- Faculty roster with designation, qualification, experience, and approvals (AICTE/UGC).

- Student strength (S) calculations as per Criterion 5.1.

- Appointment letters, approvals, and payroll records to validate full-time status.

# 16. Faculty Cadre Proportion (25 marks) with your given table format and formula

## 1. Objective

- NBA evaluates whether a department maintains the right balance of senior and junior faculty.

- The required distribution is set by AICTE/UGC norms and is tested against Student– Faculty Ratio (SFR) from section 5.1.

- Marks are awarded based on how close actual cadre availability (AF1, AF2, AF3) matches the required (RF1, RF2, RF3).

## 2. Cadre Definitions

- RF1 / AF1 = Professors

- RF2 / AF2 = Associate Professors

- RF3 / AF3 = Assistant Professors

**Required faculty numbers (RF) are computed from student strength (S):**

RF=S/20

**Then distributed as:**

- RF1 = (1/9) × RF (Professors)

- RF2 = (2/9) × RF (Associates)

- RF3 = (6/9) × RF (Assistants)

## 3. Marks Formula

Faculty Cadre Proportion Marks=(AF1/RF1+0.6AF2/RF2+0.4AF3/RF3)×12.5

- AF1/RF1 = fulfillment ratio for Professors

- AF2/RF2 = fulfillment ratio for Associates

- AF3/RF3 = fulfillment ratio for Assistants

- Ratios can be greater than 1 if extra faculty are available.

- If AF1 = 0 and AF2 = 0, marks = 0 (department lacks senior leadership).

- Maximum = 25 marks (cap applied).

## 4. Example Cases

- Case 1: Perfect requirement met

# 23. Faculty Retention

## 1. Objective of this Criterion

- NBA wants to measure the stability of faculty in the department.

- Frequent turnover of faculty affects continuity in teaching, mentoring, and research.

- Retention ensures a strong academic ecosystem and reduces reliance on temporary/contract staff.

## 2. How Retention is Measured

- Retention = % of faculty members who have served in the department for 3 or more years (continuously).

- **Faculty considered:**

  - Only full-time, regular/approved faculty (counted in SFR).

  - Exclude visiting/adjunct/part-time and first-year-only faculty.

## 3. Formula

Retention %=Number of faculty retained for ≥ 3 years/Total faculty required as per SFR (RF) ×100

## 4. Marks Distribution (Maximum = 10)

| **Retention**    | **Marks** |
|------------------|-----------|
| ≥ 90%            | 10 marks  |
| ≥ 75% but \< 90% | 8 marks   |
| ≥ 60% but \< 75% | 6 marks   |
| ≥ 50% but \< 60% | 4 marks   |
| \< 50%           | 0 marks   |

## 5. Step-by-Step Process

**1. Calculate RF (Required Faculty):**

- From Criterion 5.1 → RF = S / 20 (total students / 20).

**2. Identify faculty retained for ≥ 3 years:**

- Cross-check appointment date → still employed in CAY.

- Collect supporting evidence (joining report, payroll continuity).

**3. Compute Retention %:**

- Divide faculty retained ≥3 years by RF.

4\. Award marks based on NBA bands above.

## 6. Example Calculation

- **RF (Required Faculty):** 40

- **Actual Retained Faculty (≥ 3 years):** 34

Retention % = (34 ÷ 40) × 100 = 85%

Marks = 8/10 (falls in 75-90% band).

## 7. Documentation Required

- **Faculty retention register with:**

  - Faculty name, designation, date of joining, status (retained or not).

- Joining reports + payroll continuity records for verification.

- AICTE/University approvals to prove faculty is regular.

## 8. Importance for NBA

- High retention = continuity, experienced faculty pool, better outcomes in student mentoring, R&D, accreditation cycles.

- Low retention = signals instability → negative impact on marks

# 24. Faculty Retention Ratio Calculator

## 1. Objective

- NBA evaluates not just how many faculty are retained, but also their length of service in the same institute.

- A stable faculty team ensures continuity in teaching, mentoring, and research.

- Retention is converted into a quantitative score (Faculty Retention Ratio – FR).

## 2. Key Definitions

- RF = Required Faculty = S/20

  - Where S = total students in the department + allied departments (from section 5.1).

  - Exclude MCA/BCA and other non-engineering programs.

- AF = Available Faculty (in the Department + Allied Departments, counted in SFR).

- **Faculty categorization (experience at the current institute):**

  - A = Faculty with \< 1 year experience (not retained yet).

  - B = Faculty with ≥1 year and \<2 years.

  - C = Faculty with ≥2 years and \<3 years.

  - D = Faculty with ≥3 years and \<4 years.

  - E = Faculty with ≥4 years.

## 3. Formula for Faculty Retention (per year)

FR=((A×0)+(B×1)+(C×2)+(D×3)+(E×4)/RF)×2.50

- The score is capped at 10 points per year.

- **This formula gives higher weightage to longer-serving faculty:**

  - 0 for \<1 year,

  - 1 for 1–2 years,

  - 2 for 2–3 years,

  - 3 for 3–4 years,

  - 4 for ≥4 years.

## 4. Average Faculty Retention Score

Average FR=(FR1+FR2+FR3)/3

- Computed across CAYm1, CAYm2, and CAYm3.

- Limited to 10 marks maximum.

## 5. Example Calculation

Suppose in CAYm1:

- RF = 40

- **Faculty distribution:** A=3, B=4, C=5, D=8, E=20

FR=((3×0)+(4×1)+(5×2)+(8×3)+(20×4)/40)×2.5

FR=(0+4+10+24+80/40)×2.5

FR=(118/40)×2.5=7.375

Marks for CAYm1 = 7.38/10

Similarly, calculate for CAYm2 and CAYm3, then average.

## 6. Notes (as per NBA guidelines)

- RF must include students + allied departments (from Criterion 5.1 SFR).

- Exclude MCA, BCA, MBA, and other non-engineering programs (both students and faculty).

- **Faculty evidence required:**

  - Appointment letters + joining reports.

  - Service records/payroll continuity.

  - Faculty approval letters (AICTE/University).

- Remarks/Evidence → payroll records, appointment letters, service registers, university/AICTE approval.

## 3. How it Supports NBA Assessment

- Used for Criterion 5.4: Faculty Retention (10 marks).

- NBA requires calculation of Retention % or Faculty Retention Ratio (FR).

- The register shows year-wise who stayed and who left, making the computation verifiable.

- **Cross-checked against:**

  - Payroll records (salary continuity).

  - Joining reports & service registers.

  - Faculty approvals (AICTE/University).

## 4. Example Use

Suppose NBA visit year is CAY = 2024–25.

- Faculty who joined before 30-June-2022 and are still active in 2025 = "Retained ≥3 Years = Yes"

- Faculty who joined in 2023 or 2024 = "No" (less than 3 years service).

- If a faculty left in 2024, status = "Resigned/Retired" and retention = No.
