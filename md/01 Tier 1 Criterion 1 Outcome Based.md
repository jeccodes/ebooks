# Abstract

The National Board of Accreditation (NBA) framework for Tier-1 institutions outlines a comprehensive approach to developing and assessing an Outcome-Based Curriculum (OBC) carrying 120 marks across five criteria.

The first criterion, Vision, Mission, and Program Educational Objectives (PEOs) (35 marks), focuses on institutional aspirations and commitments, with 3–5 broad PEOs defined through stakeholder consultations and validated by statutory bodies, disseminated widely, and supported with correlation matrices and justification notes.

The second criterion, Curriculum Structure and Features (30 marks), emphasizes systematic curriculum design and revision aligned with NBA Program Outcomes (POs), Washington Accord Knowledge Profiles, AICTE/UGC model curricula, and NEP 2020 reforms. It includes semester-wise credit allocation under the National Credit Framework, grouping of courses across domains, and innovations such as Academic Bank of Credits (ABC), APAAR, MOOCs, industry-driven courses, and multidisciplinary electives.

The third criterion, POs, PSOs, and Mapping with Courses (20 marks), mandates articulation of the 12 graduate attributes and up to three department-specific outcomes, documented through course-to-PO/PSO mapping matrices with correlation levels and justification notes.

The fourth criterion, Course Outcomes (COs) and Course Articulation Matrix (30 marks), requires defining 4–6 measurable COs per course using Bloom’s Taxonomy verbs, mapping them to POs and PSOs with correlation levels (–, 1, 2, 3), and providing justification for strong links while ensuring alignment of assessment tools with COs.

The fifth criterion, Program Articulation Matrix (5 marks), consolidates all course-level contributions to demonstrate comprehensive coverage of POs and PSOs at the program level.

Together, these criteria emphasize systematic documentation, stakeholder involvement, global alignment with Washington Accord standards, and continuous improvement cycles, ensuring that Tier-1 institutions maintain curriculum relevance, graduate employability, quality assurance, and international recognition.

# 1 Introduction

The National Board of Accreditation (NBA) framework for Tier-1 institutions provides a comprehensive guide for the development, implementation, and assessment of an Outcome-Based Curriculum (OBC), which is central to ensuring the quality, relevance, and international recognition of engineering education. The framework is evaluated across five key criteria totaling 120 marks and aims to establish systematic processes, requirements, and evidence that reflect an institution’s commitment to outcome-based education.

The first criterion, Vision, Mission, and Program Educational Objectives (PEOs) with 35 marks, requires institutions to define long-term aspirations, action-oriented commitments, and career goals for graduates. These PEOs are framed with stakeholder input, validated by statutory bodies such as DAC, BOS, and Academic Council, disseminated widely, and logically mapped to the institutional mission using correlation matrices and justification notes.

The second criterion, Curriculum Structure and Features with 30 marks, emphasizes systematic curriculum design and revision aligned with NBA’s Program Outcomes (POs), Washington Accord Knowledge Profiles (WKs), AICTE/UGC guidelines, and NEP 2020 reforms. It mandates clear credit distribution under the L–T–P–S system with 30 learning hours equal to 1 credit as per the National Credit Framework, proper grouping of courses across categories, and adoption of innovations such as multidisciplinary electives, Academic Bank of Credits (ABC), APAAR, MOOCs, and industry collaborations.

The third criterion, Program Outcomes (POs), Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs), and Mapping with Courses worth 20 marks, focuses on defining the 12 NBA graduate attributes and up to three PSOs, supported by a consolidated course-to-PO/PSO mapping matrix that assigns correlation levels and provides justification for strong links.

The fourth criterion, Course Outcomes (COs) and Course Articulation Matrix with 30 marks, requires every course to define 4–6 measurable outcomes using Bloom’s Taxonomy action verbs and to map these COs to POs and PSOs through a matrix that records the strength of correlation (3 = High, 2 = Medium, 1 = Low, – = None) with justification notes and alignment to relevant assessment tools.

The fifth criterion, Program Articulation Matrix with 5 marks, demands a program-level consolidated matrix that demonstrates how all courses, through their COs, collectively ensure comprehensive coverage of all 12 POs and the department’s PSOs.

Across all criteria, NBA stresses the importance of systematic documentation, stakeholder involvement, logical alignment of Vision–Mission–PEOs–POs–COs, and continuous quality improvement. Institutions are expected to maintain evidence such as lesson plans, BOS/DAC minutes, industry feedback, surveys, and attainment analyses to validate compliance with NBA’s Outcome-Based Education framework, thereby strengthening graduate employability, institutional credibility, and global recognition.

# 2 Outcome Based Curriculum 120 Marks

## A Vision Mission and Program Educational Objectives 35 Marks

### 1 State the Vision and Mission of the Institute and the Department 05 Marks

- Vision and Mission must be formally defined, approved (BoS/AC), and published.

- Vision: Longterm aspiration (e.g., “To be a globally recognized center of excellence in engineering education, research, and innovation.”).

- Mission: Action-oriented statements reflecting teaching, research, industry, and societal contributions.

### 2 State PEOs of the Program 05 Marks

- PEOs are broad program-level goals, achievable 4–5 years after graduation.

- Typically 3–5 PEOs, aligned with Vision/Mission.

**Example for Computer Science**

- PEO1: Excel in professional career using computing fundamentals.

- PEO2: Demonstrate research and innovation in emerging technologies.

- PEO3: Exhibit ethics, leadership, and lifelong learning.

### 3 Process of Defining Vision Mission and PEOs 10 Marks

- Inputs: Stakeholder feedback (faculty, alumni, industry, employers, students).

- Validation: Department Advisory Committee (DAC), Board of Studies (BoS), Academic Council (AC).

- Outcome: Documented process flow (Feedback → DAC → BoS → AC).

### 4 Dissemination of Vision Mission and PEOs 05 Marks

**Displayed on**

- Institute website, department website.

- Student/Faculty handbooks.

- Classrooms, laboratories, program brochures.

- Awareness created through induction programs, meetings, workshops.

### 5 Mapping of PEOs with Mission 10 Marks

- Use a Mission ↔ PEO mapping matrix (Low/Medium/High correlation).

- Provide justification notes for each mapping.

- Example: Mission element on “Research & Innovation” strongly correlates with PEO2.

## B Curriculum Structure and Features 30 Marks

### 1 State the Process for Developing or Revising the Program Curriculum 10 Marks

- Process flow: Stakeholder Feedback → DAC Draft → BoS Review → AC Approval → Implementation.

- Alignment with NBA POs/WKs, AICTE/UGC model curricula, NEP 2020 guidelines.

- Evidence: BoS minutes, IQAC reports, curriculum revision documents.

### 2 Curriculum Structure 10 Marks

- Semester-wise listing of courses with L–T–P–SL → Credit conversion (30 hrs = 1 credit as per NCF).

- Clear separation of Core, Electives, Labs, Projects, Internships.

### 3 Components of Curriculum 05 Marks

**Grouping by categories**

- Basic Sciences, Engineering Sciences, Humanities/Social Sciences, Program Core, Electives, Projects, Internships.

- Show credit distribution (%) across categories.

### 4 Strategies for Education Reforms 05 Marks

- Multidisciplinary courses.

- Open electives, minor/major degree pathways.

- Academic Bank of Credits (ABC), APAAR integration.

- MOOCs/NPTEL adoption.

- Interdisciplinary projects, industry collaborations.

## C POs PSOs and their Mapping with Courses 20 Marks

### 1 POs and PSOs 05 Marks

- POs: 12 graduate attributes defined by NBA (Engineering Knowledge, Problem Analysis, Design, Ethics, Communication, etc.).

- PSOs: Program-specific outcomes (max 3) framed by department.

**Example EEE**

- PSO1: Design and analyze power systems.

- PSO2: Apply renewable energy technologies.

- PSO3: Demonstrate ethical and professional practice in industry.

### 2 Mapping between Courses and POs and PSOs 15 Marks

- Consolidated Course ↔ POs/PSOs Matrix.

**Example**

- Data Structures → PO1, PO2, PO3, PSO1.

- Ethics → PO6, PO7, PO8, PSO3.

- Capstone Project → All POs + all PSOs.

<!-- -->

- Evidence: Course–PO/PSO mapping sheets, justification notes.

## D Course Outcomes and Course Articulation Matrix 30 Marks

### 1 Course Outcomes Semester Wise 15 Marks

- Define 4–6 measurable COs per course.

- Provide for two core courses per semester (I–VIII).

**Example Data Structures Sem III**

- CO1: Explain linear and nonlinear data structures.

- CO2: Apply DS for problem solving.

- CO3: Implement using modern tools.

- CO4: Evaluate algorithm efficiency.

### 2 Course Articulation Matrix 15 Marks

- Rows = COs, Columns = POs + PSOs.

- Fill correlation (1–2–3/–).

**Example Data Structures**

- CO1 → PO1(3), PO2(3), PSO1(3).

- CO2 → PO2(3), PO3(3), PSO1(3).

- CO3 → PO3(3), PO5(3), PSO2(2).

## E Program Articulation Matrix 05 Marks

- Consolidated Course ↔ POs/PSOs table (program-wide).

- Demonstrates how all courses together cover all outcomes.

**Example**

- Math → PO1, PO2.

- Ethics → PO6, PO7, PO8.

- Projects → PO3, PO4, PO5, PO9, PO10, PO11, PO12 + PSOs.

# 4 Vision and Mission of the Institute and Department 05 Marks

## Purpose of Criterion

- To ensure that the Institute and Department have well-defined Vision and Mission statements that align with national educational priorities, industry needs, and societal development.

- These statements reflect the long-term aspirations (Vision) and present commitments (Mission) that guide all academic and administrative activities.

- NBA assigns marks based on clarity, relevance, dissemination, and alignment of these statements with program outcomes.

## Vision Statement Institute Level

- Definition: A concise declaration of the future aspirations of the institute – where it wants to be in 10–20 years.

**Features**

- Inspirational and forward-looking.

- Addresses the role of the institute in education, research, and contribution to society.

- Short, crisp, and easy to remember.

*Example: “To be a globally recognized centre of excellence in engineering education, research, and innovation for sustainable development of society.”*

## Mission Statement Institute Level

- Definition: A statement of the current purpose and actions undertaken by the institute to achieve its vision.

**Features**

- Defines academic, research, and community service objectives.

- Specifies the approach: teaching-learning process, industry collaboration, innovation, value-based education, etc.

- More detailed than the vision.

**Example**

*“To provide quality education through innovative pedagogy and advanced infrastructure.”*

*“To foster research and industry collaboration for real-world problem solving.”*

*“To nurture responsible professionals with ethics, leadership, and entrepreneurial skills.”*

## Vision Statement Department Level

- Should align with the institute’s vision but be discipline-specific.

- Reflects the department’s aspiration in terms of its core area (e.g., Computer Science, Mechanical Engineering).

*Example: “To be a leading department in producing globally competent computer science engineers capable of innovation and lifelong learning.”*

## Mission Statement Department Level

- Departmental mission explains how the department will realize its vision, mapped to the discipline.

- Includes aspects like curriculum design, research thrust areas, industry collaboration, student development.

**Example**

*“To impart strong theoretical and practical knowledge in computing to meet industrial and societal needs.”*

*“To promote research, innovation, and entrepreneurship in emerging areas of computer science.”*

*“To instil professional ethics, teamwork, and continuous learning culture in graduates.”*

## NBA Requirements for Marks 05 Marks

1\. Clarity and Appropriateness – Vision and Mission statements must be clear, relevant, and realistic.

2\. Alignment – Department’s vision/mission should align with the Institute’s vision/mission.

3\. Consistency – Should be in line with Program Educational Objectives (PEOs), Program Outcomes (POs), and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs).

4\. Dissemination – Statements should be displayed on the institute’s website, handbooks, notice boards, and classrooms.

5\. Stakeholder Involvement – Framing/reviewing vision and mission should involve faculty, students, alumni, industry experts, and management.

## Supporting Evidence for NBA Documentation

- Officially approved Vision & Mission statements (signed by Governing Body/Academic Council).

- Screenshots of website display of Vision/Mission.

- Photographs of classroom/department boards where Vision & Mission are displayed.

- Meeting minutes of Academic Council/IQAC/Departmental Board of Studies showing review/revision of Vision & Mission.

- Mapping document showing how Vision/Mission aligns with PEOs, POs, and PSOs.

**Scoring Note**

- Well-defined, clearly articulated, aligned, and widely disseminated Vision & Mission of both Institute and Department → Full 05 Marks.

- If vague, misaligned, or not disseminated → partial marks.

# 5 Program Educational Objectives 05 Marks

## Purpose of PEOs in NBA Accreditation

- PEOs describe the broad career and professional achievements that graduates are expected to accomplish within 3–5 years after graduation.

- They act as a bridge between the Vision/Mission of the Department and the Program Outcomes (POs).

- NBA evaluates whether PEOs are clearly stated, consistent with mission statements, and mapped to stakeholder needs (students, industry, alumni, society).

## Definition of PEOs

- Long-term objectives describing what graduates will be able to achieve in their professional career, higher studies, entrepreneurship, or societal contribution.

- PEOs are not course-level outcomes but program-level goals.

- They are qualitative, broad in nature, and align with Bloom’s higher levels (application, synthesis, evaluation).

## Key Features of Good PEOs

- Must be few (3–5 statements) – not too many or too vague.

**Should align with**

- Department Vision and Mission.

- Graduate attributes specified by NBA/ABET (e.g., engineering knowledge, problem analysis, design, ethics, teamwork).

- National priorities (e.g., employability, innovation, sustainability).

<!-- -->

- Should be measurable indirectly via surveys (alumni, employer feedback, placement records).

## Example PEOs for Engineering Program

1\. Professional Competence – Graduates will excel in professional careers by applying fundamental engineering knowledge and modern tools.

2\. Higher Studies & Lifelong Learning – Graduates will pursue higher education, research, and continuous professional development.

3\. Innovation & Entrepreneurship – Graduates will demonstrate innovation, leadership, and entrepreneurial skills to develop solutions for societal needs.

4\. Ethics & Social Responsibility – Graduates will practice professional ethics and contribute responsibly to sustainable development.

5\. Teamwork & Communication – Graduates will work effectively in multidisciplinary teams and communicate efficiently in global environments.

## NBA Requirements for Marks 05 Marks

1\. Clarity – PEOs must be clearly stated, specific, and realistic.

2\. Alignment – PEOs should flow from the Vision & Mission of the Institute/Department.

3\. Stakeholder Involvement – Framing PEOs should involve faculty, industry experts, alumni, and students.

4\. Assessment & Evaluation Process – There should be a mechanism to assess PEO attainment (alumni surveys, employer surveys, placement data, higher studies data).

5\. Documentation – PEOs must be displayed on the website, curriculum documents, and classrooms.

## Supporting Evidence for NBA Documentation

- PEO Statements (approved by Academic Council/BoS).

- Mapping table showing linkage between Department Mission ↔ PEOs ↔ Program Outcomes (POs).

- Process document of how PEOs were framed (committee meeting minutes with stakeholders).

- Assessment reports of PEO attainment (alumni surveys, employer feedback).

- Dissemination evidence – screenshots of website, program brochures, and handbooks.

**Scoring Note**

- If the program has well-defined, aligned, measurable, and disseminated PEOs, it secures the full 05 Marks.

- Vague, unaligned, or missing evaluation process → marks deduction.

# 6 Process of Defining Vision Mission and PEOs 10 Marks

## 1 Purpose of this Criterion

- NBA expects institutions not only to state Vision, Mission, and PEOs but also to document the systematic process through which they were defined.

**Marks are awarded based on**

- Transparency of the process.

- Stakeholder participation (faculty, students, alumni, industry, governing bodies).

- Alignment with Institute Vision/Mission, national priorities, and program outcomes.

- Review and continuous improvement mechanisms.

## 2 Process of Defining the Vision and Mission of the Department

### Step 1 Review Institute Vision and Mission

- Departmental Vision/Mission must align with the larger goals of the institute.

- Reference to statutory bodies like AICTE, UGC, NEP-2020 is considered.

# 19 Clearly defined Mission Elements M1 to Mn

## 1 Purpose of Mission Elements M1 to Mn

- NBA requires the Department Mission to be broken into distinct elements (M1, M2, … Mn).

**This ensures that each Mission statement is concise measurable and mappable to**

- Vision of the Department

- Program Educational Objectives (PEOs)

- Program Outcomes (POs) and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs).

## 2 Example Department Mission Broken into Elements

**Department Mission Statement**

*“To provide strong theoretical and practical foundations, foster research and innovation, instill professional ethics and teamwork, and collaborate with industry for societal development.”*

**Mission Elements**

- M1: Provide strong theoretical and practical foundations in \[Discipline\].

- M2: Foster research, innovation, and entrepreneurship in emerging technologies.

- M3: Instill professional ethics, teamwork, leadership, and effective communication skills.

- M4: Collaborate with industry and society to solve real-world challenges.

## 3 Why Break into Mission Elements

**Each Mission Element M1 to Mn becomes a building block for mapping**

- Mission → PEOs (broad objectives of graduates).

- Mission → POs (immediate measurable skills and competencies).

<!-- -->

- Provides clarity and traceability in NBA SAR.

## 4 Usage of Mission Elements in NBA Mapping

**PEO Mission Mapping Table**

Each PEO is mapped against M1, M2, M3, M4 with correlation levels (High–3, Medium–2, Low–1).

**Mission PEO PO Linkage Table**

Demonstrates flow from Mission → PEOs → POs.

**Justification Narrative**

Explains how each Mission element contributes to long-term graduate outcomes.

## 5 NBA Documentation Tip

**Present Mission in two layers**

- Full Mission Statement (one paragraph).

- Broken-down Mission Elements (M1–Mn).

<!-- -->

- Keep elements short, action-oriented, discipline-specific.

- Always map Mission Elements forward to PEOs & POs in tables.

**Example for SAR Write up**

*The Department Mission has been articulated into four distinct Mission Elements (M1–M4). These elements provide clarity and enable systematic mapping to PEOs and POs, thereby ensuring alignment with the Department Vision and NBA graduate attributes.*

# 22 Narrative Justification for each mapping row beyond just filling numbers in the matrix

## 1 Why Narrative Justification is Required

- NBA expects not just quantitative mapping (1–3 levels) but also qualitative explanations.

- This ensures the mapping is rational, evidence-based, and non-arbitrary.

- Each PEO–Mission linkage must be explained in words.

## 2 Example Narrative Justification for PEO and Mission Mapping

### PEO1 Graduates will excel in professional careers through strong domain knowledge and problem solving skills

- M1 (High – 3): A solid theoretical and practical foundation directly ensures employability.

- M2 (Medium – 2): Research culture moderately supports problem-solving skills needed in careers.

- M3 (Low – 1): Ethics and teamwork are helpful but not the primary driver for careers.

- M4 (Medium – 2): Industry collaboration provides real-world exposure, improving professional competence.

### PEO2 Graduates will pursue higher education research and lifelong learning

- M1 (Medium – 2): Knowledge base provides essential preparation for advanced studies.

- M2 (High – 3): Research & innovation directly support higher education and lifelong learning.

- M3 (Low – 1): Ethics/teamwork contribute indirectly but are not primary enablers.

- M4 (Medium – 2): Industry collaboration provides research projects, motivating higher studies.

### PEO3 Graduates will demonstrate innovation leadership and entrepreneurial competencies

- M1 (Low – 1): Core knowledge helps but is not sufficient alone for entrepreneurship.

- M2 (High – 3): Mission explicitly emphasizes innovation & entrepreneurship.

- M3 (Low – 1): Ethics and teamwork are supportive but not central.

- M4 (Medium – 2): Industry partnerships create platforms for innovation and startups.

### PEO4 Graduates will practice professional ethics and contribute responsibly to society

- M1 (Low – 1): Knowledge contributes minimally to ethics & societal development.

- M2 (Low – 1): Research indirectly supports responsible solutions.

- M3 (High – 3): Ethics, teamwork, and communication directly ensure social responsibility.

- M4 (Low – 1): Industry collaboration contributes marginally by promoting socially relevant projects.

## 3 How to Present in SAR

- Present Matrix Table (with numbers) + Narrative justification for each PEO row.

**Example format**

- Table 1.1.5.1: Mapping Matrix with numbers (1–3).

- Table 1.1.5.1a: Narrative justification (bullet points under each PEO).

## 4 NBA Documentation Tip

- Keep narrative short, precise, and discipline-specific.

- Avoid generic statements – link to actual departmental practices (e.g., labs, projects, MoUs, startup cells).

- Cross-reference evidence (e.g., industry projects for M4, ethics courses for M3).

**SAR Ready Example Line**

*The mapping of PEOs with Mission elements has been supported with a narrative justification for each correlation level. This demonstrates a clear and logical relationship between departmental commitments and expected graduate achievements, thereby ensuring transparency and traceability in outcome-based education.*

# 43 Course Wise Credit Calculation as per the National Credit Framework NCF

## 1 Purpose
