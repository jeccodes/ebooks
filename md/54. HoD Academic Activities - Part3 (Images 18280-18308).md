**23. HoD's Role in Conducting Student Counseling**

**Policy Statement**

- The HoD ensures that every student receives structured academic, career, and personal counseling at department level, fostering academic success, emotional well-being, discipline, and holistic growth.

- Counseling is preventive (early support) and corrective (guidance for issues/backlogs/discipline).

**Actions by HoD/Division**

- Planning & Allocation

<!-- -->

- Appoint faculty mentors/counselors for small student groups (mentor–mentee ratio).

- Define counseling schedule (monthly/semester-wise).

<!-- -->

- Execution

<!-- -->

- Conduct one-to-one and group counseling sessions.

- Address academic, personal, and career issues.

- Refer students with serious issues to professional counselors or DC (disciplinary cases).

<!-- -->

- Monitoring & Documentation

<!-- -->

- Maintain counseling records signed by faculty mentor and HoD.

- Track remedial classes, mentoring interventions, and outcomes.

- Collect student feedback on effectiveness of counseling.

<!-- -->

- Continuous Improvement

<!-- -->

- Review counseling outcomes periodically.

- Suggest remedial/bridge classes, mentoring programs, or skill development workshops.

**Evidence/Documents**

- Counseling policy/guidelines.

- Mentor–mentee allocation list.

- Counseling schedules.

- Counseling records (signed by mentor & HoD).

- Academic progress reports (before vs after counseling).

- Attendance for remedial classes/mentoring sessions.

- Referral records (cases escalated to professional counselors or DC).

- ATRs from feedback and review meetings.

**Tangible Outcomes (Numeric & Measurable)**

- 100% students assigned mentors.

- Academic backlogs reduced by 20% YoY after counseling interventions.

Student attendance improved from 78% → 90%.

- Placement readiness training participation ↑ by 30%.

Student satisfaction on counseling ↑ from 3.6 → 4.4/5.

- Cases referred to DC reduced by 15% YoY (due to preventive counseling).

**Evidence/Documents – Student Counseling**

1\. Counseling Policy/Guidelines

- Official departmental or institutional policy on student counseling.

- Defines mentor–mentee ratio, frequency of sessions, escalation procedures.

2\. Mentor–Mentee Allocation List

- List of students assigned to each faculty mentor.

- Signed by HoD for authentication.

3\. Counseling Schedules

- Semester/monthly plan for counseling sessions.

- Circulated to students and mentors in advance.

4\. Counseling Records (Signed by Mentor & HoD)

- Individual session records (date, issues discussed, action points).

- Group counseling meeting notes.

5\. Academic Progress Reports (Before vs After Counseling)

- GPA, backlog, and attendance comparisons.

- Shows measurable impact of counseling.

6\. Attendance for Remedial Classes/Mentoring Sessions

- Registers or ERP logs showing students attended follow-up interventions.

7\. Referral Records

- Documentation of cases escalated to professional counselors or Disciplinary Committee (DC).

- Includes outcomes of referred cases.

8\. Action Taken Reports (ATRs)

- Summary of feedback from students on counseling effectiveness.

- Steps taken to improve mentoring (extra sessions, bridge courses, stress management workshops).

**HoD's Role in Conducting Parent Meetings and Capturing Suggestions for Improvement**

**Policy Statement**

- The HoD ensures structured Parent–Teacher Meetings (PTMs) are conducted periodically (semester/yearly) to engage parents in academic and co-curricular development of the division, collect feedback, and implement suggestions for continuous improvement.

**Actions by HoD/Division**

- Planning & Scheduling

<!-- -->

- Schedule parent meetings once per semester.

- Circulate notices/invitations in advance.

<!-- -->

- Execution

<!-- -->

- Present academic progress of students (class performance, attendance, placements).

- Gather parent feedback and suggestions.

- Discuss improvement plans for academics, labs, facilities, and student support.

<!-- -->

- Follow-Up

<!-- -->

- Document suggestions in meeting minutes.

- Prepare Action Taken Reports (ATRs).

- Communicate implemented changes to parents and faculty.

**Evidence/Documents**

- Parent meeting circulars/invitations.

- Attendance sheets of parents.

- Meeting agenda and presentations.

- Minutes of Meeting (MoM) signed by HoD.

- Feedback forms collected from parents.

- Action Taken Reports (ATRs) on parent suggestions.

- Photos/videos of PTMs.

- Consolidated parent feedback analysis (for AQAR/SSR).

**Tangible Outcomes (Numeric & Measurable)**

Parent participation rate ↑ from 45% → 70% in 3 years.

Parent satisfaction (survey rating) ↑ from 3.9 → 4.6/5.

- Suggestions implemented:

Additional remedial classes (↑ 20% attendance improvement).

- Lab upgrades (3 new labs added).

Placement training hours ↑ by 30%.

- Student retention rate improved by 12% YoY (due to better parent–faculty engagement).

**Evidence/Documents – Parent Meetings**

1\. Parent Meeting Circulars/Invitations

- Notices sent to parents announcing PTM schedule.

- Includes agenda, date, time, and venue (or online link).

2\. Attendance Sheets of Parents

- Signed attendance records of participants.

- Can also include ERP-generated logs for online PTMs.

3\. Meeting Agenda and Presentations

- Agenda circulated in advance.

- Presentations covering student performance, placements, academic calendar, and facilities.

4\. Minutes of Meeting (MoM) Signed by HoD

- Documented summary of discussions.

- Parent suggestions, faculty responses, and action points.

5\. Feedback Forms Collected from Parents

- Structured feedback on academics, facilities, placements, and student support.

- Numeric ratings + qualitative suggestions.

6\. Action Taken Reports (ATRs) on Parent Suggestions

- Records of how parent inputs were acted upon.

- Examples: more remedial classes, lab upgrades, additional placement training.

7\. Photos/Videos of PTMs

- Visual documentation for evidence submission in SSR/AQAR.

8\. Consolidated Parent Feedback Analysis (for AQAR/SSR)

- Year-on-year analysis of participation, satisfaction scores, and implemented improvements.

- Highlights CQI (Continuous Quality Improvement).

**24. HoD's Role in Establishing and Operating a Grievance Redressal Committee (GRC) at the Division Level**

**Policy Statement**

- The HoD ensures a functional, transparent, and documented grievance redressal mechanism for faculty, staff, and students of the division.

- The committee provides a fair platform for complaints, ensures timely resolution, and prevents escalation by promoting accountability and trust.

**Actions by HoD/Division**

- Constitution of the Committee

<!-- -->

- Nominate faculty/staff as members.

- Include representation of students and female faculty (as per UGC norms).

- Display committee structure on the department noticeboard and website.

<!-- -->

- Grievance Collection

<!-- -->

- Provide multiple modes (complaint box, online form, ERP, direct submission).

- Acknowledge receipt of grievance to the complainant.

<!-- -->

- Grievance Redressal Process

<!-- -->

- Review grievances in monthly/quarterly meetings.

- Classify issues (academic, administrative, harassment, infrastructure, etc.).

- Provide resolution within a fixed timeline (15–30 days).

- Refer unresolved/serious cases to University-level committee.

<!-- -->

- Monitoring & Documentation

<!-- -->

- Maintain records of grievances received, actions taken, and resolutions.

- Publish summary reports (without personal details) for transparency.

- Include grievance records in AQAR/SSR submissions.

**Evidence/Documents**

- Official order constituting the GRC.

- List of committee members with roles & tenure.

- Notices/circulars displaying grievance submission process.

- Complaint registers/online grievance records.

- Minutes of grievance committee meetings (signed by HoD).

- Action Taken Reports (ATRs) on grievances.

- Acknowledgement letters/emails to complainants.

- Consolidated grievance redressal report for AQAR/SSR.

**Tangible Outcomes (Numeric & Measurable)**

- 100% grievances acknowledged within 48 hours.

- 90% grievances resolved within 30 days.

- Grievances reduced by 25% YoY due to preventive measures.

Student satisfaction with grievance redressal ↑ from 3.7 → 4.5/5.

- Zero pending cases escalated beyond University-level GRC.

**Evidence/Documents – Grievance Redressal Committee (Division Level)**

1\. Official Order Constituting the GRC

- HoD/Principal's notification establishing the committee.

- Specifies compliance with UGC/AICTE/NAAC guidelines.

2\. List of Committee Members with Roles & Tenure

- Chairperson (HoD or senior faculty), student reps, female faculty, admin staff.

- Duration of appointment clearly mentioned.

3\. Notices/Circulars Displaying Grievance Submission Process

- Instructions on grievance channels (complaint box, ERP, email, form).

- Displayed on notice boards, website, and circulated to students.

4\. Complaint Registers/Online Grievance Records

- Serially numbered grievance entries.

- Includes date, category, complainant ID (kept confidential), and status.

5\. Minutes of Grievance Committee Meetings (Signed by HoD)

- Periodic review discussions.

- Case-wise status and decisions.

6\. Action Taken Reports (ATRs) on Grievances

- Detailed follow-up actions.

- Closure confirmation within stipulated time.

7\. Acknowledgement Letters/Emails to Complainants

- Proof of grievance receipt within 24–48 hours.

- Ensures transparency and accountability.

8\. Consolidated Grievance Redressal Report for AQAR/SSR

- Annual/semester-wise summary of grievances received, resolved, and pending.

- Linked to NAAC Criterion 5 (Student Support), Criterion 6 (Governance).

**25. HoD's Role in Inviting Guests for Lectures, and Conducting Seminars, Workshops, and Symposiums in the Division**

**Actions by HoD/Division**

- Planning

<!-- -->

- Identify themes aligned to curriculum, industry needs, and research trends.

- Finalize resource persons (academia/industry experts).

- Approve budgets and logistics.

<!-- -->

- Execution

<!-- -->

- Send invitations to guests and confirm participation.

- Prepare event schedule, publicity, and student participation plan.

- Conduct sessions with active student–faculty interaction.

<!-- -->

- Integration with OBE

Map each event to relevant COs/POs/PSOs (e.g., PO3 – Problem Analysis, PO10 – Communication, PO12 – Lifelong Learning).

- Collect participant feedback on relevance and impact.

<!-- -->

- Documentation & Review

<!-- -->

- Prepare event reports highlighting objectives, participation, outcomes, and evidence.

- Submit Action Taken Reports (ATRs) for improvements in future events.

**Evidence/Documents**

- Invitation letters/emails to guests.

- Approval from HoD/Principal.

- Event schedule and agenda.

- Attendance sheets of students/faculty.

- Photos/videos/media coverage of events.

- Feedback forms and analysis reports.
