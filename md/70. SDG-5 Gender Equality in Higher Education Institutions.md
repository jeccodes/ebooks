**Abstract**

The pursuit of Sustainable Development Goal 5 (SDG-5): Gender Equality within Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) is central to achieving inclusive, equitable, and transformative education systems that empower women and men equally. This goal emphasizes eliminating all forms of discrimination and violence against women and girls, ensuring equal access to educational and leadership opportunities, and recognizing women's contributions to social, economic, and academic progress. In the context of higher education, SDG-5 calls for institutional reforms that promote gender parity in governance, teaching, research, and campus culture.

HEIs serve as pivotal agents for advancing gender equality through curriculum design, policy formulation, and the creation of gender-sensitive infrastructure and environments that encourage safety, participation, and leadership among women. National frameworks such as the University Grants Commission (UGC) and the All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE) have integrated gender equity into accreditation and policy guidelines, while government initiatives like Beti Bachao Beti Padhao, Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana, Mahila Shakti Kendra, and Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana strengthen the foundation for women's education, empowerment, and social protection.

At the international level, UNESCO plays a key role in promoting SDG-5 by advocating for women's empowerment through education, science, culture, and technology. Its policy recommendations support female participation in STEM fields, leadership development, and media representation, while encouraging nations to adopt legal and institutional frameworks that ensure gender justice and equal opportunity.

Embedding SDG-5 in HEIs requires a multidimensional approach—ranging from gender-inclusive policies and leadership representation to research advancement and gender-responsive budgeting. The creation of Internal Complaints Committees (ICCs), gender audit systems, and women empowerment cells has become integral to institutional accountability and sustainability.

In essence, SDG-5 in higher education is not confined to gender representation but extends to systemic transformation—where inclusivity, respect, and equity define academic excellence. By empowering women as learners, educators, leaders, and innovators, HEIs contribute significantly to the national and global vision of sustainable development. Thus, achieving gender equality in higher education is both a moral imperative and a strategic necessity for building a just, progressive, and knowledge-driven society aligned with the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development.

**1. SDG-5: Achieve Gender Equality and Empower all Women and Girls**

**SDG-5: Gender Equality**

**Overview of SDG-5**

- Sustainable Development Goal 5 (SDG-5) focuses on achieving gender equality and empowering all women and girls by 2030.

- It recognizes that gender equality is not only a fundamental human right but also a foundation for a peaceful, prosperous, and sustainable world.

- The goal calls for eliminating discrimination, violence, and harmful practices against women and ensuring equal opportunities in leadership, education, employment, and decision-making.

**Core Objectives of SDG-5**

- End all forms of discrimination against women and girls everywhere.

- Eliminate gender-based violence, including trafficking and exploitation.

- Ensure equal participation of women in political, economic, and public life.

- Guarantee access to education and reproductive health rights for women and girls.

- Promote equal pay, equal work, and shared domestic responsibilities between men and women.

- Adopt sound policies and enforceable legislation to strengthen gender equality at all levels.

**Importance of Gender Equality in Higher Education Institutions (HEIs)**

- HEIs are key drivers of social transformation and play a central role in achieving SDG-5 through education, research, and policy influence.

- Promoting gender equality in HEIs ensures balanced leadership, inclusive governance, and equitable academic environments.

- Empowered women in academia contribute to diverse perspectives in teaching, research, and innovation, enriching institutional performance.

- Gender equality enhances student success, institutional reputation, and alignment with global sustainability rankings like QS I-GAUGE, NIRF, and THE Impact Rankings.

**Institutional Responsibilities under SDG-5**

- **Policy Integration:** HEIs should embed gender equity principles into institutional charters, governance structures, and quality assurance systems.

- **Leadership Representation:** Achieving 50% women representation in senior roles ensures balanced decision-making and inclusive leadership.

- **Safe and Inclusive Campuses:** Establish mechanisms like Internal Complaints Committees (ICC), gender-sensitization cells, and anti-harassment policies to ensure safety and dignity.

- **Gender-Responsive Budgeting:** Allocate dedicated funds for women's development programs, leadership training, and fellowships.

- **Curricular Inclusion:** Integrate gender studies, women's rights, and inclusive policy education into the academic curriculum.

**Link to National and Institutional Frameworks**

- Align with National Education Policy (NEP) 2020, which promotes inclusivity, equality, and gender-sensitive governance in education.

- Comply with University Grants Commission (UGC) gender equity guidelines and the Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH) Act.

- Contribute to the National Mission for Empowerment of Women and support the vision of “Beti Bachao, Beti Padhao” for educational empowerment.

**Monitoring and Evaluation Indicators for SDG-5 in HEIs**

- Percentage of women in leadership, faculty, and research positions.

- Gender ratio in student enrollment and graduation rates.

- Number of gender equity programs, workshops, and awareness campaigns conducted annually.

- Presence and effectiveness of grievance redressal and mentoring mechanisms.

- Availability of gender-sensitive infrastructure and support systems (childcare, safety, maternity facilities).

**Expected Outcomes of Implementing SDG-5 in HEIs**

- Creation of gender-balanced academic ecosystems promoting fairness, respect, and opportunity.

- Empowerment of women to participate equally in research, innovation, and decision-making.

- Reduction of gender gaps in pay, promotions, and professional development.

- Establishment of HEIs as models of inclusive leadership, contributing directly to national and global gender equality benchmarks.

- Strengthened contribution of higher education to the United Nations 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development through measurable progress on gender equity indicators.

**Conclusion**

- Achieving SDG-5 in HEIs is not merely about equality in numbers but about building a culture of empowerment, respect, and shared responsibility.

- Gender equality enhances institutional excellence, innovation, and social justice—key pillars of sustainable development.

- Through policy action, leadership reform, and inclusive practices, HEIs can become powerful agents of gender transformation and contribute meaningfully to a more equitable world.

**Target 5.1 — End All Forms of Discrimination Against Women and Girls**

**Goal:** Ensure that no woman or girl faces discrimination in law, policy, or daily life.

- **Indicator 5.1.1:** Checks whether national legal frameworks exist to promote, enforce, and monitor gender equality and prohibit discrimination based on sex.

- **Purpose:** Evaluates how effectively countries translate gender equality commitments into enforceable laws and institutions (e.g., equal pay acts, anti-harassment laws, property rights).

**Target 5.2 — Eliminate All Forms of Violence Against Women and Girls**

**Goal:** Protect women from all kinds of violence—domestic, sexual, physical, psychological, and economic—both in public and private spaces.

- **Indicator 5.2.1:** Measures the percentage of women aged 15 and above who experienced violence from an intimate partner in the past year.

- **Indicator 5.2.2:** Captures sexual violence committed by non-partners (e.g., in workplaces, schools, or public places).

- **Purpose:** Quantifies the prevalence of gender-based violence, guiding policy and law enforcement interventions.

**Target 5.3 — Eliminate Harmful Practices (Child Marriage and Female Genital Mutilation)**

**Goal:** End cultural and traditional practices that harm women's health and autonomy.

- **Indicator 5.3.1:** Tracks the share of women married before 15 and 18 years, reflecting early marriage trends.

- **Indicator 5.3.2:** Measures the proportion of girls and women (15–49 years) subjected to female genital mutilation (FGM).

- **Purpose:** Helps identify vulnerable communities and evaluate progress of child protection and women's rights programs.

**Target 5.4 — Recognize and Value Unpaid Care and Domestic Work**

**Goal:** Highlight and reduce the inequality of unpaid household labor carried mostly by women.

- **Indicator 5.4.1:** Measures time spent on unpaid domestic and care work (e.g., childcare, cooking, eldercare) by sex, age, and location.

- **Purpose:** Promotes social protection policies such as childcare services, flexible work, and shared family responsibility to balance workloads.

**Target 5.5 — Ensure Equal Participation and Leadership Opportunities**

**Goal:** Achieve gender parity in decision-making roles across politics, business, and administration.

- **Indicator 5.5.1:** Tracks women's representation in parliaments and local governments.

- **Indicator 5.5.2:** Measures women in managerial or leadership positions across public and private sectors.

- **Purpose:** Encourages countries to remove barriers to women's leadership through quotas, mentorship, and equal promotion policies.

**Target 5.6 — Ensure Universal Access to Reproductive and Sexual Health and Rights**

**Goal:** Empower women to make informed choices about their bodies, health, and family planning.

- **Indicator 5.6.1:** Measures how many women (15–49 years) can independently decide on sexual relations, contraceptive use, and healthcare.

- **Indicator 5.6.2:** Tracks existence of national laws and regulations guaranteeing access to reproductive health care and education.

- **Purpose:** Monitors policy commitments ensuring bodily autonomy, maternal health, and reproductive rights.

**Target 5.a — Reform Laws for Equal Economic Resources and Ownership**

**Goal:** Guarantee women's equal rights to land, property, inheritance, and financial resources.

- **Indicator 5.a.1:** Evaluates the percentage of women with ownership or secure rights over agricultural land, by tenure type.

- **Indicator 5.a.2:** Reviews countries' legal frameworks that formally recognize women's equal property rights.

- **Purpose:** Supports reforms for economic independence and inclusion of women in land and resource ownership.

**Target 5.b — Enhance Use of Enabling Technology for Empowerment**

**Goal:** Close the digital gender gap and promote women's access to modern technology.

- **Indicator 5.b.1:** Measures the proportion of women and men owning a mobile phone, a basic ICT access indicator.

- **Purpose:** Ensures women can use digital tools for education, entrepreneurship, financial inclusion, and civic participation.

**Target 5.c — Adopt and Enforce Gender Equality Policies and Legislation**

**Goal:** Build strong institutional systems and legal mechanisms for gender equality.

- **Indicator 5.c.1:** Tracks countries with mechanisms to monitor and publicly report how much government spending supports gender equality and women's empowerment.

- **Purpose:** Promotes accountability, transparency, and measurable progress toward gender-responsive governance.

**Summary Insight**

- SDG-5 aims to ensure that women and girls enjoy equal rights, safety, opportunities, and access across every sphere—legal, social, political, and economic.

- The targets describe the goals (policy direction), while the indicators provide measurable evidence of progress through data collection, legislation tracking, and periodic global reporting.

- Together, these metrics guide governments, HEIs, and civil society in building inclusive institutions that empower women and uphold human rights worldwide.

**3. UNESCO Policy Recommendations on SDG-5: Achieving Gender Equality and Empowering All Women and Girls**

**1. Support the Education of Girls and Women**

**Key Focus:**

- Ensure that every girl and woman has equal access to quality education at all levels — primary, secondary, tertiary, and vocational.

- Promote women's empowerment through inclusion in science, media, and culture, recognizing their critical role in sustainable development.

- Strengthen educational programs that combat all forms of violence, discrimination, and harassment against women and girls within educational institutions and society.

**Implementation Strategy:**

- Partner with governments, NGOs, and international organizations to develop gender-sensitive curricula and safe learning environments.

- Increase female literacy rates and improve retention in schools, particularly in rural and marginalized communities.

- Integrate gender awareness and equality education across all subjects and teacher training programs.

**2. Promote Global Partnerships for Educating Girls and Combating Gender-Based Violence**

**Key Focus:**

- Build international alliances and partnerships to support the education of girls and women, especially in developing nations.

- Address gender-based violence (GBV) through education, advocacy, and legal awareness.

- Leverage UNESCO's convening power to mobilize resources for inclusive education and women's rights initiatives.

**Implementation Strategy:**

- Encourage cross-border collaborations between ministries of education, UN agencies, and civil society groups.

- Launch awareness campaigns and community programs on the importance of girls' education and the dangers of GBV.

- Support policy reforms that ensure safe access to education, including protection against child marriage, harassment, and abuse.

**3. Increase Women's Participation in Science, Technology, and Innovation (STI)**

**Key Focus:**

- Strengthen women's participation in STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) through targeted initiatives like SAGA – STEM and Gender Advancement.

- Address gender disparities in research, academia, and scientific leadership roles.

- Encourage equal representation in research institutions, innovation hubs, and technology enterprises.

**Implementation Strategy:**

- Provide scholarships, fellowships, and mentorship programs for women pursuing STEM fields.

- Encourage gender-responsive research policies and institutional frameworks that promote diversity in science.

- Highlight the achievements of women scientists and innovators as role models for younger generations.

**4. Enhance Gender Equality Across Scientific, Cultural, and Environmental Domains**

**Key Focus:**

- Promote gender balance in ocean science, disaster risk reduction, and climate change initiatives.

- Ensure women's involvement in environmental governance, marine policy, and ecological restoration projects.

- Support women's participation in cultural life, heritage management, and the media.

**Implementation Strategy:**

- Increase funding and training opportunities for women researchers in environmental and ocean sciences.

- Involve women in climate change negotiations and leadership roles at both local and international levels.

- Promote inclusive cultural policies that empower women artists, journalists, and media professionals.

**5. Support Women's Empowerment and Gender Equality through Capacity Building and Policy Frameworks**

**Key Focus:**

- Strengthen capacity-building, research, and policy advocacy to promote gender equality in all sectors.

- Provide technical and policy support to member states to integrate gender perspectives into their national development strategies.

- Foster collaborative frameworks between academia, government, and international organizations for gender advancement.

**Implementation Strategy:**

- Develop gender audit tools and research indicators to measure equality outcomes.

- Encourage policy dialogue and consultation forums on gender-responsive governance.

- Mainstream gender considerations in national education and science policies through advisory support and best practice models.

**6. Generate Transformative Social Change through Culture**

**Key Focus:**

- Empower women as creators, producers, and custodians of cultural heritage.

- Strengthen women's participation in cultural industries such as arts, media, music, and literature.

- Protect women's cultural expressions and intellectual property rights within the global creative economy.

**Implementation Strategy:**

- Support women artists, craftswomen, and cultural leaders through funding and international exhibitions.

- Implement programs safeguarding intangible cultural heritage (e.g., traditional knowledge, folk art) led by women.

- Promote equal access to creative platforms and cultural institutions for women from all backgrounds.

**7. Improve Access to Information and Digital Empowerment for Women**

**Key Focus:**

- Foster gender equality in media organizations, ensuring fair representation and leadership opportunities for women.

- Eliminate gender stereotypes and biases in media content and communication channels.

- Empower women through ICT (Information and Communication Technology) skills and digital literacy.

**Implementation Strategy:**

- Launch media gender audits to evaluate representation and workplace diversity.

- Provide training in journalism, communication, and ICT for women and girls, particularly in developing countries.

- Develop programs that promote responsible media practices and balanced gender portrayal in content creation.

**8. Expected Outcomes**

- Greater female participation and leadership in education, science, media, and culture.

- Increased global collaboration and investment in gender-responsive education and research.

- Enhanced policy coherence on gender equality across environmental, cultural, and technological domains.

- Improved digital inclusion and media representation for women worldwide.

- Sustainable social transformation through empowerment, creativity, and equal opportunity.

**9. Conclusion**

- UNESCO's policy recommendations for SDG-5 emphasize that gender equality is a cross-cutting priority—essential for achieving peace, innovation, and sustainable development.

- By empowering women and girls through education, technology, science, and culture, and by eliminating systemic barriers, UNESCO envisions a future where every woman has the freedom, resources, and respect to lead and contribute fully to society.

- These actions collectively drive transformative change, advancing not only SDG-5 but the entire 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development.

**4. Leadership Parity Goals**

**Promoting Equal Representation in Leadership:**

- Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) must proactively strive for at least 50% representation of women in senior leadership positions such as Vice-Chancellors, Pro-Vice Chancellors, Deans, Directors, and Heads of Departments.

- Achieving this balance ensures that women have an equal voice in institutional decision-making, influencing governance, policy direction, and academic strategies.

- Gender-balanced leadership creates role models for younger women, fostering an institutional culture that values diversity and inclusiveness.

- Among its various quality benchmarks, gender equity has emerged as a core criterion in assessing institutional performance and social responsibility.

**Criterion VII – Institutional Values and Best Practices**

- **Under Criterion VII:** Institutional Values and Best Practices, NAAC emphasizes gender equity and inclusion as a key component of institutional evaluation.

- This criterion assesses how effectively HEIs promote gender justice, ensure safety, and foster empowerment among all genders.

- The focus extends to gender inclusivity in teaching-learning processes, administrative structures, and campus life.

**Mandatory Evidence and Documentation**

- Every institution applying for NAAC accreditation is required to upload data and verifiable supporting documents on gender equity initiatives conducted during the previous five consecutive years.

- These documents serve as evidence of institutional commitment toward equality, inclusiveness, and women's empowerment.

- NAAC uses these records to evaluate the depth, continuity, and impact of gender equity programs on the institutional community.

**2. Parameters Assessed under Gender Equity Criterion**

**Gender Equity Programs and Activities**

- Workshops, awareness sessions, and seminars on gender sensitization and women's rights.

- Campaigns and capacity-building initiatives promoting leadership, safety, and empowerment for women students and staff.

- Gender equity cells, Internal Complaints Committees (ICC), and grievance redressal systems functioning under the POSH Act (2013).

**Infrastructure and Safety Provisions**

- Availability of separate restrooms, common rooms, childcare centers, and safe hostel accommodations for women.

- Implementation of campus lighting, security systems, CCTV surveillance, and transport facilities for female students and staff.

- Presence of emergency helplines, security guards, and awareness boards around the campus.

**Gender Sensitization and Inclusivity Measures**

- Regular gender awareness and equality drives, including observance of Women's Day and gender sensitization weeks.

- Inclusion of gender perspectives in curriculum design, research projects, and extension activities.

- Initiatives for transgender inclusion and non-discrimination policies within institutional frameworks.

**Statistical and Qualitative Data**

- Gender-wise data on student enrollment, faculty representation, promotion rates, and leadership roles.

- Documentation of mentorship programs, scholarships, and awards supporting women's academic and professional growth.

- Reports of outreach programs involving rural or marginalized women, promoting inclusivity and social equity.

**3. Evaluation and Grading Process**

**Five-Year Assessment Period**

- NAAC considers gender equity programs implemented continuously for at least five years as part of its evaluation cycle.

- The continuity of efforts demonstrates an institution's sustained commitment to gender inclusion, rather than ad-hoc or one-time initiatives.

**Quantitative and Qualitative Metrics (QnM and QlM)**

- **Quantitative Metrics (QnM):** Include measurable data such as the number of women faculty, students, and gender-based initiatives conducted.

- **Qualitative Metrics (QlM):** Assess the qualitative impact of policies, cultural transformation, and long-term institutional practices promoting gender justice.

**Supporting Documentation Required**

- Gender equity policy documents and annual activity reports.

- Such action plans should be legally binding frameworks requiring institutions to:

- Set measurable targets for women's representation in academia and administration.

- Establish monitoring mechanisms for equity programs and inclusion practices.

- These plans also help integrate gender perspectives into strategic planning, budgeting, research evaluation, and recruitment policies.

**4. Challenges and Weak Implementation**

- Despite regulatory frameworks and national policies promoting gender equality, implementation across HEIs remains poor and inconsistent.

Common challenges include:

- Limited awareness and capacity among staff to operationalize GEAPs.

- **Capacity Building:** Establish gender resource centers and training programs to help HEIs design, execute, and monitor feminist strategies effectively.

- **Financial Incentives:** Introduce grants or incentives for institutions that demonstrate excellence in gender mainstreaming, diversity hiring, and women-led research.

HEIs must evolve from being passive supporters to active enablers of gender justice, ensuring that equality is embedded in their governance, curriculum, and institutional culture.

**27. Women's Leadership in Higher Education: Global and Indian Perspectives**

- According to the Times Higher Education (THE) World University Rankings 2021, only 20% of the top 200 universities worldwide are led by women.

- This data reveals a persistent gender gap in higher education leadership, reflecting underlying structural and cultural biases within academic systems.

- While women now constitute a significant portion of students and faculty globally, their transition to senior administrative roles—such as Vice-Chancellor, Dean, or Director—remains limited due to entrenched institutional hierarchies.

**2. Barriers to Women's Leadership in Higher Education**

- **Structural Barriers:** Gender bias in recruitment and promotion, lack of transparency in leadership selection, and minimal institutional support hinder women's upward mobility.

- **Cultural Barriers:** Patriarchal attitudes and gender stereotypes often frame leadership as a male domain, discouraging women from aspiring to top roles.

- **Work-Life Balance:** The dual burden of professional responsibilities and family duties restricts women's ability to commit to administrative positions.

- **Networking and Mentorship Gaps:** Limited access to professional networks and mentorship programs results in fewer women being groomed for senior positions.

- **Perception of Leadership:** Women leaders often face the “double bind”—expected to display authority while being judged for not fitting traditional gender norms.

**3. The Indian Scenario: Underrepresentation in Leadership**

- In India, the statistics on women's leadership in higher education present a more concerning picture compared to global trends.

- As per recent data, less than 7% of Vice Chancellors in Indian universities are women.

- Moreover, this representation is inflated by the fact that most of these appointments are confined to women-only universities, where the Vice-Chancellor position is mandated by policy to be held by a woman.

- Across co-educational and public universities, women's presence in senior roles such as Registrars, Deans, and Directors remains disproportionately low.

- Despite the large number of women graduates and research scholars emerging from Indian HEIs, leadership opportunities remain largely inaccessible to them.

**4. Factors Contributing to Limited Leadership Opportunities in India**

- **Institutional Patriarchy:** Leadership recruitment processes in many HEIs continue to favor male candidates, reflecting deep-seated patriarchal biases.

- **Lack of Policy Enforcement:** Although frameworks like UGC's Gender Equity Guidelines and NAAC's Criterion VII promote inclusivity, enforcement remains weak.

- **Insufficient Mentorship and Capacity Building:** The absence of structured leadership development programs for women faculty limits career progression.

- **Social Norms and Gender Roles:** Traditional expectations around marriage and caregiving often discourage women from pursuing leadership careers that require geographic mobility or long hours.

- **Lack of Role Models:** Few visible women leaders in academia result in fewer aspirational pathways for younger women academics.

**5. The Way Forward: Strengthening Women's Leadership in HEIs**

- **Policy Mandates:** The Ministry of Education and UGC should set minimum representation quotas for women in leadership and administrative positions across all HEIs.

- **Leadership Training Programs:** Establish Women Leadership Academies under UGC and AICTE to train women faculty in management, policy-making, and governance.

- **Mentorship Networks:** Create national and institutional mentorship platforms connecting senior women leaders with emerging female academics.

- **Transparent Recruitment Processes:** Mandate gender-balanced selection committees and objective evaluation parameters for appointments to leadership roles.

- **Workplace Flexibility:** Encourage flexible work hours, research sabbaticals, and family-friendly policies to support women in leadership pipelines.

- **Recognition and Incentives:** Introduce national awards and institutional incentives recognizing excellence in women's academic leadership and administration.

- **Cultural Transformation:** Promote gender sensitization among faculty and governing bodies to dismantle stereotypes that associate leadership with masculinity.

**6. Global Best Practices for Inspiration**

- Many universities worldwide have implemented Gender Equality Action Plans (GEAPs) and Athena SWAN Charters to monitor and promote women's advancement in academia.

- Scandinavian and European models demonstrate the effectiveness of quota-based representation, leadership fellowships, and mentorship programs for women in education governance.

- Indian HEIs can adapt these frameworks to develop institution-specific action plans aligned with the NEP 2020 and SDG-5 objectives.

**7. Conclusion**

The underrepresentation of women in leadership across HEIs is a global and national challenge that undermines the principles of equality and inclusivity in education.

While women increasingly dominate the academic workforce, their marginal presence in leadership exposes a structural imbalance of power and opportunity.

HEIs must therefore go beyond policy declarations and actively implement gender-transformative leadership reforms supported by government regulation, mentorship, and accountability mechanisms.

Empowering women to lead in higher education is not only a question of fairness—it is a strategic imperative for institutional innovation, social justice, and sustainable development aligned with SDG-5 (Gender Equality) and SDG-4 (Quality Education).
