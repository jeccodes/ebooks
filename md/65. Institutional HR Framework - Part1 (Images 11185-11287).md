**ABSTRACT**

The book Institutional HR Framework: Service Rules, Conduct, Leave Policies, and Administrative Governance presents a comprehensive and integrated Human Resource governance structure designed specifically for Higher Education Institutions. It brings together all essential HR components—service rules, employee conduct standards, leave regulations, disciplinary mechanisms, and administrative governance—into a single, cohesive framework. The document outlines clear guidelines on recruitment, appointments, probation, confirmation, seniority, promotions, career advancement, and performance appraisal, ensuring fairness, transparency, and accountability across both teaching and non-teaching staff. It details the expected professional behaviour, ethical standards, conflict-of-interest norms, and discipline applicable to employees, thereby strengthening the institutional work culture. Further, the framework provides a complete leave administration system, defining eligibility, types of leave, procedural norms, and conditions for sanction to ensure uniformity in practice. Administrative governance structures—such as the powers of the Board of Management, Chairman, Appointing Authority, Competent Authority, and Disciplinary Authorities—are clearly explained to establish operational clarity. The book also elaborates on disciplinary rules, penalties, suspension procedures, inquiry processes, and the rights of employees to appeal as per principles of natural justice. In addition, modern HR dimensions such as employee welfare, research incentives, professional development, intellectual property rights, and POSH compliance are incorporated to align the institution with contemporary higher education standards. Overall, this framework serves as a foundational governance document that enhances institutional efficiency, regulatory compliance, ethical functioning, and the creation of a safe, inclusive, and professionally enriching environment for all employees.

**1. Introduction**

T*Institutional HR Framework: Service Rules, Conduct, Leave Policies, and Administrative Governance*, is developed to serve as a comprehensive reference manual that codifies these essential elements into an organized and transparent system. It provides a unified structure that supports institutional governance, ensures procedural consistency, and aligns with regulatory expectations set by statutory bodies such as the University Grants Commission (UGC), All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE), National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC), and the National Board of Accreditation (NBA).

In higher education, the role of HR governance goes beyond basic administrative compliance. It influences the institutional climate, promotes a culture of accountability, and enhances employee satisfaction and productivity. As universities grow in scale and complexity, managing faculty, administrative staff, and technical personnel requires clearly defined rules, streamlined processes, and a well-structured service framework. This book provides a comprehensive consolidation of all such rules and policies, making them accessible, consistent, and applicable to all teaching and non-teaching employees.

The Service Rules section outlines the fundamental norms governing appointments, recruitment methods, probation, confirmation, seniority, promotions, increment regulations, and retirement. It clearly distinguishes between regular, contractual, temporary, and outsourced modes of employment to eliminate ambiguity and ensure fair treatment. These rules help define the lifecycle of an employee within the institution—from appointment to retirement—while ensuring that procedural fairness, transparency, and objectivity are maintained at every stage. By establishing uniform standards, the institution reinforces trust and credibility in its HR practices.

The Conduct Rules provide a detailed framework for professional behaviour, ethical expectations, discipline, and integrity. They guide employees on acceptable conduct, responsibilities towards students, peers, and the institution, and adherence to institutional values. The conduct guidelines cover a wide range of areas, including attendance, respect for authority, conflict of interest, prohibition of private tuition, academic integrity, responsible use of institutional resources, political neutrality, and professional ethics. The inclusion of these rules highlights the institution’s commitment to maintaining a respectful, ethical, and dignified working environment essential for academic excellence.

Leave administration is an integral aspect of institutional HR management, as it ensures employee well-being, work-life balance, and efficient workforce planning. This book provides comprehensive rules for various types of leave, including Casual Leave, Earned Leave, Half Pay Leave, Maternity Leave, Duty Leave, Vacation Rules, Leave Without Pay, and Special Leave categories. The leave policy clearly defines eligibility, conditions, limitations, approval mechanisms, and the responsibilities of employees and sanctioning authorities. By offering clarity on leave entitlements and procedures, the framework minimizes ambiguity and ensures uniformity in decision-making across all departments.

Administrative Governance forms the backbone of institutional functioning. The book outlines the roles, powers, and responsibilities of the Board of Management (BoM), Chairman, Appointing Authority, Competent Authority, Disciplinary Authority, and Appellate Authority. It describes the institutional hierarchy, channels of communication, delegation of powers, and mechanisms for policy implementation. By clearly defining administrative authority, the framework ensures efficient decision-making, reduces procedural delays, and provides accountability at all levels. The authority structure, as detailed in this document, reinforces institutional order and governance discipline necessary for smooth functioning.

Another significant component of this framework is the set of Disciplinary Rules. They specify what constitutes minor and major misconduct, procedures for conducting inquiries, penalties that may be imposed, and the rights of employees to defend themselves. The disciplinary framework emphasizes fairness, principles of natural justice, and due process. The aim is not punitive action alone, but maintaining institutional discipline, safeguarding academic integrity, and ensuring responsible behaviour. Suspension rules, subsistence allowance provisions, appeal mechanisms, and reinstatement guidelines further add to the completeness of the disciplinary governance system.

In addition to service, conduct, and leave rules, the book incorporates modern HR dimensions essential for contemporary higher education. Policies related to employee welfare, health and well-being, training and professional development, performance appraisal, research incentives, consultancy guidelines, sabbatical leave, intellectual property rights (IPR), entrepreneurship support, start-up incubation, and industry collaborations are included to enhance institutional competitiveness and employee growth. These sections promote a culture of continuous learning, innovation, and professional advancement, in alignment with global trends in higher education HR practices.

A critical component integrated into this HR framework is the Anti-Sexual Harassment and Gender Sensitization Policy (POSH). Ensuring a safe and inclusive work environment is fundamental to institutional excellence. The inclusion of POSH compliance guidelines demonstrates the institution’s commitment to safeguarding dignity, preventing sexual harassment, and promoting gender equity. It outlines the role of the Internal Complaints Committee (ICC), complaint procedures, inquiry mechanisms, and support systems in compliance with statutory obligations under the POSH Act, 2013.

This book also addresses grievance redressal mechanisms that enable employees to seek remedies for workplace issues through proper channels. Transparent and efficient grievance procedures contribute to employee satisfaction and prevent escalation of conflicts. The structured system ensures that employees are heard, concerns are reviewed impartially, and suitable resolutions are provided promptly.

Overall, the *Institutional HR Framework* serves as a foundational governance document aimed at enhancing transparency, accountability, ethical conduct, and administrative efficiency across the University. It reflects the institution’s commitment to a structured HR ecosystem that values professionalism, fairness, employee welfare, and regulatory compliance. This book not only codifies rules and responsibilities but also establishes a culture of disciplined governance and institutional excellence. By harmonizing service policies with modern HR practices, it equips both employees and administrators with the clarity, structure, and guidance required for effective institutional functioning. It stands as a comprehensive reference that will continue to support the University’s mission to foster a collaborative, respectful, and academically vibrant environment.

**2. Institutional HR Framework**

**1. Concept and Purpose of an Institutional HR Framework**

- A comprehensive set of policies, rules, procedures, and governance mechanisms that manage the entire employee lifecycle within a University or Higher Education Institution (HEI).

- Ensures systematic, transparent, and compliant HR operations across teaching, non-teaching, technical, administrative, and outsourced staff.

- Aligns institutional HR practices with statutory guidelines issued by UGC, AICTE, NAAC, NBA, MHRD, POSH Act, EPF Regulations, and Labour Laws.

- Creates a unified structure that promotes institutional discipline, professionalism, accountability, and employee satisfaction.

- Supports the institution’s strategic goals, academic excellence, and research output by ensuring a capable and motivated workforce.

**2. Core Components of the HR Framework**

**A. Service Rules**

- Policies governing appointments, including regular, contractual, temporary, ad-hoc, and deputation-based employment.

- Clear norms for eligibility, recruitment procedures, selection committees, and approvals.

- Rules for probation, confirmation, extension of probation, and termination.

- Definition of seniority, merit ranking, and inter-se position within a cadre.

- Guidelines for promotion, career progression, and internal mobility.

- Norms for increments, including performance-linked increments and withholding of increment.

- Rules for retirement, voluntary retirement (VRS), re-employment, and superannuation.

**B. Conduct & Ethics Rules**

- Standards of professional behaviour, integrity, discipline, and decorum expected of all employees.

- Confidentiality obligations regarding University data, internal decisions, and official information.

- Restrictions on political activity, media interaction, private tuition, external employment, and conflict of interest.

- Rules addressing academic integrity, dignity of the profession, proper interaction with students, colleagues, and superiors.

- Prohibition of misconduct including insubordination, negligence, unauthorized absence, harassment, discrimination, and misuse of University property.

- Guidelines on dress code, communication etiquette, and responsible use of institutional resources.

**C. Leave Policies**

- Full classification of leave types: Casual Leave, Earned Leave, Half Pay Leave, Maternity Leave, Paternity Leave, Medical Leave, Special Leave, Duty Leave, Vacation Leave, Leave Without Pay, Extraordinary Leave, etc.

- Clear rules for eligibility, accumulation, encashment, lapsing, and sanctioning authority.

- Procedures for applying for leave, availing vacation, combining leave, and returning to duty.

- Norms for medical certificates, joining reports, and consequences for overstaying sanctioned leave.

- Specific rules for faculty vacation, leave during notice period, and leave restrictions for probationers.

**D. Administrative Governance**

- Roles, powers, and functions of:

- Board of Management (BoM)

- Chairman

- Vice Chancellor

- Registrar

- Principals/Directors of Institutions

- Head of the Department (HOD)

- Competent Authority

- Appointing Authority

- Disciplinary Authority

- Appellate Authority

- Delegation of financial and administrative powers.

- Approval mechanisms for policies, service matters, and disciplinary cases.

- Reporting hierarchy, communication channels, and administrative accountability.

**E. Disciplinary Procedures**

- Classification of minor and major penalties, including censure, withholding increments, suspension, reduction in rank, compulsory retirement, removal, and dismissal.

- Procedure for conducting domestic enquiries following principles of natural justice.

- Rules governing suspension, subsistence allowance, and review of suspension.

- Mechanisms for appeals, reviews, and reinstatement after inquiry.

- Handling of criminal cases, arrest, and custody of employees.

**3. Advanced HR Policies Integrated into the Framework**

**A. Professional Development & Training**

- Structured programs for faculty development, skill enhancement, and capacity building.

- Policies for workshops, FDPs, refresher courses, conferences, and training programs.

- Support for higher education, qualification improvement, and certification.

**B. Research and Consultancy**

- Policies for research incentives, publication rewards, patent filing support, and grants.

- Guidelines for industry consultancy, testing services, revenue sharing, and external funding.

- Ethical and procedural guidelines for research integrity.

**C. Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)**

- Ownership, licensing, revenue sharing, and filing procedures for patents, copyrights, and commercialization.

- Roles of IPR cell and responsibilities of inventors.

**D. Entrepreneurial & Start-up Support**

- Institutional provisions for entrepreneurship incubation, start-up policies, faculty start-up participation, and innovation support.

- Guidelines for conflict of interest, disclosure norms, and time commitment.

**E. Employee Welfare & Well-being**

- Medical reimbursement, insurance, PF benefits, gratuity, LTC, and leave encashment.

- Policies for mental health support, counselling, and safe workplace environment.

- Facilities like housing, transport, childcare support, staff welfare activities.

**F. Gender Sensitization & POSH Compliance**

- Complete adherence to The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013.

- Formation and role of Internal Complaints Committee (ICC).

- Awareness, sensitization programs, safeguarding dignity of all employees.

**4. Importance and Impact of the Institutional HR Framework**

- Creates uniform HR practices, ensuring consistency across departments and campuses.

- Enhances trust, accountability, and employee morale through clarity and fairness.

- Strengthens institutional governance and regulatory compliance.

- Helps achieve NAAC, NBA, QS, and NIRF benchmarks under Governance, Human Resource Management, and Administrative Efficiency criteria.

- Ensures a safe, inclusive, ethical, and professionally conducive environment for both faculty and staff.

- Supports long-term institutional growth through clear policy direction, workforce stability, and well-defined responsibilities.

**7. Definition of “Duty”**

**Purpose**

- To clearly define the circumstances under which an employee is considered to be “on duty” for the purposes of service continuity, pay, leave accrual, and eligibility for institutional benefits.

- This definition ensures uniformity in the interpretation of “duty” across all departments and staff categories within the University.

A Person is Said to be “On Duty” Under the Following Circumstances:

**a. Performing Official Duties:**

- When the employee is performing the duties of a post to which he/she has been regularly appointed or is undergoing probation, instruction, or training prescribed for that post.

- Provided that the period of such probation, instruction, or training is followed by confirmation in the said post.

- This includes:

<!-- -->

- Periods spent in official training programs.

- Institutional workshops or induction sessions approved by the competent authority.

**b. Authorized Absence from Duty:**

- When the employee is absent from duty on authorized holidays or is on Casual Leave in accordance with the prescribed Leave Rules, provided that he/she was on duty immediately before and after such leave.

- Such authorized absence shall be treated as “on duty” for all purposes including:

<!-- -->

- Calculation of leave accrual.

- Counting of service for increment and pension.

**c. During Vacation:**

- When the employee is absent during officially declared vacations (such as summer or winter breaks), the period shall be treated as “on duty,” provided the employee is eligible for vacation under the University’s Leave Rules.

**d. Attendance at Academic or Professional Events:**

- When the employee is attending a conference, seminar, workshop, or meeting of learned societies as a nominee or delegate of the University.

- Such absence shall be treated as “on duty” when the employee is:

<!-- -->

- Officially deputed or sponsored by the University, or

- Authorized by the competent authority to represent the institution.

**e. Assigned Work Outside Headquarters:**

- When the employee is absent from headquarters but attending to work not connected with his/her routine duties, specifically assigned by the competent authority.

- Examples include:

<!-- -->

- Participation in university inspections, accreditation, or committee duties.

- Conduct of official university examinations or evaluation work.

- Representation of the University in government or inter-institutional programs.

**Interpretation**

- Any period recognized as “on duty” shall be counted as active service for all administrative purposes including:

<!-- -->

- Leave accrual, service continuity, increment, and pension benefits.

<!-- -->

- However, any absence not covered under the above clauses or not duly authorized shall not be treated as duty and may result in deduction of leave or pay.

Sanctioning and Certification

- The competent authority (Head of Department, Registrar, or Vice-Chancellor) shall issue the duty certification in all relevant cases, especially for deputations or external assignments.

- A Duty Certificate must be enclosed with the leave or travel record for inclusion in the employee’s service file.

**8. Service Status Terminology: Lien and Officiating**

**a. Lien**

Definition:

- “Lien” refers to the title of an employee to hold a permanent post in the University substantively—that is, after successful completion of probation and confirmation—either:

<!-- -->

- Immediately, or

- Upon termination of a period or periods of absence (such as deputation, study leave, or other sanctioned leave).

Explanation:

- The concept of lien ensures that a confirmed employee retains a permanent right to the post they hold, even if they are temporarily appointed, deputed, or transferred elsewhere.

- During the period of absence, the employee’s substantive appointment remains secured and they are eligible to resume duties in the same post upon return.

Key Principles:

1\. Lien can only be held on one post at a time.

2\. A lien may be suspended when the employee is:

- On deputation,

- Serving under another organization, or

- Occupying a tenure or temporary post outside their substantive appointment.

3\. The employee shall resume their lien upon return to their parent post unless otherwise transferred or promoted permanently.

4\. The right of lien does not apply to employees on purely contractual, ad-hoc, or probationary appointments.

5\. Lien may be terminated if the employee:

- Resigns from the post,

- Is absorbed permanently elsewhere, or

- Fails to report back after completion of approved leave or deputation period.

Institutional Implications:

- Lien guarantees job security and forms the basis of continuity of service for leave, pension, increment, and seniority calculations.

**b. Officiating**

Definition:

- An employee of the University is said to officiate in a higher post when he/she performs the duties of that post temporarily, while another person holds a lien on it.

- Officiating appointments are made at the discretion of the appointing authority, either:

<!-- -->

- To fill a temporary vacancy (e.g., due to leave, deputation, or suspension of the regular incumbent), or

- To test the employee’s ability to perform higher responsibilities before regular promotion.

Rules Governing Officiating Appointments:

1\. The appointing authority (Board of Management / Vice-Chancellor) may assign officiating charge when:

- A post is temporarily vacant, or

- No other employee holds a lien on the said post.

2\. Officiating appointments shall be purely temporary and do not confer any right of permanency or automatic promotion.

3\. The employee shall be entitled to:

- Additional pay or allowance (if admissible under University rules) during the officiating period.

- Full pay protection for the substantive post they hold.

4\. The officiating arrangement ceases automatically:

- When the regular incumbent returns, or

- When the vacancy is filled by regular selection.

5\. The period spent on officiating duty shall count towards experience and service benefits if followed by regular appointment or promotion.

Illustrative Examples:

- A senior lecturer performing duties of an Associate Professor while the latter is on deputation is said to be officiating in the higher post.

- A section officer temporarily managing the work of an Assistant Registrar during leave is also considered to be officiating.

**9. Definitions Relating to Pay Structure**

**a. “Pay”**

- “Pay” refers to the amount drawn monthly by an employee of the University as compensation for holding a post, and includes the following components:

I. Basic Pay:

- The pay, other than special pay or personal pay, which has been sanctioned for a post held by the employee either:

<!-- -->

- Substantively (on a permanent basis), or

- In an officiating capacity (temporarily performing duties of another post), or

- By virtue of position in a cadre (as per the approved pay scale).

II\. Special Pay and Personal Pay:

- Includes special pay (for additional duties or responsibilities) and personal pay (for protection or merit).

III\. Other Emoluments:

- Any other emoluments or monetary benefits that may be specifically classified as “Pay” by the Board of Management (BoM).

→ Note: “Pay” forms the basis for calculating allowances, leave salary, pension, gratuity, and other service benefits.

**b. “Personal Pay”**

- “Personal Pay” means an additional amount of pay granted to an employee of the University:

I. To save the employee from loss of substantive pay in respect of a permanent post (other than a tenure post) due to:

- A revision of pay, or

- Any reduction in substantive pay not imposed as a disciplinary measure.

II\. In exceptional circumstances, based on personal considerations such as outstanding merit, qualification, or institutional recognition.

→ Nature of Personal Pay:

- It is personal to the employee and does not transfer with the post.

- It ceases when the circumstances that justified it no longer exist.

- It is not automatically inheritable by successors to the post.

**c. “Special Pay”**

- “Special Pay” is an additional amount, of the nature of pay, granted in recognition of special duties or responsibilities attached to a post or to an employee.

I. Granted in consideration of the specially arduous nature of duties, such as those involving extra workload, risk, or travel.

II\. Granted for a specific addition to the work or responsibility, such as:

- Acting as Head of Department or Coordinator in addition to regular duties.

- Supervising special projects, academic events, or research initiatives.

- Handling administrative or financial responsibilities beyond normal duties.

→ Nature of Special Pay:

- It is linked to the post or responsibility and ceases once the duty or assignment ends.

- The quantum of special pay shall be sanctioned by the Board of Management or other competent authority.

**d. “Substantive Pay”**

- “Substantive Pay” refers to the basic pay (excluding special or personal pay) drawn by an employee in a post held in a permanent capacity.

- It represents the core salary attached to a confirmed appointment, following probation and confirmation.

→ Key Points:

- Substantive pay is the foundation for calculating increments, pension, and retirement benefits.

- It ensures continuity of service and security in the substantive post even if the employee is temporarily officiating elsewhere.

**e. “Time Scale of Pay”**

- “Time Scale of Pay” means the structured pay range that rises by periodical increments from a minimum to a maximum, as prescribed in the University’s pay rules.

- It represents a graded salary system within which an employee progresses based on length of service and performance.

→ Features:

1\. Increment intervals are usually annual, subject to satisfactory performance.

2\. The time scale may vary for different cadres (e.g., Teaching, Non-Teaching, Technical).

3\. The minimum represents the starting pay, and the maximum the limit of progression within the grade.

4\. Employees are eligible for increment only after satisfactory service during the prescribed period.

5\. Termination during Probation:

- Services of a probationer may be terminated without notice or compensation, if performance, conduct, or integrity is found unsatisfactory, as per service rules.

**12. Definition of “Probationer”**

**a. Meaning**

- A “Probationer” is an employee who has not yet completed the prescribed period of probation as determined by the Board of Management (BoM) or specified in their appointment order.

- During this period, the employee is considered to be under evaluation for suitability, efficiency, integrity, and fitness for confirmation in the post.

**b. Characteristics of a Probationer**

1\. Temporary Evaluation Status:

- A probationer holds the post on a trial basis and does not yet acquire the rights and privileges of a confirmed employee.

2\. Performance Assessment:

- The probationer’s work performance, conduct, punctuality, and adherence to institutional regulations are reviewed periodically by the supervising authority or department head.

3\. No Automatic Confirmation:

- Mere completion of the probation period does not automatically confer confirmation.

- Confirmation requires a formal order issued by the Appointing Authority, based on satisfactory reports and recommendations.

4\. Termination During Probation:

- Services of a probationer may be terminated at any time during the probation period without assigning any reason and without entitlement to compensation.

- Such termination is considered discharge from service, not a disciplinary penalty.

5\. Rights During Probation:

- Probationers are entitled to:

- Pay and allowances applicable to the post.

- Leave and benefits as admissible under probationary service rules.

- However, they are not entitled to permanent benefits such as long-term leave encashment, promotion, or lien.

6\. Extension of Probation:

- The period may be extended if performance is found unsatisfactory.

- The extension order must be formally communicated in writing before the expiry of the initial probation.

**c. Confirmation of Probationer**

- Upon successful completion of the probation period and receipt of satisfactory performance reports, the probationer is confirmed in the post.

- The confirmation order must be issued in writing by the competent authority (Registrar or Vice-Chancellor).

**13. Definition of “Subsistence Grant”**

**a. Meaning**

- A “Subsistence Grant” refers to a monthly monetary allowance sanctioned to an employee of the University who has been placed under suspension and, therefore, is not entitled to receive regular pay or leave salary during the period of suspension.

- It serves as a temporary financial support to enable the suspended employee to maintain himself/herself and dependents during the pendency of disciplinary proceedings.

**b. Purpose**

- The grant ensures that the employee under suspension:

<!-- -->

- Is not left without any means of livelihood.

- Is able to meet minimal living expenses until the case is resolved.

- Can cooperate with the investigation without undue hardship.

**c. Conditions for Grant of Subsistence Allowance**

1\. Eligibility:

- Admissible only to employees under formal suspension orders issued by the competent authority.

- Not applicable to those on unauthorized absence, deemed suspension, or voluntary non-attendance.

2\. Amount Payable:

- The rate of Subsistence Grant shall be determined as per service regulations or government norms, typically:

- 50% of basic pay + admissible allowances for the first 90 days of suspension.

- May be increased to 75% if the delay in disciplinary proceedings is not attributable to the employee.

- May be reduced to 25% if the delay is caused by the employee’s actions or non-cooperation.

3\. Other Allowances:

- Along with the subsistence grant, the employee may receive:

- Dearness Allowance (DA) on the admissible portion of pay.

- No entitlement to leave salary, increments, or other benefits during the suspension period.

4\. Recovery / Adjustment:

- If the employee is later exonerated or reinstated without penalty, the difference between the subsistence grant and full pay for the suspension period shall be paid retrospectively as arrears.

- If the charges are proved and a penalty is imposed, the subsistence allowance already paid shall be treated as final settlement for that period.

**d. Duration and Review**

- The subsistence grant shall be reviewed periodically, generally every 90 days, by the disciplinary authority, to:

<!-- -->

- Assess continuation or modification of the amount.

- Ensure that the employee’s case is progressing without undue delay.

**e. Administrative Control**

- The Registrar shall maintain a record of all suspended employees receiving subsistence grants, including:

<!-- -->

- Duration of suspension.

- Amount paid.

- Periodic review notes.

<!-- -->

- The Vice-Chancellor or Appointing Authority shall approve any revision or discontinuance of the grant.

**15. Definition of “Service”**

**a. Meaning**

- The term “Service” refers to the period of employment under the University or Higher Education Institution (HEI), unless otherwise specified in the context of deputation, collaboration, or external assignment.

- It includes all periods during which an employee holds a post, performs duties, or is treated as being on duty under the University’s administrative control and payroll.

**b. Scope of Service**

“Service” shall include the following periods, subject to confirmation by the competent authority:

1\. Active Service:

- The period during which an employee is actively performing the duties of the post to which he/she is appointed.

2\. Probationary Service:

- The period of probation preceding confirmation in a post, provided the employee is subsequently confirmed in the same or higher position.

3\. Approved Leave Periods:

- Periods of authorized leave (Earned Leave, Half Pay Leave, Medical Leave, Maternity Leave, etc.) which are countable for service continuity under the Leave Rules.

4\. Training and Deputation:

- Periods spent on training, study leave, or deputation officially sanctioned by the University.

5\. Suspension (When Treated as Duty):

- Period of suspension, if the employee is fully exonerated and the suspension is ordered to be treated as “on duty.”

6\. Foreign or Collaborative Service:

- Service rendered under collaborative, sponsored, or inter-institutional assignments approved by the University, provided the employee retains a lien on the University post.

**c. Exclusions from Service**

**E. Contract Renewal, Disengagement, and Absorption Restrictions Policy**

**Purpose**

- To lay down the regulations governing renewal, termination (disengagement), and absorption restrictions for personnel appointed on contract, project-based, or temporary terms in the University.

- This policy ensures transparency, fairness, and compliance with the University’s administrative and financial norms while maintaining institutional flexibility in workforce management.

**I. Contract Renewal Policy**

1\. Eligibility for Renewal:

- Renewal of a contractual or project-based appointment shall be considered only if:

- The project or assignment continues to exist;

- Performance and conduct of the employee are satisfactory; and

- Funds are available for continued engagement.

2\. Performance Evaluation:

- A performance appraisal report must be submitted by the Immediate Supervisor / Principal Investigator (PI) at least one month before contract expiry.

- Evaluation shall include:

- Quality of work and punctuality.

- Attitude and cooperation with peers.

- Adherence to institutional discipline and project goals.

3\. Duration of Renewal:

- Renewal may be granted for a period not exceeding the original term or until the project’s completion, whichever is earlier.

- Normally, contracts are renewed annually or as per funding agency terms.

4\. Approval Process:

- Renewal shall be approved by the competent authority, depending on post type:

- Registrar / Dean (Research) for project staff.

- Vice-Chancellor (V.C.) for University contract staff.

- Board of Management (BoM) for high-value or senior contract appointments.

5\. Documentation:

- Each renewal shall be supported by:

- Previous appointment order.

- Performance appraisal.

- Updated contract agreement with revised tenure.

- Renewal orders must be issued before the expiry date to avoid service gaps.

**II. Disengagement (Termination) Policy**

1\. Grounds for Disengagement:

- Disengagement or early termination of a contract may occur under the following conditions:

- Completion or discontinuation of the project / assignment.

- Unsatisfactory performance or misconduct.

- Violation of service rules or breach of confidentiality.

- Financial constraints or withdrawal of funding support.

2\. Notice Period:

- Unless otherwise specified in the appointment order:

- Either party may terminate the contract with one month’s notice or one month’s pay in lieu of notice.

- In cases of misconduct or moral turpitude, the employee may be terminated without notice.

3\. Exit Formalities:

- Before disengagement, the employee shall:

- Submit No Dues Certificate (NDC) from concerned departments.

- Return all University property, documents, and equipment.

- Complete handover of records, passwords, and project materials.

4\. Final Settlement:

- The HR/Finance Section shall ensure:

- Payment of outstanding remuneration up to the last working day.

- Deduction of any recoveries as per institutional rules.

- Issue of Service Certificate for the completed period.

**III. Absorption Restrictions Policy**

1\. No Right to Regularization:

- Employees appointed on contract, temporary, daily wage, or project basis shall have no claim to regular absorption in the University’s permanent establishment.

- Their engagement is purely time-bound and co-terminus with the purpose for which they were appointed.

2\. Fresh Recruitment Requirement:

- All regular posts must be filled through open advertisement and formal selection procedures as per UGC / AICTE / University norms.

- Past service on contract may be considered for experience but not for seniority or continuity in regular cadre.

3\. Restriction on Automatic Absorption:

- Continuation in successive contracts does not constitute regularization.

- Any conversion of contract service to regular appointment shall require:

- Specific BoM approval, and

- Compliance with statutory recruitment norms.

4\. Deputation / Transfer Cases:

- In cases where contractual employees are deputed to other projects or departments, their lien or continuity remains limited to their original project or contract term.

- No claim for permanent transfer or absorption shall be entertained.

5\. Government / UGC Guidelines:

- The University shall comply with all directives of the UGC, AICTE, or State Government regarding:

- Engagement and renewal of contractual staff.

- Restriction on conversion of temporary employment into regular positions.

**IV. Administrative Control and Monitoring**

1\. HR & Establishment Section:

- Shall maintain a Contract Employee Register recording:

- Appointment date, renewal history, and performance ratings.

- Notices of termination or disengagement.

- Compliance with audit and service verification.

2\. Registrar / Dean (Research):

- Must submit quarterly reports on contract staff status to the Vice-Chancellor and BoM.

3\. Finance Department:

- Shall ensure budgetary provision and fund utilization are in compliance with sanction orders and institutional audit norms.

**F. Probation, Confirmation, and Seniority Policy**

**Purpose**

- To define the rules and procedures governing the probation, confirmation, and determination of seniority for employees of the University.

- This policy ensures that appointments are regularized only after satisfactory assessment, and seniority is maintained in a transparent and equitable manner across all cadres.

**I. Probation Policy**

**1. Definition**

- “Probation” refers to the trial period during which an employee’s suitability, conduct, efficiency, and integrity are assessed prior to confirmation in the post.

- The probation period is intended to evaluate both technical competence and behavioral adaptability within the University system.

**2. Duration of Probation**

- The Board of Management (BoM) or the Appointment Order prescribes the specific period of probation.

<!-- -->

- Teaching and Administrative Staff: Normally two years.

- Non-Teaching and Technical Staff: Normally one year.

<!-- -->

- The BoM may extend or reduce this duration based on performance and organizational requirement.

**3. Assessment During Probation**

- The Head of Department (HoD) / Immediate Supervisor shall prepare periodic performance reports assessing:

<!-- -->

- Knowledge of work and efficiency.

- Conduct, punctuality, and discipline.

- Initiative, teamwork, and sense of responsibility.

<!-- -->

- Reports are submitted every six months to the Registrar / HR Section for review and record.

**4. Extension of Probation**

- Probation may be extended if:

<!-- -->

- Performance is below expectations.

- Integrity or conduct requires further observation.

- The employee has taken long leave (other than casual leave) during probation.

<!-- -->

- Extension shall be formally communicated in writing before the expiry of the original probation period.

- The total probation period, including extensions, shall not ordinarily exceed double the prescribed duration.

**5. Termination During Probation**

- The services of a probationer may be terminated at any time during probation:

<!-- -->

- Without assigning any reason, by giving one month’s notice or one month’s salary in lieu thereof.

- Such termination shall not be treated as a penalty, but as non-confirmation in the post.

**II. Confirmation Policy**

**1. Definition**

- “Confirmation” refers to the formal recognition of an employee’s permanent appointment to a post after successful completion of probation and performance evaluation.

**2. Conditions for Confirmation**

An employee shall be confirmed in a post only if:

- The prescribed probation period has been satisfactorily completed.

- The employee’s performance appraisal reports are favorable.

- There is no disciplinary or vigilance case pending against the employee.

- The Appointing Authority approves the recommendation for confirmation.

**3. Confirmation Process**

1\. **Performance Review:** Conducted by the HoD / Registrar and forwarded to the Appointing Authority.

2\. **Recommendation:** The Evaluation Committee recommends confirmation or extension.

3\. Approval:

- Teaching & Administrative Staff: Vice-Chancellor / Board of Management.

- Non-Teaching Staff: Registrar / Vice-Chancellor (depending on level).

**4. Effect of Confirmation**

- Upon confirmation:

<!-- -->

- The employee gains eligibility for:

- Annual increments.

- Full leave and service benefits.

- Participation in promotions and selection processes.

- The employee’s lien on the post is established permanently.

**III. Seniority Policy**

**1. Definition**

- “Seniority” means the relative position of an employee in a cadre or grade determined with reference to the date of appointment, confirmation, or promotion, as per University regulations.

**2. Determination of Seniority**

1\. Direct Recruits:

- Seniority shall be determined by the date of joining duty after selection.

- When two or more candidates join on the same day, merit position in the selection panel shall determine seniority.

2\. Promoted Employees:

- Seniority shall be determined by the date of promotion to the higher post.

- When multiple promotions occur on the same date, previous lower-grade seniority shall prevail.

3\. Transfer Cases:

- Employees transferred from another institution (with BoM approval) shall take seniority after the last regular employee in the corresponding cadre, unless otherwise specified.

4\. Probationers and Temporary Appointments:

- Seniority shall accrue only after confirmation in the post.

- Temporary service before confirmation does not count for seniority unless explicitly approved.

**3. Maintenance of Seniority List**

- The Registrar / Establishment Section shall maintain an updated seniority list for each category of post (Teaching, Non-Teaching, Technical).

- The list shall be:

<!-- -->

- Reviewed annually and displayed for verification.

- Open for representation within 15 days of publication.

- Finalized after disposal of any objections.

**4. Inter-Se Seniority Between Direct Recruits and Promotees**

- When both direct recruits and promotees are appointed in the same year, their inter-se seniority shall be fixed according to the rotation of quota (e.g., 1:1 or 2:1) approved by the BoM.

**5. Loss of Seniority**

Seniority shall be lost if:

- The employee resigns or is terminated and later reappointed.

- The employee remains absent without authorization or overstays leave beyond permissible limits.

- The employee is demoted as a result of disciplinary action.

**IV. Administrative Responsibilities**

- Head of Department / Registrar:

<!-- -->

- Conducts probationary assessments and forwards reports for confirmation.

<!-- -->

- Human Resources / Establishment Section:

<!-- -->

- Maintains probation registers, confirmation records, and seniority lists.

<!-- -->

- Vice-Chancellor / BoM:

<!-- -->

- Exercises final approval authority for confirmation and resolution of seniority disputes.

**K. Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) and Patent Policy**

**Purpose**

- To establish a comprehensive framework for the creation, protection, management, and commercialization of intellectual property (IP) generated by faculty, researchers, students, and staff of the University.

- To encourage innovation, research, and entrepreneurship, ensuring that intellectual outputs are ethically managed, legally protected, and equitably shared among contributors and the University.

**I. Objectives**

- Promote and safeguard intellectual property arising from University-led research and development.

- Ensure fair and transparent ownership rights between inventors, collaborators, and the University.

- Facilitate patent filing, copyright registration, and technology transfer.

- Encourage innovation, product development, and start-up creation aligned with national policies (Startup India, National IPR Policy 2016, Atal Innovation Mission).

- Integrate IPR literacy into academic and research culture through awareness, training, and incubation support.

**II. Scope and Coverage**

- Applicable to all faculty members, researchers, students, project staff, consultants, and visiting scholars engaged in research within the University or using its resources.

- Covers all types of intellectual creations including:

<!-- -->

- Inventions, processes, and prototypes

- Software, algorithms, and databases

- Books, artistic works, designs, and teaching materials

- Plant varieties, biological materials, and trade secrets

- Trademarks, logos, and institutional branding assets

**III. Ownership of Intellectual Property**

| **Scenario**                                                 | **Ownership Rights**                                | **Remarks**                                |
|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Research conducted entirely with University resources        | University (jointly with inventor)                  | University has ownership and filing rights |
| Research conducted with external funding                     | Shared between University and funding agency        | As per MoU or project terms                |
| Research conducted without University support                | Individual inventor(s)                              | Subject to disclosure and approval         |
| Collaborative projects with other institutions or industries | Shared ownership as per MoU or NDA                  | Terms defined case-wise                    |
| Student or scholar inventions under faculty supervision      | Joint ownership (Student + Supervisor + University) | Must disclose through IPR Cell             |

**IV. Invention Disclosure and Patent Filing Process**

**1. Disclosure Procedure**

- Every inventor must submit a Confidential Invention Disclosure Form (IDF) to the IPR Cell before any public disclosure.

- The IPR Cell will evaluate:

<!-- -->

- Novelty and utility of the invention.

- Potential for patentability or commercialization.

- Funding source and ownership claims.

**2. Evaluation and Approval**

- The IPR Cell forwards the disclosure to the University IPR Evaluation Committee (UIEC) consisting of:

<!-- -->

- Dean (Research) — Chairperson

- Head, IPR Cell — Member Secretary

- Legal Officer / Patent Attorney — Member

- One Subject Expert — Member

<!-- -->

- Based on evaluation, the UIEC decides:

<!-- -->

- Whether to file a patent.

- Ownership and cost-sharing structure.

- Commercialization strategy.

**3. Filing of Patent / Copyright**

- Approved inventions are filed through registered patent agents in the name of the University and inventor(s).

- The University bears the initial cost of filing, subject to available budget.

- Inventors must not publish or present the invention publicly before filing to preserve patent rights.

**4. Maintenance and Renewal**

- The IPR Cell shall monitor renewal fees and deadlines.

- Costs for continued maintenance will be borne jointly by the University and inventor in a 70:30 ratio, unless waived by BoM.

**Q. Grievance Redressal and Disciplinary Policy**

**Purpose**

- To establish a fair, transparent, and structured system for the prevention, reporting, and resolution of grievances and disciplinary issues among faculty, staff, and students.

- This policy ensures institutional justice, ethical conduct, and accountability, aligning with UGC (Redressal of Grievances) Regulations, 2023, and other statutory frameworks applicable to Higher Education Institutions (HEIs).

**I. Objectives**

- Provide an accessible mechanism for the timely redressal of genuine grievances of employees.

- Maintain a harmonious work environment through open communication and fairness.

- Define disciplinary standards and procedures for handling misconduct, negligence, or violation of institutional policies.

- Safeguard the rights of employees and ensure decisions are based on natural justice and due process.

- Ensure compliance with NAAC, UGC, and labour law requirements on grievance handling and discipline management.

**II. Scope and Coverage**

- Applicable to all categories of faculty, administrative, technical, and support staff (permanent, probationary, contractual, or part-time).

- Covers grievances related to:

<!-- -->

- Working conditions, salary, leave, and welfare benefits.

- Service matters (promotion, appraisal, transfer, workload, etc.).

- Discrimination, harassment, or victimization.

- Misuse of authority, procedural delays, or lack of transparency.

- Workplace misconduct or policy violations.

<!-- -->

- Excludes matters pending before a court or statutory authority.

**III. Types of Grievances**

| **Category**               | **Nature of Grievance**                 | **Examples**                                   |
|----------------------------|-----------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Service-related            | Administrative or HR issues             | Pay anomalies, promotion delay, leave disputes |
| Workplace-related          | Environment or safety issues            | Overwork, harassment, lack of resources        |
| Academic-related           | Teaching or research issues             | Workload imbalance, recognition disputes       |
| Interpersonal / Behavioral | Misconduct or discrimination            | Verbal abuse, bias, victimization              |
| Policy-related             | Violation of rules or unfair procedures | Non-compliance with institutional policy       |

**IV. Grievance Redressal Mechanism**

**1. Hierarchical Framework**

| **Level** | **Authority / Committee**                          | **Scope**                               | **Timeline for Resolution** |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Level 1   | Immediate Supervisor / HoD                         | Informal grievance resolution           | Within 7 working days       |
| Level 2   | Institutional Grievance Redressal Committee (IGRC) | Formal grievance submission and hearing | Within 30 working days      |
| Level 3   | University Grievance Redressal Committee (UGRC)    | Appeal or unresolved cases              | Within 45 working days      |
| Level 4   | Board of Management (BoM)                          | Final review and decision               | Within 60 working days      |

**2. Composition of Institutional Grievance Redressal Committee (IGRC)**

| **Member**                         | **Designation**  |
|------------------------------------|------------------|

**3. Procedure for Filing Grievance**

1\. Submission:

- Grievance must be submitted in writing through email or prescribed Grievance Form (Form–GR–01).

- It must include clear details of the complaint, evidence, and relief sought.

2\. Acknowledgment:

- HR Cell acknowledges receipt within 3 working days.

3\. Investigation:

- Committee conducts fact-finding, interviews, and document review.

4\. Resolution & Communication:

- Decision communicated to the complainant within the prescribed time frame.

5\. Appeal:

- Dissatisfied complainants may appeal to the next higher authority within 15 working days of receiving the decision.

**V. Disciplinary Policy Framework**

**1. Definition of Misconduct**

Includes but not limited to:

- Willful insubordination or disobedience.

- Negligence of duty, habitual late attendance, or absenteeism.

- Theft, fraud, or dishonesty in connection with institutional property.

- Bribery, corruption, or misuse of authority.

- Violation of confidentiality or data protection norms.

- Harassment, discrimination, or abusive conduct.

- Unauthorized communication of official information.

- Violation of University Code of Conduct or policies.

**2. Classification of Misconduct**

| **Category**     | **Examples**                                         | **Nature of Penalty**                           |
|------------------|------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Minor Misconduct | Habitual lateness, negligence, misbehavior           | Warning, deduction of leave, reprimand          |
| Major Misconduct | Fraud, harassment, breach of trust, moral misconduct | Suspension, demotion, termination, legal action |

**3. Disciplinary Procedure**

1\. Complaint / Report: Misconduct reported by any supervisor or employee.

2\. Preliminary Inquiry: Conducted by HR or appointed officer to assess prima facie evidence.

3\. Charge Sheet: Formal written notice of allegations to the employee.

4\. Explanation / Defence: Employee must submit written explanation within 10 working days.

5\. Formal Inquiry: Inquiry Committee constituted (includes neutral members).

6\. Report Submission: Findings submitted to Registrar / VC within 30 days.

7\. Decision: Disciplinary action approved by Competent Authority (Registrar / VC / BoM).

8\. Right to Appeal: Employee may appeal within 15 working days to the BoM.

**S. Equal Opportunity, Diversity, and Inclusion Policy**

**Purpose**

- To promote an equitable, inclusive, and non-discriminatory environment within the University that ensures fair treatment and equal access to opportunities for all employees, students, and stakeholders.

- This policy reflects the institution's commitment to diversity, social justice, and inclusivity, in line with the Constitution of India (Articles 14–16), UGC Guidelines (2022), and NEP 2020 – Equity and Inclusion Framework.

**I. Objectives**

- Ensure equal opportunity in recruitment, training, promotion, and participation in institutional activities.

- Foster a diverse and inclusive community that values gender balance, cultural diversity, and accessibility.

- Prevent and eliminate discrimination or bias based on caste, religion, gender, disability, socioeconomic background, or ethnicity.

- Encourage the active inclusion of women, persons with disabilities (PwDs), minority groups, and economically weaker sections (EWS) in academic and administrative processes.

- Strengthen the University's alignment with SDG 5 (Gender Equality), SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities), and SDG 4 (Quality Education for All).

**II. Scope and Applicability**

- Applicable to all:

<!-- -->

- Teaching and Non-Teaching Employees (regular, contract, or temporary).

- Students, Researchers, and Visiting Faculty.

- Vendors and Partners associated with institutional operations.

<!-- -->

- Covers all institutional functions including:

<!-- -->

- Recruitment, promotion, and performance appraisal.

- Admission and academic processes.

- Training, research, and innovation participation.

- Use of facilities, welfare programs, and grievance mechanisms.

**III. Principles of Equality and Inclusion**

| **Core Principle** | **Description**                                                     |
|--------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Equity             | Fair treatment according to individual needs and circumstances.     |

| Inclusion          | Ensuring representation, voice, and belonging for all groups.       |
| Transparency       | Open and unbiased decision-making in all institutional processes.   |

**T. Appointments – Method of Recruitment**

**I. Purpose and Scope**

- To define the procedures and authorities involved in the recruitment and appointment of University personnel, ensuring transparency, merit-based selection, and adherence to statutory norms.

- This policy applies to all categories of appointments — regular, ad-hoc, temporary, contractual, and promotional, across both teaching and non-teaching cadres.

**II. General Principles of Recruitment**

- All appointments shall be made strictly based on merit, following established qualifications and eligibility norms prescribed by UGC, AICTE, State Government, and the University's Board of Management (BoM).

- Equal Opportunity and Diversity shall be maintained as per the Equal Opportunity, Diversity & Inclusion Policy.

- Every appointment must be approved by the competent authority before the candidate assumes charge.

- Recruitment to posts shall be carried out through:

1\. Open Advertisement (Regular Appointments).

2\. Invitation (Eminent Scholars / Distinguished Professionals).

3\. Promotion / Internal Selection (Eligible Staff within the University).

4\. Contractual / Ad-hoc Engagement (for limited period or special need).

**III. Teaching Faculty Recruitment**

**a. Constitution of Faculty Selection Committees**

- The constitution of the Faculty Selection Committee shall be in accordance with the University Grants Commission (UGC) Regulations, 2018 and subsequent amendments.

- The composition shall typically include:

| **Position**                                    | **Composition / Member**                                      |
|-------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Chairperson                                     | Vice-Chancellor or Nominee                                    |
| Subject Experts                                 | Two or more external experts nominated by the Vice-Chancellor |
| Dean of Faculty                                 | Member                                                        |
| Head of the Department                          | Member                                                        |
| Registrar / HRD Head                            | Member Secretary                                              |
| Representative of SC/ST/OBC/Women (if required) | Co-opted Member                                               |

- The Committee shall evaluate candidates on the basis of academic record, teaching experience, research performance, publications, and interview performance.

- The recommendations of the Selection Committee shall be placed before the Board of Management for final approval.

**IV. Faculty on Ad-Hoc and Temporary Appointments**

**a. Authority for Ad-hoc Appointments**

- The Board of Management (BoM) shall have the power to determine:

1\. Whether a post is to be filled by open advertisement, invitation, or promotion from within the University staff.

2\. The tenure, remuneration, and terms of engagement for the temporary appointment.

3\. The duration of appointment, which normally shall not exceed one academic year, unless extended by BoM.

**b. Procedure**

- Ad-hoc faculty shall be selected through a local selection panel comprising:

<!-- -->

- Vice-Chancellor / Dean (Chairperson)

- Head of the Department

- One Subject Expert from another University / Institution

- Registrar / HR Representative

<!-- -->

- The appointment order shall specify the duration, salary, and conditions of service.

**V. Contractual Appointments**

**a. Special Circumstances Clause**

- Notwithstanding any provisions under the regular recruitment rules, the Board of Management shall have the authority, in special circumstances, to appoint any person on contract for a period up to five (5) years.

- Such appointments may made:

<!-- -->

- For newly introduced academic programs.

- For industry experts, professionals, or distinguished academicians.

- For research, consultancy, or development projects.

**b. Ratification**

- All contractual appointments made under this clause shall be ratified by the Board of Management at its next meeting.

- The appointment order must clearly indicate:

<!-- -->

- Duration of the contract.

- Consolidated or scale-based pay structure.

- Renewal and termination conditions.

**VI. Method of Recruitment by Promotion / Internal Selection**

- Promotion shall be based on merit-cum-seniority, academic performance, and service record.

- Selection for higher positions (e.g., Assistant → Associate Professor) shall follow UGC Career Advancement Scheme (CAS) norms.

- Internal candidates shall be given equal opportunity to apply for higher posts where eligible.

- Promotion panels shall include:

<!-- -->

- Vice-Chancellor (Chairperson)

- Dean / Head of Department

- Two External Experts

- Registrar / HR Head

**VII. Non-Teaching and Administrative Recruitment**

- Recruitment for non-teaching positions shall be conducted by Selection Committees constituted by the Board of Management, following notified qualifications and experience criteria.

- The Committee composition shall include:

<!-- -->

- Vice-Chancellor / Registrar (Chairperson)

- Head of Concerned Department / Section

- External Expert (if required)

- HR Officer (Member Secretary)

<!-- -->

- Recruitment shall be conducted through written tests, skill assessments, and interviews, depending on the nature of the post.

**VIII. Documentation and Transparency**

- Every stage of the recruitment process must be documented and retained in official records for audit and accreditation.

- Documents include:

<!-- -->

- Notification / Advertisement Copy

- Shortlisting Report

- Selection Committee Minutes

- Approval by Competent Authority

- Offer Letter and Appointment Order

<!-- -->

- Records shall be preserved for a minimum of 10 years in compliance with NAAC and ISO 9001:2015 standards.

**X. Policy Principles**

- Recruitment shall always be transparent, merit-based, and inclusive.

- No appointment shall be made without adherence to prescribed qualifications and procedures.

- The Board of Management retains ultimate authority in approving, ratifying, or rejecting any recommendation.

- Short-term academic and administrative needs may be addressed through contractual or ad-hoc appointments, without compromising on quality and compliance.

- The University is committed to fostering academic excellence and ethical governance through a robust, compliant, and equitable recruitment system.
