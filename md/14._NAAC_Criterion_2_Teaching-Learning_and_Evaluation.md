**Synopsis**

The teaching-learning process and evaluation framework emphasizes the importance of structured academic planning, continuous assessment, and a student-centric learning environment. Institutions are encouraged to adopt innovative teaching methods such as flipped classrooms, problem-based learning, experiential case studies, and blended learning approaches to enrich student engagement. A core focus lies in the definition of Course Outcomes (COs) and Program Outcomes (POs), their systematic mapping to internal and external assessments, and the identification of learning gaps to initiate corrective actions. Transparency in academic operations—ranging from well-publicized exam schedules to prompt result communication and structured grievance redressal—forms the cornerstone of credibility and fairness.

The Student Enrolment and Profile component highlights institutional efforts to attract, admit, and retain a diverse student body, ensuring transparency, equity, and inclusiveness in admission processes. Metrics such as Enrollment Percentage (2.1.1) and Percentage of Seats Filled Against Reserved Categories (2.1.2) are carefully defined with formulas, data formats, and evidence requirements for NAAC compliance. Additionally, this section outlines mechanisms for catering to student diversity, including assessment of entry-level learning abilities, remedial and bridge courses, advanced assignments for high achievers, and dedicated support services such as counseling and financial aid.

The Teacher Profile and Quality section defines the hallmarks of a high-quality faculty, emphasizing qualifications (Ph.D., super-specialty degrees), years of teaching experience, research output, and continuous professional development. Metrics covered include the Student–Full-Time Teacher Ratio (2.2.2), Percentage of Full-Time Teachers Appointed Against Sanctioned Posts (2.4.1), Percentage of Full-Time Teachers with Ph.D./Super-Specialty Degrees (2.4.2), and Average Teaching Experience of Full-Time Teachers (2.4.3). For each metric, the guide provides calculation methods, supporting document requirements, and expected validation during the DVV (Data Validation and Verification) process.

The Examination Process and Reforms section underscores the robustness, efficiency, and transparency of evaluation practices. Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) mechanisms, use of digital platforms, and reforms in assessment strategies are detailed to ensure accountability. Key metrics such as the Average Number of Days for Result Declaration (2.5.1), Percentage of Student Complaints/Grievances about Evaluation (2.5.2), and the Status of Automation of the Examination Division (2.5.3) are explained with precise data collection methods, calculation formulas, and NAAC scoring scales.

The Student Performance and Learning Outcomes section reinforces the principles of Outcome-Based Education (OBE), where Program Outcomes (POs), Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs), and Course Outcomes (COs) are systematically mapped, assessed, and analyzed for effectiveness. Attainment levels are measured, corrective measures are implemented, and progress is tracked in line with accreditation expectations. The Pass Percentage of Students (excluding backlogs) (2.6.2) serves as a vital quality indicator, with emphasis on evidence-based reporting through certified results from the Controller of Examinations.

The Student Satisfaction Survey (SSS) is positioned as a direct reflection of teaching-learning quality and institutional climate. The guide elaborates on survey design, methodology, sampling, question categories, and reporting protocols in accordance with NAAC guidelines. Institutions are encouraged to use SSS findings as a tool for feedback-driven improvement in pedagogy and support services.

In essence, this document serves as a blueprint for higher education institutions, guiding them to meticulously document academic quality processes while aligning with NAAC accreditation requirements. It integrates conceptual clarity with practical steps, offering institutions structured formats, formulas, and documentation checklists to foster transparency, accountability, and continuous improvement in their teaching-learning and evaluation ecosystems.

**1. Introduction**

The comprehensive guide serves as a structured reference for educational institutions in preparing for accreditation processes such as those conducted by the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC). It focuses on the planning, implementation, and evaluation of teaching–learning processes, student enrolment, faculty profiles, evaluation systems, and student outcomes. In the teaching–learning domain, the guide emphasizes student-centric approaches such as flipped classrooms, experiential learning, problem-solving, and project-based learning, while also detailing the importance of continuous internal evaluation (CIE) through assignments, tests, and projects. Transparency in evaluation, grievance redressal, and stakeholder feedback are highlighted as key quality indicators, supported by evidence such as academic calendars, lesson plans, CO–PO mapping sheets, and action taken reports.

In terms of student enrolment and profile, the document underlines inclusive and transparent admission policies, compliance with state and central reservation rules, and a focus on diversity across gender, region, and socio-economic backgrounds. Metrics like the enrollment percentage (2.1.1) and percentage of seats filled against reserved categories (2.1.2) are explained with formulas, data requirements, and supporting documentation. Catering to student diversity is seen as a critical aspect, requiring institutions to conduct learning-level assessments, arrange remedial and bridge courses for slow learners, and provide advanced learning opportunities for high achievers, along with counseling and mentorship programs.

The section on faculty quality and profile stresses the importance of sanctioned versus filled posts, qualifications such as Ph.D. and super-specialty degrees, and teaching experience, with relevant metrics including the student–teacher ratio (2.2.2), percentage of full-time teachers against sanctioned posts (2.4.1), percentage of faculty with Ph.D. or equivalent (2.4.2), and average teaching experience (2.4.3). Supporting evidence includes appointment letters, sanctioned post documents, degree certificates, and experience proofs. The examination process and reforms are another major focus, where robustness, fairness, and efficiency are assessed through metrics such as the average number of days for result declaration (2.5.1), percentage of student grievances about evaluation (2.5.2), and the extent of automation in the examination division (2.5.3). Institutions are expected to provide exam timetables, result sheets, grievance registers, and evidence of examination management systems with AMC or purchase records. Student performance and learning outcomes are linked to outcome-based education (OBE), where course outcomes (COs), program outcomes (POs), and program-specific outcomes (PSOs) are mapped, assessed, and analyzed to measure attainment levels. Corrective actions are expected to be taken based on analysis, and the pass percentage of final-year students (2.6.2) is considered a crucial indicator of success.

Finally, the student satisfaction survey (SSS) is presented as a direct quality indicator that reflects students’ perceptions of teaching quality, fairness of evaluation, and the overall learning environment. Institutions must collect, analyze, and act upon survey responses and publicly display results on their websites. Overall, this guide acts as a blueprint for institutions to align their academic practices with NAAC’s quality benchmarks, ensuring transparency, inclusivity, and readiness for Data Validation and Verification (DVV). By providing both the conceptual framework and the documentary requirements, it enables higher education institutions to systematically plan, document, and demonstrate their compliance with national accreditation standards.

**4. Student Enrolment and Profile**

The institution adopts a transparent, inclusive, and well-structured admission process that aligns with its commitment to access, equity, and academic excellence. Admissions are conducted in accordance with the guidelines laid down by affiliating universities, state governments, and regulatory bodies such as UGC and AICTE. The process ensures that merit, diversity, and equal opportunity are upheld across all programs.

Each academic year begins with the announcement of admissions through various communication channels including the institutional website, local and national newspapers, and online portals. The admission policy, eligibility criteria, reservation norms, and fee structure are clearly stated in the prospectus and made accessible to all prospective students. The institution adheres strictly to the reservation policy of the Government, providing equitable access to SC, ST, OBC, EWS, and differently-abled candidates.

The number of sanctioned seats in each program is approved by the affiliating university or competent authority, based on parameters such as faculty strength, infrastructure availability, and past academic performance. A detailed admission extract is prepared and verified by the competent authority to maintain transparency.

Over the last five academic years, the institution has maintained a healthy enrolment percentage averaging over 90% for first-year admissions. This indicates the trust and preference of stakeholders for the institution’s academic standards, industry-relevant curriculum, and student support ecosystem. Enrollment data shows consistent intake across disciplines, with a notable gender balance and increasing representation from rural and economically disadvantaged backgrounds.

To ensure smooth academic integration, newly admitted students undergo a structured induction and bridge course program. These are designed to address learning gaps and orient students to institutional culture, academic rigor, and available support services such as mentoring, counseling, and skill development programs.

The student profile is documented and analyzed each year for academic preparedness, regional diversity, socio-economic background, and educational needs. This data enables the institution to identify and address student diversity, plan for remedial or enrichment programs, and ensure that no student is left behind due to linguistic, economic, or academic challenges.

All relevant documents including sanctioned intake letters, admission extracts, and student profile reports are uploaded to the institutional website as part of public disclosure and are made available to regulatory and accreditation agencies on demand.

**8. Percentage of seats filled against reserved categories**

**Reserved Category Admissions**

The institution strictly adheres to the reservation policies mandated by the Government of India and the respective State Government. These policies ensure inclusive and equitable access to higher education for all sections of society, especially those from socially and economically disadvantaged backgrounds.

In line with these regulations, the institution earmarks a specific percentage of seats in each academic program for candidates belonging to Scheduled Castes (SC), Scheduled Tribes (ST), Other Backward Classes (OBC), Economically Weaker Sections (EWS), and Persons with Disabilities (PwD/Divyangjan). The allocation follows the exact percentages as notified by the competent authorities and is applicable for each academic year.

The data for the last five academic years shows a consistently high rate of seat utilization under the reserved category quotas. For example:

- In Year 5, out of 300 reserved seats, 275 were filled, yielding a fill rate of 91.67%.

- In Year 4, the institution achieved a 94.12% fill rate with 320 out of 340 seats filled.

- This upward trend reflects the institution's proactive efforts in promoting inclusive education.

Key initiatives supporting this performance include:

- Timely dissemination of admission notifications and reservation guidelines on the institutional website and notice boards.

- Dedicated help desks and counseling teams to assist reserved category applicants during the admission process.

- Scholarships and fee waivers for eligible students from disadvantaged categories.

- Tie-ups with community organizations to raise awareness about reservation benefits.

The institution maintains a well-documented admission database showing:

- Number of seats reserved per category as per the government order

**11. Catering to Student Diversity-SSR**

The institution recognizes that students admitted to various academic programs come from diverse educational, socio-economic, linguistic, and cultural backgrounds. In order to bridge these differences and promote inclusive learning, the institution has adopted a structured approach to assess student learning levels and implement differential strategies accordingly.

At the commencement of every academic year, the institution conducts a diagnostic assessment to identify the academic preparedness and learning needs of students. This includes reviewing their previous academic performance, administering bridge course assessments, and conducting informal orientation interviews. Based on the outcomes, students are broadly categorized into three groups: advanced learners, average learners, and slow learners.

For slow learners, the institution organizes regular remedial classes, peer mentoring, and tutorial support. Faculty members provide simplified notes, additional assignments, and extra practice sessions to enhance their understanding of core concepts. Language support is also provided for students facing difficulties in the medium of instruction. Continuous internal assessments and feedback mechanisms help monitor their progress and provide timely interventions.

Advanced learners are encouraged to explore their academic potential through participation in seminars, conferences, subject clubs, and minor research projects. They are offered opportunities to pursue MOOCs through NPTEL/SWAYAM platforms, and are motivated to prepare for competitive examinations and professional certifications. These students are often identified as peer tutors to guide others, fostering a collaborative learning environment.

The institution’s teaching strategies are based on the principle of differentiated instruction, ensuring that each learner receives the attention and resources required to thrive. Faculty members employ diverse pedagogical tools such as multimedia content, case-based learning, flipped classrooms, and blended learning platforms. Academic progress is reviewed periodically during departmental meetings, and specific strategies are revised based on data and student feedback.

To support overall well-being and learning effectiveness, the institution provides access to academic counseling, psychological support, and financial assistance to students in need. Equal Opportunity Cells and grievance redressal mechanisms ensure that every student has access to a safe and inclusive academic environment.

In essence, the institution's approach to student diversity is holistic, proactive, and student-centric. By addressing differential learning needs through customized academic support systems, the institution ensures equitable learning opportunities and improved academic outcomes for all categories of learners.

**65. NAAC Metric 2.5.3 (QnM – Status of automation of Examination division with approved Examination Manual/Ordinance)**

**1. Purpose of Metric 2.5.3**

- To evaluate how much the institution has automated its examination processes.

- Automation reduces errors, delays, bias, and manual workload.

- Ensures transparency, efficiency, and compliance with regulatory frameworks.

**2. Key Components**

- Examination Division → Institutional body handling student registration, exam conduction, result processing, and certification.

- Examination Manual/Ordinance → Official, approved document outlining exam policies, procedures, and regulations.

- Examination Management System (EMS) → Software that automates end-to-end exam processes, from registration to certification.

**3. Scoring Pattern (10 Marks)**

This is a descriptive, categorical metric (A–E), not numerical.

- A. 100% automation of entire division & EMS (10 marks)

<!-- -->

<!-- -->

- B. Student registration + Hall ticket issue + Result processing (8 marks)

<!-- -->

- Partial automation, but still lacks end-to-end integration.

- Manual elements remain in exam conduction and post-exam activities.

<!-- -->

- C. Student registration + Result processing only (6 marks)

<!-- -->

- Some automation exists but limited.

- Hall ticket issue and other processes are manual.

<!-- -->

- D. Only result processing (4 marks)

<!-- -->

- Minimal automation, usually through spreadsheets or simple software.

- Registration, hall ticketing, and exam conduction are manual.

<!-- -->

- E. Entirely manual methodology (0 marks)

<!-- -->

- No automation, completely dependent on paperwork and human intervention.

**70. NAAC Metric 2.7.1 (QnM – Online Student Satisfaction Survey regarding the Teaching-Learning Process, 30 marks)**

**2. Nature of Data Collection**

- The Student Satisfaction Survey (SSS) is conducted online by NAAC after submission of the Self-Study Report (SSR).

- Questions cover different dimensions of teaching-learning:

<!-- -->

- Provide authentic student data (email, phone, enrolment details) to NAAC.

**71. Metric-wise Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for Teaching-Learning and Evaluation**

**Student Enrolment and Profile**

**Enrolment Percentage**

- **Objective:** Measures the % of sanctioned intake filled by fresh first-year admissions.

- Procedure:

1\. Collect sanctioned intake for each program (approved by affiliating body).

2\. Record actual admissions (first-year only, no lateral entries).

3\. Compute: (Seats filled ÷ Sanctioned intake) × 100.

- **Documents:** Approval letters for intake, Admission list, COE reports.

- **Avoid:** Lateral entry, diploma/certificate/doctoral admissions.

**Reserved Category Admissions**

- **Objective:** Measures % of reserved-category seats filled as per Govt. policy.

- Procedure:

1\. Obtain official reservation policy copy.

2\. Collect admission list with categories.

3\. Count only those admitted under reservation quota.

4\. Compute: (Reserved seats filled ÷ Reserved sanctioned seats) × 100.

- **Documents:** Govt. reservation order, Admission list.

- **Avoid:** Counting reserved students admitted under general merit.

**Catering to Student Diversity**

**Student–Full Time Teacher Ratio**

- **Objective:** Ratio of enrolled students to full-time teachers.

- Procedure:

1\. Count students (latest completed academic year, all programs).

2\. Count full-time teachers (≥90% workload).

3\. Compute ratio = Students ÷ Teachers.

- **Documents:** Student enrolment list, Faculty list with workload.

- **Avoid:** Part-time/ad-hoc/visiting faculty.

**Teaching–Learning Process**

- Generally qualitative (SSR + DVV verification). SOP is institutional documentation of:

<!-- -->

- Experiential learning methods.

- Participative learning (projects, discussions).

- Peer learning activities.

<!-- -->

- **Evidence:** Lesson plans, activity reports, photos, circulars, student reflections.

**Teacher Profile and Quality**

**Teachers Appointed Against Sanctioned Posts**

- **Objective:** Avg. % of full-time teachers appointed against sanctioned posts (last 5 years).

- Procedure:

1\. Collect sanctioned posts each year.

2\. Collect appointment records.

3\. Compute: (Teachers appointed ÷ Sanctioned posts) × 100.

- **Documents:** Sanction letters, Appointment letters.

- **Avoid:** Counting librarians/physical directors unless teaching relevant programs.

**Teachers with Ph.D./Equivalent**

- **Objective:** % of full-time teachers with doctoral qualifications.

- Procedure:

1\. Collect faculty Ph.D./equivalent degree details.

2\. Verify with certificates (year-wise).

3\. Compute: (Teachers with Ph.D. ÷ Total full-time teachers) × 100.

- **Documents:** Degree certificates.

- **Avoid:** Honorary doctorates, provisional/unsigned certificates.

**Average Teaching Experience**

- **Objective:** Avg. teaching experience (in years) of full-time teachers (latest year).

- Procedure:

1\. Collect teacher-wise experience (institution + previous).

2\. Compute average across all full-time teachers.

- **Documents:** Experience certificates, Appointment orders.

- **Avoid:** Part-time/temporary teachers.

**Evaluation Process and Reforms**

**Result Declaration Period**

- **Objective:** Avg. no. of days from last exam to result declaration.

- Procedure:

1\. Collect exam timetable (end date).

2\. Collect result publication date.

3\. Compute days difference, average across programs.

- **Documents:** Exam schedule, Result sheet, Academic calendar.

- **Avoid:** Odd semester data (only even semester considered).

**Student Complaints on Evaluation**

- **Objective:** % of students filing grievances on evaluation.

- Procedure:

1\. Collect no. of students applying for revaluation/re-totaling.

2\. Collect total students appeared.

3\. Compute: (Complaints ÷ Total appeared) × 100.

- **Documents:** COE certified revaluation applications.

- **Avoid:** Counting same student multiple times.

**Automation of Examination Division**

- **Objective:** Status of automation in exam management.

- Procedure:

1\. Identify automation level (EMS full/partial/manual).

2\. Collect software contracts, AMC bills, screenshots.

- **Documents:** EMS screenshots, Purchase bills, Contracts.

- **Avoid:** Bills in Trust's name (must be HEI's).

**Student Performance and Learning Outcomes**

**Pass Percentage**

- **Objective:** Pass percentage of final year students (latest year).

- Procedure:

1\. Collect final year appeared vs. passed students (excluding backlogs).

2\. Compute: (Passed ÷ Appeared) × 100.

- **Documents:** COE report, Result notification.

- **Avoid:** Non-final year data, diploma/PhD students.

**Student Satisfaction Survey**

**Online SSS**

- **Objective:** Online student satisfaction survey on teaching-learning process.

- Procedure:

1\. Conduct survey (at least 10% of students).

2\. Analyze results (quantitative + qualitative).

3\. Upload report and action taken on institutional website.

- **Documents:** Survey forms, Analysis report, Action taken report, Website link.

- **Avoid:** Offline-only survey, feedback not related to teaching-learning.

**2. NAAC Metric 2.1.2 – Percentage of seats filled against reserved categories (SC/ST/OBC/etc.)**

Definition

- This metric measures the extent to which the institution fills its sanctioned reserved category seats in first-year admissions, as per the applicable State / Central Government reservation policy, across the last five years.

- It ensures compliance with social equity policies and evaluates whether reserved quota seats are utilized properly.

Formula

Percentage of seats filled against reserved categories=Number of students admitted under reserved categories quota/Number of sanctioned reserved category seats×100

- Numerator: Actual number of students admitted under reserved quota (SC, ST, OBC, EWS, PwD, etc. depending on Govt. policy).

- Denominator: Number of sanctioned reserved seats as per Govt. policy (SC/ST/OBC/etc.).

Scoring Bands (converted to 4–3–2–1–0 scale)

- 3 = 70–80%

- 2 = 50–70%

- 1 = 40–50%

- 0 = \<40%
