## Abstract

Criterion 3 of the NBA accreditation framework emphasizes evaluating the effectiveness of an academic program in achieving its intended learning outcomes. Carrying a weightage of 120 marks, this criterion highlights the alignment between student assessment and Course Outcomes (COs), Program Outcomes (POs), and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs). More importantly, it underscores the significance of using assessment results to drive Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI), ensuring that programs remain responsive, transparent, and outcome-driven.

The evaluation process covers several interconnected components. Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) forms the backbone of formative assessment by engaging students through assignments, quizzes, mid-term examinations, laboratory tests, and presentations. Designed to monitor progressive learning, CIE emphasizes transparency, fairness, and the use of multiple assessment tools beyond conventional written tests. Rubric-based evaluation plays a key role in ensuring objectivity, while structured feedback mechanisms provide early interventions for struggling students. Semester End Examinations (SEE) complement CIE by assessing overall attainment at the end of a course. Question papers are carefully designed to align with COs, cover a range of Bloom’s Taxonomy levels, and maintain a balanced distribution across syllabus units. Quality assurance mechanisms such as moderation, review systems, and the preparation of rubrics and answer keys further reinforce fairness and constructive alignment.

Practical components such as laboratory work and workshops are also evaluated continuously. Student performance is measured through attendance, viva examinations, lab records, and final demonstrations or project-based assessments. Here, rubrics are vital in assessing execution skills, teamwork, problem-solving ability, and documentation quality. Similarly, industrial training and internships form an integral part of the framework, exposing students to real-world professional environments. Weekly reports, mentor feedback, and logbooks form part of the continuous assessment, while final evaluations include presentations, viva examinations, and detailed internship reports. These are evaluated through rubrics to capture technical competence, professional behavior, and communication skills.

Project evaluation, encompassing mini-projects, capstone projects, and final-year projects, forms another critical element. Continuous assessment is carried out through milestones, interim reviews, and progress reports, while the final stage includes project viva, demonstrations, and formal submissions. Comprehensive rubrics assess a range of dimensions such as problem identification, design methodology, innovation, teamwork, documentation quality, and societal relevance. Tangible outputs such as prototypes, codes, and video demonstrations provide concrete evidence of attainment. An additional dimension under Criterion 3 is the integration of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). By embedding SDG themes into projects, courses, and outreach activities, institutions can align their educational practices with global sustainability priorities. Examples include renewable energy projects (SDG 7) or waste management initiatives (SDG 12). Supporting evidence may include project reports, outreach records, publications, and student activity documentation.

At the heart of Outcome-Based Assessment lies the attainment of COs, POs, and PSOs. For COs, both direct and indirect tools are employed. Direct assessment includes CIE elements such as assignments, quizzes, and mid-terms, as well as SEE results from final examinations and project evaluations. Indirect assessments include course exit surveys, student reflections, and portfolios. Attainment levels are predefined, for example: Level 3 for ≥70% of students meeting the benchmark, Level 2 for 60–69%, Level 1 for 50–59%, and Level 0 for below 50%. Final CO attainment is calculated using a weighted combination of direct (typically 80%) and indirect (20%) assessments, followed by a gap analysis to identify areas of improvement. For POs and PSOs, attainment is derived from CO–PO/PSO mappings using weighted averages. Each CO is mapped to relevant outcomes with varying levels of contribution (3 = strong, 2 = moderate, 1 = low). Direct assessment is supplemented with indirect feedback from exit surveys, alumni surveys, and employer inputs, producing an overall measure of program effectiveness.

The overarching principle guiding Criterion 3 is Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI). The process follows a cyclical approach: Assessment → Mapping → Attainment → Gap Analysis → Action Taken Report (ATR) → Re-assessment. When outcomes fall short of the target, specific, measurable, and time-bound corrective actions are identified and implemented, with follow-up assessments used to measure improvement. This ensures that academic programs evolve continuously in response to student performance and stakeholder expectations.

Evidence for NBA audits includes a wide range of documents such as assignment papers, graded answer sheets, evaluation rubrics, feedback records, SEE question papers with CO mapping, moderation reports, project evaluation sheets, internship logbooks, mentor evaluations, CO-attainment matrices, CO–PO/PSO mapping sheets, consolidated attainment dashboards, and survey reports. Collectively, these artifacts provide tangible proof of a robust and transparent outcome-based assessment system.

**In conclusion, Criterion 3:** Outcome-Based Assessment provides a comprehensive mechanism to ensure that academic programs are not only evaluating student learning effectively but are also engaging in continuous improvement. By integrating CIE, SEE, laboratory and project assessments, internships, and SDG-focused activities, institutions can demonstrate accountability, transparency, and global relevance in line with NBA Tier-1 standards.

# 1. Introduction

**Criterion 3:** Outcome-Based Assessment (OBA) is a fundamental and comprehensive framework within the NBA (National Board of Accreditation) evaluation system, meticulously designed to assess how effectively an educational program achieves its intended learning outcomes. Carrying a substantial weightage of 120 marks in the overall NBA evaluation, this criterion underscores the principle that student assessments must be rigorously aligned with the program’s Course Outcomes (COs), Program Outcomes (POs), and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs). The framework goes beyond conventional evaluation by embedding the philosophy of accountability and transparency, ensuring that assessment is not an isolated activity but a structured process that continuously informs program quality. At its core, Criterion 3 requires that the results of assessment are not merely recorded but actively analyzed and applied within a Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) cycle, thereby guaranteeing that programs evolve dynamically in response to evidence of student learning.

The scope of Criterion 3 is multifaceted, covering all major dimensions of student learning and skill development. Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) ensures formative assessment throughout the semester by employing a variety of tools such as assignments, quizzes, unit tests, mid-term exams, and presentations. This allows for regular monitoring, rubric-based evaluation, and timely feedback to students for improved performance. Complementing CIE, the Semester End Examination (SEE) emphasizes summative assessment, requiring question papers to be carefully mapped to COs, aligned with Bloom’s Taxonomy levels, and reviewed through moderation processes for quality assurance. Laboratory work and workshops serve as critical avenues for developing practical skills, where students are evaluated on problem-solving, technical execution, teamwork, and documentation, using rubrics that reduce subjectivity. Industrial training and internships add another vital dimension, exposing students to real-world contexts and professional practices, with evaluation conducted through logbooks, mentor feedback, interim reporting, and final presentations or viva voce. Project evaluation, ranging from mini-projects to capstone and final-year projects, is equally rigorous, with assessment based on innovation, methodology, teamwork, documentation, and societal impact, supplemented by evidence of prototypes, codes, or demonstrations. In recent years, Criterion 3 has also explicitly recognized the importance of aligning academic programs with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Evidence of this alignment, through projects on renewable energy, waste management, or social equity, demonstrates an institution’s contribution to global challenges and its ability to integrate sustainability into teaching, learning, and research.

At the heart of OBA lies the structured process of outcome attainment. For COs, attainment is measured using both direct assessment tools (CIE, SEE, lab and project evaluations) and indirect assessment tools (exit surveys, alumni feedback, student reflections). These results are then mapped and benchmarked against predefined attainment levels—for example, Level 3 for ≥70% of students achieving the target, Level 2 for 60–69%, Level 1 for 50–59%, and Level 0 for below 50%. Program Outcomes (POs) and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs) are derived from CO–PO/PSO mappings using weighted averages, further supplemented by indirect measures such as employer surveys and alumni feedback to validate relevance and effectiveness. The weighted combination of direct and indirect assessments ensures a balanced, evidence-based measure of program effectiveness, while gap analysis identifies shortcomings that require attention.

Most importantly, Criterion 3 institutionalizes the principle of Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) through its iterative cycle of Assessment → Mapping → Attainment → Gap Analysis → Action Taken Report (ATR) → Re-assessment. This cycle transforms assessment into a proactive mechanism of change rather than a passive record-keeping exercise. If outcomes fall short of the defined benchmarks, specific, measurable, achievable, and time-bound corrective actions are implemented, and their effectiveness is reviewed through subsequent assessments. This ensures that the program continuously improves in terms of both content delivery and learning outcomes, while also demonstrating accountability to students, accrediting agencies, and stakeholders. Ultimately, Criterion 3 represents NBA’s commitment to fostering a culture of excellence in higher education, where teaching, assessment, and feedback converge to create a dynamic ecosystem of learning, innovation, and continuous enhancement.

# **2. Criterion 3:** Outcome-Based Assessment

### 3.1 Evaluation of Continuous Assessment (10 Marks)

- **Scope:** Internal evaluation through *Assignments, Unit Tests, Mid-term exams, Quizzes, Lab tests.*

- **Key Expectations:**

<!-- -->

- Regularity and transparency in assessment.

- Multiple tools (not only written tests).

- Rubric-based evaluation of assignments & presentations.

- Feedback provided to students to improve performance.

<!-- -->

- **Evidence:** Assignment question papers, graded answer sheets, evaluation rubrics, faculty feedback records.

### 3.2 Evaluation of the Semester End Exam (SEE) Question Paper (10 Marks)

- **Scope:** End-semester written examination.

- **Key Expectations:**

<!-- -->

- Question paper should reflect Course Outcomes (COs).

- Coverage of Bloom’s Taxonomy levels (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create).

- Balanced distribution across syllabus units.

- Moderation and review system for quality assurance.

<!-- -->

- **Evidence:** SEE question papers with CO mapping, moderation reports, question paper blueprint.

### 3.3 Evaluation of Laboratory Work and Workshop (10 Marks)

- **Scope:** Practical skills through labs and workshops.

- **Continuous Evaluation:** Performance during lab sessions (attendance, lab work, viva).

- **SEE Evaluation:** Final lab exam/project demonstration.

- **Rubrics:** Used for assessing *practical execution, problem-solving, teamwork, and documentation.*

- **Evidence:** Lab manuals, sample lab records, rubrics-based evaluation sheets, SEE practical exam sheets.

### 3.4 Evaluation of Industrial Training/Internship (10 Marks)

- **Scope:** Industry exposure through internships/industrial training.

- **Continuous Evaluation:** Based on *weekly reports, mentor feedback, logbooks.*

- **SEE Evaluation:** Final presentation, viva, and internship report.

- **Assessment Tools:** Rubrics for technical learning, professional skills, communication.

- **Evidence:** Internship logbooks, evaluation rubrics, industry mentor feedback, internship reports.

### 3.5 Evaluation of Projects (20 Marks)

- **Scope:** Mini-projects, capstone projects, and final-year projects.

- **Continuous Evaluation:** Periodic reviews, milestones, interim reports.

- **SEE Evaluation:** Final project viva, demonstration, and report submission.

- **Assessment Tools:** Rubrics covering *problem identification, design methodology, innovation, teamwork, documentation, societal impact.*

- **Evidence:** Project evaluation rubrics, internal review reports, project synopsis, final project reports, publication/patent evidence (if any).

### 3.6 Evidence of Addressing Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) (10 Marks)

- **Scope:** Linking curriculum, projects, and activities with UN SDGs.

- **Examples:**

<!-- -->

- Project on renewable energy (SDG 7).

- Waste management initiatives (SDG 12).

- Gender equality workshops (SDG 5).

<!-- -->

- **Evidence:** Project reports, student activities, outreach programs, certificates, photos, publications showing SDG alignment.

### 3.7 Attainment of Course Outcomes (25 Marks)

*a) Assessment Tools and Processes (05 Marks)*

- **Tools:** *CIE (Assignments, Midterms, Quizzes, Practical assessments), SEE (final exam), Lab/Project evaluations.*

- **Process:** Each tool is mapped to COs → evaluated → attainment calculated → compared against targets.

- **Evidence:** CO-Assessment matrices, rubrics, question papers, answer scripts.

*b) Recording CO Attainment (20 Marks)*

- **Target Setting:** e.g., “70% of students scoring ≥ average marks = Attainment achieved”.

- **Direct Assessment:** CIE + SEE mapped to COs.

- **Indirect Assessment:** Course exit surveys.

- **Evidence:** Course CO attainment sheets, consolidated attainment dashboard, gap analysis reports.

### 3.8 Attainment of Program Outcomes (POs) and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs) (25 Marks)

- **Direct Assessment:** From CO–PO/PSO mappings, weighted average across all courses.

- **Indirect Assessment:** From exit surveys, alumni/employer surveys.

- **Overall PO/PSO Attainment:** Weighted combination (e.g., 80% direct + 20% indirect).

- **Evidence:** CO–PO/PSO matrices, programme-level attainment tables, survey reports, CQI action reports.

# **3. Evaluation of Continuous Assessment:** Assignments, Unit Tests, Mid-Term, etc

- Purpose of Continuous Assessment (CA)

<!-- -->

- Ensures *formative evaluation* of students throughout the semester, not just at the end.

- Monitors *progressive learning* and provides early intervention where students are lagging.

- Acts as evidence for *attainment of Course Outcomes (COs)* and their mapping to *Program Outcomes (POs)/Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs).*

<!-- -->

- Process of Evaluation Followed

<!-- -->

- **Assignments:**

<!-- -->

- Designed to test understanding of theoretical concepts and problem-solving skills.

- Questions are mapped to specific COs.

- Rubrics are predefined (clarity, correctness, application, innovation).

- Graded assignments are documented in course files.

<!-- -->

- **Unit Tests (Class Tests/Quizzes):**

<!-- -->

- Conducted after completion of each unit/module.

- **Objective:** assess comprehension of recent topics.

- Questions prepared with *constructive alignment* (direct mapping with COs).

- Marks distribution and question types (MCQs, short answer, problem-based) reflect CO levels (remember, understand, apply, analyze).

<!-- -->

- **Mid-Term / Internal Exams:**

<!-- -->

- Typically 2 per semester.

- Cover multiple COs across units to measure cumulative knowledge.

- Paper setting follows *blueprint mapping* to COs and Bloom’s Taxonomy levels.

- Evaluation scheme is standardized (marking guidelines, moderation process).

<!-- -->

- **Seminars/Presentations (if applicable):**

<!-- -->

- Encourages independent learning, communication, and application of knowledge.

- Evaluated using rubrics aligned with soft-skill related POs (e.g., communication, teamwork).

<!-- -->

- **Practical/Project Work (where applicable):**

<!-- -->

- Lab records, mini-projects, case studies.

- Direct evidence for higher-level COs (apply, analyze, design).

- Evaluation done through viva, demonstration, and rubrics.

<!-- -->

- Constructive Alignment with COs, POs, and PSOs

<!-- -->

- *Step 1:* Each CO is defined at the start of the course with Bloom’s taxonomy verbs.

- *Step 2:* Each assignment/test/mid-term question is tagged to COs.

- *Step 3:* COs are mapped to relevant POs/PSOs in the course articulation matrix.

- *Step 4:* Attainment levels are calculated at the end (CO → PO/PSO contribution).

- Ensures transparency and traceability of student performance to program objectives.

<!-- -->

- Quality Maintenance Measures

<!-- -->

- **Question Paper Moderation:** Ensures variety, balance, and difficulty alignment.

- **Rubric-based Evaluation:** Reduces subjectivity in grading.

- **Feedback Mechanism:** Student performance analyzed, corrective measures taken.

- **Course Files:** Maintain complete documentation for NBA audit:

<!-- -->

- Question papers, assignment sheets.

- CO mapping with each question.

- Answer scripts with evaluation.

- Mark sheets, attainment calculation sheets.

- Corrective action reports (for weak COs/POs).

<!-- -->

- **Evidence for Evaluation (Course Files must include):**

<!-- -->

- Assignment topics/questions with CO/PO mapping.

- Unit test question papers with blueprint.

- Mid-term exam papers and evaluation criteria.

- Attendance and marks register.

- Rubrics and evaluated answer scripts (sample).

- Attainment analysis of COs based on CA results.

<!-- -->

- **Marks Allocation (as per NBA – 10 Marks):**

| **Marks**              | **Allocation**                                                                                                                                        |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| *Full Marks (8–10):*   | If the institution demonstrates a well-defined, CO-aligned, documented, and rubric-based continuous assessment process with clear attainment records. |
| *Partial Marks (4–7):* | If assessments are conducted but CO-PO alignment/attainment evidence is weak or incomplete.                                                           |
| *Low Marks (0–3):*     | If only conventional evaluation is done without outcome-based evidence.                                                                               |

# 9. Attainment Analysis of Course Outcomes (COs) based on Continuous Assessment (CA)

## Purpose

- To quantify how well students are achieving the intended Course Outcomes (COs).

- Uses Continuous Assessment tools (Assignments, Unit Tests, Mid-Terms, etc.) as the data source.

- Provides evidence of Outcome-Based Education (OBE) implementation.

## Step-by-Step Process

1\. Define Target Level for CO Attainment

- **Example:** 70% of students scoring ≥ 60% marks in CA indicates CO is “attained”.

- **Scale (typical 3-level):**

<!-- -->

- **Level 3 (High):** ≥70% students achieved target.

- **Level 2 (Moderate):** 60–69%.

- **Level 1 (Low):** 50–59%.

- **Level 0:** \<50%.

2\. Collect Assessment Data

- Use marks from Assignments, UTs, Mid-Terms.

- Each question already mapped to a CO.

3\. Calculate CO Attainment (Direct Method)

- **For each CO:**

<!-- -->

- Find % of students achieving the target marks.

- Assign attainment level (0–3).

4\. Indirect Attainment (Optional)

- Based on student feedback, surveys, or external exams.

- Usually given 20% weightage in Final Attainment.

5\. Final CO Attainment

- Formula:

- Final CO Attainment = (0.8 × Direct Attainment) + (0.2 × Indirect Attainment)

6\. CO → PO/PSO Mapping

- Each CO contributes to specific PO/PSO.

- Aggregate CO attainment feeds into PO/PSO attainment analysis.

| CO2    | UT1 Q2, Assignment A2                         | 2                                  | 1                                         | 2                    |

# 10. CO Attainment Analysis after Evaluation (scores, average, attainment level)

## Purpose

- Quantifies how much each Course Outcome (CO) is achieved after evaluating actual student performance.

- Uses scores from Assignments, Unit Tests, Mid-Terms, Projects, Rubric-based Evaluations.

- Demonstrates constructive alignment between assessment → CO → PO/PSO.

## Process

1\. Collect Student Scores (CA Results)

- From Attendance & Marks Register, Assignment Mapping, Unit Test Mapping, Mid-Term Mapping, Rubrics sheet.

- Each question is already mapped to COs.

2\. Calculate Average Marks per CO

- For each CO, calculate average % of marks scored across all mapped questions.

- **Example:**

<!-- -->

- CO1 has 3 questions (Assignment A1-Q1, UT1-Q1, MT1-Q1).

- Average % = (Σ student scores ÷ Σ max marks) × 100.

3\. Convert into Attainment Levels (NBA Scale 0–3)

- **Level 3:** ≥70% students above target marks.

- **Level 2:** 60–69%.

- **Level 1:** 50–59%.

- **Level 0:** \<50%.

4\. Aggregate Across Assessments

- If multiple assessments map to same CO, take weighted average.

- **Example weighting (customizable by department):**

| **Assessment**   | **Weighting** |
|------------------|---------------|
| Assignments      | 20%           |
| Unit Tests       | 30%           |
| Mid-Terms        | 40%           |
| Rubrics/Projects | 10%.          |

5\. Finalize CO Attainment

- Direct Attainment (from CA results).

- Indirect Attainment (from surveys/feedback).

- Final Attainment = 0.8 × Direct + 0.2 × Indirect.

# 30. CO Attainment is calculated from Semester End Exam (SEE)

## 1. Purpose

- To measure how well each Course Outcome (CO) is achieved through the final exam performance of students.

- SEE is considered a direct attainment tool since it evaluates the full syllabus and higherorder skills.

## 2. Steps in Calculation

## **Step 1:** Mapping SEE Questions to COs

- Each SEE question is mapped to specific CO(s) in the SEE Question Paper Mapping / Blueprint sheet.

- **Example:**

<!-- -->

- Q1a → CO1

- Q1b → CO2

- Q2 → CO3

- Q3 → CO4

## **Step 2:** Collecting Student Marks

- For every student, extract marks scored question-wise from evaluated SEE scripts.

- **Example (Q2 mapped to CO3, Max Marks=10):**

<!-- -->

- **Student A:** 8

- **Student B:** 6

- **Student C:** 5

## **Step 3:** Calculate Average % per CO

Avg % per CO =

ΣMarks Obtained (all students for that CO)/ΣMax Marks (all students for that CO)×100

- **Example:**

<!-- -->

- Total Marks Obtained (for CO3) = 8+6+5 = 19

- Total Max Marks = 10+10+10= 30

- Avg % = (19 ÷ 30) × 100 = 63.3%

## **Step 4:** Convert Avg % into Attainment Levels

- **As per NBA norms:**

<!-- -->

- ≥70% → Level 3

- 60–69% → Level 2

- 50–59% → Level 1

- \<50% → Level 0

## **Example:**

- CO3 Avg % = 63.3% → Attainment Level = 2

## **Step 5:** Consolidated CO Attainment from SEE

- **For each CO, prepare a summary:**

| **CO** | **Questions Mapped** | **Total Marks** | **Avg %** | **Attainment Level** |
|--------|----------------------|-----------------|-----------|----------------------|
| CO1    | Q1a                  | 4               | 72%       | 3                    |
| CO2    | Q1b                  | 6               | 65%       | 2                    |
| CO3    | Q2                   | 10              | 63%       | 2                    |
| CO4    | Q3                   | 15              | 75%       | 3                    |

## 3. Documents to Keep in Course Files

- Answer Key / Rubrics.

- Evaluated scripts (High/Medium/Low achievers).

- Excel sheet of CO-wise attainment calculation (as above).

- Moderation committee report (if applicable).

# 38. CO/PO/PSO attainment analysis

## 1. Flow of Attainment Analysis

1\. Student-level scoring

- Continuous scores → evaluated by industry + faculty (rubrics CI–C5).

- SEE scores → evaluated by faculty panel (rubrics Sl–S3).

- Scores are normalized to a 0–3 scale.

2\. CO-wise attainment

- Each rubric criterion (CI–C5, Sl–S3) is mapped to a Course Outcome (CO).

- Compute Continuous Avg (0–3) and SEE Avg (0–3) per CO.

- **Weighted:**

CO Attainment=(0.6×Continuous)+(0.4×SEE)

3\. Attainment Levels (0–3)

- ≥2.5 → Level 3

- 2.0–2.49 → Level 2

- 1.5–1.99 → Level 1

- \<1.5 → Level 0

4\. CO → PO/PSO mapping

- Each CO mapped to POs/PSOs (from Rubric Definition sheet).

- **Example:** CO3 (Task Engagement) → PO5, PO6 → PSO2.

5\. PO/PSO aggregation

- For each PO/PSO, average the attainment levels of all COs mapped to it.

- Formula:

PO/PSO = Σ(Attainment Levels mapped to PO/PSO)/Number of COs mapped

# 48. CO Attainment Sheet (Projects)

## 1. Purpose

- To show how each CO is attained based on rubric evaluations (Continuous + Final).

- To provide numerical evidence of learning outcomes achieved through projects.

- To make attainment traceable → Rubrics → CO → PO/PSO.

## 2. Formula Setup

- **Continuous Avg (0–3):** Average of rubric scores from Progress Reviews + Mentor Evaluations (CI–C6).

- **Final Avg (0–3):** Average of rubric scores from Final Evaluation (Fl–F5).

- **Weighted Avg:**

CO Attainment=(0.4×Continuous Avg)+(0.6×Final Avg)

- **Level Conversion (0–3):**

- ≥ 2.5 → Level 3

- 2.0–2.49 → Level 2

- 1.5–1.99 → Level 1

- \< 1.5 → Level 0

# 58. Assessment → Mapping → Attainment → Gap Analysis → CQI

## 1. Assessment (Data Collection Stage)

- Direct Assessments

<!-- -->

- *CIE:* Mid-semester tests, quizzes, assignments, laboratory experiments, mini projects.

- *SEE:* Semester-end examinations covering the full syllabus.

- *Rubrics-based evaluations:* Presentations, viva-voce, design reports.

<!-- -->

- Indirect Assessments

<!-- -->

- *Course Exit Surveys* (student perception of learning outcomes).

- *Feedback from employers/alumni* (industry validation of CO relevance).

- *Student portfolios* (collection of evidence of learning).

## 2. Mapping (Linking Stage)

- Each question/item from assessments is mapped to one or more Course Outcomes (COs).

- Bloom’s Taxonomy levels (Remember → Create) are also tagged.

- Faculty prepares a Question Paper–CO Mapping Table (e.g., Q1 → CO1, Q2 → CO2).

- Ensures that *all COs are fairly covered* across CIE + SEE.

## 3. Attainment (Measurement Stage)

- Step 1 – Compute Attainment % per CO

Attainment % (CO)=Students scoring ≥Target in CO-linked items/ Total Students×100

- Step 2 – Define Attainment Levels

<!-- -->

- **Level 3:** ≥70% students achieve target.

- **Level 2:** ≥60% students achieve target.

- **Level 1:** ≥40% students achieve target.

- **Level 0:** Below 40%.

<!-- -->

- Step 3 – Consolidate Attainment

<!-- -->

- **Direct Attainment:** Weighted average of CIE (40–50%) + SEE (40–50%).

- **Indirect Attainment:** 10–20% weight from surveys.

- **Final Attainment:** Direct + Indirect (weighted).

## 4. Gap Analysis (Evaluation Stage)

- Compare Final Attainment % with Target % set by program.

- **Compute:**

Gap=Final Attainment %–Target %

- **If Gap ≥ 0 → Status:** Met.

- **If Gap \< 0 → Status:** Not Met.

- Document reasons for non-attainment (low internal scores, weak practical exposure, poor alignment of teaching methods, etc.).

## 5. Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI Stage)

- **Corrective Actions:**

<!-- -->

- Conduct remedial/tutorial classes.

- Introduce additional problem-solving sessions.

- Improve lab manuals or teaching aids.

- Revise course delivery methods (flipped classroom, ICT tools).

<!-- -->

- **Preventive Actions:**

<!-- -->

- Revise curriculum content to strengthen weak COs.

- Faculty training workshops.

- Strengthen question bank design to balance Bloom’s levels.

<!-- -->

- **Review Mechanism:**

<!-- -->

- CO attainment discussed in *Course Review Committee* and *Department Advisory Committee*.

- Actions documented → Implemented in next cycle → Re-evaluated → Closes the CQI loop.

# 61. Attainment of Program Outcomes (POs) and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs)

## 1. Introduction

- **Program Outcomes (POs):** Twelve generic graduate attributes defined by NBA (e.g., Engineering Knowledge, Problem Analysis, Design & Development, Ethics, Communication, etc.).

- **Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs):** Department-specific competencies that graduates must demonstrate (e.g., for CSE: "Apply data structures and algorithms for real-world applications").

- NBA requires institutions to demonstrate the attainment of these POs & PSOs at the *program level*.

- Attainment is computed by mapping COs → POs/PSOs and then aggregating results.

## 2. Methodology for PO/PSO Attainment

## A. Direct Assessment (based on student performance)

- **Step 1 – CO–PO/PSO Mapping:**

<!-- -->

- Each Course Outcome (CO) is mapped to relevant POs/PSOs with correlation levels (Low=1, Medium=2, High=3).

- **Example:** CO1 (Understand stacks & queues) → PO2 (Problem Analysis: High, Level 3), PO3 (Design: Medium, Level 2).

<!-- -->

- **Step 2 – CO Attainment Calculation:**

<!-- -->

- Attainment % for each CO is calculated using student performance in CIE/SEE/Labs.

- Attainment level (LI/L2/L3) assigned.

<!-- -->

- **Step 3 – Aggregation at PO/PSO Level:**

<!-- -->

- PO attainment is computed as weighted average of all COs contributing to that PO.

- Formula:

PO = Σ(Attainment×CO–PO)/Σ(weight)

- **Step 4 – Consolidation:**

<!-- -->

- Across all courses in a semester/program, consolidated PO & PSO attainment tables are prepared.

## B. Indirect Assessment (survey-based evidence)

- **Tools used:**

<!-- -->

- *Course Exit Survey*– students’ self-perception of CO achievement.

- *Graduate Exit Survey*– students rate their achievement of POs/PSOs at the end of program.

- *Alumni Survey* – graduates reflect on relevance of POs/PSOs in professional practice.

- *Employer Survey*– industry stakeholders provide feedback on graduates' skills.

<!-- -->

- **Weightage:**

<!-- -->

- Direct Assessment → 80%

- Indirect Assessment → 20% (Institutions can decide weightage, but NBA expects direct \> indirect).

Contribution-CO Attainment %×Mapping Weight

- PO3 → 72 × 0 = 0

| PO1        | 69.5%-70%                |
| PO2        | 68.3%+                   |
| PO3        | 66.0%–66%                |

## **Interpretation (NBA View):**

- PO1 ~70% → Good attainment.

- PO2 ~68% → Moderate attainment.

- PO3 ~66% → Needs CQI action if target was 70%.

## Indirect Attainment

## 1. Concept Recap

- Indirect Attainment is measured from surveys (Student Exit Survey, Alumni Survey, Employer Survey, Stakeholder Survey).

- Each survey asks respondents to rate the attainment of each PO/PSO (usually on a scale, e.g., 1–5).

- Scores are converted into % attainment values.

- Final Indirect Attainment per PO/PSO = Indirect PO Attainment=∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ (Ai/n)

## 2. Example Dataset

| **Survey Name**     | **PO1** | **PO2** | **PO3** | **PSO1** |
|---------------------|---------|---------|---------|----------|
| Student Exit Survey | 70      | 72      | 68      | 72       |
| Alumni Survey       | 68      | 70      | 66      | 70       |
| Employer Survey     | 72      | 74      | 70      | 74       |

## 3. Step-by-Step Calculation

For PO1

- Exit Survey = 70%

- Alumni Survey = 68%

- Employer Survey = 72%

Indirect PO1=70+68+72/3=210/3=70%

For PO2

- (72 + 70 + 74) ÷ 3 = 72%

For PO3

- (68 + 66 + 70) ÷ 3 = 68%

For PSO1

- (72 + 70 + 74) ÷ 3 = 72%

## 4. Final Indirect Attainment Table

| **Assessment**      | **PO1** | **PO2** | **PO3** | **PSO1** |
|---------------------|---------|---------|---------|----------|
| Indirect Attainment | 70%     | 72%     | 68%     | 72%      |

## 5. NBA Interpretation

- Indirect Attainment supplements Direct Attainment.

- **Example:** If PO2 Direct Attainment = 65% and PO2 Indirect Attainment = 72%, NBA considers both with weights (Direct=80%, Indirect-20%) in the Overall Attainment.

- Higher Indirect scores often indicate students/alumni/employers perceive strong skills, even if exam-based direct results are slightly lower.

## Summary

- Direct = exam performance.

- Indirect = perception surveys.

- Both combined give Overall PO/PSO Attainment.

# 67. Overall PO/PSO Attainment is calculated with an example (Direct + Indirect combined).

## 1. Formula

Overall PO/PSO Attainment=(wd×Direct Attainment)+(wi×Indirect Attainment)

- wdw_dwd = weight for Direct Attainment (usually 80%)

- wiw_iwi = weight for Indirect Attainment (usually 20%)

- Direct Attainment = computed from CO → PO/PSO aggregation (exam-based evidence)

- Indirect Attainment = computed from surveys (exit, alumni, employer)

## 2. Example Dataset

| **Assessment**      | **PO1** | **PO2** | **PO3** | **PSO1** |
|---------------------|---------|---------|---------|----------|
| Direct Attainment   | 72      | 65      | 58      | 73       |
| Indirect Attainment | 70      | 68      | 69      | 72       |

## 3. Step-by-Step Calculation

For PO1

- Direct = 72%

- Indirect = 70%

- Overall = (0.8 × 72) + (0.2 × 70)

=57.6+14=71.6% ≈72%

For PO2

- Direct = 65%

- Indirect = 68%

- Overall = (0.8 × 65) + (0.2 × 68)

=52+13.6=65.6% ≈66%

For PO3

- Direct = 58%

- Indirect = 69%

- Overall = (0.8 × 58) + (0.2 × 69)

=46.4+13.8=60.2% ≈60%

For PSO1

- Direct = 73%

- Indirect = 72%

- Overall = (0.8 × 73) + (0.2 × 72)

=58.4+14.4=72.8% ≈73%

## 4. Final Table

| **Assessment**      | **PO1** | **PO2** | **PO3** | **PSO1** |
|---------------------|---------|---------|---------|----------|
| Direct Attainment   | 72      | 65      | 58      | 73       |
| Indirect Attainment | 70      | 68      | 69      | 72       |

- PO1 (72%) → High attainment, meets/exceeds target (70%)

- PO2 (66%) → Moderate attainment, slightly below target, requires CQI action

- PO3 (60%) → Low attainment, needs strong corrective measures

- PSO1 (73%) → High attainment, target met

## **Summary:**

- Direct Attainment = Exam-based evidence.

- Indirect Attainment = Survey-based perception.

- **Overall Attainment = Weighted combination (80:**20) → used by NBA to finalize Program Outcome & Program Specific Outcome attainment levels.

# 68. Process Overview for Programme PO/PSO Attainment

**1. Step 1:** CO Attainment (Course-level)

- Compute attainment for each Course Outcome (CO) using CIE + SEE performance.

**2. step 2:** Map COs → POs/PSOs

- Each CO is mapped to one or more POs/PSOs with correlation levels (1=Low, 2=Medium, 3=High).

**3. Step 3:** Compute Direct Attainment (Programme Level)

- Aggregate all CO → PO/PSO contributions across courses.

- Formula:

Direct PO/PSO Attainment=Σ(CO Attainment×Weight)/Σ(Weights)

**4. Step 4:** Compute Indirect Attainment (Programme Level)

- Based on Exit Surveys, Alumni Surveys, Employer Surveys.

- Formula:

Indirect PO/PSO Attainment=∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿAi/n

**5. Step 5:** Combine Direct + Indirect for Overall Attainment

- Apply weights (generally 80% Direct + 20% Indirect).

- Formula:

Overall Attainment=(0.8×Direct)+(0.2×Indirect)

# 69. SEE (Semester End Examination)

## 1. What is SEE?

- SEE = Semester End Examination (also called End-Sem exam / University exam / Final exam).

- Conducted at the end of each semester to evaluate student learning outcomes.

- Typically external / centralized (university set), ensuring standardization.

- Complemented by CIE (Continuous Internal Evaluation) which covers mid-terms, assignments, quizzes, etc.

## 2. Role of SEE in NBA

- SEE is a direct assessment tool used to measure attainment of Course Outcomes (COs).

- Every question in the SEE must be mapped to one or more COs.

- Helps evaluate higher-order learning (Bloom’s Taxonomy – Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create).

- Marks obtained by students in CO-linked questions contribute to CO attainment levels.

## 3. Process of SEE Evaluation (NBA)

## **Step 1:** Blueprinting the Question Paper

- Ensure coverage of entire syllabus.

- **Map each question to:**

<!-- -->

- CO (Course Outcome)

- Bloom’s Level (Knowledge/Comprehension → Apply → Analyze → Evaluate → Create).

<!-- -->

- Maintain weightage balance (e.g., 20% Knowledge, 50% Application, 30% Higher-order).

## **Step 2:** Mapping Questions to COs

| **Q. No.** | **CO Mapped** | **Bloom’s Level** | **Max Marks** |
|------------|---------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Q1a        | CO1           | Understand        | 5             |
| Q1b        | CO2           | Apply             | 5             |
| Q2         | CO3           | Analyze           | 10            |
| Q3         | CO4           | Evaluate          | 10            |

## **Step 3:** Attainment Calculation

- **For each CO, calculate:**

CO Attainment % from SEE=No. of students scoring ≥ target in CO-linked questions/ Total students×100

- **Example:**

<!-- -->

- **Target:** 60% of max marks in CO2-linked questions.

- 40 out of 60 students scored ≥ target.

- Attainment = (40 ÷ 60) × 100 = 66.7%.

## **Step 4:** Consolidation

- **Attainment values from SEE are averaged with CIE attainment (usually weighted 50:**50 or as per institute policy).

- This gives Final Direct Attainment for each CO.

## 4. Evidence Required for NBA

- SEE Question Papers (with CO mapping).

- Question Paper Blueprint (coverage of syllabus & Bloom’s levels).

- Sample Answer Scripts (evaluated).

- CO-wise attainment calculations from SEE.

- Consolidated CO attainment sheet (CIE + SEE).

### 5. Marks in NBA (Criterion 3)

- Evaluation of the SEE Question Paper → 10 Marks (out of 120 in Criterion 3).

- **Focus:**

<!-- -->

- CO mapping.

- Bloom’s taxonomy alignment.

- Balanced question paper design.

- Transparency in evaluation.

# 70. CO (Course Outcome)

## 1. What is a Course Outcome (CO)?

- **Definition:**

A Course Outcome (CO) is a specific and measurable statement of what a student is expected to know, understand, and demonstrate at the end of a course.

- **Nature:**

<!-- -->

- Narrow in scope (focused on a course).

- Directly measurable using assignments, tests, labs, projects, presentations.

- Linked to higher-level Program Outcomes (POs) and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs).

## 2. Role of CO in NBA

- COs are the foundation of OBE (Outcome-Based Education).

- They ensure that teaching, learning, and assessment are aligned.

- NBA requires at least 4–6 COs per course.

- Every question in CIE (internal exams) and SEE (end-sem exams) must be mapped to COs.

## 3. Structure of a CO Statement

**A CO statement typically begins with:**

- “At the end of this course, the student will be able to...”

## **Examples:**

- **CO1:** Apply knowledge of probability to solve engineering problems.

- **CO2:** Design and conduct experiments using data analytics tools.

- **CO3:** Analyze algorithms for efficiency and scalability.

- **CO4:** Develop solutions considering environmental and ethical aspects.

## 4. CO–PO/PSO Mapping

- Each CO contributes to one or more POs/PSOs.

- **Strength of mapping:** 1 = Low, 2 = Medium, 3 = High.

## **Example:** “70% of students scoring ≥ average marks = Attainment achieved.”

## **Step 2:** Collect Assessment Data

- Assignments, Mid-term Tests, SEE, Labs, Projects.

## **Step 3:** Compute Attainment

CO Attainment %=No. of students scoring ≥ target in CO-linked items/Total students×100

## **Step 4:** Consolidation

- Indirect Attainment = Course Exit Surveys.

# 71. PO (Program Outcome)

## 1. What is a Program Outcome (PO)?

- **Definition:**

A Program Outcome (PO) is a statement of competencies (knowledge, skills, and attitudes) that a graduate of a program (e.g., B.Tech. MCA, MBA) is expected to achieve at the time of graduation.

- **Scope:**

<!-- -->

- Broad compared to Course Outcomes (COs).

- Common across all programs of a discipline (e.g., all B.Tech in Engineering must address the 12 NBA-mandated POs).

- Derived from Washington Accord graduate attributes.

## 2. The 12 NBA-Mandated POs (for Engineering Programs)

1\. Engineering Knowledge → Apply knowledge of mathematics, science, engineering fundamentals.

2\. Problem Analysis → Identify, analyze, and solve complex engineering problems.

3\. Design/Development of Solutions → Design system components/processes for societal needs.

4\. Conduct Investigations of Complex Problems → Research-based knowledge, experiments, analysis.

5\. Modern Tool Usage → Apply modern IT, tools, prediction & modeling.

6\. The Engineer and Society → Apply reasoning for societal, health, legal, cultural responsibilities.

7\. Environment and Sustainability → Understand environmental impact & sustainable development.

8\. Ethics → Apply ethical principles, commit to professional ethics.

9\. Individual and Team Work → Function effectively as individual and in teams.

10\. Communication → Communicate effectively with society, write reports, make presentations.

11\. Project Management and Finance → Apply management & finance principles.

12\. Life-long Learning → Recognize need for continuous learning & adapt to change.

*(For MBA/MCA/Science programs, equivalent POs are defined separately but with same philosophy).*

## 3. Relationship Between CO, PO, PSO

- CO (Course Outcome) → *What a student learns in a particular subject.*

- PO (Program Outcome) → *What a student should demonstrate at graduation.*

- PSO (Program Specific Outcome) → *Program-specific skills, aligned to specialization.*

**Hierarchy:**

COs → contribute to → POs & PSOs → lead to → PEOs (Program Educational Objectives).

| C102            | CO1    | 1       | 2       | 2       |

| **Course Code** | **PO4** | **PO5** | **...** | **PO12** |
|-----------------|---------|---------|---------|----------|
| C101            | 0       | 0       |         | 0        |
| C101            | 0       | 0       |         | 0        |
| C102            | 0       | 0       |         | 0        |

- **Mapping strength:** 1 = Low, 2 = Medium, 3 = High.

- Contribution from each CO is aggregated to evaluate PO attainment.

## 5. PO Attainment Calculation

## **Step 1:** Aggregate Direct Attainment

Direct PO = Σ(Attainment × Mapping Weight)/ΣMapping Weights

## **Step 2:** Add Indirect Attainment (Exit/Alumni/Employer Surveys)

Overall PO Attainment=(0.8×Direct)+(0.2×Indirect)

## **Step 3:** Gap Analysis & CQI

- Compare PO attainment vs Target (e.g., 70%).

- Take Action Taken Reports (ATR) for improvement.

# 72. PSO (Program Specific Outcome)

## 1. What is a PSO?

- **Definition:**

A Program Specific Outcome (PSO) is a specialized competency that a graduate should acquire in line with the specific program or specialization.

- **Nature:**

<!-- -->

- More narrow and specialized than POs.

- Unique to each program/department, unlike the 12 common POs.

- Derived from the vision & mission of the department and Program Educational Objectives (PEOs).

## 2. Role of PSOs in NBA

- PSOs define what makes a program distinct.

- For NBA accreditation, each program should define 2 to 4 PSOs.

- They demonstrate how the program prepares graduates with domain-specific knowledge and skills.

## 3. Examples of PSOs

- Computer Science & Engineering (CSE)

- **PSO1:** Apply concepts of data structures, algorithms, and software engineering to develop efficient computing solutions.

- **PSO2:** Use modern tools and technologies (Al, ML, Cloud) to solve real-world problems.

- **PSO3:** Design and manage databases, networks, and information systems securely.

- Mechanical Engineering (ME)

- **PSO1:** Apply mechanical design principles to develop sustainable products and processes.

- **PSO2:** Use modern tools like CAD/CAM/CAE for analysis and manufacturing.

- **PSO3:** Engage in thermal, fluid, and energy system analysis for industrial applications.

- MBA

- **PSO1:** Demonstrate analytical and decision-making skills in finance, marketing, HR. and operations.

- **PSO2:** Apply leadership and entrepreneurship skills in dynamic business environments.

## 4. Relationship Between CO, PO, and PSO

- **CO (Course Outcome):** *What a student achieves in a course (short-term).*

- **PO (Program Outcome):** *Broad competencies for all graduates (common to all programs).*

- **PSO (Program Specific Outcome):** *Specialized skills unique to a particular program.*

## **Example:**

- **A Data Mining course (COs) contributes to:**

<!-- -->

- POs like *Problem Analysis, Modern Tool Usage.*

- PSOs like *Application of AI/ML in data analysis.*

## 5. PSO Attainment Calculation

## **Step 1:** Mapping

- Each CO from relevant specialization courses is mapped to PSOs.

## **Step 2:** Direct Attainment

- Derived from CIE + SEE performance in COs linked to PSOs.

## **Step 3:** Indirect Attainment

- Derived from Exit/Alumni/Employer surveys focusing on specialization outcomes.

## **Step 4:** Consolidation

Overall PSO Attainment=(0.8×Direct)+(0.2×Indirect)

## 6. Evidence Required for NBA (PSOs)

- Clearly stated PSOs (2–4 per program).

- CO–PO–PSO Mapping Matrices.

- PSO Attainment Tables (Direct, Indirect, Overall).

- Survey Reports (Exit/Alumni/Employer feedback on program-specific skills).

- Gap Analysis + ATR for unmet PSOs.

- **Evidence:** Project reports, lab manuals, specialized course outcomes, industry collaboration documents.

### 7. NBA Marks Allocation

- PSOs are evaluated together with POs in Criterion 3 (25 marks).

- **NBA looks for:**

<!-- -->

- Well-defined PSOs.

- Transparent attainment calculation.

- Gap analysis + CQI actions.

# 73. CQI (Continuous Quality Improvement)

## 1. What is CQI?

- Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) is the systematic process of measuring, analyzing and improving student outcomes (COs, POs, PSOs).

- It ensures that feedback loops are in place → identify gaps → take corrective actions → remeasure outcomes → demonstrate improvement.

- NBA views CQI as evidence that the institute is not just teaching, but improving every cycle.

## 2. Role of CQI in NBA

- Proves that the program is dynamic, responsive, and continuously improving.

- **Directly linked with:**

<!-- -->

- CO attainment (Course level improvements).

- PO/PSO attainment (Program level improvements).

- Gap Analysis (Targets vs Attainment).

- Action Taken Reports (ATRs).

<!-- -->

- CQI is part of Criterion 3 (Outcome-based Assessment) and Criterion 8 (Continuous Improvement).

## 3. CQI Cycle (NBA Framework)

## **Step 1:** Assessment

- Use tools like CIE, SEE, Labs, Projects, Internships, Surveys.

## **Step 2:** Mapping

- Map assessment results to COs → POs/PSOs.

## **Step 3:** Attainment

- Calculate Direct + Indirect attainment and compare with Targets.

## **Step 4:** Gap Analysis

- Identify whether Targets were Met or Not Met.

## **Step 5:** CQI (Action Taken Reports)

- **If Met:** Maintain strategy, share best practices.

- **If Not Met:** Introduce specific, measurable, time-bound corrective measures.

# 74. Rubrics

## 1. What is a Rubric?

- A rubric is a scoring guide used to assess student performance on assignments, projects, labs, presentations, etc.

- It defines criteria (what is being assessed) and levels of performance (how well it is done).

- Ensures fair, consistent, and measurable evaluation.

## 2. Why Rubrics are Important in NBA?

- NBA emphasizes direct evidence of student learning.

- Helps measure Course Outcomes (COs) objectively.

- Ensures Bloom’s Taxonomy levels (Apply, Analyze, Create, etc.) are assessed.

- Reduces subjectivity in evaluation (all evaluators follow same standards).

- Provides students with clear expectations and feedback.

## 3. Structure of a Rubric

| **Criteria (What to assess)** | **Excellent(3)**                    | **Good(2)**             | **Needs Improvement(1)** |
|-------------------------------|-------------------------------------|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| Problem Understanding         | Demonstrates deep understanding     | Partial understanding   | Poor understanding       |
| Methodology Approach          | Innovative, well-structured         | Correct but basic       | Incorrect /weak          |
| Analysis& Results             | Accurate, insightful, linked to COs | Mostly accurate         | Major errors             |
| Presentation/ Report          | Well-structured, professional       | Adequate                | Poor clarity             |
| Teamwork/ Professional Skills | Active participation, leadership    | Participated moderately | Minimal contribution     |

- **Levels:** Usually 3–5 (Excellent–Poor).

- **Scores:** Can be numeric (e.g., 3, 2, 1) or percentage-based.

- **Weightage:** Each criterion can carry a different weight.

## 4. Types of Rubrics

1\. Analytic Rubric (NBA preferred)

- Lists criteria separately with performance levels.

- Allows detailed feedback.

- **Example:** *Project Rubric with 5 criteria, each scored 1–3.*

2\. Holistic Rubric

- Gives a single overall score for performance.

- Less detailed, not preferred for NBA.

## 5. Example – Rubric for Project Evaluation

| **Criteria**           | **Weightage** | **Excellent(3)**                           | **Good(2)**               | **Needs Improvement(1)** |
|------------------------|---------------|--------------------------------------------|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| Problem Identification | 20%           | Clearly defined with relevance to POs/PSOs | Defined but lacks clarity | Poorly defined           |
| Innovation Creativity  | 20%           | Highly innovative, original                | Some innovation           | No innovation            |
| Methodology Execution  | 20%           | Systematic, correct, complete              | Adequate execution        | Weak execution           |
| Results& Analysis      | 20%           | Accurate, well-analyzed                    | Partial results           | Inaccurate results       |
| Presentation Report    | 20%           | Professional, well- documented             | Adequate                  | Poor clarity             |

- Final Score = Σ (Criterion Score × Weightage).

- **Example:** A group scores (3,2,3,2,3) → Weighted average = 80%.

## 6. Where Rubrics are Used in NBA?

- Laboratory/Workshop evaluations (experiments, practical skills).

- Mini/major projects (problem-solving, innovation, teamwork).

- Assignments & reports (critical thinking, clarity).

- Seminars/presentations (communication, professional skills).

- Internship evaluation (industry mentor + faculty evaluation).

## 7. NBA Evidence

- Rubrics should be attached in course files.

- Evaluated samples (high, medium, low achievers).

- Consolidated rubric scores mapped to COs/POs/PSOs.

- Proof that rubrics-based assessment is implemented consistently.

# 75. DAC (Department Advisory Committee)

- DAC = Department Advisory Committee.

- It is a department-level committee that guides, monitors, and evaluates the implementation of Outcome-Based Education (OBE) and NBA accreditation requirements.

## 2. Composition of DAC (typical as per NBA norms)

- Head of Department (HOD) → Chairperson.

- Senior faculty members (2–3).

- Industry representative (from relevant sector).

- Alumni representative.

- Student representative (final year).

- NBA/OBE Coordinator (Convener/Secretary).

(Exact composition may vary across institutes, but inclusion of external members = important for

## NBA.)

- Action taken reports (based on DAC recommendations).

- Evidence of stakeholder involvement (industry/alumni feedback).

- Follow-up records showing improvements.

## 6. Importance of DAC in NBA

- Demonstrates that departments are not working in isolation but in consultation with stakeholders.

- Proves existence of structured governance at the program level.

- Shows NBA that continuous monitoring & improvements are systematically implemented.

# 76. ATR (Action Taken Report)

## 1. What is ATR?

- ATR = Action Taken Report

- A formal document that records the corrective and preventive measures taken by a department or institution in response to:

<!-- -->

- Gap Analysis (CO/PO/PSO attainment below target).

- Stakeholder Feedback (students, alumni, industry, employers).

- IQAC / DAC recommendations.

<!-- -->

- ATR shows NBA evaluators that continuous quality improvement (CQI) is actually implemented, not just planned.

## 2. Role of ATR in NBA

- Demonstrates accountability → documents *who did what, when, and how*.

- Closes the CQI loop → from *assessment → gap → corrective action → improvement*.

- Provides traceable evidence to support claims during NBA visits.

- Integral part of Criterion 3 (Outcome-based Assessment) and Criterion 8 (Continuous Improvement).

## 3. Structure of an ATR

| **Issue Identified (Gap/Feedback)**              | **Action Planned**                   | **Action Taken (with timeline)**                                                        | **Responsible Person/Committee** | **Evidence/ Reference**                        | **Outcome/ Improvement**          |
|--------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Low attainment in PO2 (Target70%, Actual66%)     | Extra tutorials& revised assignments | Conducted tutorials (Feb–Mar 2025), redesigned assignments with higher- order questions | Course Faculty                   | Tutorial attendance, revised assignment sheets | PO2 improved to 72% in next cycle |
| Weak CO3attainment in C302 (Lab course)          | Add mini- projects                   | Introduced lab-based mini-projects (April 2025)                                         | Lab In-Charge + HoD              | Project reports, lab manuals                   | CO3 improved by 10%attainment     |
| **Alumni feedback:** Need more industry exposure | Invite industry lectures             | Organized2 guest lectures (May 2025)                                                    | DAC + NBA Coordinator            | Guest lecture reports, photos                  | Positive student feedback         |
| **Student feedback:** Poor communication skills  | Communication workshops              | Conducted English/soft skill workshops (Jan 2025)                                       | Training Placement Cell          | Workshop attendance, feedback forms            | Improved performance in PO10      |

## 4. Key Features of a Good ATR

- Specific → clearly describes the action (not generic).

- Measurable → shows outcomes or % improvement.

- Time-bound → includes dates or semester when implemented.

- Accountability → assigns responsibility to faculty/committee.

- Evidence-based → attaches documentary proof.

## 5. NBA Evidence for ATR

- DAC/IQAC meeting minutes approving the action.

- ATR tables with action taken + evidence reference.

- **Supporting proofs:**

<!-- -->

- Revised lesson plans, assignments, projects.

- Photos of workshops/guest lectures.

- Attendance sheets.

- Before/after attainment data showing improvement.

## 6. Importance of ATR in CQI

**Without an ATRI Gap Analysis remains incomplete. NBA expects:**

1\. Gap Analysis Table (Target vs Attainment vs Gap vs Status).

2\. ATR Table (Action Taken, Responsibility, Evidence).

3\. Before/After Result showing improvement.

This proves that the institution follows Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) in practice.

# 77. IQAC (Internal Quality Assurance Cell)

## 1. What is IQAC?

- IQAC = Internal Quality Assurance Cell.

- A permanent internal body created in every Higher Education Institution (HEI).

- **Objective:** To plan, guide, monitor, and improve quality assurance and enhancement activities in academics, research, and administration.

- Acts as the quality nerve center of the institution.

## 2. Composition of IQAC (Typical)

- **Chairperson:** Head of the Institution (Vice Chancellor / Principal / Director).

- **Coordinator / Director:** A senior faculty with quality assurance expertise.

- **Administrative Officers:** Registrar, Deans, HoDs.

- **Faculty Representatives:** From each department.

- **External Experts:** Industry, Alumni, Employers, Local Community.

- **Student Representatives:** UG, PG. Research scholars.

- **Support Staff:** Office/technical staff.

*(This ensures a multi-stakeholder representation.)*

## 3. Functions of IQAC

1\. Quality Assurance & Enhancement

- Develop quality benchmarks for academic, research, and administrative activities.

- Ensure effective implementation of Outcome-Based Education (OBE).

2\. Curriculum & Teaching-Learning

- Promote innovative teaching-learning practices.

- Ensure CO–PO–PSO attainment monitoring.

3\. Research & Development

- Encourage publications, patents, consultancy.

- Organize FDPs, workshops, seminars.

4\. Feedback Mechanisms

- Collect & analyze student, teacher, alumni, employer feedback.

- Integrate feedback into quality improvement.

5\. Accreditation & Compliance

- Prepare for NBA, NAAC, NIRF, QS-I Gauge.

- Maintain and update institutional quality policies.

6\. Continuous Improvement (CQI)

- Monitor Gap Analysis & ATRs across departments.

- Ensure corrective actions are implemented.

## 4. IQAC in Action (Example)

**Case:** Poor attainment in PO3 (Design & Development) across multiple programs

- **IQAC Meeting (Jan 2025):**

<!-- -->

- Reviewed PO3 attainment reports from departments.

- **Suggested:**

<!-- -->

- Increase design-based projects.

- Conduct industry design workshops.

- Revise curriculum in next BOS meeting.

<!-- -->

- **Action Taken:**

<!-- -->

- Mini-projects introduced in 3rd year.

- Industry workshop on product design held in March 2025.

<!-- -->

- **Next Cycle Result:** PO3 attainment improved from 62% → 72%.

IQAC thus monitors and drives institution-wide CQI.

## 5. Evidence Required for NBA/NAAC

- IQAC Composition document (with members & roles).

- Meeting Minutes & Action Taken Reports.

- Quality Policy & Manuals.

- Annual Quality Assurance Report (AQAR).

- Feedback analysis & action reports.

- Proof of improvements (before/after attainment data).

## 6. Importance of IQAC in Accreditation

- Proves existence of a formal internal quality mechanism.

- Ensures sustainability of improvements (not one-time).

- Connects department-level DACs → institution-level quality assurance.

- Essential evidence for Criterion 8 (Continuous Improvement) in NBA and all NAAC criteria.
