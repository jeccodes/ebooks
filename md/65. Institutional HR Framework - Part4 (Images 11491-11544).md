**Policy on Furnishing False Information or Misrepresentation**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To uphold honesty, transparency, and integrity in all stages of employment — from recruitment to retirement.

- To define the consequences of false declarations, forged documents, or misrepresentation by any employee in connection with University service.

- To protect the institution from fraudulent or misleading employment practices.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**    | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
|---------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Prohibition   | Furnishing false, fabricated, or misleading information regarding one’s name, age, father’s name, qualification, experience, ability, or previous employment record, or any other information relevant to employment, at the time of appointment or during service, is strictly prohibited. |
| Applicability | This rule applies to all categories of employees — teaching, non-teaching, administrative, technical, temporary, and contractual.                                                                                                                                                           |

**2. FORMS OF MISREPRESENTATION**

| **Type of Misrepresentation**     | **Examples**                                                                                     |
|-----------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Identity-related falsification    | Submitting incorrect or forged information regarding name, date of birth, parentage, or address. |
| Academic fraud                    | Producing forged or altered mark sheets, degree certificates, or professional credentials.       |
| Experience misstatement           | Inflating duration or nature of previous employment, or providing false reference letters.       |
| Skill / ability misrepresentation | Claiming technical or professional expertise that is unverified or false.                        |
| Service-related deception         | Concealing previous termination, disciplinary action, or criminal record.                        |
| In-service falsification          | Providing incorrect information during appraisal, promotion, or leave applications.              |

**3. CONSEQUENCES AND PENALTIES**

| **Stage of Discovery**                | **Disciplinary Action**                                                                                                                 |
|---------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Before appointment confirmation       | Cancellation of appointment offer or withdrawal of selection order.                                                                     |
| After joining but before confirmation | Immediate termination of service without notice and forfeiture of salary and benefits.                                                  |
| After confirmation or during service  | Major penalty — dismissal from service for misconduct involving moral turpitude and loss of trust.                                      |
| If discovered post-retirement         | The University reserves the right to withdraw pensionary or post-retirement benefits and initiate legal proceedings if fraud is proven. |

**4. PROCEDURAL REQUIREMENTS**

| **Step**                 | **Description**                                                                                                                            |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Verification Process | All documents submitted at the time of appointment shall be verified for authenticity by HR/Establishment section.                         |
| 2\. Detection of Fraud   | On suspicion or discovery of falsification, the case shall be reported to the Registrar, who will initiate a preliminary inquiry.          |
| 3\. Formal Action        | The employee shall be given an opportunity to explain, failing which disciplinary proceedings will commence under the Conduct Rules.       |
| 4\. Legal Recourse       | In cases involving forged documents or impersonation, the University shall file a formal police complaint under relevant penal provisions. |

**5. IMPACT ON SERVICE RECORD**

- Any confirmed instance of false information or document forgery shall:

<!-- -->

- Result in deletion of the employee’s service from official records,

- Disqualify the individual from future employment in the University, and

- Lead to forfeiture of benefits, increments, and service continuity.

**6. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- Employment in the University is based on trust, merit, and verifiable credentials.

- Any form of deceit or misrepresentation undermines institutional integrity and is treated as gross misconduct.

- The University shall ensure rigorous verification at recruitment and throughout service tenure.

- The Board of Management (BoM) shall be the final authority for deciding on cases, interpretations, and appeals arising under this rule.

**Policy on Acts Prejudicial to the Interests of the Institution**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To preserve the reputation, integrity, and operational stability of the University by prohibiting any behavior or action that undermines its interests.

- To define what constitutes prejudicial conduct, and to outline the disciplinary consequences for such actions.

- To ensure that all employees demonstrate loyalty, responsibility, and professionalism in upholding the institutional mission and values.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**     | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                    |
|----------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Prohibition    | No employee shall act in any manner prejudicial to the interests of the University or its stakeholders, whether directly or indirectly, by conduct, communication, or association.               |
| Scope          | This rule applies to all forms of professional, personal, or public behavior that may harm the reputation, disrupt operations, or compromise the functioning of the University.                  |
| Binding Nature | The obligation to act in the University’s best interest extends throughout the employee’s service and, in certain cases, beyond employment where institutional data or reputation are concerned. |

**2. ILLUSTRATIVE ACTS CONSIDERED PREJUDICIAL**

| **Category**            | **Examples of Prohibited Actions**                                 |
|-------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Professional Misconduct | Sharing confidential University information with external parties. |

- Misuse of institutional property, funds, or resources.

- Negligence leading to loss or damage of University assets.

- Undermining the authority of superiors or the governing body.

**Reputational Damage**

- Making false or defamatory statements about the University in public, print, or social media.

- Associating the University’s name with unauthorized events, organizations, or controversies.

- Spreading misinformation that harms the University’s image.

**Conflict of Interest**

- Engaging in activities or employment that conflict with official duties.

- Using University position or influence for personal or external benefit.

**Operational Disruption**

- Inciting students or staff against the administration.

- Participating in or encouraging strikes, protests, or non-cooperation movements within the campus.

- Refusing to perform assigned duties without justification.

**3. DISCIPLINARY ACTIONS AND PENALTIES**

| **Severity of Misconduct** | **Possible Disciplinary Measures**                      |
|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| Minor Violations           | Censure, written warning, or withholding of increments. |

**Major Violations**

- Suspension pending inquiry.

- Reduction in rank or pay scale.

- Compulsory retirement.

- Removal or dismissal from service.

**Legal Violations**

- Institution of legal proceedings under applicable civil or criminal laws.

- Recovery of losses caused to the University.

**4. PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK**

| **Step**                  | **Action**                                                                                                                 |
|---------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Detection / Reporting | Any suspected act prejudicial to University interests must be reported in writing to the Head of Institution or Registrar. |
| 2\. Preliminary Review    | The competent authority may initiate a fact-finding inquiry to assess the nature and impact of the act.                    |
| 3\. Formal Inquiry        | If prima facie evidence exists, a disciplinary inquiry shall be conducted, ensuring the employee’s right to respond.       |
| 4\. Final Decision        | Based on inquiry findings, penalties will be imposed by the Vice Chancellor or Board of Management, depending on severity. |

**5. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- Every employee serves as a representative and custodian of the University’s mission and reputation.

- Loyalty to the institution is a fundamental condition of service.

- Actions that harm institutional trust, credibility, or operational harmony constitute gross misconduct.

- Preventive vigilance and ethical conduct are expected at all levels to protect institutional interests.

- The Board of Management (BoM) is the final authority in determining whether an act is prejudicial and deciding the appropriate disciplinary measure.

**Policy on Willful Insubordination or Disobedience to Superiors**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To ensure a disciplined, respectful, and efficient work environment where the chain of command is maintained.

- To define willful insubordination and disobedience as acts of gross misconduct that undermine the authority and effective functioning of the University.

- To preserve a culture of professionalism, accountability, and mutual respect among all employees.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**  | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
|-------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Prohibition | Willful insubordination or disobedience, whether individually or in concert with others, to lawful and reasonable orders of a superior shall be treated as a serious breach of discipline and gross misconduct. |
| Definition  | Willful insubordination means a deliberate refusal, defiance, or failure to comply with legitimate instructions, directions, or orders issued by a superior officer in the course of official duties.           |
| Scope       | Applies to all categories of employees — teaching, non-teaching, administrative, and technical staff — in all departments and offices of the University.                                                        |

**2. ILLUSTRATIVE ACTS OF INSUBORDINATION OR DISOBEDIENCE**

| **Category**        | **Examples**                                              |
|---------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Direct Disobedience | Refusal to perform assigned duties without justification. |

- Ignoring written or verbal orders issued by the Head of Department or superior authority.

**Defiance or Non-Cooperation**

- Showing open defiance or disrespect toward superiors.

- Deliberately delaying compliance or acting contrary to explicit instructions.

**Disruptive Conduct**

- Influencing others to disregard lawful orders or administrative directions.

- Collective refusal to follow instructions or participate in assigned institutional activities.

**Verbal or Behavioral Misconduct**

- Using disrespectful, abusive, or confrontational language toward a superior.

- Exhibiting hostile attitude or creating an environment of non-cooperation.

**3. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Severity of Misconduct**            | **Possible Penalties**                                                              |
|---------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| First or minor instance               | Written warning or censure.                                                         |
| Repeated or deliberate refusal        | Withholding of increment, suspension, or reduction in rank.                         |
| Grave or collective insubordination   | Major penalty — compulsory retirement, removal, or dismissal from service.          |
| Acts involving threat or intimidation | Immediate suspension pending inquiry, followed by termination of service if proved. |

**4. PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK**

| **Step**                        | **Action**                                                                                                                                          |
|---------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Reporting                   | The superior officer shall report the act of insubordination in writing to the Head of Institution / Registrar with evidence or witness statements. |
| 2\. Preliminary Review          | The competent authority shall conduct a preliminary inquiry to verify facts and assess severity.                                                    |
| 3\. Formal Disciplinary Inquiry | The employee shall be issued a show-cause notice and given an opportunity to explain his/her conduct.                                               |
| 4\. Final Action                | Based on inquiry findings, the Vice Chancellor or Board of Management (BoM) shall decide on the penalty to be imposed.                              |

**5. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- Respect for hierarchy and compliance with lawful orders is fundamental to University governance.

- Constructive disagreement may be expressed professionally and respectfully, but deliberate defiance is unacceptable.

- Insubordination, particularly when organized or deliberate, is considered gross misconduct.

- The University recognizes only lawful and reasonable orders as binding — employees cannot be penalized for refusing unlawful or unethical instructions.

- The Board of Management (BoM) shall act as the final authority in interpretation, appeal, and disciplinary decision-making under this rule.

**Policy on Defiance of Orders, Disrespectful Communication, and Improper Correspondence with Higher Authorities**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To uphold the discipline, decorum, and institutional hierarchy essential for smooth academic and administrative functioning.

- To prohibit defiance of lawful orders and disrespectful communication with senior officials.

- To define clear boundaries of professional correspondence and tone in official communication with higher authorities.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**  | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
|-------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Prohibition | Any act of defiance of lawful orders or directions issued by higher authorities, or writing of letters, representations, or communications in a disrespectful, accusatory, or insubordinate tone, constitutes gross misconduct. |
| Scope       | Applies to all employees — academic, administrative, technical, and contractual — at all levels of the University.                                                                                                              |
| Expectation | All employees shall maintain professional tone, language, and courtesy while addressing superiors in verbal or written communication, even in matters of disagreement or grievance.                                             |

**2. ILLUSTRATIVE ACTS OF MISCONDUCT**

| **Category**       | **Examples**                                                                                          |
|--------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Defiance of Orders | Refusal to follow instructions or directions from Head of Department, Registrar, or higher authority. |

- Deliberate delay or omission in carrying out assigned work.

- Publicly questioning or disobeying official circulars, office orders, or administrative decisions.

**Disrespectful Communication**

- Writing letters, emails, or notes with insulting, threatening, or sarcastic language addressed to higher authorities.

- Using high tone or derogatory remarks questioning the authority, integrity, or competence of superiors.

- Circulating offensive or inflammatory communications among staff or on social media regarding institutional policies or decisions.

**Indirect Defiance**

- Influencing others to disregard instructions.

- Displaying passive resistance or non-cooperation in implementing approved policies.

**3. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Nature of Misconduct**                                                       | **Disciplinary Action**                                                              |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Minor defiance or isolated incident                                            | Written warning or censure; counseling by the Head of Institution.                   |
| Repeated or deliberate defiance                                                | Suspension, withholding of increment, or reduction in responsibilities.              |
| Serious insubordination or disrespectful communication                         | Major penalty — reduction in rank, compulsory retirement, or dismissal from service. |
| Public defamation or written communication harming the University’s reputation | Immediate suspension pending inquiry, followed by major disciplinary action.         |

**4. PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK**

| **Step**               | **Action**                                                                                                                              |
|------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Reporting          | The superior officer shall submit a written report with documentary evidence (letters, emails, memos, etc.) to the competent authority. |
| 2\. Preliminary Review | The Registrar / Vice Chancellor shall evaluate the incident and decide whether it warrants a formal inquiry.                            |
| 3\. Formal Inquiry     | The concerned employee shall be issued a show-cause notice and given the opportunity to explain or apologize for the conduct.           |
| 4\. Final Decision     | Based on the findings, the competent authority shall impose appropriate penalties as per the Conduct and Discipline Rules.              |

**5. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- The University expects all employees to respect institutional hierarchy and follow lawful and reasonable directions from superiors.

- Constructive disagreement or representation must be expressed in measured, courteous, and written form through proper channels.

- The use of disrespectful tone, accusatory language, or defiant communication is incompatible with the professional ethos of an academic institution.

- Persistent defiance or rude correspondence constitutes gross misconduct and erodes institutional harmony.

- The Board of Management (BoM) shall be the final authority for interpretation, appeals, and disciplinary decisions under this policy.

**Policy on Unauthorized Absence and Overstaying Sanctioned Leave**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To maintain discipline, accountability, and institutional efficiency through regular attendance of all employees.

- To define unauthorized absence and overstaying sanctioned leave as acts of misconduct that disrupt the functioning of the University.

- To ensure that every employee adheres to the leave approval process and provides timely intimation of absence with valid justification.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**     | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
|----------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Prohibition    | Absence from duty without prior sanction of leave, or overstaying the period of sanctioned leave for more than four consecutive days without satisfactory cause or explanation, constitutes serious misconduct.                                                          |
| Responsibility | Every employee must ensure that their leave application is duly approved by the competent authority before availing leave. In case of unavoidable absence, they must inform the Head of Department or Registrar immediately and provide medical or supporting documents. |
| Scope          | This rule applies to all employees, including teaching, non-teaching, technical, administrative, contractual, and project-based staff.                                                                                                                                   |

**2. ILLUSTRATIVE CASES OF MISCONDUCT**

| **Category**           | **Examples of Unauthorized Absence**                   |
|------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Without Leave Approval | Remaining absent from duty without applying for leave. |

- Proceeding on leave before it is sanctioned.

**Overstaying Leave**

- Failing to report back after expiry of sanctioned leave for four or more consecutive days without valid reason.

- Extending personal or medical leave without written approval.

**Failure to Communicate**

- Not informing the department during emergency absence.

- Ignoring official communications or reminders to rejoin duty.

**Pattern of Negligence**

- Frequently remaining absent without intimation.

- Using leave privileges irresponsibly, causing disruption in academic or administrative duties.

**3. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Type of Misconduct**                                   | **Action / Penalty**                                                                                              |
|----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Absence of 1–4 days without approval                     | Written warning or censure.                                                                                       |
| Overstaying sanctioned leave beyond 4 consecutive days   | Suspension of pay for the absent period, and entry of misconduct in service record.                               |
| Absence exceeding 10 consecutive days without intimation | Initiation of disciplinary proceedings; may lead to termination of service under “willful absence.”               |
| Absence beyond 30 days without communication             | Employee shall be deemed to have voluntarily abandoned employment, and his/her name will be struck off the rolls. |

**4. PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK**

| **Step**               | **Action**                                                                                                                              |
|------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Detection          | The Head of Department or Registrar reports unauthorized absence to the competent authority with attendance records.                    |
| 2\. Notice to Employee | A show-cause notice is issued directing the employee to explain the reason for absence within a specified period (usually 7 days).      |
| 3\. Inquiry            | If no satisfactory explanation is received, a disciplinary inquiry may be initiated.                                                    |
| 4\. Decision           | Based on the inquiry outcome, the Vice Chancellor or Board of Management (BoM) decides the penalty — ranging from warning to dismissal. |

**5. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- Regular attendance and punctuality are integral duties of every University employee.

- Absence without permission disrupts the functioning of departments, affects students, and damages institutional reputation.

- Emergencies or medical issues must be promptly communicated and documented.

- The period of unauthorized absence will be treated as “dies non” — not counting for pay, increment, leave, or service benefits.

- The Board of Management’s decision on interpretation and disciplinary measures shall be final and binding.

**Policy on Habitual Late Attendance, Irregular Attendance, and Habitual Absenteeism**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To ensure punctuality, discipline, and reliability in the functioning of all academic and administrative units.

- To address and correct instances of repeated late reporting, irregular attendance, and habitual absenteeism that affect institutional efficiency and student learning.

- To establish a fair and transparent procedure for monitoring attendance and enforcing accountability among all employees.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**  | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|-------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Definition  | Habitual late attendance means repeated or frequent arrival after the scheduled reporting time without valid reason. Habitual absenteeism refers to repeated absence from duty without proper sanction or justification, even for short durations. |
| Prohibition | Repeated lateness, irregular attendance, or absenteeism, whether with or without prior intimation, shall constitute misconduct under University Service and Conduct Rules.                                                                         |
| Scope       | This rule applies to all teaching, non-teaching, administrative, and technical staff, including employees on probation, contract, or regular appointment.                                                                                          |

**2. ILLUSTRATIVE CASES OF MISCONDUCT**

| **Category**             | **Examples**                                                              |
|--------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Habitual Late Attendance | Reporting late on three or more occasions in a month without valid cause. |

- Arriving after commencement of classes or office hours repeatedly despite reminders.

**Irregular Attendance**

- Frequently signing attendance registers at irregular intervals.

- Leaving the workplace early without permission.

- Failing to complete the minimum required working hours.

**Habitual Absenteeism**

- Frequently availing short or casual leaves without proper planning.

- Repeatedly being absent on important institutional days (e.g., examinations, meetings, events).

- Exhibiting a pattern of absence around weekends or holidays.

**3. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Level of Misconduct**                                    | **Action / Penalty**                                                                                       |
|------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| First instance                                             | Oral or written warning by the Head of the Department.                                                     |
| Repeated lateness (3 instances per quarter)                | One day of casual leave forfeited for every three late attendances. (As per existing institutional norms.) |
| Persistent irregularity                                    | Written censure and withholding of increment or temporary suspension.                                      |
| Chronic absenteeism (beyond 10% working days per semester) | Formal disciplinary inquiry, possible reduction in rank, suspension, or termination.                       |
| Absence without information exceeding 10 consecutive days  | Treated as abandonment of service and removal from rolls after due process.                                |

**4. MONITORING AND RECORD KEEPING**

| **Responsibility**                      | **Action**                                                                                                                   |
|-----------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Heads of Departments / Section Officers | Maintain daily attendance registers, biometric or digital logs, and report habitual irregularities monthly to the Registrar. |
| Registrar / HR Department               | Review attendance data and issue notices to employees with unsatisfactory attendance records.                                |
| IQAC / Audit Committee                  | Include attendance and punctuality as part of the annual performance and compliance audit.                                   |

**5. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- Regular attendance is a core professional responsibility and reflects commitment to institutional duties.

- Occasional delay due to valid reasons may be condoned if properly reported and documented.

- Habitual lateness and absenteeism disrupt teamwork, student instruction, and operational planning.

- Disciplinary actions will be progressive, starting with warnings and escalating to penalties for repeated violations.

- Attendance compliance will be linked to performance appraisal, annual increments, and promotion eligibility.

- The Board of Management’s (BoM) interpretation and final decision in all attendance-related disciplinary matters shall be binding.

**Policy on Neglect of Work, Negligence in Duty, and Slowing Down of Work**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To uphold a culture of professional responsibility, efficiency, and accountability in all academic and administrative functions of the University.

- To define and address instances of negligence, careless conduct, deliberate delay, or slowing down of work, which undermine productivity and institutional integrity.

- To ensure that all employees perform their duties with due diligence, timeliness, and sincerity.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**  | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|-------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Definition  | “Neglect of work” or “negligence in duty” means failure to perform assigned duties effectively, responsibly, and within the prescribed time, whether by deliberate omission, carelessness, or willful disregard. “Lingering or slowing down of work” refers to deliberate reduction in output, efficiency, or cooperation in carrying out official tasks. |
| Scope       | Applies to all categories of employees — teaching, non-teaching, administrative, technical, and contractual.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Prohibition | Negligence or indifference in official duties, whether partial or total, intentional or habitual, is considered gross misconduct under the University’s Service and Conduct Rules.                                                                                                                                                                        |

**2. ILLUSTRATIVE ACTS OF MISCONDUCT**

| **Category**   | **Examples of Neglect or Negligence**                                |
|----------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Academic Staff | Failure to complete syllabus or evaluation work within the schedule. |

- Ignoring students’ academic or disciplinary needs.

- Failure to attend meetings, invigilation, or examination duties.

**Administrative Staff**

- Delay in processing official files or approvals.

- Failure to maintain records, accounts, or correspondence properly.

- Neglect in implementing orders or instructions from competent authorities.

**Technical / Support Staff**

- Carelessness in handling laboratory, workshop, or equipment.

- Failure to maintain or repair instruments leading to academic loss.

- Deliberate slowing down of assigned duties or disobedience of supervisors.

**General Negligence**

- Repeated failure to meet deadlines.

- Careless execution of work leading to loss, error, or safety hazard.

- Indifference to assigned responsibilities or avoidance of duties during office hours.

**3. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Nature of Negligence**                   | **Disciplinary Action**                                                 |
|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Minor negligence or isolated lapse         | Verbal or written warning; counseling by Head of Department.            |
| Repeated or habitual negligence            | Withholding of increment, censure, or short suspension.                 |
| Deliberate neglect or slowing down of work | Major penalty — reduction in rank, compulsory retirement, or dismissal. |
| Negligence causing loss to University      | Recovery of financial loss plus disciplinary action as per rules.       |

**4. PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK**

| **Step**                          | **Procedure**                                                                                                                                            |
|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Reporting                     | Head of the Institution / Department reports instances of neglect or slow performance with supporting documentation (e.g., delays, reports, complaints). |
| 2\. Notice to Employee            | A show-cause notice is issued, seeking written explanation within a specified period.                                                                    |
| 3\. Inquiry                       | If negligence is substantiated, a formal disciplinary inquiry is conducted.                                                                              |
| 4\. Action by Competent Authority | Based on findings, the Vice Chancellor may impose appropriate penalty; appeals may lie with the Board of Management (BoM).                               |

**5. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- Every employee has a duty of care toward their assigned role and responsibilities.

- Negligence or lack of diligence undermines institutional credibility and adversely affects students, research, and administration.

- “Lingering” or “slowing down” work—whether individually or collectively—is deemed as industrial indiscipline and subject to strict disciplinary action.

- Accountability for delays, errors, or omissions must be traceable through performance reports and departmental audits.

- The Board of Management’s decision on interpretation and final disciplinary measures shall be binding and conclusive.

**Policy on Damage to University Property and Violation of Safety Measures**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To ensure the protection, preservation, and responsible use of all University property and infrastructure.

- To promote a culture of safety awareness and accountability among employees in the handling of institutional assets and equipment.

- To define and address acts of negligence, misuse, damage, or interference with safety mechanisms as serious misconduct warranting disciplinary action.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**  | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Definition  | Damage to property includes any act of negligence, misuse, destruction, or unauthorized tampering with movable or immovable assets belonging to the University. Disregard for safety measures refers to careless or intentional violation of safety norms, operating instructions, or procedures established for protecting personnel and property. |

| Intentional or repeated misinformation                  | Suspension pending inquiry; withholding of increment or reduction in rank.                                 |
| Defamatory or malicious act (causing reputational harm) | Major penalty — dismissal from service and initiation of legal proceedings under defamation or cyber laws. |
| Use of digital media to defame the institution          | Termination of service, blacklisting, and reporting to law enforcement authorities.                        |

**4. PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK**

| **Step**                     | **Procedure**                                                                                                                                                          |
|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Reporting                | Any instance of false or defamatory communication shall be reported to the Registrar / Vice Chancellor with documented evidence (emails, screenshots, messages, etc.). |
| 2\. Preliminary Verification | A Disciplinary Subcommittee shall verify the authenticity and intention behind the act.                                                                                |
| 3\. Inquiry                  | A formal inquiry will be conducted to establish whether the information was false, misleading, or defamatory, and whether it was shared intentionally.                 |
| 4\. Decision and Action      | Based on inquiry findings, the competent authority (Vice Chancellor or BoM) shall impose disciplinary penalties proportionate to the severity of misconduct.           |

**5. COMMUNICATION ETHICS AND RESPONSIBILITIES**

- Employees must ensure that all information shared publicly or internally is accurate, verified, and authorized.

- Institutional communication with external stakeholders must align with official channels and not be shared independently.

- Any employee unsure about the accuracy of information must consult the concerned authority before circulating it.

- The use of social media, emails, or public platforms for airing grievances or criticisms without following due process is prohibited.

- All employees are encouraged to report instances of misinformation to maintain institutional credibility.

**6. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- The University’s reputation depends on the truthfulness and integrity of its employees.

- Spreading false or defamatory information is a serious ethical and disciplinary violation.

- Transparency and truth in communication strengthen trust within the University community.

- Offenders will be held personally accountable for damages caused by misinformation.

- The decision of the Board of Management (BoM) regarding disciplinary measures or appeals shall be final and binding.

**Policy on Submission of Representations or Claims Without Following the Proper Channel**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To uphold disciplinary hierarchy, procedural integrity, and institutional order in all administrative and grievance matters.

- To ensure that all representations, applications, claims, or grievances are properly examined, verified, and routed through the designated administrative channels.

- To discourage bypassing authority and direct communication with higher offices without due approval, which undermines administrative discipline.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**  | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
|-------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Definition  | “Improper submission” refers to any representation, complaint, claim, or communication made directly to a higher authority (e.g., Vice Chancellor, Board of Management, Chancellor) without routing through the prescribed administrative hierarchy, such as the Head of Department or Registrar. |
| Scope       | Applies to all University employees — teaching, non-teaching, technical, administrative, and contractual — in all official matters including service, pay, leave, promotion, grievance, or redressal issues.                                                                                      |
| Prohibition | No employee shall submit or forward any representation, claim, or grievance directly to higher authorities without routing through the proper channel as prescribed in the Service and Conduct Rules. Any such direct submission constitutes misconduct and administrative indiscipline.          |

**2. ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLES OF MISCONDUCT**

| **Category**        | **Examples**                                                                                                                 |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Bypassing Hierarchy | Sending grievance letters directly to the Vice Chancellor or Board Members without prior approval of the Head of Department. |

- Forwarding salary, promotion, or leave-related representations without departmental review.

**Direct Escalation Without Verification**

- Submitting allegations or claims directly to external agencies, media, or government bodies without informing the University.

- Filing complaints against colleagues or authorities without first using the institutional grievance mechanism.

**Repeated Violations**

- Continuously bypassing the chain of command despite prior instructions or warnings.

- Circulating copies of such representations to unauthorized persons or departments.

**3. PRESCRIBED PROCEDURE (PROPER CHANNEL)**

| **Type of Representation**                            | **Routing Channel**                                                          |
|-------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Service-related matters (pay, leave, promotion, etc.) | Employee → Head of Department → Registrar → Vice Chancellor                  |
| Academic grievances / departmental concerns           | Employee → HOD → Dean (Academics) → Vice Chancellor                          |
| Administrative / Establishment grievances             | Employee → Section Officer → Registrar → Vice Chancellor                     |
| Appeals or Review Petitions                           | Employee → Registrar → Vice Chancellor → Board of Management (if applicable) |

*Only after written response or inaction (beyond two months) by the immediate authority may the matter be legitimately escalated.*

**4. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Nature of Violation**                                  | **Action / Penalty**                                                           |
|----------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| First instance (unintentional)                           | Written warning and instruction to resubmit through proper channel.            |
| Repeated or deliberate bypassing of hierarchy            | Formal censure, withholding of increment, or adverse entry in service record.  |
| Submission of grievance with defamatory or false content | Major penalty — suspension or dismissal from service after due inquiry.        |
| Communication to external bodies or media                | Treated as gross misconduct and subject to legal and disciplinary proceedings. |

**5. PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK**

| **Step**                 | **Procedure**                                                                                                                                          |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Receipt and Review   | If any higher authority receives a representation directly, it shall be returned to the concerned employee for routing through proper channel.         |
| 2\. Logging and Tracking | Departments must maintain a register of employee representations to ensure transparency and timely redressal.                                          |
| 3\. Escalation Clause    | If an employee does not receive a response within 60 days, he/she may escalate the matter to the next higher authority, attaching proof of submission. |
| 4\. Grievance Mechanism  | Employees may also use the Internal Grievance Redressal Cell (IGRC) for structured resolution of issues.                                               |

**6. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- Observance of hierarchical discipline ensures efficient communication, fairness, and institutional stability.

- Direct communication with higher authorities without routing through the proper channel leads to administrative confusion and loss of procedural control.

- Employees must respect the chain of command and maintain decorum in written communication.

- Any deviation from this rule is treated as administrative indiscipline and may affect performance appraisal or promotion eligibility.

- The decision of the Board of Management (BoM) regarding interpretation or appeal shall be final and binding.

**Policy on Submission of Employment Applications to Outside Agencies Without Prior Permission**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To maintain institutional discipline, transparency, and accountability in all matters of employment transition.

- To ensure that employees do not apply for or accept outside employment without the knowledge and consent of the University.

- To prevent conflict of interest, disruption of duties, or breach of service bond obligations.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**  | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

| Prohibition | No employee shall apply for, negotiate, or accept any post or employment in another organization without prior written approval from the University’s competent authority.                                                                                                     |
| Objective   | To ensure that the University can maintain staff continuity, plan for succession or replacement, and protect its operational and academic commitments.                                                                                                                         |

**3. ILLUSTRATIVE VIOLATIONS**

- Submitting falsified experience letters or misrepresenting employment status while applying elsewhere.

- Withholding information about having received an offer from another institution.

**Breach of Bond / Agreement**

- Applying externally before completing mandatory service period as per the bond or contract.

**Conflict of Interest**

- Applying to competing organizations or institutions while holding sensitive administrative or research positions in the University.

**4. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Nature of Offence**                                                     | **Action / Penalty**                                                                              |

| **Step**                              | **Procedure**                                                                                                              |

| 2\. Forwarding through Proper Channel | The request should be recommended by the HOD/Dean and forwarded to the Registrar or Vice Chancellor.                       |

**Policy on Unauthorized Absence from Appointed Place of Work**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To ensure discipline, accountability, and punctuality among all categories of University employees.

- To prevent disruption of academic, administrative, and technical activities due to unauthorized or habitual absence.

- To establish clear procedures and consequences related to absence without permission or valid cause from the assigned place of duty.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**  | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Definition  | An employee shall be deemed absent without permission when he/she is not present at the designated workplace or leaves the premises without prior approval from the competent authority during working hours.            |
| Scope       | This rule applies to all categories of employees—teaching, non-teaching, administrative, and technical—irrespective of employment status (permanent, temporary, contractual, or probationary).                           |
| Prohibition | No employee shall absent himself/herself from duty, leave the assigned station, or fail to report for work without written permission or sufficient cause. Such conduct constitutes misbehavior and dereliction of duty. |

**2. TYPES OF UNAUTHORIZED ABSENCE**

| **Type**                            | **Illustration**                                                                                                                   |
|-------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Short-Term Unauthorized Absence     | Leaving the classroom, laboratory, or office for an extended period during working hours without intimation or permission.         |
| Full-Day Unauthorized Absence       | Remaining absent for a full working day without prior approval or proper justification.                                            |
| Continuous Absence                  | Absence from duty for more than three consecutive working days without sanction of leave or sufficient cause.                      |
| Unauthorized Departure from Station | Leaving the city/town of posting without obtaining prior sanction from the competent authority, even during weekends or vacations. |
| Absence During Events or Duties     | Failing to attend assigned University events, examinations, invigilation duties, or official meetings without valid reason.        |

**3. CONDITIONS AND EXPECTATIONS**

| **Aspect**              | **Requirement**                                                                                                                                                                         |
|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Prior Permission        | Employees must obtain written approval (email or signed leave form) from their immediate superior or competent authority before leaving the workplace.                                  |
| Intimation of Emergency | In case of medical or personal emergencies, the employee must inform the Head of Department or Registrar immediately by phone or email, followed by a formal leave application.         |
| Attendance Recording    | All employees must sign the attendance register or use biometric systems at prescribed times daily. Unauthorized absence will be automatically recorded for disciplinary consideration. |
| Accountability          | Supervisors and Heads of Departments are responsible for reporting cases of unauthorized absence to the HR or Registrar’s office.                                                       |

**4. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Nature of Offence**               | **Action / Penalty**                                                                                              |
|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Isolated or First Instance          | Verbal warning or written caution; pay deduction for the period of unauthorized absence.                          |
| Repeated Unauthorized Absence       | Withholding of increment, entry of adverse remarks in the service record, or suspension pending inquiry.          |
| Prolonged Absence (beyond 3–7 days) | Treated as “willful absence” — employee deemed to have abandoned service or subject to termination after inquiry. |
| Leaving Premises Without Permission | Treated as misconduct — loss of pay for the day and disciplinary warning.                                         |
| Non-Reporting Post Leave            | Considered as “ovestaying leave” — disciplinary action including pay loss, censure, or suspension.                |

*Note: Absence without permission is treated as “Dies Non” (non-duty period), meaning the time lost does not count for increment, leave, or service continuity.*

**5. PROCEDURE FOR REPORTING AND ACTION**

| **Step**                  | **Procedure**                                                                                                                                              |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Reporting by Superior | The immediate superior (HOD/Dean/Supervisor) shall report the unauthorized absence to the Registrar’s Office within 24 hours.                              |
| 2\. Preliminary Notice    | A “Show Cause Notice” shall be issued to the concerned employee asking for justification within 3 working days.                                            |
| 3\. Review and Inquiry    | If the explanation is unsatisfactory, the matter shall be referred to the Disciplinary Committee for formal inquiry.                                       |
| 4\. Decision              | Based on findings, the competent authority shall impose appropriate penalties as per the Service Conduct Rules.                                            |
| 5\. Record Keeping        | HR Section shall record each instance of unauthorized absence in the Service and Attendance Register for reference during appraisal and promotion reviews. |

**6. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- Attendance and presence at the workplace are fundamental duties of every employee.

- Unauthorized absence is a sign of indiscipline and neglect of duty.

- Even a single day of absence without sanction is subject to deduction of pay and loss of service benefits.

- Emergency absence may be condoned only if supported by authentic proof and accepted by the competent authority.

- Continuous absence beyond three months without authorization will be treated as voluntary abandonment of service.

- The decision of the Board of Management (BoM) in such matters shall be final and binding.

- The decision of the Board of Management (BoM) regarding the interpretation, permission, or disciplinary action under this policy shall be final and binding.

**Policy on Commission of Acts Involving Criminal Offence or Moral Turpitude**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To uphold the moral integrity, ethical conduct, and legal compliance of all University employees.

- To establish clear provisions and consequences in cases where an employee commits or is convicted of a criminal offence involving moral turpitude.

- To preserve the reputation and credibility of the institution by ensuring that persons of high moral and ethical character remain in service.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**  | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Definition  | Any employee who commits or is convicted of a criminal offence involving moral turpitude—whether inside or outside the University—shall be deemed guilty of gross misconduct.                                                       |
| Scope       | Applies to all employees—teaching, non-teaching, technical, administrative, and contractual—irrespective of rank or designation.                                                                                                    |
| Prohibition | No employee shall engage in any act or omission that constitutes an offence involving dishonesty, corruption, moral depravity, or violation of law, whether punishable under the Indian Penal Code (IPC) or any other law in force. |

**2. WHAT CONSTITUTES “MORAL TURPITUDE”**

| **Nature of Offence**     | **Examples / Illustrations**                                                                        |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Dishonesty & Corruption   | Bribery, forgery, embezzlement, fraud, or misappropriation of institutional funds.                  |
| Sexual Misconduct         | Harassment, molestation, assault, or exploitation of students, colleagues, or others.               |
| Criminal Acts of Violence | Assault, intimidation, or acts leading to physical harm.                                            |
| Socially Immoral Acts     | Drug trafficking, gambling, or acts against public decency and morality.                            |
| Abuse of Position         | Using official authority for personal gain, or manipulation of institutional processes.             |
| Cyber or Data Crime       | Unauthorized access, data theft, or dissemination of confidential information for personal benefit. |

*Moral turpitude refers to acts that are inherently base, vile, or depraved, and contrary to accepted moral standards and ethical conduct.*

**3. PROCEDURE AND ADMINISTRATIVE ACTION**

| **Stage**                   | **Action / Responsibility**                                                                                                                                                  |
|-----------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Preliminary Information | Upon knowledge of an alleged criminal offence, the Registrar / Head of Institution shall immediately report the matter to the Vice Chancellor and Board of Management (BoM). |
| 2\. Suspension              | The concerned employee shall be placed under suspension with immediate effect pending investigation or trial, as per service rules.                                          |
| 3\. Departmental Enquiry    | A Disciplinary Committee shall be constituted to ascertain facts and decide whether continuation in service is in the University’s interest.                                 |
| 4\. Legal Coordination      | The University may cooperate with law enforcement agencies during the criminal investigation.                                                                                |
| 5\. Final Decision          | Based on the court’s verdict or inquiry findings, the BoM may impose suitable disciplinary action, including termination.                                                    |

**4. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Offence Status**                      | **Action / Penalty**                                                                          |
|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Employee Convicted by Court             | Immediate termination from service without notice, as per moral turpitude clause.             |
| Employee Under Criminal Investigation   | Placed under suspension pending final outcome. No reinstatement until acquittal or clearance. |
| Proven Misconduct by Internal Enquiry   | Major penalties such as removal, dismissal, or compulsory retirement.                         |
| Minor Offence (without moral turpitude) | May lead to censure, withholding of increments, or temporary suspension based on severity.    |

*A conviction by a court of law for any criminal offence involving moral turpitude shall automatically disqualify the employee from continued service.*

**5. IMPACT ON SERVICE RECORD**

- All employees convicted or dismissed under this clause shall be disqualified from re-employment in the University or any of its constituent institutions.

- Service during suspension pending investigation may not be counted for increments, pension, or leave benefits unless the employee is acquitted.

- In case of acquittal, reinstatement shall be subject to review and approval by the BoM, and not automatic.

**6. REPORTING OBLIGATION**

| **Condition**             | **Requirement**                                                                                                                               |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Arrest or Criminal Charge | The employee must immediately inform the Head of Department / Registrar about any arrest, charge, or court proceeding initiated against them. |
| Failure to Report         | Suppression of such information shall itself constitute separate misconduct punishable under University rules.                                |
| During Suspension         | The employee must not leave headquarters without written permission from the competent authority.                                             |

**7. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- The University expects integrity, moral uprightness, and lawful behavior from every employee.

- Any act involving moral corruption or legal violation undermines the trust and dignity of the institution.

- Employees must maintain conduct worthy of the profession of education, both inside and outside the workplace.

- Conviction or proven involvement in a criminal offence automatically disqualifies the employee from holding office.

- The decision of the Board of Management (BoM) shall be final and binding in determining whether an act involves moral turpitude.

**Policy on Abetment or Attempt of Any Act Amounting to Misconduct**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To preserve institutional discipline, professional integrity, and ethical behavior among all employees.

- To make clear that abetment (encouragement, assistance, or instigation) or attempt to commit any act of misconduct is itself a punishable offence, even if the act is not completed.

- To ensure that all members of the University community are held accountable not only for their actions but also for their intentions and influence in acts of indiscipline.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**           | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                         |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Definition           | Any act of abetment or attempt to commit an offence or misconduct, even if unsuccessful or incomplete, shall be treated as misconduct under University service rules. |
| Scope                | Applies to all categories of University employees—teaching, non-teaching, administrative, contractual, and technical—regardless of position or seniority.             |
| Nature of Misconduct | Includes any direct or indirect involvement, instigation, assistance, or collusion in an act that violates service conduct, ethics, or discipline.                    |

**2. MEANING OF ABETMENT AND ATTEMPT**

| **Term** | **Explanation / Illustration**                                               |
|----------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Abetment | Encouraging, aiding, or conspiring with another person to commit misconduct. |

- Providing information, resources, or moral support to facilitate the wrongdoing.

- Concealing evidence or failing to report a known act of misconduct.

**Attempt**

- Making deliberate efforts or preparations to commit an act of misconduct, even if not completed.

- Engaging in actions that show clear intention to violate institutional rules or service conduct.

**3. ILLUSTRATIVE INSTANCES**

| **Category**              | **Examples of Abetment or Attempt**                                                                  |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Academic Misconduct       | A faculty member helping another to leak question papers or manipulate examination results.          |
| Administrative Misconduct | An employee conspiring to forge documents or conceal institutional records.                          |
| Financial Misconduct      | Attempting to divert funds or helping another to embezzle University resources.                      |
| Ethical Misconduct        | Encouraging or protecting an offender involved in harassment, corruption, or defiance of authority.  |
| Operational Misconduct    | Failing to report a known violation, such as tampering with attendance registers or property damage. |

**4. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Type of Offence**                       | **Action / Penalty**                                                                   |
|-------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Attempted Misconduct (Unsuccessful)       | Treated at par with actual misconduct — warning, censure, or withholding of increment. |
| Abetment Leading to Misconduct by Another | Major penalty — suspension, demotion, or dismissal depending on severity.              |
| Conspiracy or Collusion                   | Considered gross misconduct, punishable by termination from service.                   |
| Failure to Report Known Misconduct        | Disciplinary warning or reduction of privileges; repeat offence treated as abetment.   |

*The seriousness of the punishment depends on the degree of involvement and the gravity of the misconduct abetted or attempted.*

**5. PROCEDURE FOR DISCIPLINARY ACTION**

| **Step**                   | **Process**                                                                                                                                 |
|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Preliminary Report     | The Head of Department or immediate supervisor shall report suspected abetment or attempt to the Registrar or Vice Chancellor.              |
| 2\. Inquiry Committee      | The Disciplinary Committee shall investigate the facts, review evidence, and determine whether intent and participation can be established. |
| 3\. Opportunity to Respond | The concerned employee shall be given a written notice and chance to explain before any disciplinary action is taken.                       |
| 4\. Decision               | Based on inquiry findings, the competent authority shall impose an appropriate penalty as per the Service Rules.                            |
| 5\. Appeal                 | The employee may file an appeal before the Board of Management (BoM) within 30 days of the order.                                           |

**6. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- The attempt or encouragement of misconduct is equally harmful to the institutional environment as the act itself.

- Every employee has a moral and professional obligation to uphold the University’s integrity and to report violations promptly.

- Concealment, connivance, or silent support of misconduct constitutes a breach of trust and is punishable.

- Disciplinary actions for abetment shall be proportionate to intent and consequence, ensuring fairness and due process.

- The decision of the Board of Management (BoM) on such matters shall be final and binding.

**Explanation: Definition and Scope of Sexual Harassment**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To provide a clear and comprehensive explanation of what constitutes sexual harassment in accordance with the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (POSH Act).

- To ensure that all employees understand the forms, scope, and implications of such misconduct and maintain professional, respectful, and safe workplace behavior.

**1. DEFINITION OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT**

For the purpose of this rule, “sexual harassment” includes any unwelcome sexually determined behavior, whether direct or indirect, verbal or non-verbal, physical or psychological, committed by any employee at the workplace.

Such conduct includes but is not limited to the following:

| **S.No.** | **Type of Conduct**                                                           | **Illustration / Description**                                                                                                                                                                    |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\.       | Physical contact and advances                                                 | Any unwanted or inappropriate physical contact such as touching, patting, pinching, brushing against, hugging, or any form of physical intrusion that violates personal boundaries.               |
| 2\.       | Demand or request for sexual favours                                          | Any demand, request, or suggestion (explicit or implied) for sexual cooperation in exchange for employment benefits, promotions, marks, or favorable treatment.                                   |
| 3\.       | Sexually colored remarks                                                      | Making obscene jokes, comments, innuendos, or remarks about a person’s body, appearance, gender, or sexuality.                                                                                    |
| 4\.       | Showing any pornography                                                       | Displaying, sharing, or circulating pornographic material—physically, digitally, or electronically—within workplace premises or through official communication channels.                          |
| 5\.       | Any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature | Includes gestures, sounds, suggestive messages, stalking, sending inappropriate texts or images, displaying sexually suggestive posters, or creating a hostile and intimidating work environment. |

**2. NATURE OF OFFENCE**

- The key factor is “unwelcome” — any conduct that is not solicited or invited, and is perceived as offensive, humiliating, or intimidating by the recipient.

- Sexual harassment may occur between any two individuals, regardless of hierarchy, gender, or status.

- It may take place:

<!-- -->

- At the workplace or during official travel, conferences, or events.

- Through digital platforms, emails, messages, or social media interactions.

**3. IMPACT AND CONTEXT**

- Even seemingly trivial acts, if repeated or unwanted, can create a hostile or intimidating environment.

- Such conduct can affect work performance, dignity, and psychological well-being, constituting a clear violation of institutional ethics.

- The University considers intent and impact equally important in determining the gravity of the offence.

**4. NOTE**

The above instances of misconduct are illustrative in nature and not exhaustive. Any other act or behavior of a sexually suggestive, humiliating, or intimidating nature—whether physical, verbal, or digital—shall also be treated as sexual harassment under this policy.

**Policy on Consumption of Intoxicating Drinks and Drugs**

**I. PURPOSE**

- To maintain a professional, healthy, and disciplined work environment within the University.

- To prohibit the use of intoxicating substances that can impair judgment, affect productivity, or harm institutional reputation.

- To ensure all employees uphold safety, responsibility, and sobriety during official duties and on University premises.

**1. GENERAL RULE**

| **Clause**                             | **Provision**                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
|----------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| i\. Prohibition within Premises        | No employee of the University shall consume or possess any intoxicating drinks or drugs within the University premises, hostels, laboratories, classrooms, offices, or residential quarters provided by the institution.               |
| ii\. Reporting to Duty under Influence | Employees are strictly prohibited from attending or performing official duties after consuming intoxicating drinks or drugs, regardless of time or place of consumption.                                                               |
| iii\. Influence during Duty Hours      | Employees shall not be under the influence of alcohol or drugs during office hours or while performing any University-related work, and must ensure that their conduct, performance, and safety are not compromised by such influence. |

**2. EXPLANATION OF TERMS**

| **Term**            | **Meaning / Clarification**                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Intoxicating Drinks | Includes alcoholic beverages such as wine, beer, liquor, or any drink containing alcohol capable of impairing judgment or conduct.                                                                                      |
| Drugs               | Refers to narcotics, psychotropic substances, or any substance prohibited under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985. This includes non-prescribed sedatives, hallucinogens, and stimulants. |
| Under the Influence | A condition in which an employee’s behavior, speech, alertness, or efficiency is adversely affected due to alcohol or drug consumption.                                                                                 |
| University Premises | Includes the entire campus area, academic and administrative buildings, hostels, staff quarters, vehicles, and any site under University control.                                                                       |

**3. CONDUCT EXPECTATIONS**

- Employees must demonstrate self-discipline and responsibility at all times.

- Possession or consumption of intoxicating substances, even in small quantities, tarnishes the institutional image and shall be viewed as gross misconduct.

- Employees undergoing prescribed medical treatment involving controlled substances must inform the competent authority in advance, providing a medical certificate.

- Employees representing the University at external events, meetings, or conferences must maintain sobriety and uphold institutional dignity.

**4. DISCIPLINARY CONSEQUENCES**

| **Nature of Violation**                            | **Action / Penalty**                                                                                             |
|----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Consumption within Campus or During Duty           | Treated as gross misconduct — immediate suspension pending inquiry.                                              |
| Reporting to Duty under Influence                  | Written warning for first instance; repetition may lead to withholding of increment, suspension, or termination. |
| Possession or Distribution of Intoxicants or Drugs | Severe misconduct — liable for dismissal from service and criminal prosecution under relevant laws.              |
| Impact on Performance or Safety                    | Disciplinary action based on the gravity of negligence or risk caused to others.                                 |
| Use of Prescribed Drugs without Declaration        | Treated as misconduct if it leads to impairment of work performance or behavior.                                 |

*The University enforces zero tolerance for drug or alcohol use that affects professional integrity, safety, or institutional reputation.*

**5. PROCEDURE FOR HANDLING VIOLATIONS**

| **Step**                | **Process**                                                                                                                                           |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Reporting           | Any instance of intoxication or possession must be reported by supervisors, security personnel, or colleagues to the Registrar or Head of Department. |
| 2\. Verification        | The Disciplinary Committee shall verify facts through observation, medical test (if necessary), and witness statements.                               |
| 3\. Show Cause Notice   | The employee shall be given an opportunity to explain in writing within 48 hours of notice.                                                           |
| 4\. Disciplinary Action | Based on inquiry findings, appropriate disciplinary measures will be taken in line with the Service Conduct and Disciplinary Rules.                   |
| 5\. Appeal              | The employee may appeal to the Board of Management (BoM) within 30 days of the decision.                                                              |

**6. POLICY PRINCIPLES**

- The University campus must remain a drug-free and alcohol-free zone.

- Employees are expected to act as role models for students by maintaining high ethical and behavioral standards.

- Violation of this rule affects individual health, safety, and institutional decorum, and will not be condoned under any circumstance.

- The University encourages rehabilitative counseling or medical support for employees with substance abuse issues, provided they voluntarily seek assistance before disciplinary proceedings begin.

- The decision of the Board of Management (BoM) regarding any violation shall be final and binding.
