## Analogy

> Directions: identify the relationship (or letter transformation) in the first pair and choose the option that completes the second pair the same way. 'A : B :: C : ?' reads 'A is to B as C is to ?'.  
Source: https://www.indiabix.com/verbal-reasoning/analogy/ (Analogy 1, pages 1–6)   •   28 questions   •   Every answer independently re-solved and cross-checked against the site key.

**Q1.** CUP : LIP :: BIRD : ?

- **(A)** BUSH
- **(B)** GRASS
- **(C)** FOREST
- **(D)** BEAK

**Answer:** D — Beak

**Explanation:** Body-part relationship: lips are used at the rim of a cup, and a beak is the mouth-part a bird uses. Answer: Beak.

---

**Q2.** Flow : River :: Stagnant : ?

## Blood Relation Test

> Directions: work out each family/coded relationship step by step and choose the option that matches. For coded questions, decode each symbol using the key given in the question.  
Source: https://www.indiabix.com/verbal-reasoning/blood-relation-test/ (Blood Relation 1, pages 1–10)   •   49 questions   •   Every answer independently re-solved and verified against the site.

**Q1.** While looking at a boy's photograph, Suresh said, “He is the son of my mother's only son.” How is Suresh related to the boy?

- **(A)** Brother
- **(B)** Uncle
- **(C)** Cousin
- **(D)** Father

**Answer:** D — Father

**Explanation:** Suresh's mother has just one son — Suresh himself. So the boy is Suresh's own son, which makes Suresh the boy's father.

---

**Q2.** Let A + B mean 'A is the mother of B', A − B mean 'A is the brother of B', A % B mean 'A is the father of B', and A × B mean 'A is the sister of B'. Which expression shows that P is the maternal uncle of Q?

- **(A)** Q - N + M x P
- **(B)** P + S x N - Q
- **(C)** P - M + N x Q
- **(D)** Q - S % P

**Answer:** C — P - M + N x Q

**Explanation:** Reading P − M + N × Q: P is M's brother, M is N's mother, and N is Q's sister. As M is mother to both N and Q, and P is her brother, P is the maternal uncle of Q.

---

**Q3.** A is the brother of B, B is the sister of C, and C is the father of D. How is D related to A?

- **(A)** Brother
- **(B)** Sister
- **(C)** Nephew
- **(D)** Cannot be determined

**Answer:** D — Cannot be determined

**Explanation:** A, B and C are siblings and D is C's child, so D is A's nephew or niece. Since D's gender is not stated, the exact relationship cannot be fixed.

---

**Q4.** Let A + B mean 'A is the brother of B', A − B mean 'A is the sister of B', and A × B mean 'A is the father of B'. Which option means C is the son of M?

- **(A)** M - N x C + F
- **(B)** F - C + N x M
- **(C)** N + M - F x C
- **(D)** M x N - C + F

**Answer:** D — M x N - C + F

**Explanation:** In M × N − C + F: M is N's father, N is C's sister, and C is F's brother. So M fathers C, and since C is male, C is the son of M.

---

**Q5.** Introducing a boy, a girl said, “He is the son of the daughter of my uncle's father.” How is the boy related to the girl?

- **(A)** Brother
- **(B)** Nephew
- **(C)** Uncle
- **(D)** Son-in-law

**Answer:** A — Brother

**Explanation:** The father of the girl's uncle is her grandfather, and the only daughter of that grandfather is the girl's own mother. The boy, being that woman's son, is the girl's brother.

---

**Q6.** Pointing at a photograph, Lata said, “He is the son of my grandfather's only son.” How is the man in the photo related to Lata?

- **(A)** Brother
- **(B)** Uncle
- **(C)** Cousin
- **(D)** Data is inadequate

**Answer:** A — Brother

**Explanation:** Lata's grandfather's only son is Lata's father, so the man is her father's son — that is, Lata's brother.

---

**Q7.** Let A + B mean 'A is the brother of B', A × B mean 'A is the son of B', and A % B mean 'B is the daughter of A'. Which option means M is the maternal uncle of N?

- **(A)** M + O x N
- **(B)** M % O x N + P
- **(C)** M + O % N
- **(D)** None of these

**Answer:** D — None of these

**Explanation:** For M to be the maternal uncle of N, M must be the brother of N's mother. In every option the sex of the middle person (O) is left undefined, so O cannot be confirmed as N's mother — no code establishes the required link.

**Q8.** Given that D is the brother of B, which statement(s) are needed to decide how B is related to C? (1) D's son is C's grandson. (2) B is the sister of D.

- **(A)** Only 1
- **(B)** Only 2
- **(C)** Either 1 or 2
- **(D)** 1 and 2 both are required

**Answer:** D — 1 and 2 both are required

**Explanation:** Statement 1 shows D is C's child (D's son being C's grandson). Statement 2 shows B is female. Only by combining both do we learn B is C's daughter, so both are required.

**Q9.** Let A + B mean 'A is the father of B', A − B mean 'A is the brother of B', A % B mean 'A is the wife of B', and A × B mean 'A is the mother of B'. Which option shows M is the maternal grandmother of T?

- **(A)** M x N % S + T
- **(B)** M x N - S % T
- **(C)** M x S - N % T
- **(D)** M x N x S % T

**Answer:** A — M x N % S + T

**Explanation:** In M × N % S + T: M is N's mother, N is S's wife, and S is T's father. So N is T's mother and M is N's mother, making M the maternal grandmother of T.

## Syllogism

> Directions: treat both statements as true (even if unrealistic), then decide which conclusion(s) must logically follow. A = only (1); B = only (2); C = either (1) or (2); D = neither; E = both.

**Q1.** Statements: Some actors are singers. All the singers are dancers.  
Conclusions: 1. Some actors are dancers.  2. No singer is an actor.

- **(A)** Only (1) conclusion follows
- **(B)** Only (2) conclusion follows
- **(C)** Either (1) or (2) follows
- **(D)** Neither (1) nor (2) follows
- **(E)** Both (1) and (2) follow

**Answer:** A — Only (1) conclusion follows

**Explanation:** The actors who are singers must be dancers (all singers are dancers), so 'some actors are dancers' holds. Conclusion 2 directly contradicts the premise that some actors are singers, so it fails. Only (1) follows.

**Q2.** Statements: All the harmoniums are instruments. All the instruments are flutes.

**Explanation:** Harmoniums ⊆ instruments ⊆ flutes, so all harmoniums are flutes — conclusion 2 holds. Conclusion 1 is the invalid converse of 'all instruments are flutes' (flutes need not all be instruments). Only (2) follows.

## Cube and Cuboid

> Directions: all five questions refer to ONE painted cuboid. A wooden block 6 cm long, 4 cm wide, 1 cm high has its two 4x1 cm faces painted BLACK, its two 6x1 cm faces RED, and its two 6x4 cm faces GREEN. It is cut into 1 cm cubes (6 x 4 x 1 = 24 small cubes).  
Source: https://www.indiabix.com/verbal-reasoning/cube-and-cuboid/ (Cube and Cuboid 1)   •   5 questions   •   Every answer independently re-solved and verified.

**Q1.** How many of the small cubes will have red, green and black colour on at least one side each?

- **(A)** 16
- **(B)** 12
- **(C)** 10
- **(D)** 4

**Answer:** D — 4

**Explanation:** A cube showing all three colours must sit at a corner of the cuboid, where a black end face, a red side face and a green top/bottom face all meet. A cuboid has 4 such corner cubes in this single layer, so the answer is 4.

---

**Q2.** How many small cubes will be formed in all?

- **(A)** 6
- **(B)** 12
- **(C)** 16
- **(D)** 24

**Answer:** D — 24

**Explanation:** Cutting the block into 1 cm cubes gives length × breadth × height = 6 × 4 × 1 = 24 small cubes.

---

**Q3.** How many cubes will have 4 coloured sides and 2 uncoloured sides?

- **(A)** 8
- **(B)** 4
- **(C)** 16
- **(D)** 10

**Answer:** B — 4

**Explanation:** Only the corner cubes get four painted faces (green top and bottom, one red side and one black end) while their two inward faces stay unpainted. There are 4 corner cubes, so the answer is 4.

---

**Q4.** How many cubes will have green colour on two sides and the remaining four sides without any colour?

- **(A)** 12
- **(B)** 10
- **(C)** 8
- **(D)** 4

**Answer:** C — 8

**Explanation:** Every cube in this one-layer block already has green on its top and bottom. Those with no other colour are the interior cubes touching no side or end: (6−2) × (4−2) = 4 × 2 = 8.

**Q5.** How many cubes will remain if all the cubes having black and green colour are removed?

- **(A)** 4
- **(B)** 8
- **(C)** 12
- **(D)** 16

**Answer:** D — 16

**Explanation:** The cubes carrying black (which are also green) lie along the two 4-cube end columns, giving 8 such cubes. Removing them from the 24 leaves 24 − 8 = 16.

## Direction Sense Test

**Q1.** One morning Udai and Vishal stood at a crossing, facing each other. If Vishal's shadow fell exactly to Udai's left, which direction was Udai facing?

- **(A)** East
- **(B)** West
- **(C)** North
- **(D)** South

**Answer:** C — North

**Explanation:** At sunrise the sun is in the east, so shadows fall towards the west. For Vishal's (westward) shadow to lie on Udai's left, Udai must be facing North.

- **(C)** South-East
- **(D)** None of these

**Answer:** D — None of these

**Explanation:** Placing X at the centre: Y is east of X, Z is south of X, and P is further south of Z. So P lies to the south and west of Y — that is, South-West, which is not listed.

**Q3.** If South-East becomes North, North-East becomes West, and so on, what will West become?

- **(A)** North-East
- **(B)** North-West
- **(C)** South-East
- **(D)** South-West

**Answer:** C — South-East

**Explanation:** Each direction shifts by the same amount that carries South-East to North (a 135° clockwise turn). Applying that same shift to West lands on South-East.

**Answer:** D — South-West

**Explanation:** South 5 km, then (facing west) 3 km west, then (facing south) 5 km south leaves him west and south of the start — the South-West direction.

**Explanation:** With 6 o'clock aligned to north, the dial is rotated 180° from normal, so the 3 position points west. At 9:15 the minute hand sits on 3, hence it points West.

**Q1.** The ages of Amar, Akbar and Anthony add up to 80 years. What was the sum of their ages three years ago?

- **(A)** 71 years
- **(B)** 72 years
- **(C)** 74 years
- **(D)** 77 years

**Answer:** A — 71 years

**Explanation:** Three years ago each of the three was 3 years younger, so the total was 80 − (3 × 3) = 80 − 9 = 71 years.

**Q2.** Two tickets from city A to B and three from A to C cost Rs. 77, while three from A to B and two from A to C cost Rs. 73. What are the fares to B and C from A?

- **(A)** Rs. 4, Rs. 23
- **(B)** Rs. 13, Rs. 17
- **(C)** Rs. 15, Rs. 14
- **(D)** Rs. 17, Rs. 13

**Answer:** B — Rs. 13, Rs. 17

**Explanation:** With fares x (to B) and y (to C): 2x + 3y = 77 and 3x + 2y = 73. Eliminating x gives 5y = 85, so y = 17 and x = 13.
