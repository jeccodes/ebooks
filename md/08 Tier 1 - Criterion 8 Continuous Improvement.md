**Abstract**

Criterion 8: *Continuous Improvement* is a pivotal component of the NBA accreditation framework, carrying a weightage of 80 marks. It represents the essence of the Outcome-Based Education (OBE) philosophy by evaluating how effectively an institution transforms assessment data and evaluation results into structured corrective actions that lead to measurable progress. This criterion goes beyond routine assessment to test the institution’s ability to critically reflect on its practices, identify areas of weakness, implement evidence-based interventions, and demonstrate tangible improvements across student learning, faculty development, and institutional performance. By emphasizing quality enhancement as a continuous and dynamic process, Criterion 8 ensures that higher education institutions function as evolving ecosystems of learning, innovation, and accountability.

The core objective of Criterion 8 is to assess the institution’s effectiveness in operating within the OBE framework by examining improvements at the Course Outcome (CO), Program Outcome (PO), and Program Specific Outcome (PSO) levels, while also integrating academic audit findings, faculty growth indicators, and student performance metrics. The evaluation is divided into four major sub-criteria. The first, Actions Taken Based on CO, PO, and PSO Attainment (40 marks), emphasizes semester-wise reviews of CO attainment, identification of gaps between targets and actual outcomes, and corrective measures such as remedial classes, revised teaching methodologies, or enhanced practical exposure. At the program level, annual PO/PSO attainment is aggregated, weak outcomes are identified, and structured two-year action plans are implemented to demonstrate long-term improvements. The second sub-criterion, Academic Audit and Actions Taken (15 marks), assesses the robustness of internal and external audits in reviewing teaching–learning, curriculum delivery, and outcome attainment, and examines the effectiveness of corrective actions arising from audit recommendations. The third, Improvement in Faculty Qualification and Contribution (15 marks), highlights institutional efforts to strengthen faculty capacity, including increased faculty strength, completion of higher qualifications such as Ph.Ds., growth in research output, and professional contributions through Faculty Development Programs (FDPs), professional memberships, and industry linkages. The final component, Improvement in Academic Performance (10 marks), measures year-wise progress in student outcomes, primarily through pass percentages, the Academic Performance Index (API), and reduced dropout rates, thereby reflecting the impact of institutional reforms on student success.

A distinctive feature of Criterion 8 is its emphasis on the *“closing the loop”* approach. This process entails systematic evaluation of outcomes, identification of gaps, planning and implementation of corrective actions, and subsequent re-assessment to verify improvements. Institutions are expected to maintain comprehensive documentation to substantiate their claims of continuous improvement, including attainment reports, Action Taken Reports (ATRs) from departmental and institutional meetings, records of remedial classes and student support activities, evidence of workshops and FDPs, and comparative attainment charts demonstrating *before vs. after* progress. In doing so, Criterion 8 ensures that quality enhancement is not treated as a one-time exercise but as an embedded, ongoing culture within the institution.

In summary, Criterion 8 functions as a powerful mechanism to foster accountability, transparency, and progress within higher education institutions. By requiring demonstrable evidence of corrective action and improvement, it ensures that institutions remain dynamic, student-centered, and aligned with both national and global standards of academic excellence.

# **1. Introduction**

Criterion 8, titled *“Continuous Improvement,”* is a vital component of the NBA accreditation framework, carrying a total of 80 marks. This criterion captures the essence of the outcome-based education (OBE) philosophy by ensuring that institutions do not merely assess student learning but also utilize evaluation results to identify weaknesses, implement corrective actions, and demonstrate measurable improvements over time. It emphasizes that quality education is not static but rather a dynamic, iterative process of reflection, action, and enhancement. Through this criterion, the NBA seeks to determine whether an institution systematically “closes the loop” in its assessment processes by connecting outcome attainment data with actionable strategies for continuous quality improvement (CQI).

The core purpose of Criterion 8 is to evaluate how effectively an institution functions within the OBE framework by analyzing attainment at the Course Outcome (CO), Program Outcome (PO), and Program Specific Outcome (PSO) levels. It specifically examines whether the results of outcome attainment are meaningfully employed to identify areas of concern, implement remedial actions, and demonstrate tangible improvements in student learning, faculty performance, and institutional processes. Institutions are thus required to go beyond measuring attainment and provide evidence of corrective interventions, sustained monitoring, and year-on-year progress.

Criterion 8 is systematically structured into several critical components that collectively reflect the institution’s capacity for continuous improvement. The largest portion of marks is allocated to Actions Taken Based on the Results of Evaluation of COs, POs, and PSOs (40 marks). This section is further divided into two parts: *Actions Based on CO Attainment (20 marks)*, which focuses on semester-wise evaluation of course outcomes, identification of gaps, and corrective measures such as remedial classes, curriculum redesign, or additional practice sessions; and *Actions Based on PO/PSO Attainment (20 marks)*, which emphasizes annual aggregation of PO/PSO attainment, identification of weak POs/PSOs, and a structured two-year cycle of corrective actions to ensure measurable improvement at the program level.

In addition to outcome-based corrective actions, Criterion 8 also evaluates broader institutional processes. Academic Audit and Actions Taken (15 marks) assess the robustness of internal and external audits in reviewing teaching–learning processes, curriculum delivery, and outcome attainment. Institutions are expected to document audit recommendations, implement them effectively, and track improvements over subsequent cycles. Improvement in Faculty Qualification and Contribution (15 marks) is another important area, emphasizing year-wise enhancements in faculty strength, advanced qualifications such as Ph.D. completions, research contributions in the form of publications and patents, and professional development through Faculty Development Programs (FDPs), workshops, and professional body memberships. This ensures that faculty growth directly contributes to improving teaching quality and institutional reputation. Improvement in Academic Performance (10 marks) focuses on measurable progress in student outcomes, typically demonstrated through pass percentages across successive years, the Academic Performance Index (API), and reduced dropout rates.

A consistent theme across Criterion 8 is the emphasis on evidence-based improvement. Institutions are expected to maintain and present documented proof such as attainment reports, Action Taken Reports (ATRs), records of remedial interventions, evidence of curriculum revisions, FDP participation records, audit reports, and comparative attainment charts that clearly demonstrate the positive impact of implemented measures. This structured approach ensures that continuous improvement is not an abstract idea but a documented and measurable reality within the institution.

In essence, Criterion 8 operationalizes the philosophy of continuous quality enhancement by embedding the principles of reflection, corrective action, and measurable progress into the academic ecosystem. It positions institutions as dynamic entities that evolve in response to evidence of student learning and institutional performance, thereby ensuring that higher education programs remain relevant, accountable, and aligned with both national priorities and global benchmarks of quality.

# **2. Criterion 8: Continuous Improvement**

This criterion checks whether the institution is actively using evaluation results (COs, POs, PSOs, audits, faculty/student performance) to implement corrective actions and demonstrate measurable improvement.

### **1. Actions Taken Based on the Results of Evaluation of the COs, POs, and PSOs (40 Marks)**

***a) Actions Based on CO Attainment (20 Marks)***

- **What it means:**

<!-- -->

- After each semester, CO attainment is evaluated (direct + indirect methods).

- Gaps are identified between target vs actual attainment.

- Corrective measures are implemented to improve weak areas.

<!-- -->

- **Examples of Weaknesses Identified:**

<!-- -->

- CO2 (Data Structures) – weak in coding implementation.

- CO5 (Thermodynamics) – low attainment due to lack of practical exposure.

<!-- -->

- **Actions Taken:**

<!-- -->

- Curriculum Interventions → Adding lab assignments, bridge courses, coding bootcamps.

- Pedagogical Initiatives → ICT-enabled teaching, flipped classrooms, group projects.

- Support Systems → Remedial classes for slow learners, peer mentoring.

<!-- -->

- **Evidence Required:**

<!-- -->

- CO attainment reports (course-wise, year-wise).

- Action Taken Reports (ATR) of faculty meetings.

- Remedial class/workshop records.

- Before vs. After attainment charts.

***b) Actions Based on PO/PSO Attainment (20 Marks)***

- **What it means:**

<!-- -->

- Department reviews aggregated PO/PSO attainment every year.

- Weak POs (e.g., teamwork, communication, research skills) are identified.

- Measures are implemented to improve them over 2 years.

<!-- -->

- **Examples of Weaknesses:**

<!-- -->

- PO4 (Investigation of problems) – weak due to lack of open-ended experiments.

- PO9 (Communication skills) – low attainment in technical writing.

- PSO1 (Domain Tools) – poor exposure to industry-standard software.

<!-- -->

- **Actions Taken:**

<!-- -->

- Introduced mini-projects, simulation-based experiments, and internships.

- Conducted seminars, technical report writing workshops.

- Upgraded labs with industry-standard tools (MATLAB, PSPICE, ANSYS).

- Made MOOCs mandatory for lifelong learning (PO12).

<!-- -->

- **Evidence Required:**

<!-- -->

- PO/PSO attainment reports.

- DAB, BoS, Academic Council meeting minutes with ATR.

- Records of internships, industry projects, FDPs.

- Improved attainment charts (before vs. after).

### **2. Academic Audit and Actions Taken During the Period of Assessment (15 Marks)**

- **Definition:** Academic Audit is a structured review of teaching-learning, curriculum delivery, CO/PO attainment, assessment quality, faculty development, and student support.

- **Process:**

1.  Audit Planning → IQAC/HoD sets framework.

2.  Audit Execution → Internal + external experts review CO-PO mapping, attainment, teaching files, assessments.

3.  Findings → Strengths, weaknesses, gaps noted.

4.  Recommendations → Suggested corrective measures.

5.  Actions Taken → Implemented in following semester/year.

- **Examples of Actions Taken:**

6.  Introduced FDP on Bloom’s Taxonomy for assessment design.

7.  Bridge courses in programming & mathematics.

8.  Mentoring and remedial classes.

9.  Curriculum updated with industry-relevant topics.

- **Evidence Required:**

10. Academic Audit Reports (year-wise).

11. Minutes of Audit Meetings with recommendations.

12. ATR on audit findings.

13. Improved attainment records.

### **3. Improvement in Faculty Qualification/Contribution (15 Marks)**

- **Objective:** To show improvement in faculty strength, qualifications, research, and professional contributions.

- **Parameters:**

<!-- -->

- Faculty completing Ph.D./Post-Doc during assessment.

- Increase in peer-reviewed journal & conference publications.

- Patents filed/granted, books authored, consultancy projects.

- Faculty attending FDPs/STTPs/MOOCs.

- Membership in professional bodies (IEEE, ISTE, ACM).

<!-- -->

- **Evidence Required:**

<!-- -->

- Ph.D. award letters/registration certificates.

- Publication proofs (DOI, Scopus/WoS indexing).

- FDP/STTP/MOOC certificates.

- Excel trackers showing year-wise improvement.

### **4. Improvement in Academic Performance (10 Marks)**

- **Objective:** To track academic growth of students across 1st, 2nd, and 3rd years.

- **Measured via:**

<!-- -->

- Pass % (successful students / appeared).

- Academic Performance Index (API):

- API=(Mean GPA/10)×(No. of successful students/No. appeared)

- Year-wise improvement trends (CAY, CAYm1, CAYm2).

<!-- -->

- **Examples of Improvement Actions:**

<!-- -->

- Bridge courses & remedial classes for weak students.

- Peer mentoring and academic counseling.

- ICT-enabled learning and workshops.

- Industry projects in higher years to improve engagement.

<!-- -->

- **Evidence Required:**

<!-- -->

- Academic Performance Tracker (year-wise).

- API calculations for 1st, 2nd, 3rd year.

- Remedial/Bridge course records.

- Improvement charts (CAYm2 → CAYm1 → CAY).

### **5. NBA Scoring Distribution**

- Actions taken on CO Attainment → 20 Marks.

- Actions taken on PO/PSO Attainment → 20 Marks.

- Academic Audit & Actions Taken → 15 Marks.

- Improvement in Faculty Qualification/Contribution → 15 Marks.

- Improvement in Academic Performance → 10 Marks.

- **Total = 80 Marks**

# **3. Actions Taken Based on the Results of Evaluation of the COs, POs, and PSOs**

### **1. Context & Purpose**

- NBA accreditation is outcome-based → focuses on what students are able to achieve (attain) at the end of a course (CO), program (PO), and program-specific area (PSO).

- This criterion assesses continuous improvement, i.e., how the institution uses attainment results to identify weaknesses, implement corrective actions, and measure improvements.

- Marks (40) are awarded based on systematic process, corrective actions, evidence, and demonstrated impact.

### **2. Evaluation Process**

- **CO Attainment**

<!-- -->

- Direct: internal tests, end-semester exams, assignments, labs, projects.

- Indirect: student surveys, course exit surveys.

<!-- -->

- **PO & PSO Attainment**

<!-- -->

- Aggregated CO attainment mapped to POs/PSOs.

- Supported by project evaluations, internships, employability, surveys (alumni, employers).

<!-- -->

- **Gap Analysis**

<!-- -->

- Benchmark: e.g., 70% target.

- If actual \< target → identify shortfall.

- Gaps classified: conceptual, practical, analytical, communication, industry-exposure related.

### **3. Areas of Weakness Identified**

1.  **CO Level**

- Weak understanding of higher-order concepts (recursion, thermodynamics, simulations).

- Poor performance in problem-solving or lab-based COs.

- Gaps in project-based COs requiring innovation/creativity.

2.  **PO Level**

- Low attainment in PO4 (Investigations), PO9 (Communication), PO12 (Lifelong learning).

- Students underperforming in teamwork, ethics, and project management.

3.  **PSO Level**

- Lack of domain-specific tools and advanced technology usage.

- Limited exposure to current industry practices.

### **4. Actions Taken**

**A. Curriculum Interventions**

- Revised syllabi to include advanced/emerging topics.

- Introduced bridge courses to strengthen fundamentals.

- Added value-added courses (MOOCs, NPTEL, Coursera) mapped to specific POs/PSOs.

**B. Pedagogical Initiatives**

- Adopted ICT-enabled learning (simulations, virtual labs, coding platforms).

- Implemented flipped classrooms, project-based learning, and case studies.

- Introduced rubrics for evaluating communication, ethics, teamwork.

**C. Student Support Measures**

- Conducted remedial classes for slow learners.

- Organized workshops, technical training, hackathons.

- Established mentoring system for personalized guidance.

- Soft skills and employability training.

**D. Faculty Development**

- FDPs on Outcome-Based Education (OBE), pedagogy, and advanced tools.

- Industry internships for faculty to align with real-world practices.

**E. Industry & Research Exposure**

- MoUs with industries → internships & live projects.

- Invited industry experts for seminars and guest lectures.

- Encouraged student mini-projects, publications, and startup incubation.

### **5. Documentation & Evidence**

- CO Attainment Reports (course-wise, year-wise).

- PO/PSO Attainment Analysis (aggregated over years).

- Action Taken Reports (ATRs) from Faculty Meetings & DAB discussions.

- Records of interventions → remedial classes, bridge courses, FDPs, workshops.

- Improved attainment charts (before vs. after).

- Feedback forms from students, alumni, employers confirming effectiveness.

### **6. Demonstrated Impact**

- **CO Improvement:** Example – CO2 in Data Structures improved from 52% → 69% after weekly coding challenges.

- **PO Improvement:** Example – PO4 (Investigations) improved from 55% → 68% after introducing simulation labs.

- **PSO Improvement:** Example – PSO1 (Domain-specific tools) improved from 52% → 66% after installing industry-grade software.

- **Indirect Indicators:**

<!-- -->

- Better student feedback.

- Increased placements and higher studies admissions.

- Research outputs and innovation activities increased.

### **7. Marking Distribution (40 Marks)**

- Systematic evaluation and identification of gaps – 10 marks.

- Corrective actions taken at CO, PO, and PSO levels – 10 marks.

- Evidence of implementation (ATRs, remedial records, FDP reports) – 10 marks.

- Demonstrated impact through improved attainment charts – 10 marks.

# **4. Actions Taken Based on the Results of Evaluation of the COs Attainment**

**Context and Purpose**

- Course Outcomes (COs) describe what a student is expected to learn by the end of a course.

- Institutions must evaluate the attainment of COs every semester/year through direct (exam results, assignments, lab work) and indirect (surveys, feedback) assessments.

- This criterion emphasizes how weaknesses are identified from CO attainment analysis and how institutions implement corrective measures to ensure continuous improvement.

**Evaluation of CO Attainment**

- **Direct Methods:**

<!-- -->

- Internal assessments, semester-end examinations, lab work, projects, viva, class performance.

- Rubrics-based assessment of experiments, presentations, assignments.

<!-- -->

- **Indirect Methods:**

<!-- -->

- Student surveys, course exit surveys, alumni feedback, employer input.

<!-- -->

- **Benchmarks/Thresholds:**

<!-- -->

- Example: If 70% of students scoring ≥ 50 marks is the target, but only 55% achieved it, then a gap exists.

<!-- -->

- **Gap Analysis:**

<!-- -->

- Identify specific topics or skills where students underperformed.

- Classify weaknesses as conceptual gaps, lack of practical exposure, or inadequate higher-order skill development.

**Areas of Weakness Identified (Examples)**

4.  Low attainment in analytical/problem-solving oriented COs.

5.  Poor performance in application-based/laboratory COs.

6.  Weak fundamentals in mathematical and programming-related COs.

7.  Difficulty in higher Bloom’s Taxonomy levels (Analyze, Evaluate, Create).

8.  Limited exposure to industry practices in project-related COs.

**Measures Taken / Improvement Actions**

9.  **Curriculum Intervention**

- Revision of syllabi to include additional topics or case studies.

- Introduction of bridge courses in mathematics, coding, or communication.

- Integration of emerging technology tools/software in labs.

10. **Pedagogical Initiatives**

- Use of ICT-enabled teaching (simulations, virtual labs, video lectures).

- Flipped classroom approach and problem-based learning.

- Assigning mini-projects, design tasks, and open-ended experiments.

11. **Support System Improvements**

- Remedial classes for slow learners.

- Special tutorials, doubt-clearing sessions, peer-assisted learning.

- Mentoring for academically weak students.

12. **Assessment Reforms**

- Redesigning question papers to balance Bloom’s levels.

- Rubrics for evaluating higher-order skills in labs/projects.

- Frequent low-stakes quizzes to improve learning retention.

13. **Faculty Development**

- Training workshops on pedagogy, digital tools, and outcome-based teaching.

- Interactions with industry experts for aligning teaching with real-world applications.

14. **Student Exposure and Motivation**

- Guest lectures, industrial visits, and internships.

- Encouraging participation in hackathons, coding challenges, innovation contests.

- Counseling and motivational sessions to reduce dropouts.

**Evidence and Documentation**

- CO Attainment Reports (course-wise, year-wise).

- Action Taken Reports (ATR) of faculty meetings, DAB (Department Advisory Board) discussions.

- Records of remedial classes, workshops, bridge courses, FDPs.

- Improved attainment charts after actions were implemented.

- Feedback forms from students and stakeholders confirming effectiveness.

**Impact Demonstration**

- Improved CO attainment in the next cycle (comparison of CAYm1 with previous years).

- Increased pass percentages and reduction in failure rates.

- Higher student satisfaction in feedback surveys.

- Enhanced placement, internships, or project outcomes linked to improved COs.

**Marking Distribution (20 Marks)**

- **Identification of Weaknesses (5 marks) –** Based on data-driven gap analysis.

- **Corrective Actions Taken (5 marks) –** Clearly documented measures.

- **Implementation Evidence (5 marks) –** Proof of remedial classes, FDPs, interventions.

- **Impact/Improvement Shown (5 marks) –** Demonstrated improvement in CO attainment.

# **5. Action Taken Reports (ATR) of Faculty Meetings and DAB (Department Advisory Board) Discussions**

### **1. Purpose of ATR in NBA**

- ATR demonstrates that feedback and evaluation of CO/PO/PSO attainment are not just collected but acted upon.

- Provides evidence of continuous improvement through faculty and board-level discussions.

- Shows how decisions taken in meetings translate into measurable actions.

### **2. Key Elements of ATR**

Each ATR should include:

- **Meeting Details**

<!-- -->

- Meeting Type: Faculty / DAB / BoS / Academic Council

- Date, Time, Venue

- Chairperson & Attendees

<!-- -->

- **Agenda Points Discussed**

<!-- -->

- Review of CO attainment gaps

- Feedback from stakeholders (students, alumni, employers)

- Identification of weak areas in program/course delivery

<!-- -->

- **Decisions Taken**

<!-- -->

- Specific actions approved for curriculum, pedagogy, labs, student support

<!-- -->

- **Action Taken**

<!-- -->

- What was implemented after the decision (remedial classes, lab upgrades, FDPs, guest lectures)

<!-- -->

- **Evidence/Documentation**

<!-- -->

- Links to attendance sheets, minutes, remedial timetables, workshop circulars, feedback reports

<!-- -->

- **Impact/Outcome**

<!-- -->

- Improvement in CO attainment, student feedback, placement outcomes

# **9. Actions Taken Based on the Results of Evaluation of the POs/PSOs Attainment**

### **1. Context & Purpose**

- Program Outcomes (POs) and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs) are broader competencies (knowledge, skills, attitudes) expected from graduates.

- NBA requires institutions to evaluate attainment levels of POs/PSOs and take corrective actions if gaps exist.

- This criterion evaluates the closing-the-loop mechanism at the program level, focusing on continuous improvement over two years.

### **2. Evaluation Process of POs/PSOs**

- **Direct Assessment:**

<!-- -->

- Aggregated CO attainment mapped to relevant POs/PSOs.

- Capstone projects, internships, labs, and design-oriented activities.

<!-- -->

- **Indirect Assessment:**

<!-- -->

- Exit surveys, alumni feedback, employer surveys, placement statistics, higher studies.

<!-- -->

- **Benchmarking & Gap Analysis:**

<!-- -->

- Targets set (e.g., 70%).

- Actual attainment calculated.

- Identify gaps (e.g., PO4 “Conducting Investigations” attained at 55% vs. target 70%).

### **3. Areas of Weakness Typically Identified**

22. **Technical/Problem-Solving Skills (PO2, PO3, PO4, PO5):**

- Students struggle with analytical/problem-solving tasks.

23. **Research Orientation & Lifelong Learning (PO4, PO12):**

- Limited exposure to open-ended investigations or self-directed learning.

24. **Communication & Teamwork (PO9, PO10):**

- Weak presentation, report writing, and team collaboration skills.

25. **Ethics & Professional Responsibility (PO8):**

- Need reinforcement of professional ethics and sustainability practices.

26. **Program-Specific Outcomes (PSOs):**

- Lack of industry-grade software/tools.

- Limited exposure to latest domain technologies.

### **4. Measures Identified and Implemented (Two Years)**

27. **Curriculum Interventions**

- Introduced electives on emerging technologies (AI/ML, IoT, Sustainability).

- Revision of lab manuals to include industry-oriented experiments.

- Integration of mini-projects & case studies into courses.

- Inclusion of multidisciplinary and open electives.

28. **Pedagogical Initiatives**

- Shift from lecture-based to active learning (flipped classroom, problem-based learning).

- Adoption of ICT tools (Moodle, virtual labs, coding platforms, simulation software).

- Rubrics-based assessment to measure teamwork, ethics, and communication.

- Guest lectures and expert talks linked to specific POs/PSOs.

29. **Support System Improvements**

- Remedial classes and bridge courses for weak students.

- Peer-assisted learning groups and mentoring system.

- Soft-skills and communication training modules.

- Career guidance and placement support for industry-readiness.

30. **Faculty Development**

- FDPs on Outcome-Based Education and new pedagogies.

- Industry training for faculty to align teaching with real-world practices.

- Internal workshops on assessment of higher-level POs (ethics, lifelong learning).

31. **Industry & Research Linkages**

- MoUs with industries for internships and live projects.

- Hackathons, ideation camps, and innovation challenges aligned with POs.

- Encouraging student research publications and conference participation.

### **5. Documentation and Evidence**

- PO/PSO Attainment Analysis Reports (year-wise).

- Action Taken Reports (ATR) from Department Advisory Board (DAB), BoS, Academic Council.

- **Evidence of interventions:**

<!-- -->

- Remedial/bridge course attendance.

- FDP/workshop circulars & reports.

- Internship/project completion reports.

- Student/alumni/employer feedback forms.

<!-- -->

- Improvement Charts: PO/PSO attainment before vs. after interventions (CAYm2 → CAYm1 → CAY).

### **6. Impact & Continuous Improvement**

- **Demonstrated Improvements:**

<!-- -->

- Example: PO4 (Investigation of problems) improved from 55% → 68% over two years due to simulation labs & projects.

- PO9 (Communication) improved due to seminar courses & presentation rubrics.

- PSO1 (Domain-specific tools) improved after industry tie-ups & new lab installations.

<!-- -->

- **Closing the Loop:**

<!-- -->

- Actions → Implementation → Evidence → Measurable improvement → Reviewed in next cycle.

### **7. Marking Scheme (20 Marks)**

- Identification of weaknesses (based on PO/PSO evaluation) → 5 Marks.

- Corrective actions & implementation across two years → 5 Marks.

- Documentation & evidence (ATRs, remedial/workshop records, FDPs) → 5 Marks.

- Demonstrated impact with improved attainment levels → 5 Marks.

### **1. Define Benchmarks / Targets**

- Institution fixes target attainment levels for COs, POs, PSOs (commonly 70%).

- Targets are approved by DAB / BoS / Academic Council.

- Example: *“At least 70% of students should score ≥ 50% marks in CO1.”*

### **2. Collect Attainment Data**

- **Direct Assessment**

<!-- -->

- Internal exams, assignments, lab records, university exams.

- Project evaluations, viva, capstone projects.

<!-- -->

- **Indirect Assessment**

<!-- -->

- Student exit surveys.

- Alumni feedback.

- Employer/industry surveys.

### **3. Calculate Attainment Levels**

- **For each CO:**

<!-- -->

- % of students meeting benchmark.

<!-- -->

- **For each PO/PSO:**

<!-- -->

- Weighted aggregation of relevant COs.

- Overall attainment = (Direct % × weight) + (Indirect % × weight).

### **4. Compare with Targets**

- Formula: Gap = Target – Actual Attainment

- **Example:**

<!-- -->

- Target = 70%, Actual = 55%

- Gap = 15%

### **5. Identify Weak Areas**

- **At CO Level:**

<!-- -->

- Topics where students scored low (e.g., recursion, thermodynamics).

- Lab/experiment outcomes with poor attainment.

<!-- -->

- **At PO Level:**

<!-- -->

- Low scores in teamwork, communication, investigations, lifelong learning.

<!-- -->

- **At PSO Level:**

<!-- -->

- Poor exposure to industry-standard tools/software.

- Lack of emerging technology knowledge.

### **6. Categorize Gaps**

- **Knowledge Gaps →** Lack of conceptual clarity.

- **Skill Gaps →** Inadequate practical/coding/analytical exposure.

- **Attitudinal Gaps →** Weakness in teamwork, ethics, leadership.

- **Industry Readiness Gaps →** Limited internships, outdated labs, poor domain tool usage.

### **7. Record Gaps in Excel Tracker**

| **Level (CO/PO/PSO)** | **Code**              | **Target (%)** | **Actual (%)** | **Gap (%)** | **Weakness Identified**             |
|-----------------------|-----------------------|----------------|----------------|-------------|-------------------------------------|
| CO                    | CO2 – Data Structures | 70             | 52             | 18          | Lack of coding practice             |
| PO                    | PO4 – Investigations  | 70             | 55             | 15          | Weak in problem analysis & research |
| PSO                   | PSO1 – Domain Tools   | 70             | 52             | 18          | Limited exposure to software tools  |

### **8. Verify with Stakeholder Feedback**

- **Students →** Did remedial classes help?

- **Alumni →** Was curriculum relevant for jobs/higher studies?

- **Employers →** Are graduates industry-ready?

### **9. Finalize Gaps for Action Plan**

- Present gap analysis in faculty meetings, DAB, BoS.

- Approve corrective measures (remedial, bridge courses, FDPs, industry tie-ups).

# **13. Target Attainment Fixing Policy**

### **1. Objective**

- To establish a transparent and systematic method for fixing attainment targets for Course Outcomes (COs), Program Outcomes (POs), and Program Specific Outcomes (PSOs).

- To ensure that targets are realistic, measurable, and aligned with the Vision, Mission, Program Educational Objectives (PEOs), and industry requirements.

### **2. Scope**

- Applicable to all UG/PG programs of the institution.

- Covers direct attainment (academic performance) and indirect attainment (surveys/feedback).

### **3. Principles of Target Setting**

35. **Data-Driven:** Based on past 3–5 years’ performance trends.

36. **Progressive:** Targets should encourage continuous improvement (not fixed permanently).

37. **Inclusive:** Consider stakeholder feedback (faculty, students, alumni, employers).

38. **Approved Process:** Targets finalized only after approval from DAB → BoS → Academic Council.

### **4. Methodology**

- **Step 1: Historical Data Review**

<!-- -->

- Calculate average attainment (CO/PO/PSO) for the last 3 years.

- Identify trends and variation.

<!-- -->

- **Step 2: Benchmark Fixing**

<!-- -->

- COs (Direct): % of students scoring ≥ 50% in internal & external exams.

- Typical target: 65–75%.

- POs & PSOs (Direct): Aggregated attainment from COs + project work.

- Typical target: 70%.

- Indirect (surveys): Student/alumni/employer surveys on a scale of 1–5.

- Typical target: ≥ 3.5 (70%).

<!-- -->

- **Step 3: Weightage Fixing**

<!-- -->

- Direct : Indirect = 80 : 20 (default NBA practice).

- May vary (e.g., 70:30 for research-based programs).

<!-- -->

- **Step 4: Committee Approval**

<!-- -->

- Draft targets → presented to Department Advisory Board (DAB).

- Reviewed by Board of Studies (BoS).

- Ratified by Academic Council (AC).

<!-- -->

- **Step 5: Periodic Review**

<!-- -->

- Annual review for CO targets.

- Biennial review for PO/PSO targets.

- Targets revised upwards if consistently achieved for 2 consecutive cycles.

# **14. Academic Audit and Actions Taken Thereof during the Period of Assessment**

- **Purpose:**

<!-- -->

- To ensure compliance with institutional vision/mission and NBA standards.

- To measure quality of teaching–learning, curriculum delivery, assessment, research, and student support.

- To serve as a feedback-based mechanism for continuous improvement.

39. **Course Delivery Audit**

- Syllabus coverage, lesson plans, teaching methodologies.

- Alignment of COs with curriculum delivery.

40. **Assessment Audit**

- Quality of internal tests, assignments, lab records.

41. **Outcome Attainment Audit**

42. **Research & Innovation Audit**

43. **Faculty Performance Audit**

44. **Student Support Audit**

# **23. Improvement in Academic Performance (API)**

### **1. Purpose of the Template**

- NBA expects you to show year-wise improvement in the Academic Performance Index (API) of 1st, 2nd, and 3rd year students.

- **The template helps you:**

<!-- -->

- Enter raw data (appeared, passed, GPA).

- Auto-calculate Pass % and API.

- Summarize results across CAYm1, CAYm2, CAYm3.

- Track year-over-year (YoY) improvements and 3-year overall improvement %.

### **2. Structure of the File**

***Sheet 1: API Calc (Input)***

- **Columns:**

<!-- -->

- Academic Year Label → (CAYm1, CAYm2, CAYm3).

- Cohort → 1st Year, 2nd Year, 3rd Year.

- Students Appeared → Total who sat for exams.

- Students Passed → Total who passed.

- Pass % → Auto formula (Passed / Appeared).

- Avg GPA (out of 10) → Mean of successful students.

- API → Auto formula ((Avg GPA / 10) \* Pass %).

**Example (1st Year, CAYm1)**

- Appeared = 130, Passed = 120 → Pass % = 92.3%.

- Avg GPA = 7.8/10.

- API = 0.92 × 0.78 = 0.71.

***Sheet 2: API Summary (Year-wise)***

- **Rows (fixed):**

<!-- -->

- API of 1st-Year Students (Ref 4.3).

- API of 2nd-Year Students (Ref 4.4).

- API of 3rd-Year Students (Ref 4.5).

<!-- -->

- **Columns:**

<!-- -->

- CAYm1 API, CAYm2 API, CAYm3 API → Auto-fetched from

- YoY Δ (CAYm2→CAYm1) → Difference in API.

- YoY Δ (CAYm3→CAYm2) → Difference in API.

- 3-Year Improvement % (CAYm3→CAYm1) → Overall growth.

- Remarks → Space for your notes (e.g., *“Improved due to remedial classes”*).

### **3. How It Works**

82. Enter student numbers + Avg GPA in API Calc (Input).

83. The system calculates Pass %, API automatically.

84. The API Summary sheet pulls values via SUMIFS formulas.

85. YoY and 3-year improvement % are auto-calculated.

86. You just add remarks/actions taken (bridge courses, FDPs, mentoring, etc.).
