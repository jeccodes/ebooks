## Abstract

This document presents a comprehensive overview of Criterion 7: Facilities and Technical Support, one of the most significant components of the NBA (National Board of Accreditation) process for Tier-1 institutions, carrying a total weightage of 100 marks. The criterion evaluates the adequacy, quality, safety, and effectiveness of a department’s physical, technical, and digital infrastructure, with a focus on its ability to provide students with hands-on learning, research exposure, and a safe and supportive academic environment. It emphasizes not only the presence of well-equipped laboratories and qualified technical manpower but also the department’s capacity to create additional facilities that enrich the student learning experience beyond the minimum prescribed norms. Systematic maintenance practices— including calibration, annual maintenance contracts, and feedback mechanisms—are assessed alongside adherence to safety measures, such as fire, electrical, chemical, PPE, and ICT protocols, supported by regular audits and training drills. A special focus is placed on the establishment of project laboratories, research laboratories, and Centres of Excellence (CoEs) that serve as platforms for innovation, research, and entrepreneurship, directly contributing to tangible outputs like patents, publications, prototypes, and startups. The evaluation framework under Criterion 7 is further segmented into five sub-criteria that collectively examine laboratory adequacy and manpower (40 marks), creation of additional student-centric facilities (20 marks), maintenance and ambiance (10 marks), safety measures (10 marks), and research-driven infrastructure such as project labs and CoEs (20 marks).

Robust documentation and evidence are central to meeting the requirements of this criterion, with evaluators seeking detailed laboratory lists mapped to curriculum courses, utilization schedules, technical staff profiles, safety audit reports, maintenance records, student feedback, and evidence of collaborative research or entrepreneurial outcomes. Importantly, Criterion 7 underscores the alignment of infrastructure and facilities with Program Outcomes (POs) and Program-Specific Outcomes (PSOs), thereby reinforcing the principles of Outcome- Based Education (OBE) and ensuring that graduate attributes are nurtured through a conducive learning ecosystem. The overarching goal is to validate that the department provides a well-equipped, research-oriented, safe, and innovation-driven environment that directly contributes to academic quality, fosters student growth, and strengthens industry and research linkages, thereby supporting the institution’s mission and accreditation objectives.

# 1. Introduction

Criterion 7: Facilities and Technical Support of the NBA (National Board of Accreditation) accreditation framework for Tier-1 institutions is a vital measure of the adequacy, functionality, and sustainability of the physical, technical, and digital infrastructure of a department. With a total weightage of 100 marks, this criterion focuses not only on the availability of laboratories and facilities but also on their effective utilization, safety, and contribution to enhancing the student learning experience. The evaluation process under this criterion covers multiple dimensions. It begins with the verification of adequate and well-equipped laboratories and technical manpower (40 marks), ensuring that the department has sufficient labs, properly scaled equipment, and qualified staff to support academic delivery. It then considers the creation of additional facilities (20 marks), highlighting student-centric innovations such as ICT-enabled smart labs, advanced simulation spaces, or industry-supported infrastructure that go beyond the mandatory baseline. The maintenance of laboratories and overall ambiance (10 marks) is another critical element, examining whether labs are regularly serviced, clean, and conducive to effective teaching-learning, supported by practices like annual maintenance contracts (AMC), calibration records, and logbooks. Similarly, safety measures (10 marks) form an integral component, requiring evidence of compliance with safety standards in fire, electrical, chemical, PPE usage, and digital safety, along with proof of student and staff safety training. Further, the criterion emphasizes the importance of project laboratories, research laboratories, and Centres of Excellence (20 marks) as enablers of innovation, research, and entrepreneurship, with dedicated spaces supporting student projects, industry collaborations, and incubation activities.

To meet the requirements of Criterion 7, departments must provide comprehensive documentation, including laboratory lists, manpower records, equipment utilization schedules, maintenance registers, safety audit reports, circulars on safety drills, and student feedback. Evidence of research outcomes such as patents, prototypes, and publications, along with industry MoUs and incubation records, strengthens compliance. A summary sheet is also recommended to serve as a quick reference and dashboard for NBA evaluators, ensuring transparency and easy verification of evidence. Overall, Criterion 7 underlines the importance of offering students a well-equipped, safe, research-driven, and innovation-friendly learning ecosystem, positioning infrastructure not just as a physical asset but as a cornerstone of academic quality and institutional excellence.

# **2. Criterion 7:** Facilities and Technical Support

## Overview

- This criterion evaluates whether the physical, technical, and digital infrastructure in the department is adequate, well-maintained, safe, and effectively utilized.

- It checks laboratory adequacy, technical manpower, safety, maintenance, research facilities, collaborations, and student support.

- Marks are distributed across laboratory adequacy, additional facilities, maintenance, safety, and research/innovation support.

### 7.1 Adequate and Well-Equipped Laboratories, and Technical Manpower (40 Marks)

- **Objective:** To verify if labs are sufficient in number, size, equipment, and technical staff.

- **What NBA looks for:**

<!-- -->

- List of laboratories with area (sq. m), equipment, software.

- Batch size compliance (students per setup).

- Weekly utilization schedules (lab timetables).

- Technical manpower – names, qualifications, years of experience, responsibilities.

- Avoid duplication with Criterion 7.2 and 7.5.

### 7.2 Additional Facilities Created for Improving Learning Experience (20 Marks)

- **Objective:** Show innovation and student-friendly initiatives beyond minimum requirements.

- **Examples:**

<!-- -->

- ICT-enabled labs, virtual labs, simulation software.

- Innovation spaces (3D printers, Arduino kits, GPU clusters).

- Industry-donated equipment, specialized tools.

<!-- -->

- **Evidence:**

<!-- -->

- List of additional facilities → Purpose → Utilization → Relevance to POs/PSOs.

- Photos, procurement docs, usage logs.

### 7.3 Maintenance of Laboratories and Overall Ambiance (10 Marks)

- **Objective:** Ensure labs are functional, clean, safe, and accessible.

- **What to show:**

<!-- -->

- Maintenance practices – AMC copies, calibration certificates, servicing records.

- Logbooks – equipment usage, repairs, downtime.

- Ambiance features – ventilation, lighting, seating, ICT support, internet.

<!-- -->

- **Evidence:**

<!-- -->

- Maintenance logbooks, AMC agreements.

- Student feedback reports on lab ambiance.

### 7.4 Safety Measures in Laboratories (10 Marks)

- **Objective:** Ensure labs comply with safety norms and that students/staff are trained.

- **What to show:**

<!-- -->

- Fire safety (extinguishers, drills).

- Electrical safety (MCB/ELCB, proper wiring, insulated mats).

- PPE availability (gloves, goggles, lab coats).

- Chemical safety (fume hoods, storage, disposal).

- ICT safety (cybersecurity for digital labs).

<!-- -->

- **Evidence:**

<!-- -->

- Safety audit reports (internal + external).

- Circulars / notices on safety drills.

- Student feedback on lab safety.

### 7.5 Project Laboratory / Research Laboratory / Centre of Excellence (20 Marks)

- **Objective:** Evaluate innovation and research support infrastructure.

- **What to show:**

<!-- -->

- Dedicated Project Labs for final-year projects.

- Research Labs for faculty + PG research.

- Centres of Excellence (CoEs) linked to industry or government schemes.

- **Outputs:** Publications, patents, prototypes, startups, hackathon achievements.

<!-- -->

- **Evidence:**

<!-- -->

- List of labs/CoEs with area, facilities, utilization.

- Research outputs → publications, patents, prototypes.

- Industry/research MoUs and incubation records.

- Utilization records (students trained, projects executed).

# 5. List of Laboratories with name, area (sq. m), major equipment/software, mapped course(s)

## Purpose

- NBA wants to check adequacy of labs for the program.

- Each program (CSE, ECE, ME, etc.) must have department-specific labs that support theory courses.

- Labs should be spacious, well-equipped, and mapped to curriculum courses.

- This proves that students get hands-on exposure relevant to what they study.

## Components Explained

> 1\. Name of Laboratory

- Official name used in timetable and syllabus.

- **Example:** “Programming Lab”, “Electronics Lab”, “AI & Data Science Lab”.

> 2\. Area (sq. m)

- Physical space of the laboratory.

- As per AICTE norms, minimum 66 sq. m is required for a core lab.

- Larger areas (100–200 sq. m) are better for workshops and project labs.

> 3\. Major Equipment/ Software

- Key hardware/software that defines the lab.

- **Example:**

<!-- -->

- Programming Lab → PCs, IDEs, Compilers.

- AI Lab → GPU Servers, MATLAB, Python libraries.

- Electrical Lab → Transformers, Motors, Measurement Kits.

> 4\. Mapped Courses

- The courses from curriculum that require this lab.

- **Example:**

<!-- -->

- Programming Lab → Data Structures, OOPS, DBMS.

- Electronics Lab → Digital Electronics, Embedded Systems.

- IoT Lab → Internet of Things, Robotics.

## Why Important for NBA

- Shows direct mapping between theory courses and practical exposure.

- Ensures curriculum delivery is hands-on (not only theoretical).

- Demonstrates investment in infrastructure.

- Helps reviewers verify that all courses with practical components have supporting labs.

## How to Present to NBA

- Put this in a tabular Excel annexure (one sheet per department/program).

- **Columns:** S. No. \| Lab Name \| Area (sq. m) \| Major Equipment/Software \| Mapped Courses.

- Attach photos, equipment purchase bills, AMC records, and timetables as supporting evidence.

- Ensure no duplication with library or co-curricular facilities (those are separate).

## Why NBA Asks for “List of Technical Staff”

- NBA Criterion 7 gives 40 marks for Adequate Laboratories & Technical Manpower.

- It’s not enough to just show labs – you must also prove that qualified technical staff are in place to support them.

- Technical manpower ensures smooth functioning, maintenance, and hands-on support during practical classes.

## What to Include in the List

> 1\. Name of Technical Staff

- Full name as per appointment order.

- Helps reviewers verify staff records.

> 2\. Designation

- Lab Instructor, Technician, Workshop Instructor, Technical Assistant, System Administrator, etc.

- Diploma, ITI, B.Sc., B.Tech, or M.Sc. depending on the lab type.

- Specialized certifications (like CCNA, AutoCAD, MATLAB, PLC) can also be mentioned.

- Shows relevance of staff background to the lab they manage.

> 4\. Years of Experience

- Total professional experience (in industry + academia).

- NBA wants to see staff stability and expertise, not freshers only.

> 5\. Responsibilities

- **Specific duties in each lab, e.g.:**

<!-- -->

- Preparing and calibrating equipment.

- Assisting faculty during practical classes.

- Maintaining lab logbooks and stock registers.

- Ensuring lab safety compliance.

- Supporting students in projects, mini-projects, and final-year work.

## Why It Matters in NBA Evaluation

- Shows adequacy of technical manpower relative to the number of labs.

- Proves that labs are not just “on paper” but functional and supported.

- **Demonstrates quality assurance:** students get practical training with guided support.

- Helps NBA assess whether staff qualifications match the lab requirements.

# 8. Lab Utilization Schedules (Timetables)

## Why NBA Needs Lab Utilization Schedules

<!-- -->

<!-- -->

## What to Show in the Utilization Schedule

- Lab Name → e.g., Programming Lab, Electronics Lab.

- Course → Course mapped (e.g., Data Structures).

- Faculty in charge → Who supervises the session.

- Batch/Section → Which group of students use the lab.

- Planned Hours → Hours scheduled in the week.

- Available Hours → Total weekly availability (usually 40 hrs).

- **Demonstrates adequacy:** Each lab has enough slots to serve all batches.

<!-- -->

<!-- -->
