## Abstract

Criterion 4, titled *Students’ Performance*, is one of the most significant pillars of the National Board of Accreditation (NBA) framework, with an allocation of 120 marks. It reflects the effectiveness of the institution’s teaching–learning process, the quality of students admitted, and the strength of support mechanisms offered throughout the academic journey. Unlike input-based measures that assess resources or curriculum alone, this criterion takes a student-centric approach by focusing on actual academic progression, timely graduation, employability, entrepreneurial ventures, and success in competitive examinations. By emphasizing outcomes rather than mere processes, Criterion 4 serves as a true indicator of how well an institution nurtures student talent and transforms potential into performance.

The first sub-criterion, Enrolment Ratio (4.1), assesses the attractiveness and demand for a program, which indirectly highlights its credibility among stakeholders. It is calculated as the ratio of students admitted in the first year to the sanctioned intake, averaged over the current academic year and the two preceding years. The formula incorporates not only regular admissions but also separate division and supernumerary admissions while accounting for migrations. A high enrolment ratio (≥90%) is an indicator of sustained student interest, institutional reputation, and alignment of programs with market and societal needs. Conversely, lower enrolment ratios suggest declining demand, highlighting the need for strategic curriculum revision, improved outreach, or better alignment with industry expectations. Thus, the enrolment ratio acts as a leading indicator of both program relevance and institutional competitiveness.

The second sub-criterion, Success Rate in the Program (4.2), is a measure of institutional effectiveness in ensuring that students admitted complete their programs on time. It calculates the percentage of students who graduate within the minimum stipulated duration, factoring in different modes of admission, such as first-year entries, lateral entries, and multiple entries under the Academic Bank of Credits (ABC). Importantly, it adjusts for multiple exits, ensuring accuracy and fairness. A success rate of 90% or higher indicates robust academic delivery, effective mentoring, and comprehensive student support systems that minimize dropouts and delays in graduation. Institutions achieving such benchmarks demonstrate that they are not only admitting students but also successfully guiding them through to completion. This measure links directly to teaching effectiveness, faculty commitment, and the availability of academic and personal support services such as counseling, tutoring, and remediation.

The third sub-criterion, Academic Performance in First, Second, and Third Year (4.3), delves into the internal progression of students across successive years of study. This is evaluated through the Academic Performance Index (API), which combines both student achievement and institutional effectiveness. The API considers the average marks or GPA of successful students along with the success ratio, i.e., the proportion of students who pass relative to those who appeared. Separate API values are computed for the first, second, and third years, each averaged over three consecutive academic years. This approach ensures fairness and reliability by smoothing out year-on-year variations. High API values demonstrate the institution’s ability to integrate students effectively into the academic ecosystem, support them during their formative years, and sustain performance as the curriculum advances in complexity. Institutions with consistently strong API scores reveal an environment where teaching practices, learning resources, and evaluation methods collectively contribute to student success.

The fourth sub-criterion, Placement, Higher Studies, and Entrepreneurship (4.4), is perhaps the most direct reflection of employability and professional readiness. It recognizes that the ultimate goal of education is to prepare students for meaningful careers, advanced studies, or entrepreneurial ventures. The Placement Index (P) is calculated by accounting for the number of students placed in jobs, admitted to postgraduate studies, or engaged in entrepreneurial activities, expressed as a percentage of the total final-year students or the sanctioned intake with lateral entries, whichever is higher. Averaged over three graduating batches, this measure provides a longitudinal perspective on institutional effectiveness. High placement and higher studies figures indicate strong industry–academia linkages, effective career guidance, and alignment of curriculum with evolving professional demands. Entrepreneurship figures further highlight institutional support for innovation, incubation, and risk-taking. This sub-criterion demonstrates how academic learning is translated into tangible career outcomes.

The fifth sub-criterion, Performance in Competitive Examinations (4.5), evaluates students’ ability to excel in examinations beyond the regular curriculum. This includes national and international examinations such as GATE, GRE, CAT, UPSC, GMAT, IELTS, TOEFL, and NET, each carrying different weights based on their rigor and prestige. Success in these examinations reflects not only the academic preparation imparted by the institution but also its ability to cultivate critical thinking, problem-solving, and aptitude skills in students. Institutions with strong performance in this domain display their capacity to nurture graduates capable of excelling in global and national competitive environments. This further enhances institutional credibility, reputation, and ranking in the higher education landscape.

A distinguishing feature of Criterion 4 is its reliance on rigorous documentation and evidence-based assessment. Institutions are required to provide year-wise records of enrolments, success rates, and academic performance indices. This includes admission registers, transcripts, university result sheets, placement records, proof of higher studies through admission letters, entrepreneurship certifications, and official records of competitive examination results. For transparency, students admitted under the Multiple Entry and Exit System (MEES/ABC) are tracked separately, ensuring clarity in reporting and compliance with outcome-based accreditation methodologies. This approach enforces accountability and prevents inflating success indicators through non-standard admissions.

The significance of Criterion 4 extends beyond scoring in accreditation. It functions as a benchmarking tool that allows institutions to assess themselves against national standards and peer institutions. A consistently high performance under this criterion enhances institutional reputation, boosts student and parent confidence, and attracts quality faculty, partnerships, and funding. Moreover, it compels institutions to provide holistic support services beyond classroom teaching, such as mentoring systems, counseling services, bridge courses, soft-skills training, career guidance, and industry internships. In essence, Criterion 4 not only measures performance but also encourages continuous improvement.

In today’s competitive higher education landscape, Criterion 4 has broader implications for national development and global competitiveness. By measuring how effectively institutions prepare graduates for placements, entrepreneurship, and competitive examinations, it aligns higher education outcomes with the nation’s socio-economic and developmental goals. Institutions that excel in Criterion 4 contribute to the creation of a skilled workforce, promote innovation and entrepreneurship, and enhance the employability quotient of their graduates. Thus, it is not only an institutional performance measure but also a significant contributor to societal and national progress.

**In conclusion, Criterion 4:** Students’ Performance is a comprehensive evaluation framework that integrates quantitative data with qualitative outcomes. It measures how well an institution attracts, retains, nurtures, and empowers students to succeed academically, professionally, and personally. By focusing on enrolment, success rates, academic performance, career outcomes, and competitive exam achievements, it provides a holistic picture of student progression and institutional effectiveness. Beyond its role in accreditation, Criterion 4 serves as a catalyst for institutions to innovate, strengthen support systems, and prioritize student success as the ultimate marker of academic excellence.

# **2. Criterion 4:** Students’ Performance

## Overview

- Criterion 4 (120 marks) assesses student performance as an indicator of the effectiveness of the teaching–learning process, admissions quality, and institutional support mechanisms.

- The focus is on academic performance, progression, placements, higher studies, and competitive exam success.

## Key Sub-Criteria under Criterion 4

1\. 4.1 Enrolment Ratio (20 marks)

- **Definition:** Ratio of students admitted to the program against the sanctioned intake over the last three academic years.

- Formula: (Total number of students admitted in 1st year for last 3 years) ÷ (Sanctioned intake × 3 years).

- **Objective:** Reflects demand for the program and the quality of intake.

- **Scoring:**

<!-- -->

- High enrolment ratio (close to 100%) → full marks.

- Lower enrolment ratio → proportionately less marks.

2\. 4.2 Success Rate in the Program (30 marks)

- **Definition:** Percentage of students successfully graduating in minimum stipulated time (on-time graduation).

- Formula: (Number of students graduated in stipulated time / Number of students admitted 4 years earlier) × 100.

- **Objective:** Indicates quality of teaching, mentoring, and student support.

- **Scoring:**

<!-- -->

- Success Rate ≥90% → Maximum marks.

- Below 40% → Minimal marks.

3\. 4.3 Academic Performance in First Year (20 marks)

- **Definition:** Average performance of students in the first year, considering CGPA/CPI or equivalent.

- Formula: (Mean of first-year GPA of all successful students) ÷ 10.

- **Objective:** Assesses the academic strength of students after entry and their adaptation to institutional teaching.

4\. 4.4 Placement, Higher Studies, and Entrepreneurship (30 marks)

- **Placement Rate:** Students placed through campus recruitment or off-campus drives.

- **Higher Studies:** Students admitted into PG programs in reputed institutions (IITs, NITS, foreign universities, etc.).

- **Entrepreneurship:** Students who start their own ventures/startups.

- Formula: (Number of students placed + admitted for higher studies + entrepreneurs) ÷ (Number of eligible students).

- **Scoring:** Weighted marks allocated (major weight on placement & higher studies).

5\. 4.5 Performance in Competitive Examinations (20 marks)

- **Definition:** Number of students qualifying in national/international level exams (e.g., GATE, GMAT, GRE, IELTS, TOEFL, UPSC, CAT, NET).

- Formula: (Number of students qualified ÷ Number of students appeared) × 100.

- **Objective:** Indicates capability of students to compete beyond curriculumbased learning.

- **Scoring:** Based on the percentage of students qualifying and the exam’s difficulty level.

## Evidence Required

- Admission registers and enrolment data (authenticated by Registrar/Controller of Examinations).

- University results / transcripts for success rate and GPA analysis.

- Placement records verified by Training & Placement Cell.

- Proof of higher studies (admission letters, university enrollment certificates).

- Startup/entrepreneurship proof (company registration, incubator certificates).

- Certificates for competitive exam qualifications (GATE scorecards, CAT results, etc.).

Impact of Criterion 4

- Directly reflects program reputation – high enrolment + placements signal strong industry/institutional recognition.

- Student-centric evaluation – ensures institutes focus not just on teaching but also on outcomes.

- Benchmarking tool – allows comparison with other institutions nationally.

- Encourages holistic support - placement training, exam coaching, and entrepreneurship incubation.

- **Adjustments required:**

- Some colleges run parallel divisions for the same program code.

- If yes, show separately here.

- **Example:** If AICTE approved an additional batch of 60 in 2019, it should be recorded here.

5\. N4 = Supernumerary Admissions

- **Admissions over and above sanctioned intake, approved under specific quotas:**

<!-- -->

- TFWS (Tuition Fee Waiver Scheme)

- EWS (Economically Weaker Section)

- PMSSS (Prime Minister’s Special Scholarship Scheme for J&K/NE students)

- Foreign Nationals/PIOs (if AICTE approved)

<!-- -->

- These should be explicitly shown; not merged with N1/N2.

6\. Total Admissions = N1 + N2 + N3 + N4

- Gives the final number of students admitted to the program each academic year.

- **Important:** Students admitted under Multiple Entry and Exit System (MEES/Academic Bank of Credits) are not included.

- **Reason:** NBA considers only regular degree-seeking cohort data for outcome-based accreditation.

## Time Labels (CAY, LYG, etc.)

CAY → Current Academic Year (the year of NBA assessment, e.g., 2024–25).

CAYm1 → Current Academic Year minus 1 (e.g., 2023–24).

CAYm2 → Current Academic Year minus 2 (e.g., 2022–23).

CAYm3 → Current Academic Year minus 3.

CAYm4 (LYG) → Last Year Graduated batch.

CAYm5 (LYGm1) → Graduated batch minus 1 year.

CAYm6 (LYGm2) → Graduated batch minus 2 years.

## Evidence Required for NBA

- Sanctioned Intake Proof → AICTE approval letters for each year.

- Admission Data → University/State admission authority records.

- Migration Records → Transfers in/out approved by affiliating body.

- Lateral Entry Proof → Diploma entry admission list.

- Supernumerary Proof → AICTE notification, admission committee records.

- Exclusion Proof → Documentation showing no MEES students were included.

## Why This is Critical in NBA Accreditation

- Enrolment Ratio is directly calculated from N vs. actual admissions.

- Reflects program popularity and student trust.

- Ensures the institution does not inflate figures by including MEES or unapproved quotas.

- Provides base data for success rate, placement ratio, and performance indicators in later sub-criteria.

# 4. Sanctioned Intake Proof (AICTE approval letters for each year)

## 1. What is Sanctioned Intake Proof?

- Sanctioned Intake (N) = The officially approved number of seats in a program (e.g., B.Tech –120 seats).

- Proof = 0fficial approval letter/document issued every academic year by AICTE (or State Government/University in some cases).

- This document validates that the institution is not exceeding its authorised intake.

## 2. Source of Documents

- AICTE Web Portal (Approval Process Handbook uploads)

<!-- -->

- Institutions download approval letters each academic year after completing AICTE’s annual approval process.

<!-- -->

- Affiliating University/State Technical Education Board

<!-- -->

- Sometimes the university also issues an affiliation order with sanctioned intake.

<!-- -->

- Institutional Records

<!-- -->

- Hard copies stamped and signed by AICTE/authority, filed year-wise in the Academic Office.

## 3. What the Letter Contains

- Name of the Institution & Program (e.g., B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering).

- Academic Year (e.g., 2024–25).

- Sanctioned Intake (e.g., 120 students).

- Any changes in intake (increase/decrease from previous years).

- **Mention of supernumerary approvals (if applicable:** TFWS, EWS, PMSSS).

- Seal and signature from AICTE Regional Office or competent approving authority.

## 4. Why NBA Needs It

- To verify that “N” in your Admission Details Table matches AICTE approval.

- Prevents over-reporting admissions beyond authorised limits.

- Confirms compliance with AICTE/State norms (NBA = outcome-based, but linked to statutory compliance).

## 5. Best Practices for Submission

- Cross-check intake in the letter vs. what you entered in NBA SAR Table (N row).

# 6. Migration Records (Transfers In/Out)

- Migration Records = Documents that show when students transfer in (join your program from another institution) or transfer out (leave your program for another institution).

## 2. Why are They Important for NBA?

<!-- -->

- University Academic Section / Controller of Examinations → official migration approval order.

- State Technical Education Board / DTE (in diploma-to-degree transfers or state-governed colleges).

- College Academic Office → should keep copies of university-approved migration letters in student files.

- Name of the student (with roll/registration number).

- From Institution / Program (for migrated-in students).

- To Institution / Program (for migrated-out students).

- Date & Academic Year of migration approval.

# **10. Supernumerary Proof for NBA Criterion 4:** Students’ Performance (Admissions)

## 1. What are Supernumerary Admissions (N4)?

- Supernumerary seats = admissions allowed over and above the sanctioned intake (N) under special AICTE/University approved categories.

- **Typical categories:**

<!-- -->

- TFWS (Tuition Fee Waiver Scheme)

- EWS (Economically Weaker Section quota)

- PMSSS (Prime Minister’s Special Scholarship Scheme –J&K/NE students)

- Foreign Nationals / PIO / Children of Indian Workers in Gulf Countries

- PwD supernumerary quota (if applicable)

## 2. Why Proof is Required

- NBA assessors need to ensure that extra admissions (N4) are valid and AICTE

approved.

- Prevents institutions from inflating admission counts beyond legitimate AICTE sanctioned intake.

- Ensures transparency in admission quota management.

## 3. Acceptable Proofs

1\. AICTE Notifications / Approval Orders

- Year-wise approval letters mentioning supernumerary categories and % permitted (e.g., 5% TFWS, % Ews).

- Published circulars available on AICTE portal.

2\. Admission Committee Records

- Internal Admission Committee minutes showing how supernumerary seats were allotted.

- Category-wise student lists with official signatures.

3\. University/State Admission Authority Lists

- CET Cell / Counselling agency allotment lists that separately mark TFWS/EWS seats.

4\. Student Records

- Admission register highlighting supernumerary status.

- Fee waiver letters / scholarship sanction copies (if applicable).

## 4. Example (Filling Criterion 4 Table with Supernumerary)

- N (Sanctioned Intake) = 120.

- N1 (First Year Admissions) = 111 (after migrations).

- N2 (Lateral Entry) = 8.

- N3 (Separate Division) = 0.

- N4 (Supernumerary Proof) = 7 (AICTE approval allows 5% TFWS +2 EWS →

documented via CET list).

- Total Admissions = 126.

## 5. Best Practices for Submission

- Keep year-wise AICTE approval letters that mention supernumerary quota explicitly.

- Maintain student-wise lists under TFWS/EWS/PMSSS signed by Admission

Committee.

- **Save all records in PDF format, with clear naming convention:**

<!-- -->

- Supernumerary\_ TFWS_CSE_2024-25.pdf

<!-- -->

- Ensure N4 numbers match exactly with admission lists shown to NBA.

- Document any unused supernumerary quota (if no student claimed TFWS/EWS in a year, mark N4 = 0).

# **11. Exclusion Proof for NBA Criterion 4:** Students’ Performance (Admissions)

## 1. What is Exclusion Proof?

- Exclusion Proof = Documentation that shows students admitted under Multiple Entry & Exit System (MEES/Academic Bank of Credits – ABC framework) are not counted in NBA Criterion 4 Admission Table (N, N1–N4).

- This ensures the admission data reflects only regular cohorts tracked for graduation outcomes.

## 2. Why is it Needed?

- NBA assessors verify that institutions are not inflating admission counts by including students admitted via:

<!-- -->

- MEES lateral re-entry (students who exited earlier with a certificate/diploma and rejoined).

- Credit transfers from other institutions via ABC.

<!-- -->

- Since MEES students don’t follow the standard cohort progression, they must be excluded.

## 3. Acceptable Proofs

1\. Institutional Declaration Note

- **A signed statement from the Principal/Registrar declaring:**

<!-- -->

- ”No MEES/ABC admitted students have been included in Criterion 4 Admission Table.”

<!-- -->

- On institutional letterhead, with date and seal.

2\. ERP/Admission Records

- Admission management system highlighting MEES students separately.

- Clear annotation showing they were excluded from N1–N4.

3\. Docs_Log Mapping

- Entry in your Docs_Log sheet linking to internal records, stating exclusion of MEES.

4\. University/State Admission Authority Confirmation (if applicable)

- Sometimes the affiliating university issues a list that separates regular vs MEES entrants.

## 4. Best Practices

- Maintain a separate MEES/ABC Register (for internal IQAC purposes).

- Clearly highlight “Not Counted in NBA” in that register.

- File a formal exclusion note signed by Admission Committee.

- *Store all MEES details in case assessors ask for verification of exclusions.*

# 13. Admission Details for the Program through Multiple Entry and Exit Points (MEES/ABC Framework)

- **Ensures transparency:** Institutions must declare MEES data even though it’s excluded from the main admission table.

# 14. University/State Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) Records → showing MEES entries/exits.

## Purpose (why ABC records matter)

- Prove MEES/ABC entries and exits separate from regular admissions for NBA Criterion 4.

- **Feed N5 (entries) and N6 (exits) calculations:** N5 = N52+N53+N54; N6 = N61+N62+N63.

- Ensure transparency that MEES data is reported but excluded from N1–N4 totals.

## Authoritative sources

- Affiliating University → ABC/credit-transfer office or Controller of Examinations.

- State CET/DTE/Academic Council → centralized ABC dashboards/reports.

- ABC National Platform (if state/university integrates) → downloadable student-wise credit ledgers.

## Acceptable ABC document types

- Student Credit Ledger/Statement showing transferred/earned credits and entry/exit level.

- Credit Transfer Approval Order citing program, semester equivalence, and year label (CAY...CAYm6).

- Re-entry/Exit Sanction Memo mapping prior credits to 2nd/3rd/4th year (entries) or after 1st/2nd/3rd year (exits).

- Consolidated Term Sheet per program with totals by category (N52/N53/N54/N61/N62/N63).

## Minimum fields the ABC record should include

- **Student identifiers:** Name, Univ Reg/Roll No, ABC ID.

- **Program mapping:** Program code/name, host institution, source/target institution (for transfers).

- **Action & level:** ENTRY (2nd/3rd/4th year) or EXIT (after 1st/2nd/3rd year).

- **Credit details:** total credits transferred/recognized; course mappings.

- **Approval metadata:** Order No, date, issuing authority, seals/signatures.

## How ABC records map to NBA items

- N52 count ABC ENTRY at 2nd year in that Year Label.

- N53 count ABC ENTRY at 3rd year in that Year Label.

- N54 count ABC ENTRY at 4th year in that Year Label.

- N61 count ABC EXIT after 1st year in that Year Label.

- N62 count ABC EXIT after 2nd year in that Year Label.

- N63 count ABC EXIT after 3rd year in that Year Label.

## Reconciliation workflow (recommended)

- **Step 1:** Pull ABC consolidated report for the program for CAY...CAYm6.

- **Step 2:** Cross-check with MEES Register (the Excel you generated) by ABC ID/Reg No and Entry/Exit Level.

- **Step 3:** Resolve discrepancies with University ABC office; obtain revised order if needed.

- **Step 4:** Update Yearly_Summary in MEES Register → verify N5/N6 totals match ABC report.

- **Step 5:** Log proofs in Docs_Log with file paths and verifier details.

- **Step 6:** Add a note in Exclusion Declaration that ABC/MEES counts are reported separately from N1–N4.

## Audit-ready documentary bundle (per year)

- ABC Consolidated Entries & Exits PDF (program-wise).

- Student-wise Ledgers (only for those counted in N5/N6).

- Approval Orders for each transfer/exit.

- **Mapping Sheet:** ABC ID ↔ MEES Register row ↔ N52/53/54 or N61/62/63 bucket.

## Common pitfalls (and fixes)

- **Pitfall:** Counting MEES entries as N2 (lateral). Fix: MEES entries belong to N5 (N52/53/54), not N2.

- **Pitfall:** Missing entry/exit level on documents. Fix: Seek revised memo explicitly stating year equivalence.

- **Pitfall:** Double-count when a student exits and later re-enters. Fix: Record two separate events (one in N6, one in N5) with distinct dates/orders.

- **Pitfall:** Aggregates without student identifiers. Fix: Maintain student-level ledger behind every aggregate.

## Data protection & integrity

- Store ABC documents in a restricted folder (role-based access).

- Mask sensitive data in public reports; keep unredacted copies for assessors.

- Maintain hash/checksum of PDFs to prove documents weren’t altered.

## File-naming & version control (suggested)

- ABC_EntriesExits_ProgramCode_CAY2024-25_v01.pdf

- ABC_Ledger\_\<RegNo\>\_\<ABCID\>\_AY2024-25.pdf

- ABC_OrderNo\_\<XXXX\>\_Date_YYYY-MM-DD.pdf

# 16. Approval/Allotment Letters from University/DTE (Admissions including MEES/ABC, Lateral Entry, Supernumerary, etc.)

- Official documents issued by the affiliating University or State Directorate of Technical Education (DTE) that:

- Confirm seat allotments to students through central/state counselling.

- Authorise admissions beyond first year (lateral entry, MEES entries).

- Sanction supernumerary admissions (TFWS, EWS, PMSSS, foreign nationals).

- Approve student transfers or migrations (in/out).

## 2. Why NBA Needs Them

- To validate authenticity of admissions reported in Criterion 4 tables (N1–N4 + N5/N6).

- Ensure no self-admissions outside AICTE/University norms.

- To check that MEES/ABC students are tracked but excluded from regular counts.

# 17. Exit Certificates (Certificate/Diploma/Advanced Diploma) in the context of NBA Criterion 4 – Students’ Performance (MEES/ABC Exits)

## 1. What are Exit Certificates?

- Formal documents issued by the affiliating University/Board under the Multiple Entry & Exit System (MEES) or Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) framework.

- Awarded when a student exits a program before completing the full degree, but after earning minimum credit requirements.

## 2. Levels of Exit (as per NEP–2020/AICTE norms)

- N61 → Certificate (exit after 1 year, usually 40–44 credits).

- N62 → Diploma (exit after 2 years, usually 80–88 credits).

- N63 → Advanced Diploma (exit after 3 years, usually 120–132 credits).

- **These map directly into NBA Criterion 4:** N6 = N61 + N62 + N63.

## 3. Why NBA Requires Exit Certificates

- To validate N6 reporting (MEES exits) in the Admission Details table.

- Prevents inflation of student exits without proof.

- Ensures compliance with ABC credit framework and University recognition.

## 4. What an Exit Certificate Contains

- University/Board Header & Seal

- Student Details → Name, Roll No, ABC ID.

- Program & Branch (e.g., B.Tech CSE).

- Credits Earned & Level of Exit (e.g., 44 credits → Certificate).

- **Award:**

<!-- -->

- *Certificate in Engineering (1st year)*,

- *Diploma in Engineering (2nd year)*,

- *Advanced Diploma in Engineering (3rd year)*.

<!-- -->

- Reference to NEP–2020/ABC Norms.

- Approval Order Number & Date.

- Authorized Signatures (COE/Registrar/Dean).

# 25. Enrolment Ratio in the First Year (ER) under Criterion 4 (Students’ Performance)

## 1. Definition of Enrolment Ratio (ER)

- Enrolment Ratio (ER) = Percentage of students actually admitted in the first year compared to the sanctioned intake.

- Formula (for a given academic year):

ER=Number of students admitted in 1st year (N1 + N3 + N4, adjusted for migrations) /Sanctioned Intake (N)×100

- **Notes:**

<!-- -->

- N1 = First Year admissions (after adjusting migrations).

- N3 = Separate division admissions (if any).

- N4 = Supernumerary admissions (TFWS/EWS/PMSSS).

- MEES entries (N5) and exits (N6) are not included in first-year enrolment.

## 2. Calculation of Average ER

- NBA does not look at a single year, but at the average enrolment ratio of the last three academic years.

- Formula:

Average ER=(ERCAY+ERCAYm1+ERCAYm2)/3

## 3. Points Allocation (ER → 20 Marks)

- NBA awards up to 20 points for ER.

- Formula:

ER Points=20×Average ER/100

- **Examples:**

<!-- -->

- If Average ER = 100% → ER Points = 20

- If Average ER = 90% → ER Points = 18

- If Average ER = 75% → ER Points = 15

- If Average ER = 60% → ER Points = 12

## 4. Example Calculation

Suppose Sanctioned Intake = 120 seats.

| **Year**       | **Sanctioned Intake (N)** | **Actual Admitted (N1+N3+N4)** | **ER (%)** |
|----------------|---------------------------|--------------------------------|------------|
| CAY (2024-25)  | 120                       | 110                            | 91.7%      |
| CAYm1(2023-24) | 120                       | 115                            | 95.8%      |
| CAYm2(2022-23) | 120                       | 108                            | 90.0%      |

- Average ER = (91.7 + 95.8 + 90.0) ÷ 3 = 92.5%

- ER Points = 20 × (92.5 ÷ 100) = 18.5 ≈19

## 5. Interpretation

- **A high ER indicates:**

<!-- -->

- Strong demand for the program.

- Good admission reputation and intake quality.

<!-- -->

- **A low ER signals:**

<!-- -->

- Declining demand or poor intake quality.

- Issues in placement/reputation that NBA assessors may question.

## 6. Proofs/Supporting Documents

- AICTE/University Sanctioned Intake Letters (year-wise).

- Admission Committee Reports / University Admission Records.

- Migration Approval Orders (if students moved in/out).

- Supernumerary Allotment Approvals (TFWS/EWS).

- Institution Admission Register (first year).

## 7. Key Takeaways

- Enrolment Ratio = Input quality measure under Criterion 4.

- Points directly proportional to Average ER across 3 years.

- Institutions should maintain a clear admission register showing N vs N1/N3/N4.

- Target → keep ER ≥90% to secure 18–20 points.

# 26. Student Enrolment Ratio in the First Year under NBA Criterion 4 – Students’ Performance

## 1. Purpose

- The Student Enrolment Ratio (ER) shows the demand and intake strength of a program.

- NBA evaluates how effectively institutions fill their sanctioned intake seats in the first year, averaged across three consecutive academic years (CAY, CAYm1, CAYm2).

- This directly contributes to NBA scoring (20 points maximum).

## 2. Key Definitions

- N (Sanctioned Intake)

<!-- -->

- Number of seats approved by AICTE/University/Competent Authority for the 1st year.

<!-- -->

- N1 (Regular 1st Year Admissions, adjusted for migration)

<!-- -->

- = Total 1st-year students admitted

- – Students who migrated out to another program/institution

<!-- -->

- Students who migrated in from another program/institution

<!-- -->

- N4 (Supernumerary Admissions)

<!-- -->

- Additional seats admitted under categories like TFWS, EWS, PMSSS, Foreign Nationals, PIO.

<!-- -->

- Enrolment Ratio (ER)

<!-- -->

- Formula for each academic year:

ER=(N1+N4)/N×100

## 3. Step-by-Step Calculation

1\. Take sanctioned intake (N) for each academic year.

2\. Compute N1 (regular adjusted admissions).

3\. Compute N4 (supernumerary admissions).

**4. Calculate ER for each year:**

ERi=(N1+N4)/N×100

where i = CAY, CAYm1, CAYm2.

**5. Take the average across 3 years:**

Average ER=(ER1+ER2+ER3)/3

## 4. Example (Illustration)

| **Year**       | **N (Sanctioned Intake)** | **N1 (Regular Admissions, adjusted)** | **N4 (Supernumerary)** | **N1+N4** | **ER (%)** |
|----------------|---------------------------|---------------------------------------|------------------------|-----------|------------|
| CAY (2024- 25) | 120                       | 112                                   | 5                      | 117       | 97.5%      |
| CAYm1(2023-24) | 120                       | 115                                   | 3                      | 118       | 98.3%      |
| CAYm2(2022-23) | 120                       | 108                                   | 4                      | 112       | 93.3%      |

- ERI (CAY) = (112+5)/120 \*100 = 97.5%

- ER2 (CAYm1) = (115+3)/120 \*100 = 98.3%

# 29. Success Rate (SR) of students in the stipulated period of the program under NBA Criterion 4 (Students’ Performance)

## 1. Definition

- Success Rate (SR) measures how many students actually complete the program on time (within the stipulated duration: 4 years UG, 2 years PG, etc.), compared to how many were admitted in that batch.

- It reflects academic quality, teaching effectiveness, and student progression efficiency.

## 2. Formula for Success Rate (per batch)

SR=No. of students who graduated within course duration/(N1 + N2 + N3 + N5 – N6)

## **Where:**

- N1 = Students admitted in 1st year (adjusted for migration).

- N2 = Lateral entry admissions (2nd year).

- N3 = Separate division admissions (if applicable).

- N5 = Multiple entry admissions (MEES entries).

- N6 = Multiple exit students (MEES exits).

- Numerator = Students who graduated within stipulated period (on time).

## 3. Average SR

- NBA takes the average of Success Rates for the last three graduating batches (LYG, LYGm1, LYGm2).

Average SR=(SRLYG+SRLYGm1+SRLYGm2)/3

## 4. Points Allocation

- Maximum 15 marks are awarded for SR.

- Formula:

SR Points=1.5×Average SR/10

This essentially converts the % success rate into a score out of 15.

## 5. Example Calculation

Suppose a B.Tech program (Sanctioned Intake = 120) →

## Batch LYG (2020–21 entry)

- N1 = 110, N2 = 10, N3= 0, N5 = 2, N6=2

- Denominator = 110+10+0+2-2 = 120

- Graduated on time = 108

- SR (LYG) = 108 ÷ 120 = 90%

## Batch LYGm1 (2019–20 entry)

- Denominator = 118

- Graduated on time = 105

- SR (LYGm1) = 105 ÷ 118 = 89%

## Batch LYGm2 (2018–19 entry)

- Denominator = 115

- Graduated on time = 102

- SR (LYGm2) = 102 ÷ 115 = 88.7%

## Average SR

90+89+88.7/3=89.2%

## SR Points

1.5×89.210=1.5×8.92=13.4

## 6. Interpretation

- High SR (≥90%) → Almost all admitted students finish on time → strong teaching-learning system.

- Moderate SR (70–89%) → Acceptable, but NBA may question dropout reasons (failures, transfers, poor student support).

- Low SR (\< 70%) → Signals serious issues in retention, backlog handling, and program sustainability.

## 7. Documents Required for Verification

- Admission Register (showing N1, N2, N3, N5, N6).

- University Result Gazette / Passing Board Reports (graduates list).

- Degree Award Lists (convocation lists).

- Backlog Clearance Records (to confirm “on-time” graduation).

- Migration/MEES Records (entries and exits).

## 8. Key Takeaway

- Success Rate = Graduates on time ÷ Admitted base (N1+N2+N3+N5–N6).

- Average SR across 3 batches → Converted to points out of 15.

- To secure ≥13–15 points, programs must maintain ≥85–90% success rates.

# 30. The success rate in the stipulated period of a program

Shows the Success Rate (SR) of students who graduate within the stipulated period (on-time) for the last three graduating batches: LYG, LYGm1, LYGm2.

- SR is calculated batch-wise and then averaged to get the program’s Average SR used for NBA scoring.

## Column meanings (per batch)

- A\* (Adjusted admitted base for the batch)

<!-- -->

- N1 (1st-year admissions, migration-adjusted)

<!-- -->

- N2 (lateral entry to 2nd year, incl. leftover seats)

- N3 (separate division, if any)

- N5 (multiple entry via MEES into 2nd/3rd/4th year, if any) – N6 (multiple exit via MEES after 1st/2nd/3rd year, if any).

<!-- -->

- B (On-time graduates)

<!-- -->

- = Number of students from that batch who graduated within the stipulated course duration (e.g., 4 years UG, 2 years PG).

- Includes “with/without backlogs” provided they finish within time.

<!-- -->

- Success Rate (SR)

<!-- -->

- (B / A\*) × 100 → compute SRI, SR2, SR3 for LYG, LYGm1, LYGm2 respectively.

<!-- -->

- Average SR

<!-- -->

- = (SR₁ + SR₂ + SR₃) / 3.

## Special note (the “floor” rule for A\*)

- **If A\* as computed above is less than (N + N2) where:**

<!-- -->

- N = sanctioned intake for that batch’s 1st year (AICTE/Competent Authority).

- N2 = lateral entry (including leftover seats) for that batch.

<!-- -->

- Then set A *= N + N2 (i.e., do not allow A* to drop below N+N2).

- **Purpose:** prevents understating the denominator due to recording gaps, ensuring SR isn’t artificially inflated.

## Step-by-step computation (per batch)

- **Step 1:** Gather N, N1, N2, N3, N5, N6 from Admission Registers and MEES records.

- **step 2:** Compute A\* = N1 + N2 + N3 + N5 - N6.

- **Step 3:** Apply floor rule → if A *\< (N + N2), then A* = N + N2.

- **Step 4:** Count B = on-time graduates from Gazette/Degree Award, validated with backlog-clearance cutoff.

- **Step 5:** Compute SR = (B / A\*) × 100 for each of LYG, LYGm1, LYGm2.

- **Step 6:** Average SR = mean of the three SRs.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers)

- Batch LYG

<!-- -->

- N = 120, N1 = 110, N2 = 10, N3 = 0, N5 = 2, N6 = 2 → A\* (raw) = 120.

<!-- -->

- Batch LYGm1

<!-- -->

- **Floor check:** N + N2 = 120 + 8 = 128 → A\* = 128.

- B = 112 → SR₂ = 112 / 128 × 100 = 87.50%.

<!-- -->

- Batch LYGm2

<!-- -->

- N = 120, N1 = 108, N2 = 12, N3 = 0, N5 = 2, N6 = 1 → A\* (raw) = 121.

- **Floor check:** N + N2 = 120 + 12 = 132 → A\* = 132.

- B = 116 → SR₃ = 116 / 132 × 100 = 87.88%.

<!-- -->

- Average SR

<!-- -->

- = (88.46 + 87.50 + 87.88) / 3 = 87.95% (≈88.0%).

# 32. Academic Performance of the First-Year Students of the Program (10 marks)” under NBA Criterion 4 (Students’ Performance)

- This criterion evaluates the academic quality of intake and teaching effectiveness in the first year.

- It uses an Academic Performance Index (API) to measure how well first-year students performed and progressed to the 2nd year.

- Maximum marks = 10.

## 2. Key Definitions

- **Successful students:** Students who passed the 1st year and are eligible to proceed to the 2nd year.

- **Students appeared:** All students who actually appeared for the 1st year examination (including those who failed).

- **Grade Point Average (GPA):** Cumulative score on a 10-point scale (if available).

- **Percentage marks:** If GPA not available, mean percentage is used.

## 3. Formula for Academic Performance Index (API)

API =Mean GPA of successful students (on 10-point scale OR (Mean % of successful students/10))\* No. of successful students/No. of students appeared

## **Where:**

- First part = quality of academic achievement (GPA/marks).

- Second part = pass rate (progression efficiency).

2\. Find Success Ratio = Successful students ÷ Students appeared.

3\. Multiply the two to get API.

4\. Do this for CAY, CAYm1, CAYm2 (last 3 batches).

5\. Average API = (API₁ + API₂ + API₃) ÷ 3.

6\. Academic Performance Score = Average API (max = 10).

# 45. Placement, Higher Studies and Entrepreneurship (30 marks) under NBA Criterion 4 – Students’ Performance

## 1. Purpose

- NBA evaluates not just students’ performance during the program, but also outcomes after graduation.

- **This sub-criterion measures how many graduates get:**

1\. Placed in jobs (campus/industry recruitment).

2\. Admitted to higher studies (PG. research, foreign universities, etc.).

3\. Startups / Entrepreneurship.

- The combined metric is expressed as a Placement Index (P), and the marks are scaled accordingly.

## 2. Formula

Placement Index Points=0.3×Average Placement Index (P)

## **Where:**

- **P (Placement Index) is calculated year-wise for the last three batches:**

P={Number of students placed + Number of students for higher studies + Number of students as entrepreneurs}/Number of students graduated×100

- Average Placement Index (P)

Pavg=PCAY+PCAYm1+PCAYm2/3

- Placement Index Points (out of 30)

=0.3×Pavg= 0.3 \times P\_{avg}=0.3×Pavg

(So if average P = 80%, then Points = 0.3 × 80 = 24 out of 30)

## 3. Definitions

- Graduated students = Students who completed the program in stipulated time (LYG batch).

- Placed students = Students employed through campus placements or outside offers with proof.

- Higher studies = Students enrolled in PG programs (M.Tech, MS, MBA, PhD, etc.) with admission proof.

- Entrepreneurs = Students who started startups/business ventures with proper registration evidence.

- **Excluded = Students who:**

<!-- -->

- Opted out of placement voluntarily.

- Did not appear for exams or did not graduate.

## 4. Step-by-Step Computation

1\. Collect total graduated students (denominator) → from University Degree Award List.

2\. Count placed students (with offer letters, joining reports).

3\. Count higher studies students (with admission letters).

4\. Count entrepreneurs (with company incorporation/registration proof).

5\. Compute P for each cohort →

P=Placed+Higher Studies+Entrepreneurs/Graduates×100

6\. Compute average P across 3 years (CAY, CAYm1, CAYm2).

7\. Compute Points = 0.3 × Pavg (max 30).

## 5. Example Calculation

**Suppose we have 3 years' data:**

| **Batch**   | **Graduated (G)** | **Placed** | **Higher Studies** | **Entrepreneurs** | **P (%) = (Placed+HS+Ent)/G × 100** |
|-------------|-------------------|------------|--------------------|-------------------|-------------------------------------|
| CAY (2024)  | 120               | 72         | 18                 | 5                 | (72+18+5)/120 × 100 = 79.2%         |
| CAYm1(2023) | 115               | 70         | 15                 | 3                 | (70+15+3)/115 × 100 = 76.5%         |
| CAYm2(2022) | 110               | 65         | 20                 | 2                 | (65+20+2)/110 × 100 = 78.2%         |

- Average P = (79.2 + 76.5 + 78.2)/3 = 78.0%

- Placement Index Points = 0.3 × 78.0 = 23.4 ≈23/30

## 6. Supporting Documents Required

- **Placement records:** offer letters, joining reports, company HR confirmation.

- **Higher studies records:** admission letters, enrollment certificates.

- **Entrepreneurship proofs:** company registration, GST/Startup India certificate.

- **Graduation list:** University Degree Award List to confirm denominator.

- **Summary sheets:** student-wise mapping with status.

## 7. Common Pitfalls

- Counting students with multiple offers more than once → count only once.

- Counting students without proof of placement/admission → not allowed.

- Including students who did not graduate in denominator → wrong.

- Ignoring entrepreneurs due to lack of formal evidence → ensure proper documentation.

# 46. Placement, Higher Studies and Entrepreneurship Step-by-Step Calculation

- Scope of the Metric

<!-- -->

- This metric evaluates the effectiveness of an institution in providing career opportunities and post-graduation pathways to its students.

- **It considers three major aspects:**

1\. Placement – Students placed through campus recruitment or other

institutional support.

2\. Higher Studies – Students who pursue postgraduate education in India

or abroad.

3\. Entrepreneurship – Students who start their own ventures or become

self-employed after graduation.

- Placement Index Formula

<!-- -->

- Placement Index (PI) = (No. of Students Placed / No. of Students Passed in the Program) × 100

- Higher Study Index (HSI) = (No. of Students Admitted to Higher Studies/ No. of Students Passed) × 100

- Entrepreneurship Index (EI) = (No. of Students Turned Entrepreneurs / No. of Students Passed) × 100

- Combined Value P = PI + HSI + EI

<!-- -->

- Placement Index Points Calculation

<!-- -->

- **Once the combined average index (P) is obtained, the score is calculated as:**

<!-- -->

- Placement Index Points = 0.3 × Average Placement Index (P)

<!-- -->

- Step-by-Step Calculation

1\. Collect data for the last three consecutive academic years (CAYm1, CAYm2,

CAYm3).

**2. For each year:**

- Identify the number of students who passed (Npass).

- Record number of students placed (Nplaced).

- Record number of students admitted to higher studies (Nhigher).

- Record number of entrepreneurs (Nentre).

**3. Calculate:**

- PI = (Nplaced / Npass) × 100

- HSI = (Nhigher/ Npass) × 100

- EI = (Nentre / Npass) × 100

**4. Add them:** P = PI + HSI + EI

5\. Take the average of P across three years.

6\. Multiply by 0.3 → Final Points

- Example Calculation

**Suppose:**

- **Year 1:** Npass=200, Nplaced=100, Nhigher=40, Nentre=10 → P₁ = (100/200 × 100) + (40/200 × 100) + (10/200 × 100) = 50 + 20 + 5 = 75

- **Year 2:** Npass=220, Nplaced=110, Nhigher=44, Nentre=6 → P2 = 50 + 20 + 2.7 = 72.7

- **Year 3:** Npass=210, Nplaced=90, Nhigher=60, Nentre=9 → P3 = 42.9 + 28.6 + 4.3 = 75.8

- Average P = (75 + 72.7 + 75.8)/3 = 74.5

- Placement Index Points = 0.3 × 74.5 = 22.35 / 30

<!-- -->

- Documentation / Evidence Required

<!-- -->

- Placement records verified by Training & Placement Cell.

- Offer letters or appointment letters of placed students.

- Admission letters of students pursuing higher studies.

- Proof of entrepreneurship (Company registration, GST number, Startup India/lncubation records, self-declaration).

<!-- -->

- Yearly Basis (LYG, LYGm1, LYGm2)

<!-- -->

- LYG → Latest Year of Graduates (most recent batch).

- LYGm1 → One year prior to LYG.

- LYGm2 → Two years prior to LYG.

- Data is always considered for three consecutive graduating batches.

<!-- -->

- Definitions of Terms

<!-- -->

- FS\* = Total number of final-year students (eligible to graduate).

<!-- -->

- If FS recorded in the table is less than (N + N2), replace FS with (N + N2).

- N = Sanctioned intake for that batch.

- N2 = Lateral entry students including leftover seats.

- This ensures denominator = official capacity (sanctioned intake + lateral entry).

<!-- -->

- X = Number of students placed (campus placements, job offers accepted).

- Y = Number of students admitted to higher studies (PG in India or abroad).

- Z = Number of students taking up entrepreneurship (startup, self-employment).

- X + Y + Z = Total positive outcomes achieved for that year.

<!-- -->

- Formula for Placement Index (per year)

<!-- -->

- P = ((X + Y + Z) / FS\*) × 100

- **Where:**

<!-- -->

- Numerator (X+Y+Z) = Students with successful outcomes.

- Denominator (FS\*) = Total final-year students (or N+N2, whichever is higher).

<!-- -->

- This formula ensures all three career outcomes are counted together.

<!-- -->

- Yearly Indices

<!-- -->

- P₁ = Placement Index for LYG

- P2 = Placement Index for LYGm1.

- P₃ = Placement Index for LYGm2.

<!-- -->

- Average Placement Index

<!-- -->

- Average P = (P1+P2+P3)/3(P₁ + P₂ + P₃)/3(P1+P2+P3)/3.

- This gives a balanced three-year average outcome performance.

<!-- -->

- Points Calculation (NBA weightage = 30 points)

<!-- -->

- Final Points = 0.3 × Average Placement Index

- **Example:** If Average P = 70, then Points = 0.3 × 70 = 21 (out of 30).

- Maximum allowed score = 30 points.

<!-- -->

- Worked Example

<!-- -->

- **LYG:** FS\*=240, X=120, Y=36, Z=12 → (X+Y+Z)=168 → P₁=(168/240)×100=70.0

- **LYGm1:** FS\*=220, X=99, Y=44, Z=11 → (X+Y+Z)=154 → P₂=(154/220)×100=70.0

- **LYGm2:** FS\*=200, X=78, Y=50, Z=8 → (X+Y+Z)=136 → P₃=(136/200)×100=68.0

- Average P = (70 + 70+ 68)/3 = 69.33

- Final Points = 0.3 × 69.33 = 20.8/30

<!-- -->

- Evidence Requirements

<!-- -->

- **X (Placement):** Appointment/offer letters, company joining proofs.

- **Y (Higher Studies):** Admission/enrollment letters, fee receipts.

- **Z (Entrepreneurship):** Startup registration, incubation proof, Udyam/GST/ROC documents.

- **FS\*/N/N2:** University/AICTE intake sanction letters and admission records.

<!-- -->

- Validation & Integrity Rules

<!-- -->

- X+Y+Z ≤ FS\* (cannot exceed total students).

- **Unique counting:** A student can be counted in only one category.

- **FS\* correction rule:** If FS \< N+N2, replace with (N+N2).

- **Consistent data across 3 years:** avoid missing records.

<!-- -->

- Impact on Institutional Score

<!-- -->

- Higher placement, postgraduate admissions, and entrepreneurship increase P.

- Consistency across three years stabilizes average P and boosts NBA scoring.

# 75. Acceptance Letters and DOI/ISBN Details

## 1. Purpose of Acceptance Letters

- Acceptance letters serve as proof of approval for a paper/article submitted by a student to a journal or conference.

- **They are crucial when:**

<!-- -->

- The publication is “In Press” but not yet released.

- The student has presented work but the proceedings are not yet printed.

<!-- -->

- NBA evaluators accept these as valid evidence if the actual copy of publication is not available yet.

## **Contents of a Good Acceptance Letter:**

- Title of the paper.

- Name(s) of student author(s).

- Journal/Conference name.

- Confirmation of acceptance (with dates).

- Event details (conference date/venue or journal issue).

- Editor/Organizer signature/seal.

## 2. Purpose of DOI / ISBN / ISSN Details

- To provide traceability and authenticity of student publications.

- NBA requires standard bibliographic identifiers to confirm publications are genuine.

## **Identifiers to Include:**

1\. DOI (Digital Object Identifier) – unique ID for journal/conference papers published

online.

- **Example:** doi.org/10.1109/XYZ.2023.1234567

- Mandatory for most indexed publications (IEEE, Elsevier, Springer).

2\. ISSN (International Standard Serial Number) – for journals and serial publications.

- **Example:** ISSN 2395-0056 (Print), ISSN 2582-1234 (Online).

3\. ISBN (International Standard Book Number) – for conference proceedings, books,

book chapters.

- **Example:** ISBN 978-93-5407-123-4

## 3. Why NBA Evaluators Look for These

- Acceptance Letters = confirm that student publications are real and approved, not just claimed.

- DOI/ISSN/ISBN Details = establish authenticity and global recognition of the publication.

- **Together, they ensure:**

<!-- -->

- Publications are peer-reviewed or professionally recognized.

- Publications are verifiable online (especially DOIS in Scopus/WoS).

- Hyperlink scanned acceptance letters and online DOI pages for easy verification.
